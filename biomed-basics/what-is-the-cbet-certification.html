What Is the CBET Certification?

What the Certified Biomedical Equipment Technician credential means, who it is for, and what the exam actually covers

If you work in biomedical equipment technology long enough, you will probably hear someone mention:

Back to Biomed Basics

What Does CBET Stand For?

Certified Biomedical Equipment Technician.

The credential is commonly written after a person's name.

Example:

Jane Smith, CBET

What Does a CBET Do?

A CBET may work with a broad range of healthcare technology.

Examples include:

  • Patient monitors
  • Infusion pumps
  • Defibrillators
  • Ventilators
  • Anesthesia machines
  • Diagnostic equipment
  • Networked medical devices

The role can involve:

  • Preventive maintenance
  • Corrective maintenance
  • Calibration
  • Troubleshooting
  • Documentation
  • Medical-device networking
  • Clinical support

AAMI describes CBET candidates as working across electromechanical devices, computers, networks, and software used in healthcare. citeturn906531view0

Is CBET Required to Work as a Biomed?

Not universally.

Many biomedical equipment technicians work without CBET certification.

Employer requirements vary.

Some organizations may:

  • Prefer it
  • Reward it
  • Require it for certain advancement
  • Require certification after a certain period of employment

Others may not require it at all.

Certification vs Education

CBET certification should not be confused with a biomed degree.

A degree is an educational credential.

CBET is a professional certification.

Someone may have:

Associate degree + CBET

or:

Military training + CBET

or another qualifying background.

Certification vs OEM Training

CBET also does not mean:

Certified to service every medical device.

An OEM service certificate may show that someone completed manufacturer training on a specific product.

CBET is much broader.

What Does the CBET Exam Cover?

AAMI's current CBET content outline divides the exam into six broad domains:

  • Anatomy & Physiology — approximately 7%
  • Fundamentals of Electricity and Electronics — approximately 6%
  • Healthcare Information Technology — approximately 17%
  • Healthcare Technology and Function — approximately 30%
  • Healthcare Technology Problem Solving — approximately 30%
  • Public Safety in the Healthcare Facility — approximately 10% citeturn906531view0

That tells you something important about the exam.

It is not primarily an electronics test.

Healthcare Technology and Function

This is currently one of the largest portions of the exam.

It can include understanding equipment such as:

  • Patient monitors
  • Infusion pumps
  • Defibrillators
  • Ventilators
  • Anesthesia machines
  • Laboratory equipment
  • Perioperative equipment

The goal is broad familiarity with how healthcare technology works. citeturn906531view0

Problem Solving

Another approximately 30% of the current outline focuses on healthcare technology problem solving. citeturn906531view0

That should be familiar to working biomeds.

It involves things such as:

  • Fault isolation
  • Troubleshooting
  • Risk prioritization
  • Distinguishing device problems from use problems
  • Network troubleshooting
  • Communication

Healthcare IT

Networking and information technology currently account for approximately 17% of the published exam outline. citeturn906531view0

Topics can include:

  • IP addresses
  • MAC addresses
  • Wired networks
  • Wireless networks
  • Switches
  • Access points
  • Device integration
  • Cybersecurity concepts

Modern biomed is not just electronics.

Safety

The safety domain includes concepts involving:

  • Electrical safety
  • Infection control
  • Healthcare regulations
  • Standards
  • CMMS use

A working technician should understand not only how to repair equipment but how that equipment exists within a healthcare system. citeturn906531view0

Anatomy and Physiology

You do not need to become a clinician.

But you need enough physiology to understand the equipment.

Examples include understanding basic function of:

  • Heart
  • Lungs
  • Brain
  • Circulatory system
  • Respiratory system

The current exam outline assigns approximately 7% to this domain. citeturn906531view0

Electronics

Interestingly, basic electricity and electronics currently represent approximately 6% of the CBET outline. citeturn906531view0

That does not mean electronics are unimportant in real biomed work.

It means the certification is testing a much broader HTM skill set.

How Long Is the CBET Exam?

As of 2026, AAMI states that the CBET examination is:

165 multiple-choice questions

with a:

Three-hour time limit

and it is closed book. citeturn906531view0

How Is Passing Determined?

AAMI states that performance is evaluated against a predetermined standard rather than by ranking candidates against each other.

The current program uses a criterion-referenced approach and a modified Angoff process to establish the passing standard. citeturn906531view0

In plain English:

You are not trying to beat the people sitting next to you.

You are trying to demonstrate that you meet the required competency standard.

Who Can Take the CBET Exam?

There are different paths depending on education, military training, and BMET experience.

AAMI also currently distinguishes between:

  • Full-status eligibility
  • Candidate status

The exact requirements are important enough that they deserve their own article.

What Is Candidate Status?

Candidate status allows some people who do not yet have enough experience for full certification to take the exam earlier.

If they pass, they still have to satisfy the required experience conditions before receiving full certification.

AAMI currently gives successful candidate-status applicants five years to satisfy the requirements for full certification. citeturn906531view0

Is CBET an Entry-Level Credential?

Not exactly.

The exam covers broad BMET knowledge.

Full certification also requires professional experience.

For someone very early in the field, AAMI also offers the separate:

Certified Associate in Biomedical Technology, or CABT, credential.

Why Do Biomeds Pursue CBET?

Common reasons may include:

  • Employer requirement
  • Career advancement
  • Professional development
  • Personal milestone
  • Demonstrating broad field knowledge

Does CBET Make Someone a Better Technician?

The credential itself does not magically improve troubleshooting ability.

Preparing for it can encourage someone to strengthen weak areas.

But certification and skill are not identical.

Experienced Technician Without CBET

Someone can be an excellent technician without CBET.

They may have:

  • Decades of experience
  • Extensive OEM training
  • Strong troubleshooting skills

The absence of the credential does not erase that.

CBET Without Deep Experience on Every Device

The reverse is also true.

A CBET is not automatically an expert on:

  • Every ventilator
  • Every anesthesia machine
  • Every imaging system

The exam evaluates broad professional knowledge.

Professional Certification Is a Snapshot

Passing demonstrates that you met the certification standard at the time required.

Maintaining certification also involves ongoing requirements defined by the certification body.

What Did You Actually Prove?

If someone has CBET after their name, you proved:

They met the certification program's requirements and earned the credential.

You did not prove:

They know every medical device.

You did not prove:

They will diagnose every problem faster than someone without the credential.

But the credential provides evidence of:

  • Broad HTM knowledge
  • Professional experience or qualifying pathway
  • Successful completion of a standardized exam process

Final Thoughts

CBET is one of the best-known professional certifications associated with biomedical equipment technicians.

It covers much more than electronics.

The current outline reflects the modern reality of HTM:

Medical Equipment

+

Troubleshooting

+

Networking

+

Safety

+

Physiology

A strong technician still needs real-world experience.

But CBET can provide a useful professional benchmark for the broad knowledge expected of someone working in biomedical equipment technology.
