What Is the CLES Certification?

What the Certified Laboratory Equipment Specialist credential means and why you may still see CLES even though it is no longer listed as a current AAMI exam

If you have worked in HTM for a while, you may have seen a credential written as:

Back to Biomed Basics

What Does CLES Stand For?

Certified Laboratory Equipment Specialist.

The credential was associated with HTM professionals specializing in laboratory equipment.

What Kind of Equipment Did CLES Cover?

Laboratory equipment can include systems such as:

  • Centrifuges
  • Incubators
  • Refrigerators
  • Freezers
  • Microscopes
  • Chemistry analyzers
  • Hematology analyzers
  • Blood gas analyzers
  • Water baths
  • Cryostats
  • Microtomes

The exact equipment mix varies considerably between laboratories.

Laboratory Equipment Is a Specialty

A general biomed may understand:

  • Electronics
  • Safety
  • Troubleshooting

But laboratory equipment adds areas involving:

  • Fluidics
  • Optics
  • Reagents
  • Temperature control
  • Precision mechanics
  • Sample handling

Large Laboratory Analyzers

Modern analyzers may combine:

  • Robotics
  • Pumps
  • Valves
  • Optical systems
  • Barcode readers
  • Computers
  • Network interfaces

That makes them complex electromechanical systems.

Why Specialized Knowledge Matters

A chemistry analyzer failure could involve:

  • Sample aspiration
  • Reagent delivery
  • Photometric measurement
  • Temperature
  • Calibration

A technician needs to understand more than:

The machine has power.

CLES as a Legacy Credential

AAMI's current certification pages list active pathways such as:

  • CABT
  • CBET
  • CRES
  • CHTM
  • CISS

CLES is not listed as a current new-exam certification.

However, AAMI still references existing CLES credential holders in current certification materials.

Existing CLES Holders Still Matter

Current AAMI materials still recognize CLES when discussing:

  • Existing credentials
  • Management-certification eligibility
  • Legacy certification status

For example, current CHTM eligibility information recognizes CLES alongside credentials such as:

  • CBET
  • CRES
  • CCE

for one of its management-certification pathways.

Why You Still See CLES After Someone's Name

Someone may have earned CLES when the examination was available.

That credential does not suddenly become meaningless because the exam is no longer offered to new candidates.

Active vs Legacy Certification

This distinction is useful.

Active certification program

means new candidates can currently apply and test for it.

Legacy credential

means people may still legitimately hold the designation even though new testing is no longer offered.

Do Not Tell an Existing CLES Holder Their Credential Is Fake

CLES is a legitimate historical HTM credential.

The important point is:

It is not currently a standard new-certification path offered by AAMI.

Why Was CLES Created?

The basic idea was similar to CRES.

CBET covers broad biomedical equipment knowledge.

A specialty credential could recognize someone whose work was concentrated in:

Laboratory equipment.

Laboratory Equipment vs General Biomed

General biomed might include:

  • Monitors
  • Pumps
  • Defibrillators
  • Ventilators

Laboratory support might include:

  • Centrifuges
  • Analyzers
  • Incubators
  • Refrigerators

The troubleshooting foundation overlaps.

The technology does not always.

Centrifuges

A centrifuge may involve:

  • Motor
  • Speed control
  • Rotor recognition
  • Lid lock
  • Imbalance detection

Incubators

An incubator may involve:

  • Temperature control
  • Humidity
  • CO2 regulation
  • Fans

Refrigerators and Freezers

Laboratory refrigeration can be clinically critical because it may store:

  • Samples
  • Reagents
  • Blood products

depending on the application.

Analyzers

Modern laboratory analyzers may contain multiple subsystems.

Think:

Sample Handling

↓

Fluidics

↓

Reaction

↓

Measurement

↓

Software Result

A failure can occur anywhere in that chain.

Optics

Laboratory devices may use:

  • Photometers
  • Spectrophotometry
  • Optical sensors

to measure chemical reactions.

Fluidics

Many analyzers move very small quantities of:

  • Sample
  • Reagent
  • Wash solution

through:

  • Pumps
  • Valves
  • Tubing

Air leaks, restrictions, and contamination can create significant errors.

Temperature

Chemical reactions often depend strongly on temperature.

A small temperature error may matter much more than it would in some general-purpose equipment.

Calibration and Quality Control

Laboratory equipment operates in an environment where:

  • Calibration
  • Controls
  • Quality assurance

are central to patient-result reliability.

Lab Results Are Clinical Data

A device does not need to physically connect to a patient to create serious clinical risk.

A laboratory analyzer can generate information used to make treatment decisions.

That makes measurement accuracy critical.

Networking

Modern laboratory systems also connect to:

  • LIS
  • Middleware
  • Hospital networks

LIS

LIS generally means:

Laboratory Information System.

The analyzer may produce a correct result but still fail to transmit it.

Device vs Interface

If:

Analyzer result appears locally.

But:

Result does not appear in the LIS.

The measurement system may be fine.

Look downstream.

Service Model

Laboratory equipment is often heavily supported through:

  • OEM contracts
  • Specialized field service

That can affect how much in-house biomeds work directly on complex analyzers.

Why CLES May Have Become Less Common

HTM roles evolve.

Some specialty equipment is increasingly supported through:

  • OEM service
  • Dedicated specialists
  • Broader certification paths

The fact that the CLES examination is no longer currently offered does not mean laboratory technology stopped mattering.

Laboratory Equipment Still Appears in CBET

AAMI's current CBET outline specifically includes understanding and troubleshooting laboratory equipment such as:

  • Centrifuges
  • Incubators
  • Refrigerators
  • Freezers
  • Microscopes
  • Water baths
  • Analyzers
  • Cryostats
  • Microtomes

So modern CBET candidates still need some laboratory-equipment knowledge.

What Certification Should a Lab-Focused Biomed Pursue Today?

Because CLES is not currently listed as a new AAMI examination, someone entering the profession today should look at currently available credentials such as:

CBET

for broad BMET certification.

Other training may come from:

  • OEM education
  • Employer training
  • Laboratory-system specialization

Should Your Site Still Have a CLES Article?

Yes.

Because people still encounter:

CLES

in:

  • Job descriptions
  • Résumés
  • Existing credentials
  • HTM documentation

Someone searching:

What does CLES mean?

should be able to find a clear explanation.

This Is Also a Good Example of Why Certification Information Needs Dates

An old article may say:

Apply for the CLES exam.

That may have been correct when written.

It is misleading today.

Certification content should always indicate when it was reviewed.

CLES vs CRES

CLES:

Laboratory equipment specialty.

CRES:

Radiology/imaging specialty.

CRES remains a current AAMI examination.

CLES currently does not.

CLES vs CBET

CLES historically represented laboratory specialization.

CBET represents broader biomedical equipment knowledge and remains an active certification path.

Existing CLES and Career Advancement

An existing CLES credential may still have professional relevance.

AAMI's current CHTM eligibility information recognizes a current CLES certification as one possible qualifying professional credential when paired with the required management experience.

What Did You Actually Prove?

If someone has CLES after their name, you proved:

They earned the Certified Laboratory Equipment Specialist credential through the certification program when that pathway was available.

You should not assume:

They can currently retake or newly apply for the CLES examination.

And you should not tell a new technician:

Go sign up for CLES

without first checking whether the certification has been reintroduced.

A Useful Way to Think About CLES Today

Think:

Legitimate credential

but:

Legacy certification pathway.

Existing holders may still use it.

New technicians should review currently available certification options.

Final Thoughts

CLES is an important part of HTM certification history.

It recognized technicians who specialized in a part of healthcare technology that can be extremely complex:

Laboratory equipment.

That equipment combines:

  • Electronics
  • Mechanics
  • Fluidics
  • Optics
  • Computers
  • Networking

and directly affects clinical laboratory results.

But certification programs change.

As of August 2026, AAMI still recognizes CLES as an existing credential in current materials, but it does not list CLES among its current new-candidate certification exams.

That distinction is worth making clear whenever the credential is discussed.

— Jake

Important Note

This article reflects publicly available AAMI information reviewed in August 2026. CLES is referenced by AAMI as an existing credential, but it is not currently listed among AAMI's active new-candidate certification examinations. Certification programs can change, so readers interested in a future or reinstated CLES pathway should verify current information directly with AAMI.
