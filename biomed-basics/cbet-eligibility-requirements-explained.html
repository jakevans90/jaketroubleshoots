CBET Eligibility Requirements Explained

The difference between full-status CBET eligibility and candidate status in plain English

CBET eligibility can look more confusing than the exam itself.

Back to Biomed Basics

The Simple Version

As of August 2026, AAMI lists three paths to full-status CBET eligibility.

You must meet one of the following:

Path 1

Associate degree or higher in an electronics, technology, or science program

+

Two years of full-time BMET work experience.

Path 2

Completion of a U.S. military biomedical equipment technology program

+

Two years of full-time BMET work experience.

Path 3

Four years of full-time BMET work experience. citeturn906531view0

Full Status

Full status means you meet the eligibility requirements for the complete CBET credential.

You are not simply taking the exam early.

Path 1: Degree + Experience

The first current route combines:

  • Associate degree or higher in an electronics, technology, or science program
  • Two years of full-time BMET work experience citeturn906531view0

This is likely a common path for graduates of:

  • Biomedical Equipment Technology
  • Biomedical Engineering Technology
  • Electronics Technology

programs.

Does the Degree Have to Say “Biomedical”?

AAMI's currently published wording is broader than that.

It says:

Electronics, technology, or science program. citeturn906531view0

Applicants should still verify their individual education with AAMI rather than assuming a program qualifies.

Path 2: Military Training + Experience

The second full-status path currently requires:

  • Completion of a U.S. military biomedical equipment technology program
  • Two years of full-time BMET experience citeturn906531view0

Path 3: Experience Only

The third route currently allows:

Four years of full-time BMET work experience. citeturn906531view0

This means a college degree is not the only route to full-status eligibility under the current requirements.

Candidate Status

Candidate status exists for applicants who want to pursue certification but have not yet accumulated everything required for full status.

Successful candidates are currently given:

Five years

to satisfy the minimum full-certification requirements. citeturn906531view0

Who Can Test Under Candidate Status?

To test as a candidate, AAMI currently says an applicant must meet at least one of the following by the application deadline:

  • Associate degree or higher in an electronics, technology, or science program
  • Completion of a U.S. military biomedical equipment technology program
  • Two years of full-time BMET work experience citeturn906531view0

Example 1

You have:

Associate degree in Biomedical Equipment Technology.

Experience:

Six months.

You do not yet satisfy the current full-status degree-plus-two-years pathway.

But the degree itself may satisfy the minimum requirement to test through candidate status.

Example 2

You have:

No degree.

Experience:

Two years full-time as a BMET.

That currently meets one of the candidate-status minimum pathways.

It does not yet meet the four-year experience-only requirement for full status. citeturn906531view0

Example 3

You have:

Four years full-time BMET experience.

No degree.

Under AAMI's currently published requirements, that satisfies one of the full-status eligibility paths. citeturn906531view0

Candidate Status Does Not Remove the Experience Requirement

This is important.

Passing the exam through candidate status does not automatically mean:

I now qualify for full CBET certification regardless of experience.

You still have to satisfy the full-certification eligibility conditions within the allowed period.

Why Candidate Status Exists

It lets someone demonstrate the examination portion earlier in their career.

Then experience can continue accumulating.

Work Experience Matters

CBET is not designed solely as an academic test.

The eligibility requirements recognize that working on healthcare technology is part of professional competency.

What Counts as BMET Experience?

Do not guess.

Job titles vary widely.

Someone may be called:

  • BMET
  • Biomedical Technician
  • Clinical Engineering Technician
  • Field Service Technician

while doing very similar work.

If your experience is unusual or your title does not clearly identify BMET duties, verify eligibility directly with AAMI.

Documentation

AAMI reviews applications to determine eligibility and documentation requirements.

The current candidate handbook states that applications are reviewed for supporting information and documentation before approval. citeturn901135search20

Do Not Wait Until Exam Week

Eligibility review occurs before you sit for the certification.

Know which path you intend to use before applying.

Testing Windows

As of August 2026, AAMI lists CBET among the certification examinations offered during scheduled May and November testing windows.

For example, its currently published calendar lists the November 1–15, 2026 testing window and future May 2027 window. citeturn901135search1

Check the current schedule before applying because deadlines change.

Candidate Status vs CABT

Do not confuse:

CBET Candidate Status

with:

CABT.

CABT is a separate certification.

Candidate status is a pathway associated with eventually earning full CBET certification.

Degree vs Certification Eligibility

Having a degree can help satisfy an eligibility path.

It does not automatically give you CBET.

You still have to:

  • Apply
  • Be approved
  • Pass the exam
  • Meet full-status requirements

Four Years of Experience Does Not Mean Four Years of Expertise

Eligibility criteria establish a minimum pathway.

They do not guarantee equal experience.

One technician may spend four years primarily servicing:

  • Infusion pumps

while another works across:

  • OR
  • ICU
  • Imaging support
  • Networking

Both may have valuable but different experience.

Check Current Requirements Before Building a Career Plan Around Them

Certification programs can change:

  • Eligibility
  • Exam content
  • Fees
  • Deadlines

Do not rely on a ten-year-old forum post.

Use current certification information.

What Did You Actually Prove?

If someone says:

I am eligible to sit for the CBET exam.

You still need to know:

Full status or candidate status?

Those are different.

If someone passes the exam through candidate status:

You proved:

They passed the examination component.

You have not automatically proven:

They have completed the experience requirements for full CBET certification.

A Useful Eligibility Checklist

Ask:

Do I have a qualifying associate degree or higher?

Did I complete a U.S. military biomedical equipment program?

How many years of full-time BMET experience do I have?

Then determine whether you appear to fit:

Full Status

or:

Candidate Status.

Finally:

Verify it directly with AAMI.

Final Thoughts

CBET eligibility becomes much easier to understand when you separate:

Taking the exam

from:

Meeting the requirements for full certification.

As of August 2026, full status can currently be reached through:

Degree + two years

or:

Military BMET training + two years

or:

Four years of BMET experience.

Candidate status provides an earlier testing pathway for people who meet one of AAMI's minimum candidate conditions but have not yet met the complete full-status requirement. citeturn906531view0

Do not plan around rumors.

Check the current published requirements, identify your path, document your experience, and apply when you are ready.

Important Note

This article reflects AAMI's publicly posted CBET eligibility information reviewed in August 2026. Eligibility rules and application procedures can change. AAMI determines whether an individual applicant's education, military training, documentation, and work experience satisfy its requirements.
