What Is ETA’s Biomedical Electronics Technician Certification?

What the BMD credential is, what it covers, and how ETA’s electronics-based certification pathway works

The biomedical equipment field has more than one professional certification path.

Back to Biomed Basics

What Does BMD Stand For?

Biomedical Electronics Technician.

ETA classifies BMD as a:

Journeyman-level certification.

Who Is ETA International?

ETA International is a nonprofit technical certification organization founded in 1978.

It offers vendor-neutral credentials across a large number of technical disciplines.

Its certification structure grew primarily from the electronics-technician profession.

That history helps explain the structure of BMD.

BMD Is a Biomedical Specialization

ETA describes biomedical electronics technicians as needing knowledge of:

  • Modern biomedical techniques
  • Medical equipment care
  • Equipment handling
  • Maintenance
  • Professional conduct expected in healthcare

The certification combines:

Electronics knowledge

with:

Biomedical equipment knowledge.

Current BMD Exam Format

As of August 2026, ETA currently lists the BMD exam as:

75 questions

with:

Two hours allowed

and a required passing score of:

75%.

ETA currently lists the exam fee as:

$80.

No hands-on examination is currently required for BMD.

Certification Term

ETA currently lists BMD certification as valid for:

Four years.

Certification maintenance is required.

What Does “Journeyman” Mean?

ETA uses a certification ladder based on traditional electronics-technician progression.

The general pathway includes:

Basic electronics credential

↓

Journeyman specialty

↓

Senior

↓

Master

The BMD certification functions as a biomedical specialty within that system.

Basic Electronics Foundation

ETA's journeyman pathway begins with a basic electronics credential.

The main foundation credential is:

Associate Certified Electronics Technician

commonly written:

CETa.

ETA also recognizes its Electronics Modules as another basic-electronics foundation.

Why Start With Electronics?

The idea is straightforward.

Before specializing in biomedical technology, a technician should understand fundamentals such as:

  • AC
  • DC
  • Analog electronics
  • Digital electronics

Then the technician adds a technical specialty.

For BMD:

That specialty is biomedical electronics.

BMD as a Journeyman Option

ETA describes its Journeyman CET program as combining:

Basic Electronics Certification

+

Journeyman Specialty Option.

A professional is generally expected to have at least two years of combined training or experience to qualify at the journeyman level.

BMD vs CETa

These credentials serve different purposes.

CETa

demonstrates foundational electronics knowledge.

BMD

focuses on applying technical knowledge in biomedical equipment environments.

Think of the Path Like This

CETa

= Electronics foundation

BMD

= Biomedical specialization

Combined within ETA's certification structure, they can form part of the Journeyman CET pathway.

What Does the BMD Exam Cover?

ETA publishes a competency outline rather than expecting candidates to guess what may appear.

Biomedical topics include areas such as:

  • Optical wiring
  • Radiology
  • Test equipment
  • Troubleshooting
  • Operating room equipment
  • Respiratory instrumentation
  • Laboratory instrumentation
  • Electrosurgery
  • Intensive and coronary care
  • Cardiac support systems
  • Bioelectric amplifiers
  • Human nervous system
  • Medical electrodes
  • Cables
  • Computers
  • Transducers
  • Medical electronics safety
  • Hemodialysis
  • Networking
  • Medical ultrasound

This is a broad range of biomedical technology.

Troubleshooting

A biomedical technician needs more than memorized device names.

The BMD competency includes:

Troubleshooting.

That is important because real biomed work revolves around identifying:

  • Symptoms
  • Causes
  • Failed subsystems

Test Equipment

A technician should understand test tools used to evaluate equipment.

Examples may include:

  • Multimeters
  • Biomedical analyzers
  • Electrical measurement tools

Knowing how to use test equipment is part of understanding how to prove whether equipment is functioning correctly.

Operating Room Equipment

OR equipment can include systems such as:

  • Electrosurgical generators
  • Physiological monitoring
  • Specialized surgical equipment

The operating room also introduces unique safety considerations.

Respiratory Instrumentation

Biomedical technicians may encounter equipment involving:

  • Ventilation
  • Pressure
  • Flow
  • Oxygen

Understanding respiratory principles helps make the equipment easier to troubleshoot.

Medical Laboratory Equipment

The biomedical field is not limited to bedside devices.

Laboratory equipment may include:

  • Centrifuges
  • Analyzers
  • Incubators
  • Temperature-controlled equipment

Electrosurgery

Electrosurgical units generate high-frequency electrical energy for surgical cutting and coagulation.

A technician should understand:

  • Basic operating principles
  • Accessories
  • Safety
  • Output verification

Intensive and Coronary Care

ICU and cardiac-care environments contain equipment such as:

  • Patient monitors
  • Defibrillators
  • ECG systems
  • Infusion equipment

These are core areas of general biomedical technology.

Bioelectric Amplifiers

Many medical devices measure extremely small electrical signals from the body.

Examples include:

  • ECG
  • EEG

Bioelectric amplifiers must increase those signals while rejecting unwanted noise.

Electrodes

The measurement chain often begins with an electrode.

Poor electrode contact can create apparent equipment problems such as:

  • Artifact
  • Lead-off conditions
  • Unstable signals

Transducers

A transducer converts one type of physical quantity into an electrical signal or vice versa.

Examples include:

  • Pressure sensor
  • Temperature probe
  • Flow sensor

Understanding transducers is fundamental to biomedical troubleshooting.

Medical Electronics Safety

Medical equipment operates near:

  • Patients
  • Clinicians
  • Conductive fluids

Electrical safety is therefore an important part of biomedical equipment work.

Networking

ETA's biomedical material also includes networking.

That reflects the reality that modern medical equipment may communicate with:

  • Servers
  • Central stations
  • Hospital networks

Modern biomed is no longer purely electronics.

Hemodialysis Equipment

Dialysis equipment combines:

  • Fluid control
  • Pressure
  • Conductivity
  • Temperature
  • Pumps
  • Safety monitoring

It illustrates the breadth of biomedical systems a technician may encounter.

Ultrasound

Ultrasound combines:

  • Transducers
  • Electronics
  • Signal processing
  • Computing

Again, the goal is broad biomedical familiarity rather than mastery of one OEM platform.

BMD Is Vendor-Neutral

The certification is not tied to a specific equipment manufacturer.

It is not:

Philips certified.

or:

GE factory trained.

It evaluates general technical knowledge.

BMD Does Not Replace OEM Training

This distinction matters.

Passing BMD does not mean:

Authorized to perform every repair on every medical device.

Manufacturer training may still be required for specific equipment.

Certification vs Service Authorization

A certification demonstrates professional knowledge.

OEM training may establish product-specific competence or authorization.

They answer different questions.

BMD Does Not Replace Experience

Like any certification:

The exam cannot reproduce years of:

  • Clinical calls
  • Intermittent failures
  • Difficult troubleshooting

Experience still matters.

Who Might Consider BMD?

The certification may be especially attractive to someone with a strong:

  • Electronics
  • Electronics technician
  • Military electronics

background who wants a biomedical specialization.

Someone Already Pursuing CETa

BMD also fits naturally into ETA's certification ladder for someone already working through:

CETa → Journeyman specialty.

Free Retake

ETA currently states that one free retake is included with its certification exams, with the retake completed within the specified allowed period.

Always confirm current testing policy before scheduling.

Testing

ETA currently allows certification exams to be taken through approved Certification Administrators using:

  • Online testing
  • Paper testing

depending on arrangement.

A Certification Administrator must be involved.

Accreditation

ETA states that its technical certifications are accredited through the:

International Certification Accreditation Council, or ICAC.

ETA also describes its certification-maintenance system as aligning with ISO 17024 principles.

Maintenance

Technical knowledge changes.

ETA therefore requires maintenance of certifications such as BMD.

The current BMD term is:

Four years.

Check current ETA maintenance requirements before renewal.

BMD vs Biomedical Degree

BMD is a:

Professional certification.

It is not a college degree.

Someone may have:

Associate degree

+

BMD

or another combination of education and certification.

BMD vs CBET

The certification most likely to be compared with BMD is:

CBET

from AAMI.

They both relate to biomedical equipment technicians.

But their:

  • Certification organizations
  • Eligibility structures
  • Exam designs
  • Professional pathways

are different.

That comparison deserves its own article.

What Did You Actually Prove?

If someone earns BMD, you proved:

They met ETA's certification requirements and passed its biomedical electronics examination.

You did not automatically prove:

They are an expert on every hospital device.

You also did not prove:

They completed OEM-specific training.

But the credential does provide evidence of knowledge across biomedical electronics concepts within ETA's certification framework.

Final Thoughts

ETA's BMD certification represents an electronics-centered route into professional biomedical certification.

Think:

Electronics Fundamentals

+

Biomedical Technology

+

Troubleshooting

+

Safety.

It can make particular sense for technicians who already identify strongly with the electronics side of the profession or who are building through ETA's CET certification ladder.

Like every credential, however, it should complement:

  • Education
  • Hands-on experience
  • OEM training
  • Real troubleshooting

rather than replace them.
