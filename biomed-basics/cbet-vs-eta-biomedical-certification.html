CBET vs ETA Biomedical Certification

How AAMI’s CBET and ETA International’s BMD compare for biomedical equipment technicians

If you are considering professional certification as a biomed, you may encounter two different credentials:

Back to Biomed Basics

The Simple Version

CBET

Certified Biomedical Equipment Technician

Organization:

AAMI

Current exam:

165 questions

Time:

3 hours

Exam structure:

Broad healthcare technology management competency

Full certification requires defined education/experience or experience-only eligibility.

BMD

Biomedical Electronics Technician

Organization:

ETA International

Current exam:

75 questions

Time:

2 hours

Passing score:

75%

Certification structure:

Biomedical specialty within ETA's electronics-technician pathway.

Both Are Professional Certifications

Neither credential is:

  • College degree
  • Government license
  • OEM service school

Both are designed to demonstrate professional technical knowledge.

The Biggest Structural Difference

CBET is built specifically around the:

Healthcare Technology Management profession.

ETA's BMD sits within a broader:

Electronics technician certification system.

That affects how each credential is organized.

CBET

CBET is centered on someone working broadly as a biomedical equipment technician.

Its current examination blueprint includes:

  • Healthcare technology
  • Troubleshooting
  • Healthcare IT
  • Safety
  • Anatomy and physiology
  • Electronics

BMD

BMD starts from the electronics-technician tradition and adds biomedical specialization.

Its competency areas include:

  • Biomedical equipment
  • Electronics
  • Instrumentation
  • Medical safety
  • Networking
  • Troubleshooting

CBET Eligibility

As of August 2026, AAMI currently offers several full-status eligibility paths.

They include:

Associate degree or higher in electronics, technology, or science + two years full-time BMET experience

or:

U.S. military biomedical equipment technology program + two years full-time BMET experience

or:

Four years full-time BMET experience.

CBET Candidate Status

AAMI also has:

Candidate Status.

That allows certain applicants to take the examination before they have completed the full experience requirement.

Successful candidate-status examinees currently have five years to satisfy the full-certification requirements.

BMD Certification Structure

ETA classifies BMD as:

Journeyman.

Its broader Journeyman CET pathway uses a basic electronics credential such as:

CETa

as the foundation before adding a specialty option.

ETA states that a journeyman should generally have two or more years of experience.

This Is a Different Philosophy

AAMI's structure is essentially:

BMET professional experience/education → CBET certification.

ETA's broader ladder is more like:

Electronics foundation → technical specialty → higher electronics certification levels.

Exam Length

Current CBET:

165 questions

Current BMD:

75 questions.

Exam Time

Current CBET:

Three hours.

Current BMD:

Two hours.

Passing Standard

ETA currently publishes a BMD passing score of:

75%.

AAMI does not simply publish CBET as:

Get X percent correct.

AAMI uses a criterion-referenced passing process based on a predetermined competency standard.

Electronics Emphasis

This is one of the interesting differences.

ETA comes from an electronics-technician certification background.

Its certification ladder deliberately establishes:

Basic electronics

before:

Journeyman specialization.

CBET Electronics Weight

On the current CBET exam outline, Fundamentals of Electricity and Electronics represents approximately:

6%.

That surprises some biomeds.

Does That Mean CBET Does Not Care About Electronics?

No.

It means the CBET exam is evaluating a broad modern HTM role.

The technician must also understand:

  • Medical equipment function
  • Troubleshooting
  • Networks
  • Safety
  • Physiology

CBET Healthcare Technology

The current CBET outline assigns approximately:

30%

to Healthcare Technology and Function.

CBET Problem Solving

Another approximately:

30%

is Healthcare Technology Problem Solving.

Together those two domains represent a large portion of the exam.

Healthcare IT

The current CBET outline assigns approximately:

17%

to Healthcare Information Technology.

That reflects how heavily medical equipment now depends on:

  • Networks
  • Servers
  • Software
  • Integration

BMD Biomedical Breadth

ETA's BMD competency includes many equipment and technical areas, including:

  • Operating room equipment
  • Respiratory equipment
  • Laboratory equipment
  • Cardiac systems
  • Transducers
  • Hemodialysis
  • Ultrasound
  • Networking

So BMD is not simply an electronics theory exam either.

Which Is More Recognized?

This can vary by:

  • Employer
  • Region
  • Organization

Do not assume a certification has equal recognition everywhere.

Look at actual job postings.

Hospital HTM Departments

If your goal is:

  • Hospital biomed
  • Clinical engineering
  • Healthcare technology management

check which credential local employers actually mention.

Electronics-Oriented Employers

An employer that already recognizes ETA certifications or values electronics-technician credentials may view BMD differently.

Employer Policy Matters More Than Internet Arguments

If your employer says:

We require CBET.

Then a debate over which credential is theoretically better does not help you.

You need to satisfy the employer requirement.

If Employer Accepts Either

Then compare:

  • Cost
  • Eligibility
  • Content
  • Career goals
  • Renewal requirements

Cost

As of August 2026:

ETA lists BMD at:

$80.

AAMI currently lists CBET exam pricing at:

$410 for members

and:

$460 for nonmembers.

These prices can change.

Price Is a Large Difference

But price alone should not determine the decision.

A more expensive credential is not automatically better.

A cheaper credential is not automatically lower quality.

Ask what the credential does for:

Your career.

Exam Availability

AAMI currently offers CBET during scheduled certification testing windows.

ETA uses approved Certification Administrators and offers its testing through its own testing structure.

That may affect scheduling convenience.

Certification Maintenance

ETA currently lists BMD as requiring maintenance on a:

Four-year certification term.

CBET also requires ongoing recertification under AAMI's certification-maintenance program.

Always compare current continuing-education and renewal requirements before choosing.

Which One Is Better for an Entry-Level Biomed?

Possibly neither immediately.

A brand-new technician may get more value first from:

  • Strong education
  • Hands-on experience
  • Electronics
  • Networking
  • Equipment fundamentals

Certification becomes more meaningful when it builds on real technical competence.

Which One Is Better for an Electronics Technician Moving Into Biomed?

BMD may have an intuitive appeal because ETA's pathway explicitly builds on an electronics-technician foundation.

Which One Is Better for a Hospital BMET?

CBET may align particularly well with the broad HTM responsibilities represented in its exam outline.

But employer recognition should be checked rather than assumed.

Could Someone Earn Both?

Yes.

The credentials do not have to be treated as competitors.

Someone could build:

ETA electronics credentials

and:

CBET

during the same career.

Would Both Be Necessary?

Not necessarily.

Credentials should solve a purpose.

Ask:

What does the second credential add?

If the answer is:

Nothing my employer or career path values,

it may not be worth the effort.

Professional Development vs Collecting Acronyms

Certifications should represent useful development.

Do not pursue credentials simply to create a longer email signature.

Certification Does Not Replace Troubleshooting

Someone can pass either exam and still need years to become a highly experienced technician.

Real equipment does not give you four multiple-choice answers.

Real Troubleshooting

The device says:

Intermittent system failure.

You have to determine:

  • What actually happened
  • What still works
  • What changed
  • What measurement will isolate it

That skill develops through experience.

Certification Can Expose Weak Areas

One of the best reasons to prepare for a broad professional exam is discovering what you do not know.

You might realize:

  • Networking is weak
  • Anatomy is weak
  • Electronics needs review

Studying those gaps can make you a better-rounded technician.

CBET Strength

One obvious strength of CBET is how directly the content outline maps to modern HTM work.

It emphasizes:

  • Medical technology
  • Troubleshooting
  • Networking
  • Safety

BMD Strength

One obvious strength of ETA's approach is its connection to a structured electronics-technician pathway.

It emphasizes building:

Electronics foundation

before:

Technical specialization.

Different Does Not Mean Invalid

It is easy for certification discussions to become:

Mine is better than yours.

That is not particularly useful.

They are different professional frameworks.

Look at the Job You Want

Pull up actual job postings.

Search for:

  • CBET
  • BMD
  • ETA
  • Certification preferred

See what employers in your market are asking for.

Ask Your Employer

If certification is tied to:

  • Promotion
  • Pay
  • Job requirement

get clarification before spending money.

Example

Employer policy:

CBET or equivalent approved certification required.

Ask:

Is ETA BMD considered an approved equivalent?

Get the answer before taking the exam.

Career Portability

Consider whether the credential will still be useful if you:

  • Change hospitals
  • Move states
  • Join an OEM
  • Enter field service

Broader employer recognition may matter to you.

Specialty Direction

If you eventually move toward:

  • Imaging
  • Management

you may also want to consider specialty credentials rather than simply stacking more general biomedical certifications.

A Simple Comparison

CBET

Best thought of as:

Broad healthcare technology management certification for working BMETs.

BMD

Best thought of as:

Biomedical specialization within ETA's electronics-technician certification structure.

What Did You Actually Prove?

If someone holds:

CBET

you proved:

They met AAMI's credential requirements and successfully completed its CBET certification process.

If someone holds:

BMD

you proved:

They successfully completed ETA's biomedical electronics certification process within its technical certification framework.

Neither credential proves:

This technician is better than every technician holding the other credential.

That conclusion requires evidence you do not have.

A Useful Decision Framework

Ask:

Which certification does my employer recognize?

Which aligns with my technical background?

Which fits the career I want?

What are the current eligibility requirements?

What does each exam actually test?

What will maintaining it require?

Then make the decision.

Final Thoughts

CBET and BMD both provide paths for biomedical technicians to demonstrate professional knowledge.

But they come from different traditions.

CBET is deeply centered on:

Healthcare Technology Management.

BMD is part of a broader:

Electronics Technician certification pathway.

For many working hospital biomeds, employer recognition may ultimately be the deciding factor.

Do not choose based solely on:

  • Exam price
  • Number of questions
  • Which acronym sounds better

Choose the credential that best supports:

The work you actually do

and:

The career you are trying to build.

Important Note

This article reflects publicly available AAMI and ETA International certification information reviewed in August 2026. Verify current certification requirements directly with the issuing organization and confirm your employer's accepted credentials before applying.
