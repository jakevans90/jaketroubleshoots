How to Prepare for the CBET Exam

A practical study strategy for biomeds who already work on equipment but need to prepare for a broad certification exam

Studying for the CBET exam is different from learning how to troubleshoot equipment at work.

Back to Biomed Basics

Start With the Official Exam Outline

Before buying ten study guides, start with the current AAMI content outline.

As of August 2026, the approximate weighting is:

Healthcare Technology and Function — 30%

Healthcare Technology Problem Solving — 30%

Healthcare Information Technology — 17%

Public Safety in the Healthcare Facility — 10%

Anatomy & Physiology — 7%

Fundamentals of Electricity and Electronics — 6% citeturn906531view0

Those percentages should influence how you spend your study time.

Do Not Study Every Subject Equally

If one domain represents approximately:

30%

and another represents approximately:

6%,

giving both exactly the same study time may not be efficient.

That does not mean ignoring smaller sections.

It means prioritizing intelligently.

Start With a Baseline Test

Before studying deeply, take:

  • Practice exam
  • Question bank
  • Self-assessment

The goal is not to get a great score.

The goal is to discover:

Where are my weak spots?

Working Experience Creates Uneven Knowledge

A hospital biomed might be excellent at:

  • Infusion pumps
  • Patient monitors
  • Defibrillators

but weak in:

  • Laboratory equipment
  • Anatomy
  • Cybersecurity

Another technician may be strong in:

  • Networking
  • Integration

but weak in:

  • Electronics theory

Your study plan should reflect your actual weaknesses.

Build a Weakness List

After practice questions, create categories.

Example:

Strong

Patient monitoring Infusion Troubleshooting

Moderate

Electronics Safety

Weak

Anatomy Networking Laboratory equipment

Now study strategically.

Healthcare Technology and Function

At approximately 30%, this is a major area. citeturn906531view0

You should understand the general operation of common healthcare technologies.

You do not need service-manual-level knowledge for every manufacturer.

Think in systems.

Patient Monitoring

Know the basic principles behind:

  • ECG
  • NIBP
  • SpO2
  • IBP
  • Temperature
  • Respiration
  • EtCO2

Infusion Equipment

Understand differences between:

  • Large-volume pump
  • Syringe pump
  • Feeding pump
  • PCA

Know concepts such as:

  • Flow control
  • Occlusion
  • Air detection
  • Free flow

Life Support

Understand basic operation of:

  • Ventilators
  • Defibrillators
  • Anesthesia machines
  • External pacing

Perioperative Equipment

Be familiar with:

  • ESUs
  • Surgical tables
  • Lights
  • Sterilization equipment
  • Tourniquets

Diagnostic Equipment

Know the general purpose and function of equipment such as:

  • ECG machines
  • Ultrasound
  • Spirometers
  • Stress systems

Laboratory Equipment

Do not ignore lab equipment simply because you rarely touch it.

The current outline explicitly includes equipment such as:

  • Centrifuges
  • Incubators
  • Refrigerators
  • Freezers
  • Microscopes
  • Analyzers citeturn906531view0

Learn Principles, Not Brands

Do not try to memorize every:

  • Philips
  • GE
  • Dräger
  • Baxter

menu.

Instead understand:

How does this device type work?

That knowledge transfers.

Healthcare Technology Problem Solving

This is another approximately 30% of the current exam. citeturn906531view0

If you troubleshoot equipment every day, this may feel familiar.

But do not become overconfident.

Think Like the Exam

A troubleshooting question may give you:

  • Symptom
  • Test result
  • Several possible actions

Choose the action that logically follows the evidence.

Use the Troubleshooting Process

Think:

Observe

↓

Isolate

↓

Test

↓

Verify

Do not jump straight to replacing parts.

Symptom vs Cause

If:

Fuse blows.

That is a symptom.

It does not prove:

Bad fuse.

Ask what caused excessive current.

Device vs Infrastructure

Know how to distinguish:

  • Device failure
  • Accessory failure
  • Network failure
  • Facility infrastructure problem

Risk Matters

Some questions may require prioritizing equipment based on:

  • Patient risk
  • Clinical impact
  • Downtime

Technical competence includes deciding what should be handled first.

Healthcare Information Technology

The current outline assigns approximately 17% to healthcare IT. citeturn906531view0

Do not leave networking until the night before the test.

Know IP Basics

Understand:

  • IP address
  • Subnet
  • Default gateway
  • MAC address

Know Network Components

Be able to recognize the role of:

  • Switch
  • Router
  • Access point
  • Server

Understand Ping

Ping can demonstrate some level of IP reachability.

It does not prove:

  • Application works
  • Required port is open
  • EMR integration works

Wired vs Wireless

Know basic troubleshooting differences between:

  • Ethernet
  • Wi-Fi

Device Integration

Understand the concept of medical-device data moving toward:

  • Middleware
  • EMR
  • Central monitoring

You do not need to become a network architect.

But modern CBET preparation absolutely includes IT.

Cybersecurity

The current outline also includes protected-data and cybersecurity concepts. citeturn906531view0

Know the importance of:

  • Access control
  • Patching
  • Protected health information
  • Security frameworks

Public Safety

The current safety section is approximately 10%. citeturn906531view0

Study:

  • Electrical safety
  • Infection control
  • PPE
  • Healthcare regulatory concepts
  • CMMS documentation
  • Relevant standards concepts

Understand the Purpose

Do not just memorize acronyms.

Ask:

Why does this requirement exist?

Understanding is easier to retain than isolated memorization.

Anatomy and Physiology

The current exam outline assigns approximately 7% to anatomy and physiology. citeturn906531view0

You do not need medical-school depth.

Focus on the physiology tied to equipment.

Heart

Understand:

  • Basic circulation
  • ECG
  • Electrical conduction
  • Blood pressure

Lungs

Understand:

  • Ventilation
  • Oxygenation
  • CO2 removal

Nervous System

Know basic:

  • Brain
  • Nerves

concepts.

Medical Terminology

Become comfortable recognizing common terms and abbreviations.

Electronics

The current electronics domain is approximately 6%. citeturn906531view0

You should still know the fundamentals cold.

Ohm's Law

Know:

V = I × R

and how to rearrange it.

Power

Know:

P = V × I

Series and Parallel

Understand what happens to:

  • Voltage
  • Current
  • Resistance

in basic circuits.

Active and Passive Components

Know broad functions of:

  • Resistor
  • Capacitor
  • Diode
  • Transistor

Schematics

You should be able to follow a basic circuit path.

Power Supplies

Understand:

  • AC input
  • Rectification
  • DC rails
  • Batteries
  • UPS

at a practical level.

Do Practice Questions

Reading alone creates false confidence.

You can read a chapter and think:

I know that.

Then miss the question.

Practice questions force retrieval.

Review Every Wrong Answer

Do not just look at the score.

For each missed question ask:

Why was my answer wrong?

Why is the correct answer better?

Also Review Guesses You Got Right

If you guessed:

B

and B happened to be correct:

That topic is still a weakness.

Mark it.

Avoid Memorizing Question Banks

The goal is not:

Memorize this exact answer.

The goal is:

Understand the concept well enough to answer a different version.

Build Short Study Sessions

For someone working full-time, consistent study often beats marathon weekends.

Example:

30–45 minutes

several days per week.

Rotate Domains

Example week:

Monday — Equipment Tuesday — Networking Wednesday — Safety Thursday — Anatomy Friday — Practice questions

This keeps broad knowledge active.

Use Your Job as a Study Tool

Working BMETs have an advantage.

When you work on a device, ask:

What CBET concept is this showing me?

Example: NIBP

You are already seeing:

  • Pressure
  • Pneumatics
  • Patient monitoring
  • Troubleshooting

Example: Network Failure

You are seeing:

  • IP
  • Ethernet
  • Device vs infrastructure
  • Problem isolation

Tie study concepts to real equipment.

Teach the Concept

One of the best tests of understanding is whether you can explain it simply.

Try explaining:

How does SpO2 work?

without notes.

If you cannot explain the basic principle, review it.

Build Your Own Cheat Sheet While Studying

Even though the exam is closed book, creating a study sheet is useful.

Include:

  • Formulas
  • Networking definitions
  • Physiology terms
  • Device principles
  • Safety concepts

The act of organizing it helps memory.

Formulas

Do not create a giant formula list.

Know the ones you actually need.

Examples:

V = IR

P = VI

Then understand the units.

Time Management

With 165 questions over three hours, you cannot spend forever on one question. citeturn906531view0

If a question is consuming excessive time:

  • Make your best choice
  • Mark it if the testing system permits
  • Move on

Read the Entire Question

Certification questions may hinge on words like:

  • FIRST
  • BEST
  • MOST LIKELY
  • NEXT

Those words matter.

“First” Does Not Mean “Eventually”

You might eventually replace the board.

But if the question asks what to do first:

The answer may be:

Verify supply voltage.

Eliminate Bad Answers

If you are unsure, eliminate choices that:

  • Skip basic verification
  • Violate safety
  • Do not match the symptom
  • Replace parts without evidence

Then choose among the remaining options.

Do Not Overthink Every Question

Working technicians sometimes create possibilities the exam never intended.

Read the information provided.

Answer the problem given.

Do not invent:

Maybe the hospital had a lightning strike at the exact same moment.

unless evidence points there.

Use Official Resources

AAMI currently recommends starting with the certification content outline and reference material associated with the exam.

It also offers CBET study resources, including study courses and practice tools. citeturn901135search2turn901135search6

You do not necessarily need to purchase every available resource.

But the official outline should be the backbone of your plan.

A Practical 8-Week Approach

Weeks 1–2

Take baseline questions.

Identify weak areas.

Review:

  • Anatomy
  • Electronics

Weeks 3–4

Focus heavily on:

  • Healthcare technology
  • Device principles

Week 5

Focus on:

  • Networking
  • Healthcare IT

Week 6

Focus on:

  • Safety
  • Standards concepts
  • CMMS

Week 7

Focus on:

  • Troubleshooting scenarios
  • Mixed practice questions

Week 8

Review weak areas.

Take timed practice.

Avoid trying to learn the entire field during the final two days.

Adjust the Schedule to Yourself

If networking is already your strongest area:

Spend less time there.

If you barely remember anatomy:

Spend more time there.

Use evidence.

What Should You Memorize?

Memorize things that genuinely require recall.

Examples:

  • Basic formulas
  • Definitions
  • Important terminology

But understand systems whenever possible.

Understanding Beats Memorization

If you understand how:

NIBP works

you can reason through several questions.

If you only memorize:

Oscillometric

you may struggle when the wording changes.

The Night Before

Do not attempt to relearn the entire profession.

Review:

  • Key formulas
  • Weak concepts
  • Basic terminology

Then stop.

Exam-Day Materials

AAMI currently states candidates may bring a simple calculator, while internet-capable or camera-equipped electronic devices are not permitted in the testing room. citeturn906531view0

Always check the current candidate instructions before your exam.

What Did You Actually Prove?

If you score:

90%

on the same practice exam for the fifth time:

You proved:

You know that practice exam very well.

You did not necessarily prove:

You understand the entire CBET content outline.

If you can answer unfamiliar questions and explain why the other answers are wrong:

That is much stronger evidence.

A Useful Study Framework

For each topic ask:

Can I define it?

Then:

Can I explain how it works?

Then:

Can I troubleshoot a basic failure involving it?

Then:

Can I explain why one answer is better than another?

If you can do all four, you probably understand it.

Final Thoughts

Do not study for CBET as though it is a trivia contest.

Study the field.

Focus especially on:

How equipment works

and:

How technicians reason through failures.

Use the official content outline.

Find your weak areas.

Practice retrieving information.

Learn networking.

Understand the major medical-device categories.

Know your fundamentals.

And when you work through troubleshooting questions, use the same question that works on the bench:

What did you actually prove?
