## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots weak or absent pneumatic nebulizer output caused by setup, tubing, nebulizer cup, connection, obstruction, gas supply, or ventilator-output problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Therapy

If nebulized medication is clinically required and delivery is unreliable, arrange an approved alternate medication-delivery method before troubleshooting.

Do not disrupt ventilation to evaluate the nebulizer.

**Expected outcome:** Patient ventilation and required medication therapy remain uninterrupted.

### 2. Confirm the Nebulizer Problem

Determine whether there is no gas output, weak output, intermittent output, or gas output with poor aerosol generation.

Check whether the ventilator indicates nebulizer activation normally.

**Expected outcome:** The symptom is distinguished between ventilator gas output and nebulizer accessory performance.

### 3. Inspect the Nebulizer Setup

Verify that the nebulizer cup, tubing, connectors, and circuit placement are assembled as intended.

Check for loose tubing, incorrect ports, disconnected fittings, damaged connectors, or an empty/incorrectly assembled nebulizer.

**Expected outcome:** The external nebulizer setup is complete and properly connected.

If correcting setup restores aerosol delivery, complete verification and stop.

### 4. Inspect Nebulizer Tubing

Check the pneumatic drive tubing for kinks, cracks, compression, fluid contamination, or blockage.

**Expected outcome:** The tubing is patent and free of visible damage.

If replacing damaged tubing restores output, proceed to final verification.

### 5. Inspect the Nebulizer Cup

Verify that the nebulizer cup is compatible, properly assembled, and not visibly clogged or damaged.

Replace disposable components rather than modifying them.

**Expected outcome:** The nebulizer accessory can generate aerosol when supplied with appropriate drive gas.

### 6. Substitute Known-Good External Components

Use known-good compatible nebulizer tubing and cup when available.

Activate the nebulizer function on the bench.

**Expected outcome:** The problem either follows an external nebulizer component or remains with the ventilator.

If known-good accessories restore normal output, replace the defective component and stop after verification.

### 7. Verify Ventilator Gas Supply and Basic Pneumatic Operation

Confirm the C2 otherwise ventilates normally and has no concurrent gas-supply or pneumatic alarms.

Do not access internal pneumatic regulators or valves.

**Expected outcome:** Basic ventilator pneumatic operation is normal aside from the nebulizer output.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Activate the nebulizer using a controlled test setup and verify consistent gas output and appropriate aerosol generation while normal ventilation continues.

**Expected outcome:** Nebulizer output is stable and does not adversely affect basic ventilator operation.

If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If nebulizer output remains low or absent after setup, tubing, nebulizer cup, connections, and known-good accessories have been verified, the problem may involve an internal pneumatic control valve, gas pathway, output port, or control system.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service if nebulizer functionality is required for intended clinical use
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for qualified bench evaluation
- Tested using Hamilton documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired only by qualified personnel
- Functionally verified before return to service

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not delay required respiratory medication while troubleshooting integrated nebulizer output; use an approved alternate delivery method when necessary.

## Final Thought

Separate accessory failure from ventilator failure by checking setup, tubing, nebulizer components, and basic pneumatic operation first. Escalate persistent loss of output rather than attempting internal pneumatic repair.
