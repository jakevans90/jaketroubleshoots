## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots inadequate turbine output or high-temperature indications caused by airflow restriction, blocked ventilation openings, environmental conditions, or excessive circuit resistance.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Remove the Ventilator From Active Support

Do not continue using a ventilator that cannot reliably generate required airflow or is reporting abnormal temperature while supporting a patient.

Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or approved alternate ventilation method.

**Expected outcome:** The patient receives reliable ventilation and the C2 can be evaluated without clinical risk.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Operate the ventilator on the bench with a known-good test lung and appropriate circuit. Determine whether the problem is low delivered flow, inability to achieve commanded ventilation, a temperature-related message, unusual turbine noise, or a combination.

Document the exact displayed condition.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is reproduced under controlled conditions or determined to have been intermittent.

If the ventilator performs normally through repeated testing, inspect setup and environmental factors before considering return to service.

### 3. Inspect External Airflow Openings

Check externally accessible cooling and air-intake areas for dust accumulation, obstruction, coverings, wall clearance problems, or objects restricting airflow.

Do not open internal turbine or cooling assemblies.

**Expected outcome:** Ventilation and intake openings are clean and unobstructed.

If clearing an external obstruction eliminates the condition and verification passes, troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Check Environmental Conditions

Verify that the ventilator is not positioned near a heat source, inside an enclosed cabinet, directly against bedding or curtains, or in another environment that restricts cooling.

Move the device to a suitable open location and retest.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator operates in an environment allowing normal cooling airflow.

If the high-temperature condition does not recur, complete functional verification and stop.

### 5. Inspect the Breathing Circuit for Excessive Resistance

Check for kinked tubing, occluded filters, saturated humidification components, blocked connectors, improperly installed accessories, or other downstream restrictions.

Use a known-good circuit if needed.

**Expected outcome:** The patient circuit presents no obvious abnormal restriction to turbine output.

If replacing or correcting a restricted external component restores normal performance, complete final verification.

### 6. Listen for Abnormal Turbine Operation

During bench operation, listen for grinding, scraping, surging, repeated acceleration/deceleration, or other unusual sounds. Observe whether the device produces unusual vibration or odor.

Do not continue operating a unit that appears to be mechanically overheating or deteriorating.

**Expected outcome:** Turbine operation is smooth and free of unusual sound, odor, or vibration.

If abnormal mechanical signs are present, stop troubleshooting and remove the ventilator from service.

### 7. Compare Operation With a Known-Good Setup

Use a known-good compatible circuit and test lung to determine whether poor performance follows the external setup or remains with the ventilator.

**Expected outcome:** External resistance or accessory problems are isolated from a ventilator-level performance problem.

If the ventilator performs normally with the known-good setup, correct the external setup and verify operation.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Run the required preoperational checks and ventilate a test lung across appropriate operating conditions. Confirm stable airflow, normal temperature behavior, normal alarm operation, and absence of unusual turbine sound.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator delivers stable ventilation without turbine or temperature faults.

If verification passes consistently, the unit may be returned to service according to facility policy.

## If the Problem Persists

If the turbine cannot provide adequate output or continues to report abnormal temperature after external airflow, environment, circuit resistance, and accessories have been ruled out, the issue may involve the turbine assembly, internal cooling, temperature sensing, pneumatic pathways, or control electronics.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using Hamilton service documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired only by qualified personnel
- Fully performance-tested before return to patient care

Knowing when to stop before internal turbine disassembly is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any ventilator with unstable turbine output should be exchanged before troubleshooting because ventilation performance can deteriorate rapidly under increased patient demand.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, then rule out blocked airflow, environmental heat, circuit restriction, and setup problems before suspecting the turbine itself. Escalate mechanical or persistent temperature problems and document the verified conditions clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
