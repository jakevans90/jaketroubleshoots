## What This Guide Helps With

Helps isolate missing nebulization caused by module recognition, connections, accessories, setup, consumables, positioning, or external nebulizer component failure.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Required Therapy
Do not troubleshoot an unreliable nebulizer system while a patient depends on uninterrupted aerosol medication delivery.

Clinical staff should maintain ventilation and provide medication by an approved alternate method if nebulization cannot be confirmed.

**Expected outcome:** Ventilation continues safely and required medication delivery is not dependent on the malfunctioning nebulizer setup.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition
Determine whether:

- The nebulizer module is not detected
- The module is detected but will not activate
- The system indicates activation but no aerosol is observed
- Nebulization starts and stops intermittently
- The problem occurs with one nebulizer accessory or with all available accessories

Record whether the failure followed setup changes, ventilator restart, module connection, circuit replacement, or nebulizer replacement.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is clearly defined as a recognition, activation, or output failure.

### 3. Verify the Nebulizer Module Connection
Inspect the nebulizer module and its connection to the Servo-u.

Check for:

- Loose connections
- Partially seated connectors
- Damaged connector housings
- Contamination or moisture
- Cable strain
- Bent or visibly damaged contacts

Reseat accessible connectors using approved normal handling.

**Expected outcome:** The module is securely connected with no visible external damage.

If the module is recognized after reseating and nebulization operates normally, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Confirm the Correct Compatible Nebulizer Components
Verify that the module, nebulizer head or cup, cable, and associated accessories are compatible with the Servo-u setup being used.

Do not assume that visually similar nebulizer accessories are interchangeable.

**Expected outcome:** All components are appropriate for the installed nebulizer system.

If an incompatible accessory is identified, replace it with the correct compatible component and retest.

### 5. Inspect the Nebulizer Cable and Accessory
Inspect accessible nebulizer components for:

- Broken wiring
- Damaged insulation
- Bent connectors
- Fluid contamination
- Cracked housings
- Loose connections
- Damaged or improperly assembled medication cups or nebulizer heads

Do not attempt internal repair of sealed nebulizer components.

**Expected outcome:** The external nebulizer assembly is intact and correctly assembled.

If a defective accessory is found, replace it with a known-good compatible accessory and retest.

### 6. Verify Medication Cup Setup and Consumable Condition
Have clinical staff confirm that the nebulizer consumable is correctly assembled, appropriately filled, and positioned according to the approved clinical procedure.

Clinical Engineering should focus on equipment function rather than medication preparation.

Check that disposable or reusable components are not visibly blocked, improperly installed, or damaged.

**Expected outcome:** The consumable setup is appropriate and does not mechanically prevent aerosol production.

If correcting the consumable setup restores output, verify consistent operation and stop troubleshooting.

### 7. Verify Nebulizer Positioning in the Breathing Circuit
Confirm that the nebulizer is installed at the intended location and orientation within the breathing circuit according to the approved clinical configuration.

Check for:

- Kinked tubing
- Excessive condensate around the nebulizer
- Incorrect orientation
- Circuit components physically interfering with the nebulizer
- Loose circuit connections

Do not change the breathing circuit while a dependent patient remains connected without appropriate clinical control.

**Expected outcome:** The nebulizer is correctly positioned and the circuit is unobstructed.

If correcting positioning restores normal output, complete functional verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 8. Verify the Nebulizer Function Is Enabled Appropriately
Confirm through normal user-accessible controls that nebulization is available and has been activated as intended.

Check for obvious mode or setup conditions that prevent nebulizer activation.

Do not enter restricted service menus or modify protected configuration parameters.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator accepts the nebulizer command and the expected nebulizer function becomes active.

If an approved setup correction restores operation, verify aerosol output and stop troubleshooting.

### 9. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Nebulizer Component
When available, connect a known-good compatible nebulizer head, cable, or module accessory one component at a time.

This helps isolate whether the problem follows a replaceable external component or remains with the ventilator/module interface.

**Expected outcome:** The substitution either restores nebulization or confirms that the failure remains independent of the external accessory.

If a known-good component restores normal nebulization, remove the failed component from service and proceed to final verification.

### 10. Verify Actual Output
Do not rely solely on an activation indicator.

Using an approved test setup, verify that the nebulizer produces expected visible or otherwise verifiable aerosol output and that activation remains stable.

Do not use medication during technical testing unless specifically required and authorized by the applicable procedure.

**Expected outcome:** Nebulization starts, continues reliably, and stops normally when commanded.

If output is verified and no abnormal behavior remains, troubleshooting can stop.

### 11. Perform Final Ventilator Verification
After correcting the problem, confirm:

- Nebulizer module is consistently recognized
- Activation controls function normally
- Nebulizer output is present
- Circuit remains secure
- No new ventilator alarms or abnormal operation occur
- Required pre-use or functional checks pass

**Expected outcome:** The nebulizer system and ventilator operate normally and are appropriate for clinical use.

### 12. Escalate Unresolved Nebulizer Failures
If known-good external components are not detected or no output is produced despite correct setup and connections, stop external troubleshooting.

Do not attempt board-level repair or unauthorized internal interface testing.

**Expected outcome:** The affected ventilator or module is removed from service and routed for qualified evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as loose connections, incorrect accessories, consumable setup, circuit positioning, and defective external nebulizer components have been ruled out.

The remaining issue may involve the nebulizer module, ventilator accessory interface, protected configuration, software, internal power delivery, or another service-level condition.

The device or affected module should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Getinge service documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return the ventilator to service only after nebulizer functionality and overall ventilator performance have been appropriately verified.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

If aerosol therapy cannot be confirmed, maintain ventilation and have clinical staff use an approved alternate medication-delivery method rather than assuming nebulization is occurring.

## Final Thought

Separate module detection from actual aerosol production, and verify the external nebulizer path before assuming an internal Servo-u problem. Protect required therapy, test with known-good accessories when appropriate, escalate persistent faults, and document the verified cause and result.

That is successful troubleshooting.
