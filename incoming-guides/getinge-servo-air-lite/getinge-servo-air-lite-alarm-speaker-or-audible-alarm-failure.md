## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses absent, weak, distorted, or intermittent audible alarms caused by settings, obstruction, ambient conditions, or internal alarm-system faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Alarm Coverage
A Servo-air Lite without reliable audible alarms should not remain in patient service.
Provide another verified ventilator or establish an appropriate alternate alarm and ventilation arrangement before troubleshooting.
Expected outcome: Patient safety does not rely on the questionable alarm speaker.
### 2. Confirm the Audible Alarm Failure
Create an approved controlled alarm condition and determine whether the audible alarm is absent, weak, distorted, or intermittent.
Verify visual alarm indication separately.
Expected outcome: The audible alarm problem is reproduced.
### 3. Verify Alarm Volume Settings
Review normal user-accessible alarm-volume settings.
Do not use restricted menus or configuration changes to bypass alarm requirements.
Expected outcome: The alarm volume is configured appropriately. Correcting a normal setting that restores reliable audio resolves the issue after verification.
### 4. Inspect the Speaker Opening
Check the external speaker area for labels, tape, debris, contamination, or fluid residue.
Do not insert objects into the opening.
Expected outcome: The speaker opening is unobstructed.
### 5. Test in a Controlled Environment
Repeat the alarm test in a quiet bench area.
Expected outcome: The alarm is clearly audible. If it remains weak or absent, continue troubleshooting.
### 6. Perform a Normal Restart
Power the ventilator down and restart normally after removing it from patient use.
Repeat alarm testing.
Expected outcome: Alarm output is normal and stable after startup.
### 7. Test Multiple Alarm Conditions
Use safe controlled methods to produce more than one alarm condition and confirm consistent audible annunciation.
Expected outcome: The alarm speaker functions reliably during repeated testing.
### 8. Observe for Intermittent Failure
Operate the ventilator during a reasonable bench test period and repeat alarm activation.
Expected outcome: No loss, fading, or distortion occurs.
### 9. Complete Functional and Pre-Use Verification
Run required pre-use testing and confirm visual and audible alarms function throughout.
Expected outcome: Alarm performance is consistently normal. Troubleshooting can stop.
### 10. Escalate Persistent Alarm Audio Failure
If audible alarms remain absent, weak, distorted, or intermittent, stop external troubleshooting.
Expected outcome: The ventilator remains out of service for internal alarm-system evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common setting, obstruction, environmental, and restart-related causes have been ruled out. Persistent failure may involve the speaker, audio circuitry, internal wiring, software, or system electronics.
Remove the ventilator from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Getinge documentation and approved test equipment.
Following repair, verify multiple alarm conditions, audible and visual annunciation, ventilation performance, and the complete pre-use check.
Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Visual alarm indication alone does not make a ventilator with failed audible alarms acceptable for normal clinical service.

## Final Thought

Audible alarms are a critical safety function. Check simple external causes first, directly verify alarm performance, and remove the ventilator from service whenever alarm reliability remains uncertain.
