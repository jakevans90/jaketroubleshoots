## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots failure to power on, unexpected shutdowns, unstable AC power, external power connections, battery-related symptoms, and obvious environmental or accessory causes.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Monitoring

If the MX850 is actively monitoring a patient and shuts down, restarts unexpectedly, or cannot be powered reliably, transfer monitoring to another verified monitor before troubleshooting.

Do not continue using a monitor that may unexpectedly lose physiological monitoring or alarms.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is continuously monitored by reliable equipment and the affected MX850 is available for safe evaluation.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Power Complaint

Ask staff whether the monitor:

- Will not power on at all
- Starts and immediately turns off
- Shuts down intermittently
- Restarts without user input
- Operates differently on AC versus battery
- Lost power when the cart, display, or cables were moved

Attempt to reproduce the reported condition away from patient use.

**Expected outcome:** The failure mode and operating conditions are clearly identified. If the issue cannot be reproduced and repeated testing shows stable operation, continue through final verification before returning the monitor to service.

### 3. Verify Facility Power

Confirm the AC outlet is functioning using an appropriate outlet tester or another approved method.

Inspect any power strip, isolation arrangement, cart connection, or intermediate power connection used with the monitor.

If appropriate, test from a known-good approved outlet.

**Expected outcome:** Reliable facility AC power is available. If changing to a known-good power source corrects the problem, address the external power source and stop troubleshooting after functional verification.

### 4. Inspect the External Power Path

Inspect the power cord and accessible power connections for:

- Loose seating
- Damaged insulation
- Bent or damaged contacts
- Strain or pinching
- Contamination
- Evidence of overheating
- Intermittent connection when the cable is gently repositioned

Reseat detachable external connections securely.

**Expected outcome:** The external power path is intact and stable. If reseating or replacing a damaged approved external cord corrects the problem, proceed to final verification.

### 5. Compare AC and Battery Operation

When the installed configuration allows battery operation, determine whether the shutdown occurs:

- Only on AC
- Only on battery
- During transition between AC and battery
- Under both power sources

Observe available charging and power indicators without entering restricted service functions.

**Expected outcome:** The power problem is narrowed to the AC supply path, battery operation, or a broader monitor problem. If a known-good approved battery or external power arrangement resolves the issue, verify stable operation before stopping.

### 6. Inspect External Modules and Accessories

Disconnect nonessential external accessories, USB devices, displays, measurement extensions, or other removable peripherals when this can be done safely and without changing protected configuration.

Restart the monitor in a minimum practical configuration.

**Expected outcome:** The monitor remains powered in the reduced configuration. If an external accessory consistently causes shutdown or restart when connected, remove that accessory from use and verify the monitor without it.

### 7. Check for Environmental or Mechanical Factors

Inspect for:

- Blocked ventilation
- Excessive dust around vents
- Liquid contamination
- Evidence of impact
- Excessive heat
- Unusual odor
- Loose mounting that places strain on cables

Do not operate equipment showing liquid intrusion, overheating, burning odor, or significant physical damage.

**Expected outcome:** No unsafe environmental or physical condition is present. Any unsafe finding requires removal from service.

### 8. Perform a Controlled Restart and Stability Test

After external causes have been addressed, power the monitor normally and allow it to operate long enough to evaluate stability.

Gently manipulate only normal external connections while observing for power interruption.

**Expected outcome:** The MX850 starts normally and remains continuously powered without restart, flicker, or interruption. If stable, continue to final verification.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify appropriate monitor startup, display operation, connected measurement functions, alarm annunciation, and communication expected for the installed configuration.

Confirm no unexpected shutdown occurs during the test period.

**Expected outcome:** The monitor performs normally and remains stable. Troubleshooting can stop and the unit may be returned to service according to facility policy.

### 10. Escalate Unresolved Power Problems

If the monitor still will not power on or continues to shut down after external power, connections, batteries, accessories, and environmental factors have been ruled out, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** An unreliable monitor is removed from clinical use and referred for appropriate service evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve the monitor's internal power system, battery interface, power distribution, internal connections, thermal protection, or another service-level fault.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Philips service documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, perform appropriate electrical safety and functional return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any MX850 that shuts down unpredictably should be replaced at the bedside immediately rather than observed while a patient depends on its monitoring and alarms.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, verify the complete external power path before assuming an internal failure, reproduce intermittent symptoms when safely possible, escalate unreliable equipment appropriately, and document exactly what was found and verified.

That is successful troubleshooting.
