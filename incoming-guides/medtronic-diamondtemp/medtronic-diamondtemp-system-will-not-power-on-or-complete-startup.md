## What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when the generator remains off, stops during startup, or does not reach its normal ready state.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Procedure Continuity
Do not troubleshoot an unreliable ablation generator while therapy depends on it. Stop use, notify the clinical team, and transition to an approved alternate system if therapy must continue. Inspect for smoke, unusual odor, excessive heat, liquid intrusion, or obvious physical damage.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is not dependent on the affected generator, and the unit is safe to inspect externally. If hazardous damage is present, remove it from service and stop troubleshooting.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Startup Failure
Ask staff what occurred when the system was powered on. Determine whether there were no signs of power, startup began but stopped, the system restarted repeatedly, or a displayed message prevented readiness. Record the exact message if available.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is reproduced or clearly characterized. If the system now completes startup normally, continue through final verification before returning it to service.

### 3. Verify Facility Power
Confirm the power cord is fully seated at the generator and approved power source. Inspect the cord and plug for damage. Verify the outlet using an approved electrical test method or known-good equipment according to Clinical Engineering practice.

**Expected outcome:** Reliable facility power is available and the external power connection is secure. If correcting the outlet or connection restores normal startup, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Inspect External Power Components
Check accessible power switches, cord retention, external power strips or isolation-system connections when applicable, and any approved external power accessories. Bypass questionable nonessential external power accessories using an approved source when permitted.

**Expected outcome:** The generator receives stable power without a defective external accessory in the path. If startup completes normally, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 5. Disconnect Nonessential External Accessories
With the system safely powered down, disconnect nonessential external accessories that can be removed without altering required configuration. Inspect connectors for bent contacts, contamination, damage, or incomplete engagement.

**Expected outcome:** The generator starts with only the required external connections. If a particular accessory causes startup failure when reconnected, remove that accessory from use and verify the generator independently.

### 6. Perform an Approved Restart
Follow the normal shutdown and startup process available to Clinical Engineering. Do not repeatedly cycle power if the system is restarting unexpectedly or showing signs of hardware instability.

**Expected outcome:** The system completes its normal startup sequence and reaches the expected ready state. If it does, proceed to functional verification.

### 7. Check External Environment
Confirm ventilation openings are unobstructed and the generator is not exposed to excessive heat, moisture, contamination, or conditions inconsistent with normal clinical operation.

**Expected outcome:** No environmental condition is interfering with startup. Correcting an external condition should allow normal initialization.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification
After correction, restart the system and confirm normal display operation, controls, indicators, accessory recognition, and readiness using approved manufacturer procedures and test equipment as applicable. Do not deliver patient therapy solely as a troubleshooting test.

**Expected outcome:** The generator starts consistently and passes the required functional checks. The issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Escalate Persistent Startup Failure
If verified power, external connections, accessories, and environment are acceptable but startup still fails, do not proceed into unauthorized internal repair.

**Expected outcome:** The device is removed from service for qualified bench or manufacturer-level evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal power conversion, startup-control, software, cooling, communication, or other service-level faults.

Remove the generator from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it using current manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment. Internal repair or configuration changes should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Following corrective work, complete all required electrical safety, operational, and functional return-to-service testing before clinical use. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Ensure another verified ablation system is available before troubleshooting a generator needed for an active procedure.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, verify power and external causes before assuming an internal failure, confirm normal operation after correction, escalate unresolved startup faults appropriately, and document the complaint, cause, and resolution clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
