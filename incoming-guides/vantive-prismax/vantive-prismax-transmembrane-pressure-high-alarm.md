## What This Guide Helps With

Helps troubleshoot transmembrane pressure alarms caused by circuit conditions, filter loading, setup, or external restrictions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Confirm patient safety before troubleshooting
Ensure the patient is supported and clinical staff are aware of the condition. Do not troubleshoot active therapy without appropriate clinical coordination.
**Expected outcome:** Patient safety is maintained.
### 2. Verify the alarm condition
Confirm the exact alarm message, timing, and whether the alarm is intermittent or persistent.
**Expected outcome:** The reported condition is confirmed.
### 3. Inspect tubing and external circuit path
Check visible tubing for kinks, compression, clamps, or restrictions that could affect flow.
**Expected outcome:** External flow restrictions are corrected or ruled out.
### 4. Verify circuit setup and connections
Inspect visible connections and disposable installation for secure placement and proper external setup.
**Expected outcome:** Circuit setup appears correct.
### 5. Review filter and treatment-related external factors
Coordinate with clinical staff regarding treatment duration, circuit condition, and any recent changes associated with the alarm.
**Expected outcome:** External contributors are identified or excluded.
### 6. Perform final verification
After correction, verify stable operation and absence of recurring alarms.
**Expected outcome:** The device operates normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

External causes have been ruled out.
The device should be:
- Removed from service if safe operation cannot be maintained
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel
Possible remaining causes include internal monitoring or control issues requiring service evaluation.

## Clinical Use Tip

Coordinate with clinical staff before making any changes that may interrupt ongoing therapy.
