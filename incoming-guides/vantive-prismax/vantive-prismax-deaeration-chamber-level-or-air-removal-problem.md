## What This Guide Helps With

Helps troubleshoot air removal concerns related to chamber level, tubing setup, connections, or visible circuit issues.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Confirm patient safety
Ensure the patient is protected and clinical staff are aware of the alarm before troubleshooting.
**Expected outcome:** Safe patient management is maintained.
### 2. Confirm the reported condition
Verify the exact alarm and circumstances surrounding the air removal problem.
**Expected outcome:** The issue is accurately identified.
### 3. Inspect visible tubing and connections
Check for loose connections, air entry points, damaged tubing, or improper external setup.
**Expected outcome:** External causes are identified or ruled out.
### 4. Verify circuit positioning
Inspect visible circuit routing for conditions that may affect proper flow.
**Expected outcome:** Tubing routing is appropriate.
### 5. Review recent setup changes
Determine whether the issue began after circuit installation or handling.
**Expected outcome:** Setup-related causes are evaluated.
### 6. Perform final verification
Verify normal operation and absence of recurring air-related alarms.
**Expected outcome:** Device operates normally and troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

External circuit and setup issues have been ruled out.
The device should be:
- Removed from service if safe operation cannot be maintained
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired only by qualified personnel

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not continue therapy if safe circuit operation cannot be confirmed.
