## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot inaccurate or offset touchscreen input caused by contamination, protectors, mounting pressure, environmental conditions, configuration, or a service-level touchscreen problem.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Before Testing the Controls

Do not troubleshoot unreliable controls while a patient depends on the CARESCAPE B650 for active monitoring or alarm management.

Transfer monitoring to another verified device if touchscreen unreliability could prevent timely control, alarm, or patient-management actions.

Expected outcome: Clinical monitoring is maintained safely while the monitor can be evaluated without patient dependence.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Touchscreen Behavior

Determine whether touch input is consistently offset in one direction, inaccurate only in certain screen areas, intermittently unresponsive, activating adjacent controls, or drifting after the monitor has been operating for some time.

If alternate controls are available, verify whether the problem is isolated to touch input rather than the entire user interface.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen symptom is reproducible and its pattern is documented. If touch operation is accurate throughout the display and the complaint cannot be reproduced, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 3. Inspect and Clean the Screen Surface

Inspect the touchscreen for liquid, dried cleaning solution, adhesive residue, heavy contamination, cracks, or damage.

Clean only according to approved facility and manufacturer-compatible methods. Ensure the surface is dry before retesting.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen surface is clean, dry, and undamaged. If cleaning restores accurate touch response, troubleshooting can stop after confirming all tested screen areas.

### 4. Check for Screen Protectors or Added Material

Determine whether a nonstandard screen protector, film, label, tape, or other material has been added over or near the active touch area.

Remove unauthorized material when appropriate and safe.

Expected outcome: Nothing interferes with normal touch sensing. If removing an interfering layer restores correct touch alignment, troubleshooting can stop.

### 5. Check Mounting and Mechanical Pressure

Inspect the monitor installation and bezel area for external pressure, binding, impact damage, a distorted mount, or accessories contacting the screen or enclosure.

If the monitor is mounted, compare touchscreen behavior with the unit positioned normally and without abnormal external pressure where feasible.

Expected outcome: The display and bezel are free from abnormal mechanical pressure. If correcting the mounting condition restores touch accuracy, troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Evaluate the Entire Touch Area

Using noncritical menus or appropriate test screens available through normal operation, test multiple locations across the display rather than checking only one button.

Avoid making unintended clinical configuration changes during testing.

Expected outcome: Touch points correspond consistently with the selected on-screen controls across the display. If the offset occurs only in a specific region, document the affected area for service evaluation.

### 7. Check Environmental Factors

Allow the monitor to operate in a normal clinical environment and verify that the screen is dry and not exposed to condensation, unusual temperature conditions, or contaminants that could affect input.

If the problem occurs only after transport between environments, allow the monitor to stabilize before retesting.

Expected outcome: Touch input remains accurate under normal operating conditions. If environmental stabilization resolves the problem and the fault does not recur, troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Restart the Monitor Under Controlled Conditions

After the patient is no longer dependent on the monitor, perform a normal shutdown and restart.

Observe touchscreen behavior immediately after startup and again after the monitor has operated for a period sufficient to reproduce the reported drift.

Expected outcome: Touch input remains aligned and stable after restart. If normal operation persists and all screen areas respond correctly, troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Verify Approved Calibration or Configuration Only if Authorized

If the CARESCAPE B650 provides an authorized touchscreen calibration or alignment procedure available to Clinical Engineering through approved documentation, follow that procedure exactly.

Do not enter undocumented service menus, change unrelated configuration, or guess at calibration values.

Expected outcome: Approved calibration completes successfully and touch points align accurately across the display. If calibration is successful and remains stable through verification, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Confirm Controls Before Return to Service

Test representative touchscreen controls across multiple areas of the display. Verify that selections activate only the intended controls and that no regions remain offset, delayed, or unresponsive.

Confirm that alarm-related and monitoring controls remain accessible and function normally.

Expected outcome: Touchscreen input is accurate, repeatable, and stable throughout the usable display area. If all checks pass, complete applicable return-to-service testing.

### 11. Remove From Service if Touch Alignment Remains Unreliable

If touch input continues drifting, selecting adjacent controls, becoming offset, or failing in portions of the screen after cleaning, mechanical inspection, restart, and any authorized calibration, remove the device from clinical use.

Expected outcome: The CARESCAPE B650 is labeled Out of Service and referred for qualified repair or bench evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Persistent touchscreen drift after surface condition, mounting pressure, environment, restart, and authorized calibration have been addressed suggests a service-level touchscreen, display, interface, or internal processing issue.

The monitor should be removed from service, labeled Out of Service, and sent for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate GE Healthcare service documentation and approved test equipment.

Do not continue clinical use simply because the offset can be compensated for by touching beside the desired control. Internal display or touchscreen repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

After repair, verify accurate touch response across the full usable display, normal monitor controls, alarm access, parameter operation, and required return-to-service testing.

Knowing when an unreliable control interface requires removal from service is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

An offset touchscreen can cause unintended selections; do not rely on a monitor when staff must compensate for inaccurate touch locations.

## Final Thought

Treat inaccurate touch control as a patient-safety issue, rule out surface, mounting, and environmental causes first, and use only authorized calibration methods. Persistent drift requires escalation, complete verification, and clear documentation before the monitor returns to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
