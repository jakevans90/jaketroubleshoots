## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot loss of power, incomplete startup, or initialization failures caused by external power, connections, consumables, configuration, or environmental conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Testing and Confirm the Reported Condition

Do not rely on an analyzer that will not power on, initialize, or indicate that it is ready for testing. Direct urgent blood gas testing to another verified analyzer or approved alternate testing pathway.

Confirm whether the analyzer is completely unresponsive, powers on and shuts down, remains in initialization, or displays a specific message. Record the exact displayed condition before cycling power.

**Expected outcome:** The analyzer's actual failure state is confirmed and patient testing continues through a reliable alternative. If the analyzer completes initialization and becomes ready without recurrence, verify normal operation and troubleshooting can stop.

### 2. Verify AC Power

Inspect the power cord, plug, and accessible power connection for looseness, damage, contamination, or signs of overheating. Confirm the power cord is fully seated.

Verify the wall receptacle or approved power source with an appropriate tester or known-good device according to facility practice. Do not assume the outlet is functional because nearby equipment has power.

**Expected outcome:** A verified power source is available and the analyzer has a secure, undamaged AC connection. Correct an external power problem if found, then restart and verify normal initialization. If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

### 3. Check External Power Controls and Connected Equipment

Verify that accessible power switches or approved power-control devices are in the correct operating position. Inspect any externally connected UPS, power strip, isolation device, or facility power interface if one is part of the installed setup.

Do not bypass protective power equipment or substitute an unapproved power source.

**Expected outcome:** All approved external power components are operating normally. If correcting an external power-control issue restores normal startup, verify readiness and troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Inspect the Analyzer for Obvious External Problems

Check for liquid intrusion, cracked housings, blocked ventilation, unusual heat, unusual odor, evidence of impact, or loose external connections.

If there is evidence of liquid intrusion, burning, overheating, or significant physical damage, disconnect the analyzer from clinical service rather than repeatedly attempting startup.

**Expected outcome:** No unsafe physical condition is present. Any analyzer showing a safety concern is removed from service and escalated.

### 5. Verify Required Consumables and Accessible Connections

Confirm that required analyzer consumables and externally accessible fluid, waste, or reagent components are correctly installed, seated, and not obviously empty, disconnected, expired, damaged, or improperly positioned.

Follow approved operator-accessible replacement procedures only. Do not open internal fluidic or electronic assemblies.

**Expected outcome:** Required consumables and external connections are present and correctly installed. If initialization completes after correcting a seating or consumable problem, verify readiness and troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Review the Displayed Initialization Condition

If the analyzer powers on but does not reach a ready state, document the exact message, stage, or condition displayed. Verify that no open cover, improperly installed consumable, full waste condition, or other externally correctable condition is preventing initialization.

Avoid repeated restart cycles when the analyzer consistently stops at the same point.

**Expected outcome:** Any externally correctable initialization condition is identified and corrected. If the analyzer reaches its normal ready state, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 7. Perform One Controlled Restart When Appropriate

If there is no indication of physical damage or another unsafe condition, perform a controlled restart using the normal approved shutdown and startup process.

Do not repeatedly interrupt power during startup.

**Expected outcome:** The analyzer completes its startup sequence and reaches a stable ready condition. If initialization again fails at the same stage, proceed to escalation.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

After normal initialization, confirm the analyzer remains ready, recognizes required consumables, shows no unresolved fault condition, and successfully completes the facility-required functional or quality verification before clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** The analyzer demonstrates stable operation and passes required verification. It may then be returned to service.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external power, connection, consumable, and environmental causes have been ruled out. A persistent startup failure may involve an internal power supply, control system, sensor, fluidics subsystem, software condition, or other service-level fault.

The analyzer should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Radiometer documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not proceed into internal power supplies, electronic assemblies, fluidics components, or unauthorized service functions without appropriate service documentation and authorization. After repair, complete the required functional, quality-control, and return-to-service checks before releasing the analyzer.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Maintain access to another verified blood gas analyzer when an ABL90 FLEX PLUS cannot complete initialization, especially when rapid blood gas results are clinically time-sensitive.

## Final Thought

Protect patient testing first, then verify power, external connections, consumables, and visible conditions before assuming an internal failure. Escalate persistent initialization faults appropriately and document both the cause and final verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
