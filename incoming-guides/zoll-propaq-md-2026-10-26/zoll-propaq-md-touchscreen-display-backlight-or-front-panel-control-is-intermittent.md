## What This Guide Helps With

Intermittent touchscreen, display, backlight, or front-panel control operation caused by power, contamination, accessories, environmental conditions, or hardware faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Defibrillation Capability

If the Propaq MD is supporting an active patient and controls or display functions are unreliable, transfer monitoring or therapy responsibilities to another verified defibrillator before troubleshooting.

Do not continue relying on equipment with intermittent access to therapy controls, alarm information, or displayed patient data.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care continues on dependable equipment and the affected Propaq MD is available for controlled evaluation.

If another verified device restores reliable clinical operation, continue troubleshooting the affected unit off the patient.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Intermittent Condition

Clarify whether the reported problem involves:

- Touchscreen response
- Display image
- Backlight brightness
- Individual front-panel buttons
- Rotary or navigation controls
- A specific operating mode
- Failure only during battery or AC operation
- Failure only after movement, transport, or extended operation

Reproduce the complaint when safely possible without initiating patient therapy.

**Expected outcome:** The affected control or display function and the conditions under which it fails are clearly identified.

If the reported condition cannot be reproduced, document the circumstances reported by staff and continue with external inspection.

### 3. Verify Power and Basic Startup

Check that the unit starts normally using the approved power source and installed battery configuration.

Inspect the external power connection, power cord, battery seating, and visible power indicators. Compare behavior on AC power and battery power when appropriate.

A marginal power connection can appear as a display or control problem.

**Expected outcome:** The Propaq MD remains powered without flickering, rebooting, or losing display/control functionality during basic operation.

If restoring a secure power or battery connection eliminates the intermittent behavior and repeated testing remains normal, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 4. Inspect the Display and Front Panel

Inspect externally for:

- Cracked or damaged display surfaces
- Loose bezels or visible separation
- Fluid residue
- Dried cleaning solution
- Adhesive contamination
- Damaged buttons
- Controls that bind or remain partially depressed
- Evidence of impact or transport damage

Do not disassemble the display or control panel during routine external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The display and control surfaces are clean, dry, physically intact, and controls move normally.

If contamination was the cause and cleaning with an approved method restores reliable function, proceed to final verification.

### 5. Check for Touchscreen Interference

If the touchscreen is affected, remove nonessential external items that may contact or press against the screen, including protective covers or transport accessories that are incorrectly positioned.

Operate the device on a stable surface rather than while cables or equipment are pressing against the display.

**Expected outcome:** Touch input remains stable with no unintended activation, dead areas, or intermittent response caused by external pressure.

If correcting positioning or external interference restores normal touch operation, troubleshooting can stop after repeated verification.

### 6. Evaluate Environmental Conditions

Determine whether the issue occurs after:

- Movement between temperature extremes
- Exposure to moisture or condensation
- Heavy cleaning
- Storage in a hot or cold location
- Direct bright lighting affecting perceived backlight performance
- Long periods of continuous operation

Allow the device to return to an appropriate clinical operating environment before reassessment.

**Expected outcome:** Display and controls operate consistently under normal environmental conditions.

If abnormal environmental exposure explains the problem and operation remains reliable after normalization, document the finding and complete final testing.

### 7. Compare AC and Battery Operation

Operate the unit long enough to determine whether the intermittent display or control condition is associated specifically with AC power, battery operation, or both.

Do not interpret a display interruption automatically as a display assembly failure if the entire device is briefly losing power.

**Expected outcome:** The device operates consistently on each applicable power source, or the issue is isolated to a specific power condition.

If the problem occurs only with an externally correctable battery or power connection and correction restores reliable operation, proceed to final verification.

### 8. Exercise the Affected Controls Repeatedly

With the unit removed from clinical use, repeatedly operate the affected front-panel control or touchscreen area through normal user-accessible functions.

Look for:

- Missed button presses
- Multiple activations from one press
- Delayed response
- Flickering backlight
- Loss of touch response
- Controls responding differently when the chassis is moved

Do not perform destructive manipulation or force controls.

**Expected outcome:** Each control produces a consistent response with no intermittent failure.

If repeated operation remains normal and the original external cause has been corrected, proceed to return-to-service verification.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify normal startup and confirm that:

- The display remains readable
- Backlight remains stable
- Touch input responds correctly
- Front-panel controls operate consistently
- Alarm indications remain visible and audible
- No unexpected restart or freeze occurs

Perform applicable return-to-service testing using approved procedures and test equipment.

**Expected outcome:** The Propaq MD completes functional verification with stable display and control operation.

If all testing passes and the original complaint is resolved, troubleshooting can stop and the device may be returned to service according to facility policy.

### 10. Escalate Intermittent or Recurrent Failures

If the touchscreen, display, backlight, or front-panel controls continue to fail intermittently after external causes are eliminated, remove the unit from service.

Intermittent control failures on a defibrillator should not be accepted simply because the device passes one subsequent startup.

**Expected outcome:** An unreliable device is prevented from returning to clinical use and is routed for qualified bench or manufacturer-supported evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as power connections, battery seating, contamination, positioning, accessories, and environment have been ruled out.

The remaining fault may involve the display assembly, touchscreen interface, front-panel controls, internal power distribution, cabling, or other service-level electronics.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, complete all required functional, alarm, and defibrillator return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate an intermittent control problem is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A defibrillator with unreliable controls or display information should be exchanged before it is needed for time-critical therapy.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, reproduce the complaint carefully, rule out power, contamination, positioning, accessories, and environmental causes before assuming an internal display or control failure, and document both the findings and final verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
