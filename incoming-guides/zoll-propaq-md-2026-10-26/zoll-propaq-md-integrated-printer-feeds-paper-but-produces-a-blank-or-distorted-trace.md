## What This Guide Helps With

Printer paper advances but recordings are blank, faint, incomplete, or distorted because of paper, loading, contamination, settings, or printer hardware.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Preserve Clinical Documentation

Do not interrupt required monitoring or defibrillation therapy solely to troubleshoot the printer.

If a printed rhythm strip or event record is clinically necessary and the integrated printer is unreliable, use another approved documentation method or verified device while the Propaq MD is evaluated.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care and required rhythm documentation continue without dependence on an unreliable printer.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Printing Problem

Determine whether the printer:

- Feeds paper but prints nothing
- Produces a very faint image
- Prints only part of the waveform
- Produces compressed or stretched traces
- Smears or distorts the image
- Works intermittently
- Fails only during certain print functions

Print a nonpatient test or other approved accessible record when possible.

**Expected outcome:** The printing defect is reproduced and clearly characterized.

If the complaint cannot be reproduced, document the reported behavior and continue with consumable and mechanical checks.

### 3. Verify the Correct Printer Paper

Confirm that the installed paper is the correct approved thermal paper for the Propaq MD printer.

Check for:

- Incorrect paper type
- Paper installed with the nonthermal surface facing the printhead
- Damaged or moisture-exposed paper
- Severely curled paper
- Empty or nearly empty roll

Thermal paper loaded backward can feed normally while producing a blank strip.

**Expected outcome:** Correct, undamaged thermal paper is installed with the printable surface oriented properly.

If correcting the paper type or orientation restores a clear trace, troubleshooting can stop after repeated print verification.

### 4. Reload the Paper Correctly

Remove and reload the paper using the normal accessible loading path.

Verify that:

- The paper is centered
- The roll turns freely
- The paper is not folded
- The printer door closes fully
- No paper edge is trapped in the door

Do not force the printer mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** Paper advances smoothly and remains aligned through the printer path.

If proper reloading resolves the distortion or blank output, perform several additional prints before returning the unit to service.

### 5. Inspect the Paper Path and Printer Door

With the printer inactive, visually inspect accessible areas for:

- Torn paper fragments
- Adhesive residue
- Dirt
- Foreign material
- Damaged paper guides
- A door that does not latch completely

Clean accessible surfaces only using approved methods.

**Expected outcome:** The paper path is unobstructed and the printer door closes securely.

If removal of a visible obstruction restores normal printing, proceed to final verification.

### 6. Substitute a Known-Good Paper Roll

Install a known-good roll of the correct paper.

This helps distinguish a device problem from a batch of damaged, degraded, or incompatible thermal paper.

**Expected outcome:** The printer produces a clear, consistent trace using known-good paper.

If known-good paper resolves the problem, replace the affected consumable stock and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 7. Compare Different Print Functions

If accessible in normal operation, compare printing from more than one available source, such as a displayed waveform or stored event record.

The purpose is to determine whether the defect affects all printer output or only a particular data source.

**Expected outcome:** Printing is either consistently normal or the same defect appears across print functions.

If only one data source is affected while the printer produces normal output otherwise, investigate the associated configuration or data pathway using approved documentation rather than assuming a printer hardware failure.

### 8. Observe Print Quality During Continuous Feed

Print enough paper to determine whether:

- The trace fades during printing
- Image density changes across the paper width
- Distortion repeats in the same location
- Paper movement hesitates
- Feed speed appears inconsistent

Do not adjust internal printhead pressure or disassemble the printer mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** Paper movement and printed image remain uniform throughout the test.

Persistent missing sections or repeatable distortion after paper-related causes are eliminated requires escalation.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify several successful prints after correction.

Confirm:

- Paper advances normally
- Waveforms and text are legible
- Printing is not blank or excessively faint
- Trace geometry appears stable
- No paper jams occur
- Printer door remains latched

Complete any required return-to-service checks.

**Expected outcome:** Multiple consecutive printouts are clear, legible, and mechanically consistent.

If the original complaint is resolved and testing remains normal, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Printer Defects

If correct paper, proper loading, a clear paper path, and known-good consumables do not restore reliable printing, remove the printer-dependent device from service when printing capability is required by local clinical workflow or policy.

**Expected outcome:** A persistent printer defect is routed for qualified service rather than repeatedly returned to clinical use.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as incorrect thermal paper, reversed paper orientation, damaged consumables, loading errors, contamination, and paper-path obstruction have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve the thermal printhead, feed mechanism, printer electronics, internal connections, or service-level configuration.

The device should be:

- Removed from service when reliable printing is required
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Following repair, verify printer operation along with applicable defibrillator functional testing before return to clinical use.

Knowing when to stop after consumable and external checks have been exhausted is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A feeding printer is not necessarily a functional printer; verify that rhythm and event information is actually legible before clinical reliance.

Begin with the paper and loading path before assuming a printer hardware failure, verify the correction with multiple legible printouts, escalate persistent defects appropriately, and document exactly what was found and tested.
