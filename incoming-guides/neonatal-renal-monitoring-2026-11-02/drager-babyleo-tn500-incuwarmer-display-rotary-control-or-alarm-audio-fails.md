## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots missing display operation, unresponsive rotary control, or absent alarm audio caused by power, controls, connections, configuration, or external conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Alarm Coverage

Do not troubleshoot failed controls, a blank or unreliable display, or missing alarm audio while an infant depends on the Babyleo TN500.

Move the patient to another verified device or establish appropriate alternate thermal support and alarm coverage before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is supported independently of the affected unit and no critical alarm function depends on it.

### 2. Confirm Which Function Has Failed

Determine whether the problem involves:

- Entire display blank.
- Display illuminated but frozen.
- Touch or displayed controls unresponsive.
- Rotary control not responding to rotation or selection.
- Alarm audio absent or abnormally weak.
- Display and alarm audio failing together.
- Intermittent operation rather than complete failure.

Record any messages or indicators that remain functional.

**Expected outcome:** The affected function is clearly identified. If only one control is affected, troubleshooting can be directed without assuming the whole user interface has failed.

### 3. Verify Stable Power and Normal Startup

Confirm AC power and power-cord connections. Restart the unit using normal operating controls only when it is safe and clinically appropriate.

Observe whether the display, rotary control, and alarm audio initialize during startup.

**Expected outcome:** The unit completes startup with a responsive display and controls. If normal operation returns after correcting an external power condition, proceed to final verification and troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Inspect the Display and Control Area

Inspect externally for:

- Liquid contamination.
- Cleaning residue.
- Cracked or damaged surfaces.
- A rotary control that is physically obstructed.
- Debris around the control.
- Evidence of impact.
- Loose or displaced accessible external components.

Do not pry apart the display housing or control assembly.

**Expected outcome:** The control area is clean, intact, and mechanically unobstructed. If removing an external obstruction restores normal operation, continue to functional verification.

### 5. Check for an Interface Freeze Versus Complete System Failure

Observe whether other system indicators, fans, lights, or functions remain active when the display or controls appear unresponsive.

If approved by facility procedures and the patient has been removed, perform a normal controlled restart rather than disconnecting power abruptly.

**Expected outcome:** The interface initializes normally after restart. If the problem immediately returns or freezes repeatedly, remove the unit from service for further evaluation.

### 6. Verify Rotary-Control Response

With the unit off-patient, operate the rotary control through normal user-accessible functions. Verify that rotation produces the expected screen response and that selection inputs are recognized.

Do not force a stiff or damaged control.

**Expected outcome:** Each normal rotary-control action produces a consistent response. If the control is intermittent, mechanically damaged, or unresponsive, the unit should not be returned to clinical service until repaired.

### 7. Verify Alarm Audio Using an Approved Functional Test

Use an appropriate normal functional or manufacturer-approved test method to confirm audible alarm output. Do not deliberately create an unsafe patient condition to test an alarm.

Confirm that the alarm can be heard appropriately and is not being masked by an external obstruction or environmental noise.

**Expected outcome:** Alarm audio activates consistently during an approved alarm test. If alarm audio cannot be verified, remove the unit from service.

### 8. Check User-Accessible Audio and Display Configuration

Review only normal authorized settings associated with display visibility or alarm presentation. Compare with an equivalent properly functioning unit or approved configuration information if necessary.

Do not enter unauthorized service menus or change protected alarm behavior merely to clear the complaint.

**Expected outcome:** Configuration is appropriate and does not explain the reported failure. If an authorized configuration correction restores normal function, verify operation and document the change.

### 9. Test for Intermittent Operation

While the unit remains off-patient, observe the display, controls, and alarm output through normal operation. Gently operate accessible controls without stressing or manipulating internal connections.

**Expected outcome:** Display, rotary control, and alarm audio remain stable and responsive.

### 10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify normal startup, display operation, rotary-control response, visual alarm presentation, and audible alarm function. Complete any additional manufacturer-required return-to-service testing appropriate to the intervention.

**Expected outcome:** All required interface and alarm functions operate consistently. If so, the issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If the display remains blank or freezes, the rotary control is unreliable, or alarm audio cannot be verified after external power, contamination, obstruction, configuration, and normal restart checks have been completed, an internal user-interface, audio, control, software, or service-level problem may be present.

The device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Drager documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not return an infant incubator to service with unreliable controls or alarms. Complete appropriate functional and alarm verification after repair.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Missing alarm audio is a patient-safety failure even when heating functions appear normal; provide verified alternate alarm coverage immediately.

## Final Thought

A failed interface or alarm function can make an otherwise operating incubator unsafe. Protect the patient first, verify power and external causes, confirm controls and alarms with approved testing, and escalate rather than accepting intermittent operation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
