## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots bibag loading, door, conductivity, and empty-bag detection problems caused by setup, disposable, connection, positioning, or external system conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Dialysis Continuity

Do not troubleshoot a bibag, conductivity, or concentrate-detection problem while a patient depends on an unreliable dialysis fluid delivery system. If treatment is active and dialysate safety cannot be assured, follow facility procedure to place the patient on a verified alternative machine or otherwise maintain safe therapy.

Inspect for leakage, damaged concentrate connections, unusual odor, visible fluid contamination, or other conditions requiring immediate removal from service.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is protected from questionable dialysate delivery, and troubleshooting is performed only when the machine can be safely evaluated.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Determine whether the reported problem involves the bibag door not recognizing closure, failure to recognize an installed bag, premature empty-bag indication, conductivity not stabilizing, or another specific setup condition.

Record the displayed message or symptom exactly rather than assuming all bibag-related complaints have the same cause.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is reproducible or clearly defined. If the condition cannot be reproduced and repeated setup cycles operate normally, document the findings and stop troubleshooting after appropriate verification.

### 3. Inspect the bibag Disposable and Installation

Verify that the bibag is appropriate for use, is not visibly damaged, leaking, collapsed abnormally, contaminated, or incorrectly positioned.

Confirm that the disposable is fully seated and installed according to the machine's intended external loading arrangement. Inspect the contact and connection areas for residue or obstruction that could interfere with proper seating or detection.

Do not bypass bag detection or alter the disposable.

**Expected outcome:** The bibag is undamaged, correctly positioned, and fully seated. If correcting installation restores normal recognition and setup, the issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 4. Inspect the bibag Door and Latching Area

Open and close the bibag door while observing for mechanical interference, loose disposable material, dried concentrate residue, physical damage, or a latch that does not fully engage.

Do not force the door or defeat a door-interlock feature.

**Expected outcome:** The door closes smoothly, fully latches, and is recognized by the machine. If cleaning an accessible obstruction or correcting bag position restores normal operation, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 5. Verify Concentrate Connections and Supply Conditions

Inspect the acid concentrate connection and any associated external concentrate supply used for the setup. Verify that connections are complete, tubing is not kinked, and the correct concentrate source is available.

If a central concentrate system is used, determine whether other machines connected to the same source are experiencing similar problems. A shared problem may indicate an infrastructure or supply issue rather than a machine failure.

**Expected outcome:** Required concentrate sources are correctly connected and available without obvious external restriction. A supply-related problem is corrected or escalated to the appropriate infrastructure support group.

### 6. Check for External Leaks, Air, or Flow Restrictions

Inspect accessible concentrate lines and connections for leaks, loose fittings, pinching, kinks, air entry, or other conditions that could prevent stable concentrate delivery.

Do not open internal hydraulic assemblies during this external troubleshooting sequence.

**Expected outcome:** No external leak or flow restriction is present. If correcting an external tubing or connection problem allows conductivity to stabilize, the issue is resolved.

### 7. Verify Conductivity Behavior During Setup

Run the machine through the normal applicable preparation sequence without a patient and observe whether conductivity progresses toward a stable operating condition.

Do not return a machine to clinical use merely because an alarm clears momentarily. The condition should remain stable through the appropriate setup and verification period.

**Expected outcome:** Conductivity stabilizes normally and remains stable without recurring bibag or concentrate-related faults. If it does, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 8. Use a Known-Good Disposable or Comparison When Appropriate

If permitted by facility practice, repeat setup with a known-good compatible bibag or compare behavior with another verified 2008T using the same appropriate concentrate source.

Do not repeatedly consume disposables when the machine clearly has a persistent fault.

**Expected outcome:** A known-good disposable either restores normal function, identifying the original disposable or installation as the likely cause, or the problem remains machine-specific and requires escalation.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After any correction, repeat the relevant setup sequence from the beginning. Confirm that the bibag is recognized, the door state is correctly detected, empty-bag indication is appropriate, conductivity stabilizes, and no related alarms recur.

Complete any applicable return-to-service testing required by facility policy and Fresenius service documentation before clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** The machine completes the applicable preparation sequence normally and remains stable. Troubleshooting is complete.

## If the Problem Persists

If the bibag is properly installed, the door and external connections are intact, concentrate supply is verified, and conductivity still fails to stabilize or bag detection remains incorrect, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve an internal sensing, hydraulic, concentrate-delivery, calibration, wiring, configuration, or control-system problem requiring service-level evaluation.

The machine should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Fresenius Medical Care service documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired, calibrated, or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Subjected to applicable functional, safety, and dialysate-related return-to-service testing before reuse.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate a persistent dialysate-related fault is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never place a patient on a machine with unresolved conductivity or concentrate-detection behavior; verify the complete dialysate preparation sequence before return to treatment.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, verify the disposable, door, concentrate supply, connections, and conductivity behavior before assuming an internal failure. Escalate persistent dialysate-related faults appropriately and document the complaint, verified cause, corrective action, and final testing clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
