## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots startup, boot, or unexpected shutdown problems caused by external power, connections, controls, accessories, configuration, or environmental conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Thermal Support

Do not troubleshoot an unreliable Babyleo TN500 while an infant depends on it for thermal support, monitoring, alarms, or other patient-support functions. Transfer the patient to another verified device or approved alternate method before continuing.

If the unit shut down unexpectedly during clinical use, treat the interruption as a patient-safety event until reliable operation is confirmed.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is safely supported independently of the affected unit. If adequate alternate support is established, troubleshooting may continue.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Failure

Determine whether the unit:

- Shows no signs of power.
- Begins startup but does not complete boot.
- Restarts repeatedly.
- Powers off after operating for a period.
- Loses only the display while other functions appear active.
- Produces an alarm, message, or unusual sound during startup.

Record the exact behavior and any displayed message rather than assuming a failed power supply.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is reproduced or clearly characterized. If the reported problem cannot be reproduced and the unit operates normally, continue through verification before returning it to service.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source

Inspect the power cord and plug for damage, overheating, bent contacts, contamination, or incomplete insertion. Confirm the cord is fully seated at the equipment and wall outlet.

Verify the wall outlet with an appropriate approved tester or known-good load according to facility practice. Avoid relying only on the fact that another receptacle in the room works.

**Expected outcome:** Reliable AC power is present and the cord and connections are secure. If correcting the outlet or cord connection restores reliable operation, proceed to final functional verification and troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Inspect External Power Components

Inspect accessible external power components for:

- Damaged power cord insulation.
- Loose equipment-side connection.
- Damaged strain relief.
- Evidence of liquid intrusion.
- Burn marks, abnormal heat, or odor.
- Signs that the unit was recently unplugged, transported, or connected through an unsuitable extension or temporary power arrangement.

Do not open internal power assemblies during initial troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** External power components are intact and properly connected. Any damaged power component requires removal from service until corrected.

### 5. Verify Normal Power-Control Operation

Operate the normal power control as intended and observe the entire startup sequence. Do not repeatedly cycle power rapidly.

Note whether indicators illuminate, the display initializes, alarms sound, or the system reaches its normal operating screen.

**Expected outcome:** The unit progresses through startup without freezing, restarting, or shutting down. If startup completes normally after correcting an external power or control issue, continue to final verification and troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Disconnect Nonessential External Accessories

With the patient removed and the unit safely powered down, disconnect nonessential externally attached accessories or communication connections that are not required for basic operation.

Restart the unit in a basic configuration. Reconnect accessories individually if needed to determine whether one external connection is associated with the failure.

**Expected outcome:** The unit starts and remains running with basic external connections. If a specific accessory or cable causes the failure, remove that accessory from use, document the finding, and verify the incubator without it.

### 7. Check for Environmental or Mechanical Conditions

Verify that ventilation openings are unobstructed and that the device is not exposed to unusual heat, moisture, liquid contamination, or conditions inconsistent with its normal clinical environment.

Inspect for obvious transport damage or evidence that a panel, cover, or external component is not properly seated.

**Expected outcome:** No external environmental or mechanical condition explains the shutdown. If correcting an obstruction or environmental condition restores reliable operation, complete functional verification and troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Observe Operation for Stability

After successful startup, allow the Babyleo TN500 to remain operating long enough to determine that it does not unexpectedly restart or shut down.

Observe the display, indicators, alarms, thermal functions, and other required basic functions without placing a patient on the device.

**Expected outcome:** The unit remains powered and stable without unexpected restart, display loss, or alarm condition.

### 9. Perform Final Functional and Safety Verification

Before return to service, verify the functions affected by the original complaint and complete any required electrical safety or manufacturer-prescribed return-to-service testing appropriate to the repair or intervention.

Confirm that alarms and patient-support functions required for clinical use are operational.

**Expected outcome:** The unit starts normally, remains running, and passes applicable functional and safety checks. If so, the issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If the Babyleo TN500 still will not power on, cannot complete boot, repeatedly restarts, or shuts down after external power, connections, accessories, controls, and environmental causes have been ruled out, the problem may involve an internal power, control, software, or other service-level condition.

The device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Drager service documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not proceed into internal board-level troubleshooting or unauthorized service functions. After repair, complete the appropriate manufacturer-required functional, alarm, electrical-safety, and return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never continue using a Babyleo TN500 that intermittently restarts or loses power; move the infant to verified alternate thermal support first.

## Final Thought

Patient safety comes first when an infant-support device has an unreliable power condition. Verify external power, connections, accessories, controls, and environment before assuming internal failure, then escalate appropriately and document the complaint, cause, corrective action, and final verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
