## What This Guide Helps With

Vaporizer recognition or interlock problems caused by incorrect mounting, incompatible accessories, contamination, obstruction, damage, or positioning.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Anesthetic Delivery

Do not troubleshoot vaporizer recognition or interlock problems while the patient depends on the affected vaporizer for anesthetic delivery. Move to a verified alternate anesthesia-delivery method first.

**Expected outcome:** Anesthesia care continues independently of the affected vaporizer or mounting position.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Vaporizer Complaint

Record whether the vaporizer is not recognized, cannot be selected, does not mechanically engage correctly, or produces an interlock-related warning.

Identify which mounting position and vaporizer are involved.

**Expected outcome:** The fault is localized to a particular vaporizer, position, or combination.

### 3. Verify Vaporizer Compatibility and Identification

Confirm the vaporizer is approved and compatible with the Atlan A300 configuration in use. Check that the correct anesthetic vaporizer is being installed in the intended mounting location.

Do not modify adapters or defeat interlock features.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer is appropriate for the machine configuration.

### 4. Remove and Reinstall the Vaporizer

With the machine safely out of patient use, remove the vaporizer using normal approved handling and reinstall it fully. Confirm it seats evenly and that its locking mechanism is completely engaged.

Do not force a vaporizer that does not seat normally.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer mounts securely and is recognized. If normal operation returns, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 5. Inspect the Mounting Interface

Inspect the accessible vaporizer mounting surface for foreign material, damage, contamination, bent external hardware, or anything preventing full engagement.

Do not perform internal disassembly of the mounting system.

**Expected outcome:** The interface is clean, intact, and unobstructed.

### 6. Check Interlock Positioning

Verify other installed vaporizers are fully seated and that no control is left in an unintended position that mechanically prevents normal interlock operation.

Operate controls only through their normal user-accessible range.

**Expected outcome:** The interlock behaves normally and prevents inappropriate simultaneous selection while allowing intended operation.

### 7. Compare With a Known-Good Compatible Vaporizer

If available, install a known-good compatible vaporizer in the affected position and test recognition. If appropriate, test the suspect vaporizer on another compatible verified machine according to facility procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The comparison identifies whether the problem follows the vaporizer or remains with the machine mounting position.

### 8. Perform Functional Verification

After correcting the issue, complete the required machine test and verify vaporizer recognition, secure mounting, interlock function, and anesthetic-agent delivery using approved test equipment when required.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer is recognized, securely installed, and the interlock functions correctly.

### 9. Escalate an Unresolved Interlock or Recognition Fault

If recognition remains inconsistent, the vaporizer cannot mount normally, or the interlock does not function reliably after external checks, remove the equipment from service.

**Expected outcome:** The affected machine or vaporizer is withheld from clinical use pending qualified evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External mounting, compatibility, contamination, positioning, and vaporizer-related causes have been ruled out. The remaining condition may involve the machine's vaporizer interface, recognition system, interlock mechanism, or a service-level fault within the vaporizer itself.

Remove the affected equipment from service and label it Out of Service. Evaluate it using appropriate Drager and vaporizer manufacturer documentation and approved anesthetic-agent test equipment. Internal repairs, adjustments, or interlock servicing should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Complete vaporizer mounting, identification, interlock, agent-output, and machine system tests before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never bypass, defeat, or work around a vaporizer interlock; use another verified vaporizer or anesthesia machine instead.
