## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots mattress-platform movement problems caused by obstruction, loading, positioning, controls, mechanical interference, or movement-system faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Infant and Prevent Unintended Movement

Do not troubleshoot a sticking, uncontrolled, or unreliable mattress platform with an infant on the bed.

Transfer the infant safely or have clinical staff establish a safe supported condition before testing movement.

Keep hands, tubing, cables, and equipment clear of pinch points.

**Expected outcome:** The patient and personnel are protected from unintended platform movement.

### 2. Confirm Which Movement Is Affected

Determine whether the problem involves:

- Raising
- Lowering
- Tilting
- Mattress rotation
- More than one movement
- Movement only at a particular position
- Intermittent movement

Note whether controls respond, motors or mechanisms can be heard, and whether movement starts then stops.

**Expected outcome:** The failed motion and its behavior are clearly identified.

### 3. Look for External Obstructions

Inspect around and beneath the mattress platform for:

- Linens
- Tubing
- Cables
- Accessories
- Side panels
- Stored items
- Foreign objects

Ensure nothing is caught between moving and fixed surfaces.

**Expected outcome:** The platform has a clear movement path. If removing an obstruction restores normal motion, troubleshooting can stop after full verification.

### 4. Verify Normal Platform Loading

Remove unnecessary equipment and confirm the platform is not carrying an unusual external load.

Do not force movement manually if the mechanism does not move normally.

**Expected outcome:** Excessive or uneven external loading is ruled out.

### 5. Verify Side Panels and Related Components

Confirm side panels, doors, mattress components, and associated hardware are fully seated and not interfering with platform translation, tilt, or rotation.

Check for obviously bent, loose, or displaced external components.

**Expected outcome:** Surrounding components are correctly positioned and do not bind the platform.

### 6. Check the User Controls

Operate the appropriate movement control while observing whether it responds consistently.

If one button, encoder, or control does not respond but other functions work, note the distinction for further troubleshooting.

Do not access hidden menus or bypass movement controls.

**Expected outcome:** The movement command is accepted and the platform moves smoothly.

### 7. Inspect for Mechanical Binding or Damage

With the device out of patient use, inspect accessible rails, pivots, handles, knobs, and platform edges for impact damage, contamination, looseness, or abnormal resistance.

Do not dismantle the lift or tilt mechanisms as part of this external troubleshooting guide.

**Expected outcome:** No accessible mechanical defect prevents normal movement.

### 8. Test the Full Permitted Range

If movement is restored, operate the platform through its normal permitted range without a patient, stopping immediately if there is binding, jerking, unexpected motion, or abnormal noise.

Verify that movement stops when controls are released.

**Expected outcome:** Raising, lowering, tilting, and rotation operate smoothly and predictably as equipped.

### 9. Perform Final Safety Verification

Confirm the mattress platform is secure, side panels are correctly installed, controls respond normally, and no cables or accessories can be pinched during normal operation.

**Expected outcome:** The platform is mechanically stable and all available movement functions pass required testing. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If obstruction, loading, external hardware, controls, and visible mechanical interference have been ruled out, the fault may involve the lift system, tilt assembly, motor or actuator, control switch, position sensing, wiring, or internal control electronics.

The OmniBed should be:

- Removed from service if safe mattress positioning cannot be guaranteed
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using current GE Healthcare service documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired only by qualified personnel

Do not force or bypass a failed movement mechanism. Complete mechanical-safety and functional testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Before moving the mattress platform, ensure patient tubing and cables have enough slack to prevent dislodgement, entrapment, or unintended pulling.

## Final Thought

Platform movement problems should be approached as both equipment and patient-safety hazards. Rule out obstructions, cable routing, loading, and positioning before assuming an actuator failure, and never force a mechanism that does not move normally.

That is successful troubleshooting.
