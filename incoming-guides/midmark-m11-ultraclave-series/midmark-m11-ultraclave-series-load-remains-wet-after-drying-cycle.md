## What This Guide Helps With

Wet loads caused by loading, packaging, drainage, door opening, water quantity, drying airflow, or incomplete cycle performance.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Sterility and Clinical Workflow
Do not treat a visibly wet load as acceptable for storage or use when moisture compromises packaging or sterility assurance.

Reprocess the load according to facility policy after the sterilizer problem is addressed.

**Expected outcome:** Wet or compromised loads are withheld from patient use.

### 2. Confirm the Wet-Load Pattern
Determine whether moisture affects:
- All packs
- Only specific trays
- Items near the chamber walls
- The bottom of packs
- Every cycle
- Only heavily loaded cycles

**Expected outcome:** The pattern helps distinguish loading issues from equipment-wide drying problems.

### 3. Inspect Load Arrangement
Check for:
- Overloading
- Packs placed too closely together
- Stacked items
- Blocked circulation spaces
- Containers positioned in a way that traps condensate

Correct loading only within approved facility and manufacturer practices.

**Expected outcome:** The load arrangement permits steam removal and drying airflow.

### 4. Check Packaging and Item Preparation
Verify packaging materials and instrument preparation are appropriate for steam sterilization and not excessively dense or wet before the cycle begins.

**Expected outcome:** Packaging and preparation are not introducing avoidable moisture retention.

### 5. Verify Chamber Drainage
Inspect accessible drain and chamber areas for:
- Debris
- Mineral buildup
- Standing water
- Obvious drainage restriction

**Expected outcome:** Condensate can leave the chamber normally.

### 6. Verify Water Level and Condition
Confirm the reservoir is filled appropriately and not overfilled. Inspect for contaminated or heavily mineralized water.

**Expected outcome:** Water conditions are appropriate and not contributing to excess residual moisture.

### 7. Check Door and Seal Condition
Inspect the door gasket and sealing surface for contamination or damage that could affect normal steam and exhaust behavior.

**Expected outcome:** The door seals properly throughout the cycle.

### 8. Run an Empty or Light Test Load
Run an approved test cycle with an empty chamber or suitable light test load.

Observe whether the chamber and test items dry normally.

**Expected outcome:** Drying performance is normal under a controlled load. If successful, loading or packaging was the likely cause and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 9. Verify Full Cycle Completion
Confirm the sterilizer completes the drying phase normally and does not terminate early, lose power, or display abnormal status.

**Expected outcome:** The full drying portion of the cycle completes without interruption.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Wet Loads
If wet loads continue despite appropriate loading, drainage, water condition, and full cycle completion, remove the unit from service.

**Expected outcome:** A sterilizer with unreliable drying performance is not returned to routine use without evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as loading, packaging, drainage, water condition, and cycle completion have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal heating, exhaust, venting, airflow, valves, sensors, or control functions.

The sterilizer should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Midmark documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Verify complete sterilization and drying performance before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A wet sterile package can lose sterility assurance during handling or storage and should not simply be dried externally and used.

Protect sterility first, correct loading and drainage before suspecting internal drying components, and verify a complete dry load before returning the sterilizer to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
