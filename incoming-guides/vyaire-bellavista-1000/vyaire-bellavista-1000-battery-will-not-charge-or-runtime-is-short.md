## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot charging failures, unexpectedly short battery operation, external power problems, connection issues, battery condition, and usage-related causes before service escalation.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Before Testing Battery Operation

Do not intentionally disconnect AC power or test questionable battery runtime while a patient depends on the ventilator. Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or maintain a reliable power source before evaluation.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is protected from loss of ventilation caused by a depleted or unreliable battery. Once safe, continue troubleshooting.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Battery Complaint

Determine whether the battery does not charge, charging status is not indicated, battery capacity falls rapidly, the ventilator shuts down prematurely, or the complaint followed prolonged storage or repeated battery operation.

Record the displayed battery and power-source indications.

**Expected outcome:** The charging or runtime problem is clearly characterized. If normal charging and expected clinical operation are demonstrated consistently, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source

Connect the ventilator to a verified AC outlet. Inspect the outlet, plug, and power cord for looseness or damage and confirm that the ventilator recognizes external power.

A battery cannot be properly evaluated for charging until reliable AC power has been established.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator recognizes stable AC input. If correcting the AC source restores charging behavior, proceed to verification and stop troubleshooting when satisfactory.

### 4. Inspect the Power Cord and External Connection

Check the power cord for damage, loose contacts, strain-relief problems, contamination, or intermittent seating. Substitute an approved known-good cord when permitted.

Observe whether the external-power or charging indication changes when the known-good connection is established.

**Expected outcome:** External power reaches the ventilator consistently. If a defective cord caused the complaint, replace the cord and verify charging before closing the work order.

### 5. Inspect for Obvious Battery Safety Concerns

Check the accessible exterior of the ventilator and battery area for swelling, deformation, excessive heat, leakage, unusual odor, or other evidence of battery distress.

Do not continue charging or runtime testing if a battery safety concern is present.

**Expected outcome:** No evidence of unsafe battery condition is present. If swelling, overheating, leakage, or damage is observed, remove the ventilator from service and escalate immediately.

### 6. Allow a Controlled Charging Period

With the ventilator out of clinical use, connect it to verified AC power and observe whether charging status is recognized over an appropriate period according to approved manufacturer guidance.

Avoid judging battery capacity from a brief connection to AC after significant discharge.

**Expected outcome:** The battery accepts charge and the displayed battery status progresses normally. If charging does not occur despite verified external power, continue to escalation after external checks.

### 7. Compare Battery Behavior Under Controlled Conditions

After appropriate charging, evaluate battery operation under a controlled non-patient condition using approved procedures. Observe whether battery status declines abnormally quickly or the ventilator unexpectedly transfers power states or shuts down.

Do not invent a runtime threshold; compare results with applicable manufacturer requirements and the battery's documented service history.

**Expected outcome:** Battery operation is stable and satisfies applicable manufacturer or facility acceptance criteria. If it does, troubleshooting is resolved after final verification.

### 8. Review Battery Age and Service History

Check available maintenance records for previous battery complaints, replacement history, prolonged storage, repeated deep discharge, or failed battery testing.

Battery capacity can deteriorate even when the ventilator otherwise functions normally.

**Expected outcome:** Battery history supports the observed condition and guides appropriate replacement or service action. Replace only through an approved service process when indicated.

### 9. Perform Final Power-Source Verification

Verify correct indication and transition between approved external power and battery operation under non-patient conditions. Confirm alarms and power-status indications function as required.

Complete any applicable post-service safety and functional testing.

**Expected outcome:** Charging, battery operation, power-source indication, and alarms are reliable. If all required checks pass, the ventilator may be returned to service.

### 10. Stop and Escalate an Unresolved Battery Problem

If AC power and the external cord are verified but the ventilator will not charge properly, shows unsafe battery behavior, or demonstrates inadequate capacity, do not return it to clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator remains out of service until the battery system is evaluated and corrected by qualified personnel.

## If the Problem Persists

Common AC source, cord, connection, charging-time, and obvious external causes have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve a degraded battery, charging circuitry, battery monitoring, power-management electronics, configuration, or another service-level condition.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Vyaire documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired, configured, or supplied with a replacement battery only by qualified personnel.
- Fully tested for power transfer, alarms, battery operation, and applicable ventilator performance before clinical return.

Knowing when battery reliability cannot be established and stopping external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not rely on a ventilator with uncertain battery capacity for patient transport or any situation where AC power may be interrupted.

## Final Thought

Battery problems on ventilators require conservative handling because loss of power can immediately affect therapy. Verify the external power path first, evaluate battery behavior safely, avoid assuming an internal failure, and escalate whenever dependable battery operation cannot be demonstrated.

That is successful troubleshooting.
