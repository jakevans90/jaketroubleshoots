## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot flow-sensor calibration failures or drifting measurements caused by contamination, moisture, positioning, connections, circuit configuration, or a defective sensor.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Establish Alternate Ventilation

Do not troubleshoot unreliable flow or tidal-volume measurement while a patient depends on the affected ventilator. Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator when measurement accuracy or ventilation safety is uncertain.

**Expected outcome:** The patient remains safely ventilated while the Evita XL is available for controlled troubleshooting.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Flow-Sensor Problem

Determine whether the complaint involves a calibration failure, unstable displayed flow, drifting tidal volume, intermittent sensor recognition, or inconsistent readings.

Repeat the applicable check in a controlled setup and document the observed behavior.

**Expected outcome:** The reported condition is reproduced or clearly characterized. If it cannot be reproduced and all verification tests pass repeatedly, troubleshooting can stop after documentation.

### 3. Inspect the Flow Sensor and Connections

Inspect the accessible flow sensor or NeoFlow sensor, associated cable or tubing, connectors, and mounting position. Look for contamination, condensation, damage, loose connections, bent contacts, or incorrect orientation.

Do not attempt internal sensor repair.

**Expected outcome:** The sensor and connections are clean, dry, intact, correctly positioned, and secure. If correction restores stable values and calibration passes, troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Check for Moisture and Circuit Contamination

Inspect the breathing circuit, humidification components, water traps, and sensor area for condensate or secretions that could alter measurement.

Correct the external condition and replace contaminated disposable components when appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The sensing pathway is free of moisture and contamination that could affect flow measurement. If calibration now passes and readings remain stable, troubleshooting can stop.

### 5. Verify Circuit and Sensor Configuration

Confirm that the ventilator is configured for the actual circuit and sensor arrangement being used. Verify that accessories are connected in their intended locations and that no incompatible component has been introduced.

Avoid unauthorized configuration changes.

**Expected outcome:** The installed sensor and circuit match the selected clinical configuration. If correcting configuration resolves the issue, complete verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 6. Substitute a Known-Good Sensor or Cable

Where compatible and approved, substitute a known-good flow sensor, NeoFlow sensor, cable, or associated external component one item at a time.

Observe whether the calibration failure or drift follows the original component.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator calibrates normally and provides stable readings with the known-good component. If so, replace the defective external component and stop troubleshooting after verification.

### 7. Compare Against Approved Test Equipment

Use an appropriate ventilator analyzer or other approved test equipment to compare displayed flow or volume with measured performance.

Do not adjust calibration merely to force agreement without following authorized procedures.

**Expected outcome:** Displayed and measured performance meet the applicable approved verification criteria. If they do, the problem is resolved.

### 8. Repeat Calibration and Functional Testing

Perform the required calibration or device check after external causes have been corrected. Then run a functional test using a test lung and suitable analyzer.

Confirm stable values through more than one breathing cycle and operating condition as appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** Calibration completes successfully and flow-related measurements remain stable during functional testing. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Escalate Persistent Calibration or Drift Problems

If known-good sensors, cables, circuit components, and correct configuration do not resolve the problem, stop external troubleshooting. Possible causes include internal sensing, signal-processing, pneumatic, or calibration-system faults.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator is removed from service and referred for qualified service-level evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External sensor condition, moisture, circuit configuration, connections, and known-good substitutions have been ruled out.

Remove the device from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Drager documentation and approved test equipment. Internal sensing, calibration, or signal-processing work should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Complete required calibration, device checks, performance verification, alarm testing, and return-to-service testing after repair.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not rely on drifting flow or tidal-volume values for patient management; use another verified ventilator until measurement accuracy is confirmed.
