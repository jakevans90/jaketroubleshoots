## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot persistent gas-supply or mixer faults caused by hoses, outlets, fittings, source selection, external restrictions, configuration, or service-level pneumatic problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ventilation

A gas-supply or mixer problem can affect delivered oxygen concentration and ventilation. Move the patient to another verified ventilator or backup ventilation method before investigating a persistent fault.

**Expected outcome:** Patient ventilation and oxygen delivery continue safely from a verified alternate source.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Record the exact alarm or device-check message and determine whether the issue affects air, oxygen, gas mixing, or both supplies.

Confirm whether the fault occurs continuously, only during startup, or only at higher demand.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is clearly identified and reproducible. If the fault clears and repeated checks remain normal, complete functional verification before stopping.

### 3. Verify Gas Hoses and Connections

Inspect the external air and oxygen hoses for complete engagement, damage, kinks, contamination, incorrect connection, or excessive tension.

Disconnect and reconnect accessible fittings as appropriate and verify that they latch securely.

**Expected outcome:** Both gas connections are secure, undamaged, and unobstructed. If correcting a connection clears the fault and verification passes, troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Verify the Wall Outlet or Gas Source

Confirm the source with an independent approved method rather than relying solely on the ventilator indication. If appropriate, test another known-good outlet or source.

Do not assume normal facility pressure at one outlet guarantees proper delivery through the ventilator's connected hose and fittings.

**Expected outcome:** The connected gas source supplies the ventilator normally. If changing to a verified outlet resolves the problem, investigate the original infrastructure separately and stop ventilator troubleshooting.

### 5. Inspect External Gas-Supply Accessories

Check any external regulators, hose extensions, adapters, quick-connect fittings, or other accessories for restriction, incorrect installation, damage, or incompatibility.

Remove unnecessary intermediate components during controlled testing when appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator receives gas through an unobstructed, appropriate external path. If the error clears, document the defective accessory and stop troubleshooting.

### 6. Verify Intended Gas and Oxygen Configuration

Confirm that the ventilator setup and connected gas sources match the intended configuration. Review accessible settings related to oxygen delivery without entering unauthorized service menus or altering protected calibration values.

**Expected outcome:** The gas-source configuration is appropriate and no user-accessible setting explains the fault. If correcting configuration resolves the problem, complete verification and stop.

### 7. Perform a Controlled Functional Test

Connect a test lung and appropriate analyzer. Operate the ventilator through representative conditions and observe whether the gas-supply or mixer error recurs.

Verify oxygen delivery using approved test equipment where applicable.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator operates without supply or mixer alarms and delivers expected gas concentrations under test. If successful, troubleshooting can stop after final checks.

### 8. Compare With Known-Good External Gas Components

When feasible, use known-good hoses and verified sources to isolate the ventilator from external supply components.

Change one item at a time.

**Expected outcome:** If the error disappears with a known-good hose or source, the external defective component is identified. Replace or route the infrastructure issue appropriately and stop troubleshooting.

### 9. Escalate a Persistent Mixer or Supply Error

If verified gas sources, hoses, fittings, configuration, and external accessories are normal but the ventilator continues reporting a gas-supply or mixer problem, stop external troubleshooting.

The problem may involve internal pneumatic regulation, gas measurement, mixing control, valves, or calibration.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator is removed from service and sent for qualified service evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External gas pressure, wall outlets, hoses, fittings, source configuration, and accessories have been ruled out.

Remove the Evita XL from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Qualified personnel should evaluate the internal pneumatic and gas-mixing systems using appropriate Drager service documentation and approved test equipment.

Following repair, verify gas delivery, oxygen concentration, ventilation performance, alarms, device checks, and all required return-to-service functions.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never continue patient ventilation on a unit with an unresolved gas-mixing fault when delivered oxygen concentration or pneumatic performance cannot be assured.

## Final Thought

Begin with patient safety and the entire external gas path, verify the source independently, avoid assuming an internal mixer failure prematurely, escalate appropriately, and document the confirmed cause and correction.

That is successful troubleshooting.
