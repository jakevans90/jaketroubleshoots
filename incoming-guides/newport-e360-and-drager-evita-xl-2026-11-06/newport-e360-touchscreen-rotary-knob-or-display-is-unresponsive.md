## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses frozen or unresponsive controls and display problems caused by power, contamination, accessories, startup state, or control-interface failure.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Before Manipulating Controls
If controls or the display cannot be trusted, do not troubleshoot while the ventilator supports a patient. Transfer ventilation to another verified device.

Determine whether the touchscreen, rotary control, entire display, or only a specific area or function is affected.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is protected and the exact control-interface problem is identified.

### 2. Confirm Basic Power and Startup
Verify that the ventilator is connected to a known-good power source and completes normal startup as far as visible.

Observe whether the display is blank, frozen, partially illuminated, distorted, or operating normally while controls remain unresponsive.

**Expected outcome:** Power is stable and the failure is isolated to the user interface rather than a complete loss of power.

### 3. Inspect the Screen and Controls
Check for moisture, cleaning residue, protective material, physical impact damage, cracked surfaces, stuck controls, or contamination around the touchscreen and rotary knob.

**Expected outcome:** The interface is clean, dry, and physically unobstructed. If cleaning approved external contamination restores reliable operation, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 4. Remove External Accessories That Could Affect Operation
Disconnect nonessential external communication accessories or peripherals while the ventilator is off-patient if permitted by facility procedures.

Restart the ventilator normally.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator starts with responsive controls. If a peripheral consistently causes the problem, isolate the accessory or integration issue.

### 5. Perform a Controlled Restart
With the ventilator removed from clinical use, shut it down and restart it using normal operating controls.

Do not repeatedly power-cycle an unstable ventilator as a substitute for repair.

**Expected outcome:** The interface initializes and remains responsive. If the issue returns, continue troubleshooting rather than returning the device to service.

### 6. Check Rotary Control Operation
Verify that the rotary control moves freely and consistently registers permitted menu navigation and selections.

**Expected outcome:** Rotary input corresponds with on-screen movement and selection. Erratic or absent response suggests a control-interface fault requiring escalation.

### 7. Check Touchscreen Response
Test multiple areas of the touchscreen using normal non-clinical menu functions. Do not enter restricted service menus or change protected configuration.

**Expected outcome:** Touch inputs register at the intended locations. Persistent dead areas or incorrect touch registration require service evaluation.

### 8. Observe Display Stability
Allow the ventilator to operate on a test lung while observing for freezing, flickering, loss of backlighting, corrupted graphics, or delayed updates.

**Expected outcome:** The display remains stable and controls remain responsive throughout testing.

### 9. Perform Final Functional and Alarm Verification
Verify control entry, ventilation changes under controlled bench conditions, display updates, and audible and visual alarms.

**Expected outcome:** Controls and display are consistently responsive and ventilation responds correctly to permitted setting changes.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Interface Failure
If external conditions and controlled restart do not restore reliable operation, remove the ventilator from service.

**Expected outcome:** A ventilator with unreliable controls or display is not returned to patient use.

## If the Problem Persists

External contamination, power, peripherals, and temporary startup conditions have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve the touchscreen assembly, rotary encoder, display electronics, user-interface processing, internal connections, or software requiring authorized service.

Remove the ventilator from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for bench evaluation using manufacturer documentation. Do not perform board-level repair or unauthorized software changes.

Return the ventilator to service only after the display and all required controls remain responsive during extended functional testing and alarm verification.

## Clinical Use Tip

A ventilator that continues delivering breaths but has unreliable controls or an unreadable display is still unsafe for patient use.

## Final Thought

Control-interface problems require more than confirming that the ventilator still cycles. Protect the patient, check external causes, verify consistent control response, and escalate any intermittent or unresolved interface failure.

That is successful troubleshooting.
