## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot loss of communication between the display/control unit and ventilator caused by power, external connections, cable damage, seating, or service-level communication faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Immediately

Loss of the display or control interface can prevent reliable monitoring, setting changes, and alarm interaction. If communication is lost during patient use, transfer ventilation to another verified device or appropriate backup support.

Do not continue troubleshooting while a patient depends on an unreliable control interface.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is safely supported by another verified ventilation method before technical evaluation begins.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Communication Failure

Determine whether the display is blank, frozen, intermittently disconnecting, showing a communication-related message, or remaining powered while controls no longer affect the ventilator.

Document whether the ventilator itself appears to remain powered.

**Expected outcome:** The failure mode is clearly characterized. If a transient condition cannot be reproduced and all tests subsequently pass, complete extended verification before return to service.

### 3. Verify AC Power and Basic Ventilator Power

Check that the ventilator is connected to a known-good AC source and that power cords and accessible power connections are secure.

Verify outlet operation independently where appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** Stable AC power is confirmed. If restoring power corrects communication and all subsequent checks pass, troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Inspect the Display/Control Unit Connections

Inspect accessible communication and power connections between the display/control unit and ventilator. Look for loose connectors, damaged cables, strained connectors, bent hardware, contamination, or incomplete seating.

Do not disassemble the control unit or ventilator chassis.

**Expected outcome:** External connections are intact and fully secured. If reseating a connection restores reliable communication and verification passes, troubleshooting can stop.

### 5. Inspect Cable Routing and Mechanical Stress

Check whether the communication cable is pinched, sharply bent, trapped by equipment, pulled tight, or damaged from repeated movement.

Manipulate only within normal equipment handling limits while observing for reproducible communication loss.

**Expected outcome:** Cable routing is secure and movement does not interrupt communication. If an external cable is defective and approved replacement resolves the issue, troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Power-Cycle the Unused Ventilator Under Controlled Conditions

After patient removal, perform a normal power-down and restart. Observe startup of both the ventilator and control/display unit.

Do not repeatedly power-cycle a ventilator that exhibits abnormal odor, heat, smoke, or electrical damage.

**Expected outcome:** Both sections boot normally and establish stable communication. If communication remains stable through repeated functional testing, continue to final verification.

### 7. Test All External Controls and Display Functions

Confirm that the display updates normally, control inputs are accepted, alarms appear correctly, and parameter changes are reflected by the ventilator during test-lung operation.

**Expected outcome:** Communication remains stable under normal operation. If successful and no intermittent fault is observed, troubleshooting can stop after return-to-service testing.

### 8. Escalate Intermittent or Persistent Communication Loss

If connections, power, and externally replaceable cables are normal but communication remains unreliable, remove the ventilator from service.

Possible service-level causes include internal communication circuitry, power distribution, control electronics, or display/control hardware.

**Expected outcome:** The device is withheld from clinical use and routed for qualified repair.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving power, cable seating, connector condition, and cable routing have been ruled out.

Remove the Evita XL from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Drager service documentation and approved test equipment. Internal communication electronics and control-unit repair should be handled only by qualified personnel.

Following repair, verify startup, display communication, control response, alarms, ventilation performance, and all required return-to-service tests.

## Clinical Use Tip

A ventilator that continues operating while its control/display unit is unreliable is still unsafe for continued patient dependence because monitoring and control may be compromised.

## Final Thought

Treat control-interface communication as a patient-safety issue, verify power and external connections first, confirm stability before assuming internal electronics have failed, and escalate any intermittent unresolved fault.

That is successful troubleshooting.
