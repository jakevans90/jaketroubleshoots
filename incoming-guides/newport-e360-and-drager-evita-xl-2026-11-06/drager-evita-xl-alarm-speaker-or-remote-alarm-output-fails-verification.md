## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot failed audible or remote alarm verification caused by settings, connections, cabling, interface equipment, infrastructure, or service-level alarm circuitry.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Provide Reliable Alarm Coverage

Do not leave a patient dependent on a ventilator with an unreliable audible or remote alarm path.

Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or provide an approved alternate alarm-monitoring method before technical troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** Continuous patient alarm coverage is maintained independently of the failed alarm path.

### 2. Confirm Which Alarm Function Failed

Determine whether the local audible speaker, visual alarm indication, remote alarm output, nurse-call connection, or more than one alarm function is affected.

Trigger an appropriate test condition only in a controlled setup.

**Expected outcome:** The failed portion of the alarm chain is clearly identified.

### 3. Verify Accessible Alarm Settings

Check user-accessible alarm volume and relevant alarm configuration. Confirm that settings have not been reduced, disabled where allowed, or altered unexpectedly.

Do not enter restricted service configuration menus.

**Expected outcome:** Alarm settings are appropriate. If correcting a setting restores reliable alarm operation, complete verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Verify Local Alarm Operation

With the ventilator off the patient and connected to a test lung, initiate an approved alarm condition and confirm audible and visual annunciation.

Listen for distortion, intermittent audio, or absent sound.

**Expected outcome:** Local audible and visual alarms operate consistently. If the local alarm fails while settings are correct, remove the device from service and continue only with safe external evaluation.

### 5. Inspect Remote Alarm Connections

Inspect the external remote alarm or nurse-call cable, connectors, adapters, and strain relief for damage or incomplete engagement.

Verify that the correct interface cable is installed.

**Expected outcome:** The external remote-alarm connection is secure and physically intact. If reseating or replacing a defective external cable restores operation, troubleshooting can stop after end-to-end verification.

### 6. Test With a Known-Good Interface Path

Where available, substitute a known-good remote alarm cable or connect the ventilator to a known-good compatible test interface.

This helps separate a ventilator problem from room wiring or central alarm infrastructure.

**Expected outcome:** If the ventilator operates normally on the known-good path, the problem is external to the ventilator and should be routed to the appropriate infrastructure owner.

### 7. Verify the Complete Remote Alarm Chain

Do not stop after observing an output at the ventilator. Confirm that the alarm reaches the intended receiving device or monitoring location when practical and appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The alarm is transmitted and received at the intended destination. If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Escalate Internal Alarm Failures

If the local speaker fails despite correct settings, or a remote alarm output remains nonfunctional with known-good cabling and receiving equipment, remove the ventilator from service.

Possible causes include internal alarm electronics, output circuitry, connectors, or control-system faults.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator is withheld from clinical use and sent for service-level evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Alarm settings, external cabling, connectors, receiving equipment, and infrastructure have been checked as applicable.

Remove the Evita XL from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluation should follow appropriate Drager documentation using approved alarm and electrical test equipment. Internal alarm circuitry should be repaired only by qualified personnel.

After repair, verify local audible and visual alarms and the complete remote alarm path before return to service.

## Clinical Use Tip

For remote alarms, verify the complete communication path through the receiving station rather than confirming only that the ventilator generates an output.

## Final Thought

Alarm reliability is a patient-safety function. Verify the local alarm, external connection, and complete remote pathway before assuming an internal failure, and remove the device from service if reliable annunciation cannot be confirmed.

That is successful troubleshooting.
