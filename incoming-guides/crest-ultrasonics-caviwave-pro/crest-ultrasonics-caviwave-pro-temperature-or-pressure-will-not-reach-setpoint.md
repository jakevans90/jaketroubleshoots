## What This Guide Helps With
Use this guide when the cleaner cannot achieve or maintain its required temperature or reports an abnormal pressure-related process condition.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Protect Processing Integrity

Do not consider a cycle acceptable if the cleaner cannot achieve the required process conditions. Remove affected loads from release and reprocess them according to departmental procedure.

**Expected outcome:** No instruments are released from a cycle with unverified process conditions.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Symptom

Determine whether the problem is slow heating, no heating, unstable temperature, failure to reach a displayed target, or a pressure-related indication during filling, circulation, or another process phase.

Record the displayed value and message without inventing expected specifications.

**Expected outcome:** The affected process variable and cycle phase are clearly documented.

### 3. Verify Proper Chamber Fill

Confirm that the unit is filling and maintaining a normal visible operating level. Heating and process control may be affected by inadequate water volume or an abnormal fill condition.

**Expected outcome:** Fluid level appears normal. If the level is abnormal, troubleshoot the fill condition before continuing.

### 4. Check Facility Utilities

Verify that required facility water and other applicable utilities are available and that external valves are open.

Look for recent building outages, maintenance, unusually cold incoming water, or utility interruptions that correlate with the problem.

**Expected outcome:** Required external utilities are available and stable.

### 5. Inspect the Chamber and Load

Confirm the chamber is not overloaded and that instruments are arranged according to normal departmental workflow.

Excessive load, unusual load mass, or improper placement may affect cycle performance.

**Expected outcome:** The load is reasonable and not obstructing normal process operation.

### 6. Verify Operator-Accessible Cycle Selection

Confirm the intended cleaning cycle was selected and that no user-adjustable setting was unintentionally changed.

Do not alter protected temperature, calibration, or service settings.

**Expected outcome:** The unit is operating with the intended authorized cycle configuration.

### 7. Inspect for Excessive Heat Loss or Leakage

Check the lid or door for proper closure and inspect externally for significant leakage, abnormal steam or vapor release, or other evidence that the system is not retaining process conditions.

**Expected outcome:** The chamber is properly closed and no abnormal external leakage is present.

### 8. Run an Appropriate Unloaded Functional Test

If safe, run a test cycle and observe whether the displayed process value rises and stabilizes normally.

Do not use an independent measurement to recalibrate the unit unless following an approved calibration procedure.

**Expected outcome:** The unit reaches its normal cycle condition and completes successfully. If so, troubleshooting can stop after documentation.

### 9. Stop for Overtemperature or Unstable Control

If the temperature rises abnormally, fluctuates significantly, or the system displays an overtemperature condition, discontinue operation.

**Expected outcome:** Equipment with unreliable thermal control is removed from processing service.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Process-Control Failure

If fluid level, utilities, loading, closure, and normal settings are correct but the unit still cannot establish the required process condition, remove it from service.

**Expected outcome:** Service-level evaluation is initiated rather than attempting internal heater, sensor, or control repair at the equipment location.

## If the Problem Persists
External utilities, filling, loading, closure, and operator settings have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal heating components, temperature sensors, pressure or level sensing, circulation performance, control electronics, or protected calibration.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired, calibrated, or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete appropriate functional and process-performance verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
A completed timer does not prove a valid cleaning process if required process conditions were not achieved; treat the load as incompletely processed.

## Final Thought
Verify the conditions surrounding the process before suspecting a heater, sensor, or control failure. Correct fluid, utility, loading, and closure problems first, then escalate unresolved process-control issues for qualified evaluation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
