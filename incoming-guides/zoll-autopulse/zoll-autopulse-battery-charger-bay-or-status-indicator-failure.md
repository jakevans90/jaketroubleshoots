## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting charger-bay or indicator problems caused by AC power, battery condition, contact contamination, bay failure, or display issues.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Maintain Verified Battery Availability

Do not rely on a charger bay with uncertain operation for emergency-device readiness. Move batteries to a known-good charger as needed so verified charged batteries remain available.

**Expected outcome:** Clinical readiness is preserved while the suspect charger or bay is evaluated.

### 2. Confirm the Charger Complaint

Determine whether:

- One bay does not recognize batteries
- Multiple bays are affected
- A status indicator does not illuminate
- Charging appears not to begin
- Charging status is inconsistent
- The charger has no power

Document the bay involved.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is isolated to a single bay, the entire charger, an indicator, or a specific battery.

### 3. Verify AC Power

Confirm the charger power connection is secure and inspect the cord and plug for visible damage.

Test the outlet using an appropriate method or compare with a known working receptacle according to facility practice.

**Expected outcome:** The charger has reliable AC power. If restoring the external power connection resolves the issue, proceed to verification.

### 4. Inspect the Charger

Check for:

- Cracked housing
- Damaged power cord
- Fluid exposure
- Contamination
- Excessive heat
- Burn odor
- Damaged battery bays

If significant damage, heat, odor, or fluid intrusion is present, unplug and remove the charger from service.

**Expected outcome:** The charger is physically safe for further external testing.

### 5. Inspect Bay Contacts

With power handled appropriately, inspect the affected bay for accessible contamination, debris, or damaged contacts.

Clean only using approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The bay is clean and the battery can seat fully without obstruction.

### 6. Test a Known-Good Battery

Insert a known-good battery into the affected bay.

Observe whether the charger recognizes the battery and whether the status indication behaves normally.

**Expected outcome:** If the known-good battery charges normally, the original battery is likely the source of the problem.

### 7. Compare With Another Bay

Place the same known-good battery into another charger bay.

Then test the suspect battery in a known-good bay if appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The failure consistently follows either the charger bay or the battery, allowing the affected component to be isolated.

### 8. Evaluate Indicator-Only Failure

If a battery appears to charge normally but the status indicator is absent, incorrect, or intermittent, do not assume the charger is fully functional.

Verify charging behavior through approved testing or comparison.

**Expected outcome:** Charger function and status indication agree. Any unreliable status display requires service evaluation because users depend on it for readiness decisions.

### 9. Perform Final Verification

After correcting an external cause, test the affected bay with more than one known-good battery when practical.

**Expected outcome:** The bay consistently recognizes batteries and provides normal status indication. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Charger or Bay Failure

If the problem remains isolated to one bay or the charger itself, remove the charger from service as appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The defective charger is routed for bench evaluation while alternate verified charging capability remains available.

## If the Problem Persists

External causes including AC power, battery condition, seating, contamination, and bay comparison have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve charger electronics, bay circuitry, status indication, internal connections, or another service-level issue. The charger should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate ZOLL documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired only by qualified personnel

Do not perform internal charger repair or board-level troubleshooting without authorized procedures.

Complete required electrical and functional verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A charger status indicator is part of battery-readiness verification; do not place batteries into emergency inventory based on an unreliable indication.

## Final Thought

Charger faults should be isolated between power, the battery, the individual bay, and the status indication. Keep emergency battery readiness intact and escalate any charger whose operation cannot be verified reliably.

That is successful troubleshooting.
