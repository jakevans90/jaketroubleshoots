## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting a LifeBand that is jammed, twisted, misrouted, improperly installed, or obstructed during startup or readiness testing.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Remove the Device From Active Patient Support

If a LifeBand jam or twist occurs during patient use, immediately transition to manual CPR or another verified compression method.

Do not attempt to free a jammed band while relying on the device for ongoing compressions.

**Expected outcome:** CPR continues safely while the AutoPulse is taken out of active use.

### 2. Stop Operation Before Handling the LifeBand

Power the platform down as appropriate and remove the battery before manipulating a visibly jammed or misinstalled LifeBand.

Keep hands and tools clear of any mechanism that could move unexpectedly.

**Expected outcome:** The device cannot initiate band movement while the obstruction is being inspected.

### 3. Inspect the Entire Accessible LifeBand

Look for:

- Twisting
- Folding
- Bunching
- Material caught under the platform
- Tape or linens entangled with the band
- Damage to the band material
- Incorrect attachment or seating

Do not pull excessively on a jammed band.

**Expected outcome:** The cause of the jam is visible or the band appears externally unobstructed.

### 4. Correct External Positioning

If the problem is limited to a twist, fold, or incorrect routing that can be safely corrected externally, reinstall the LifeBand according to approved setup practices.

Do not attempt to straighten internal components through force.

**Expected outcome:** The LifeBand lies flat, routes correctly, and seats securely. If the issue was purely installation-related, continue to verification.

### 5. Inspect the Platform Interface

Check the accessible LifeBand engagement area for debris, dried contamination, damaged retaining features, or a foreign object preventing correct installation.

**Expected outcome:** The attachment area is clean, intact, and free of obstruction.

### 6. Inspect the LifeBand for Damage

Do not reuse a LifeBand that has:

- Tears
- Fraying
- Significant deformation
- Damaged attachment features
- Contamination that cannot be safely removed

**Expected outcome:** Only an intact, serviceable LifeBand is used for further testing.

### 7. Install a Known-Good LifeBand

If the original LifeBand remains questionable, substitute a known-good compatible LifeBand.

**Expected outcome:** The known-good LifeBand installs correctly without binding. If it operates normally, remove the original LifeBand from use.

### 8. Restore Power and Check Operation

Install a known-good charged battery and perform a non-patient startup check.

Observe the LifeBand during its normal preparation sequence without touching moving components.

**Expected outcome:** The LifeBand moves evenly, does not jam, and the system completes normal startup. If so, troubleshooting can stop after required functional testing.

### 9. Escalate Repeated Jamming

If a properly installed known-good LifeBand jams, binds, twists during movement, or cannot be installed securely, do not continue cycling the mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** The platform is removed from service for mechanical bench evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as LifeBand routing, installation, accessory condition, debris, and obstruction have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve drive alignment, mechanical engagement, sensing, or another internal service-level condition. The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate ZOLL documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired only by qualified personnel

Do not perform deep mechanical disassembly or internal drive repair without authorized service procedures.

Complete required functional and return-to-service testing after repair.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Keep linens, straps, cables, and other resuscitation equipment clear of the LifeBand path during patient positioning and device setup.

## Final Thought

A LifeBand jam should first be treated as a positioning, routing, or accessory problem, with patient support transferred before inspection. Correct only accessible causes and escalate repeated mechanical binding appropriately.

That is successful troubleshooting.
