## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots powered movement, positioning, brake release, obstruction, control, battery, and mechanical issues before assuming an internal motion-system failure.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Secure the Mobile System
Do not troubleshoot unreliable powered movement, column motion, or tube-head braking while the system is positioned over or adjacent to a patient.

Move the patient or substitute another verified imaging system when necessary. Stabilize the unit to prevent unexpected movement.

Expected outcome: The patient is protected and the mobile unit can be evaluated without risk of collision, unintended motion, or tube-head movement.

### 2. Confirm Which Motion Function Has Failed
Determine whether the problem affects:
- Powered drive
- Steering
- Column movement
- Tube-head positioning
- Brake release
- Position holding
- More than one motion function

Check whether the failure is constant or intermittent.

Expected outcome: The affected motion function is clearly identified. If normal motion returns and remains stable during repeated testing, proceed to final verification.

### 3. Inspect for Physical Obstruction or Damage
Check the wheels, column, tube support, handles, cables, detector storage areas, and accessible moving structures for:
- Foreign objects
- Tangled cables
- Impact damage
- Bent external components
- Objects preventing full release or travel

Do not force a stuck mechanism.

Expected outcome: Movement paths are clear and no visible damage is present. If removing an obstruction restores normal operation, verify all affected movements and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Verify System Power and Battery Condition
Confirm that the MobileDiagnost wDR is powered normally and that the traction battery has sufficient available charge for powered movement.

If low charge is suspected, connect the system to an approved AC source and reassess after charging as appropriate.

Expected outcome: The system has adequate power for powered drive and brake controls. If restoring charge resolves the problem, verify repeated operation and stop troubleshooting.

### 5. Check Normal Drive and Brake Controls
Inspect externally accessible drive handles, switches, brake-release controls, and positioning controls for:
- Damage
- Sticking
- Contamination
- Failure to return normally
- Loose external parts

Operate only normal user-accessible controls.

Expected outcome: Controls move freely and command the expected function. A damaged or sticking control requires removal from service or repair rather than continued clinical use.

### 6. Verify Parking and Safety Conditions
Confirm that no parking brake, emergency stop, bumper condition, or other obvious external safety interlock is preventing the requested movement.

Do not defeat safety interlocks.

Expected outcome: No active safety condition is preventing motion. If resetting a legitimate externally accessible safety condition restores normal operation, verify that the condition does not recur.

### 7. Compare Powered and Manual Mechanical Behavior
Where normal operation permits, determine whether the mechanism moves freely when using its intended non-powered positioning method.

Do not release or override brakes through unauthorized service methods.

Expected outcome: Smooth mechanical movement with failed powered operation points toward a drive, control, power, or brake-command problem rather than a gross mechanical obstruction.

### 8. Test Each Movement Separately
Evaluate powered drive, steering, column positioning, tube-head movement, and brake holding individually.

Listen for abnormal noise and observe whether movement starts, stops, or drifts unexpectedly.

Expected outcome: Each movement performs smoothly and holds position as designed. Any uncontrolled movement, slipping, or failure to hold requires removal from service.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
After correction, verify:
- Powered drive engages normally
- Steering response is predictable
- Column and tube-head positioning are smooth
- Brakes release and hold correctly
- No unusual noise or drag is present
- The system remains stable when parked

Expected outcome: All tested motion functions operate reliably. Troubleshooting can stop when repeatable safe operation is confirmed.

### 10. Escalate Unresolved Motion Problems
If motorized movement, column operation, or braking remains unreliable after external checks, remove the system from clinical use.

Expected outcome: Equipment with unreliable mobility or position-holding is prevented from creating a patient or staff safety hazard.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as obstruction, battery state, normal controls, parking conditions, and visible mechanical damage have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve drive electronics, brake actuators, motion sensors, control circuits, mechanical assemblies, or service-level configuration.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Philips documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete appropriate mobility, brake, positioning, and safety testing before return to clinical service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any failure that allows unexpected movement or prevents the tube assembly from holding position should be treated as a patient and staff safety issue.

## Final Thought

Secure the patient and equipment first, then work from obvious mechanical obstruction and power conditions toward controls and individual motion functions. Never force or bypass an unsafe mechanism; remove the system from service when reliable movement or position holding cannot be confirmed.

That is successful troubleshooting.
