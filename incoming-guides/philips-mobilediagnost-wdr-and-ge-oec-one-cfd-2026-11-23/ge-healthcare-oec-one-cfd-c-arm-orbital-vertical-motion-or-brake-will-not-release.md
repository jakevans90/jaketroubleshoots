## What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when C-arm positioning is restricted, a brake will not release, or orbital or vertical motion cannot be performed normally.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient From Unintended Movement

Do not troubleshoot positioning or brake faults while the C-arm is positioned over or against a patient. Support the imaging assembly as needed and move the patient or equipment to a safe configuration before testing.

Expected outcome: No patient or staff member is exposed to unintended C-arm movement. If an alternate C-arm is needed, remove the affected unit from the procedure.

### 2. Confirm Which Motion Is Affected

Identify whether the problem involves orbital movement, vertical movement, another positioning axis, or a brake that remains locked or fails to hold. Determine whether the condition is constant or position-dependent.

Expected outcome: The affected motion or brake is clearly identified. If normal movement is restored and remains repeatable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 3. Inspect for Physical Obstruction

Check the C-arm path for tables, accessories, cables, covers, drapes, walls, equipment, or other objects that could mechanically block motion. Look for cables trapped around wheels, columns, or moving assemblies.

Expected outcome: The movement path is unobstructed. If removing an obstruction restores normal motion, verify the full intended range before returning the unit to service.

### 4. Verify Brake and Release Controls

Operate the appropriate externally accessible brake-release or positioning control and observe whether the expected mechanical response occurs. Check for a stuck, damaged, contaminated, or physically obstructed control.

Expected outcome: The control operates freely and the corresponding brake releases or engages normally. If normal function returns after clearing an external control obstruction, troubleshooting can stop after repeated testing.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Cables and Hand Controls

Inspect external hand-control cables, connectors, strain reliefs, and related accessible interfaces for looseness or damage. If a removable control is used and an approved known-good equivalent is available, substitute it.

Expected outcome: The control path is intact and movement responds normally. If a known-good control restores operation, replace or repair the faulty accessory as appropriate and verify function.

### 6. Check System Power and Operating State

Confirm that the system is fully powered, has completed startup, and is not displaying a condition that disables motion. Verify that no emergency stop or similar external safety control has been activated.

Expected outcome: The system is in a normal operating state that permits positioning. If clearing an external safety condition restores motion, verify all related brakes and controls.

### 7. Evaluate Movement Without Forcing the Assembly

With the system unloaded and in a safe area, gently test the affected movement. Do not force a locked C-arm, defeat a brake, or attempt to overpower a mechanical resistance.

Expected outcome: The C-arm moves smoothly when released and holds position when the brake is engaged. Any binding, grinding, uncontrolled motion, or failure to hold requires removal from service.

### 8. Compare Multiple Motion Controls if Available

Where the same movement can be commanded from more than one normal operator control, compare their operation. This can help distinguish a single external control problem from a broader motion-system issue.

Expected outcome: All normal controls produce consistent motion. If one control fails while another operates normally, isolate the faulty external control and verify safe operation before release.

### 9. Perform Final Positioning Verification

Cycle the affected brake and movement repeatedly through a safe operating range. Confirm smooth movement, positive brake engagement, stable positioning, and no abnormal sound or drift.

Expected outcome: Motion and braking are repeatable and stable. If successful, troubleshooting can stop and the system may be returned to service.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Motion or Brake Failure

If motion remains unavailable, the brake will not release or hold, or mechanical resistance persists after external causes are excluded, stop troubleshooting before internal mechanical or electrical disassembly.

Expected outcome: The system is removed from clinical use and referred for qualified service.

## If the Problem Persists

External obstruction, operator controls, accessible connections, power state, and normal safety conditions have been checked. The remaining fault may involve internal brake mechanisms, motion actuators, sensors, control electronics, mechanical adjustment, or service-level configuration.

Remove the device from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved tools. Internal mechanical repair, brake adjustment, or protected configuration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Return-to-service testing should confirm smooth movement, secure brake engagement, stable positioning, and correct operation of all affected positioning controls.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A brake that intermittently releases or fails to hold is a patient and staff safety issue even if the C-arm can still be positioned.

## Final Thought

Keep the patient clear of uncertain mechanical movement, rule out obstructions and external controls first, never force a locked C-arm, and escalate unsafe brake or motion behavior promptly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
