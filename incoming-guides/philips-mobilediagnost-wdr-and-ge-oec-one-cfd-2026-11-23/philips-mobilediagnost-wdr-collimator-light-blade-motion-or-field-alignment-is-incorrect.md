## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots collimator illumination, blade movement, visible obstruction, positioning, and field-alignment concerns before assuming an internal collimator failure.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Stop Questionable Imaging
Do not use the system clinically when the light field, collimator blades, or radiation-field alignment cannot be relied upon.

Move the examination to another verified imaging system.

Expected outcome: The patient is protected from unnecessary or incorrectly collimated radiation exposure.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Collimator Complaint
Determine whether:
- The light does not illuminate
- Illumination is weak or intermittent
- One or more blades do not move
- The visible field appears asymmetric
- The light field does not appear centered
- The reported problem occurs only at certain tube positions

Expected outcome: The symptom is clearly reproduced and documented.

### 3. Inspect the Collimator Externally
Check for:
- Impact damage
- Loose external components
- Obstruction near the aperture
- Foreign objects
- Damage to accessible controls
- Evidence that the tube or collimator was struck

Do not disassemble the collimator.

Expected outcome: No external damage or obstruction is present. Visible impact damage requires removal from service.

### 4. Verify Collimator Light Operation
Operate the collimator lamp through normal controls.

Observe whether it illuminates consistently and whether illumination changes with gentle normal positioning of the tube head.

Expected outcome: The collimator light operates consistently. If operation remains intermittent, escalate rather than relying on it clinically.

### 5. Check Blade Controls and Movement
Move each normal collimator control through its intended range.

Observe whether blades:
- Move smoothly
- Respond symmetrically
- Stop unexpectedly
- Bind or lag

Do not force a control that resists movement.

Expected outcome: Blade movement is smooth and repeatable. If an external obstruction is removed and normal movement returns, continue to functional verification.

### 6. Verify Tube and Receptor Positioning
Confirm that apparent field-alignment issues are not caused by:
- Detector angle
- Tube rotation
- Poor centering
- Extreme geometry
- Misinterpretation of the projected light field

Reposition using normal workflow and reassess.

Expected outcome: Proper positioning produces a normal centered field. If alignment remains questionable, proceed to objective testing.

### 7. Perform Approved Field-Alignment Testing
Use approved radiographic test equipment and established Clinical Engineering or manufacturer procedures to evaluate light-field and radiation-field alignment.

Do not invent or apply acceptance tolerances that are not defined by applicable procedures or regulations.

Expected outcome: Alignment meets the applicable verified requirement. If it does, the system may proceed to final functional checks.

### 8. Verify Blade Function During Repeated Positioning
Move the tube head through representative normal positions and confirm blade controls and light operation remain stable.

Expected outcome: Collimator operation does not change unexpectedly with positioning.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
Confirm:
- Lamp operates
- Controls respond normally
- Blades move smoothly
- Field is appropriately centered
- Alignment testing meets applicable requirements
- No external damage is present

Expected outcome: Collimator operation and field alignment are verified. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate an Unresolved Collimator Problem
If light operation, blade movement, or field alignment remains unreliable or fails applicable testing, remove the system from service.

Expected outcome: A radiation-field control problem is corrected by qualified service before patient use resumes.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as obstruction, positioning, control operation, and obvious physical damage have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve the collimator lamp assembly, blade mechanism, control components, mechanical alignment, or other service-level components.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Philips documentation and approved radiation test equipment
- Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel

Repeat applicable collimator and radiation-field verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not substitute visual estimation for required alignment testing when the relationship between the light field and radiation field is in question.

## Final Thought

Collimation directly affects radiation safety. Rule out positioning and external mechanical causes first, then verify alignment objectively using approved methods. Remove the system from service whenever blade control or field alignment remains unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
