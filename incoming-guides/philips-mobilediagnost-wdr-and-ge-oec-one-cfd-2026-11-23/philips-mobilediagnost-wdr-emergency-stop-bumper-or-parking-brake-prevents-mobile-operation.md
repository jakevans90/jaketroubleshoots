## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots externally accessible safety controls, bumper conditions, parking brakes, obstruction, and reset conditions that prevent normal mobile operation.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Secure the Unit
Do not troubleshoot a mobility safety-system problem while the system is positioned where unexpected movement could contact a patient, bed, wall, or staff member.

Move the patient away or use another verified imaging system if necessary.

Expected outcome: The mobile unit is safely positioned for controlled testing.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Safety-Related Symptom
Determine whether:
- Powered drive is completely inhibited
- The unit behaves as though a bumper is active
- The parking brake will not release
- An emergency stop has been activated
- Mobility stops intermittently
- Manual rolling is also affected

Expected outcome: The specific condition preventing mobility is identified.

### 3. Check the Emergency Stop
Inspect externally accessible emergency-stop controls and determine whether one has been intentionally or accidentally activated.

Reset only according to normal operator procedure.

Never bypass the emergency stop.

Expected outcome: The emergency stop is in its normal operating position. If proper reset restores mobility and the problem does not recur, continue to functional verification.

### 4. Inspect Bumper and Collision Areas
Check accessible bumpers and collision-sensitive areas for:
- Objects lodged against them
- Shipping material
- Wall or equipment contact
- Misalignment from impact
- Visible damage
- A bumper that does not return freely

Do not force or bypass a stuck bumper.

Expected outcome: Bumpers are unobstructed and return normally. If clearing an obstruction restores operation, verify repeated function and stop troubleshooting after safety checks.

### 5. Verify Parking Brake Condition
Confirm the parking brake is fully released through normal controls and that no obvious mechanical obstruction prevents release.

Observe whether the brake applies and releases consistently.

Expected outcome: Parking-brake operation is normal and does not remain partially engaged.

### 6. Inspect Wheels and Floor Conditions
Check for:
- Debris around casters or wheels
- Uneven flooring
- Objects trapped underneath the unit
- Damaged wheels
- Excessive rolling resistance

Expected outcome: The unit has a clear travel path and no external mechanical condition is preventing movement.

### 7. Verify Battery and System Power
Confirm the system is powered normally and has sufficient traction-battery charge for powered drive.

Expected outcome: Mobility is not being inhibited by inadequate available power.

### 8. Test Each Safety Function Separately
In a controlled area, verify normal behavior of:
- Emergency-stop reset
- Bumper release
- Parking brake
- Powered drive

Do not intentionally defeat any safety system.

Expected outcome: Each safety control changes system behavior predictably and returns to normal operation when properly released.

### 9. Perform Final Mobility Verification
Verify:
- Powered movement starts normally
- Unit stops normally
- Steering is predictable
- Parking brake holds and releases
- Bumpers are not stuck
- Emergency-stop function does not remain falsely active
- No unexpected movement occurs

Expected outcome: Mobile operation is safe and repeatable. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Any Persistent Safety-Interlock Problem
If an emergency stop, bumper, parking brake, or related safety function continues to inhibit movement without an obvious external cause, remove the system from service.

Expected outcome: A potentially faulty safety system is repaired rather than bypassed or ignored.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as activated emergency controls, bumper obstruction, parking-brake position, wheel obstruction, and insufficient battery power have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve safety switches, bumper sensing, brake actuators, mobility controls, interlock circuits, wiring, or other service-level components.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Philips documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete mobility, braking, emergency-stop, collision-safety, and return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never bypass a bumper, emergency stop, or parking-brake interlock to restore mobility; these controls exist to prevent unintended movement and collision.

## Final Thought

Mobility safety systems should always be treated as protective functions, not obstacles to bypass. Secure the patient and equipment, rule out external activation or obstruction, verify each safety control independently, and escalate any condition that cannot be proven reliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
