## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot intermittent external power or charging caused by loose connectors, worn contacts, damaged adapters, cable strain, or docking problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Ensure Patient Safety and Confirm the Intermittent Condition

Remove the monitor from patient use if external power or charging is unreliable.

Provide another verified monitor.

Ask whether charging stops when the monitor is moved, the adapter connection is disturbed, or the monitor is placed on its charging stand.

Expected outcome: The intermittent failure is characterized without risking interruption of clinical measurements.

### 2. Inspect the AC Outlet and Power Cord

Verify that the outlet is appropriate and functioning using an approved method.

Inspect the power cord and plug for damage, loose fit, or discoloration.

Do not continue using a connector showing signs of overheating.

Expected outcome: The AC source and external power cord are stable and undamaged.

### 3. Inspect the AC Adapter

Confirm that the adapter is approved for the monitor.

Examine its housing and cable for cracks, cuts, strain damage, or unusual heat.

Check for damaged or loose accessible connections.

Expected outcome: The adapter and its connections are physically intact.

### 4. Inspect the Monitor Power Connector

Examine the accessible monitor power connector for:

- Bent or damaged contacts.
- Loose or recessed connector components.
- Contamination.
- Discoloration.
- Evidence of repeated cable strain.

Do not attempt to straighten contacts or repair the connector externally.

Expected outcome: The connector is clean, undamaged, and accepts the approved power connection securely.

### 5. Inspect the Charging Stand

If a charging stand is used, check its accessible contacts and monitor seating surfaces.

Look for debris, wear, misalignment, or a stand that does not hold the monitor correctly.

Confirm that installed accessories are not preventing proper docking.

Expected outcome: The monitor seats securely and the charging contacts align correctly.

### 6. Observe Charging Stability

Connect the monitor to the verified power source.

Observe the charging indication during normal stationary operation.

If safe and permitted, check for loss of charging during ordinary docking and undocking or normal cable positioning. Do not force, flex, or stress damaged connectors.

Expected outcome: Charging remains stable during normal intended handling.

If a corrected seating or connection issue restores stable charging, proceed to verification.

### 7. Substitute Known-Good Power Components

Where compatible and authorized, test with a known-good approved adapter, power cable, or charging stand.

Change one external component at a time and compare the results.

Expected outcome: The faulty external component is isolated or ruled out.

If substitution resolves the intermittent condition, remove the defective component from use.

### 8. Verify Charging and Battery Operation

Confirm that the monitor charges consistently using the corrected setup.

After charging according to manufacturer guidance, perform an approved battery operation check.

Verify that the monitor remains powered and functional when removed from external power.

Expected outcome: External charging is stable and battery operation meets applicable manufacturer requirements.

If all required tests pass and the intermittent problem does not recur, troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Escalate Persistent Intermittent Power Failure

If charging remains intermittent with verified external power accessories, stop troubleshooting.

Do not attempt internal connector repair or continue manipulating a questionable power connection.

Expected outcome: The monitor remains unavailable for clinical use until qualified repair and verification are completed.

## If the Problem Persists

External outlet, cord, adapter, docking, and accessible connector causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal power connector damage, charging interface failure, battery charging circuitry problems, or internal power-management faults.

Remove the monitor from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for qualified repair or bench evaluation.

Use appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment. Only qualified personnel should repair or replace power-system components.

Complete charging stability, battery operation, overall functional, and applicable electrical safety tests before returning the monitor to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Intermittent charging can leave a monitor with an unexpectedly depleted battery even when it appears properly docked between patient assessments.

## Final Thought

Intermittent power problems require careful external inspection, controlled testing, and confirmation that charging remains stable. Rule out the outlet, adapter, connectors, and stand before assuming internal failure. Escalate unsafe conditions and document the verified corrective action.

That is successful troubleshooting.
