## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot surface hose connection, mattress recognition, or therapy activation failures caused by incompatible accessories, loose connections, positioning, or configuration.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Maintain Safe Patient Support

Confirm that the patient is receiving appropriate support before evaluating the surface system.

- Transfer the patient to an appropriate verified surface if the mattress cannot provide the required support.
- Do not assume therapy is active because the surface appears inflated.
- Prevent patient contact with rigid deck components.
- Maintain pressure-injury prevention measures during equipment exchange.
- Do not troubleshoot an unreliable therapeutic surface while the patient depends on it.

Expected outcome: The patient is safely supported without relying on an unverified surface or therapy mode.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Surface Problem

Determine whether the issue involves hose connection, mattress detection, or therapy activation.

- Ask whether the surface was recently changed.
- Identify whether the mattress is recognized by the controller.
- Record any displayed message or alarm.
- Determine whether therapy selection is unavailable or fails after selection.
- Check whether the problem occurs continuously or intermittently.

Expected outcome: The affected surface function and failure conditions are identified.

### 3. Verify Bed and Surface Power

Inspect external electrical connections.

- Confirm AC power availability.
- Check the bed power cord and plug.
- Inspect applicable surface-controller power connections.
- Observe power and status indicators.
- Verify that the controller responds to normal commands.

Expected outcome: The bed and surface system have reliable electrical power.

### 4. Verify Mattress and Surface Compatibility

Confirm that the installed surface is appropriate for the bed.

- Identify the installed mattress or surface assembly.
- Verify compatibility with the Compella configuration using approved documentation.
- Check that the surface is correctly oriented.
- Confirm that the selected bed width is supported by the installed surface configuration.
- Do not substitute an unapproved mattress or adapter.

Expected outcome: The installed surface is compatible and positioned correctly.

### 5. Inspect Surface Hoses and Connectors

Examine accessible connections.

- Verify that hoses are connected to the designated ports.
- Check for loose or partially seated fittings.
- Inspect for cracked connectors or damaged seals.
- Look for pinched, kinked, or twisted hoses.
- Confirm that bed articulation and width expansion do not restrict the hose routing.

Expected outcome: All required pneumatic connections are secure and unobstructed.

### 6. Inspect Mattress Detection Connections

If the installed configuration uses mattress or surface identification connections:

- Inspect accessible identification cables and connectors.
- Verify correct seating.
- Check for visible pin damage, contamination, or cable strain.
- Confirm that the connection is not trapped beneath the mattress.
- Do not bypass identification sensors or recognition functions.

Expected outcome: The surface identification connection is intact and correctly installed.

### 7. Verify Therapy Mode and Operating Conditions

Check the authorized surface controls.

- Confirm that the intended therapy mode is supported by the installed configuration.
- Check whether the surface is in standby, transport, or another mode affecting therapy activation.
- Verify that required operating conditions are satisfied.
- Observe whether the selected mode is accepted.
- Do not change protected service configuration or bypass interlocks.

Expected outcome: The intended therapy mode is available and activates normally.

### 8. Compare With Approved Known-Good Components

If the problem remains and substitution is permitted:

- Compare the affected hose with an approved known-good hose.
- Inspect or substitute an approved compatible external connection component.
- Compare the installed surface with another verified compatible surface when available.
- Record whether the problem follows the accessory, mattress, or bed.
- Avoid unnecessary repeated connection cycles that could damage fittings.

Expected outcome: The fault is isolated to an external connection, surface assembly, or bed-side system.

### 9. Verify Surface Recognition and Therapy Operation

After correcting an external issue:

- Confirm that the surface is recognized.
- Verify normal inflation.
- Select the intended approved therapy mode.
- Confirm that the controller indicates the expected operating state.
- Perform manufacturer-approved functional verification of therapy operation.
- Check relevant alarms and indicators.
- Confirm stable hose connections through permitted bed movement.

Expected outcome: Mattress recognition, pneumatic connections, and therapy operation pass the required functional checks.

Troubleshooting can stop when the surface is recognized, therapy operates correctly, and all applicable verification is successful.

## If the Problem Persists

If power, compatibility, positioning, hoses, identification connections, and authorized therapy settings have been checked, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible service-level causes include mattress identification, pneumatic control, surface communication, therapy control, or associated wiring and electronics.

The affected bed or surface should be:

- Removed from service and labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for qualified repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using Hillrom service documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Returned to service only after successful mattress recognition, inflation, therapy operation, and alarm verification.

Do not bypass mattress recognition or operate an unverified therapy configuration.

## Clinical Use Tip

Confirm that the surface controller recognizes the correct mattress configuration and that the intended therapy is actually operating before placing a patient on the bed.

## Final Thought

Reliable surface therapy depends on compatible equipment, secure connections, correct recognition, and verified operation. Protect the patient, check external causes before assuming internal failure, escalate unresolved problems, and document the final functional results.

That is successful troubleshooting.
