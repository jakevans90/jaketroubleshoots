## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot powered transport that will not engage, moves inconsistently, or pulls unevenly due to power, controls, brakes, floor conditions, or mechanical interference.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Secure the Patient and Prevent Unintended Movement

Stop powered transport immediately if the bed moves unpredictably.

- Engage the brakes when stationary.
- Confirm the patient is safely positioned.
- Use an alternate approved transport method if powered transport cannot be trusted.
- Do not troubleshoot an unreliable transport system while moving a patient.
- Keep hands, feet, and equipment away from moving wheels and drive components.

Expected outcome: The bed is secured and powered transport can be evaluated safely without a patient.

### 2. Confirm the Transport Complaint

Determine whether powered transport fails to engage, disengages unexpectedly, or pulls to one side.

- Ask staff whether the problem occurred during startup or while moving.
- Identify the direction of uneven pulling.
- Record any warning indicators or messages.
- Determine whether the issue occurs on all floor surfaces or only in certain locations.
- Note whether the transport system makes unusual sounds.

Expected outcome: The specific transport malfunction and conditions are identified.

### 3. Verify Battery and Power Status

Powered transport may depend on the bed's available electrical power and battery condition.

- Check battery and charging indicators.
- Confirm that the bed receives AC power when connected.
- Inspect the power cord and plug.
- Verify that the bed has completed any required startup sequence.
- Check whether other battery-powered functions operate normally.

Do not attempt transport if battery condition or available power is uncertain.

Expected outcome: The bed has an appropriate power condition for the approved transport function.

### 4. Inspect Brake and Steer Conditions

Check the external brake and steering controls.

- Confirm the brakes release and engage normally.
- Verify the appropriate transport or steering position.
- Inspect accessible casters for visible damage.
- Check for wrapped linens, debris, or foreign material.
- Confirm that all wheels intended to roll can move freely.

Expected outcome: The braking and steering systems operate normally without mechanical interference.

### 5. Inspect the Powered Transport Controls

Examine the transport controls for damage or inconsistent operation.

- Check for sticking or damaged buttons, handles, or switches.
- Verify that required operating conditions are satisfied.
- Observe transport engagement indicators.
- Compare the control response with the expected operation described in the manufacturer documentation.
- Do not bypass safety switches or interlocks.

Expected outcome: The transport system responds correctly to the authorized controls.

### 6. Evaluate Floor and Environmental Conditions

Check the area where the problem occurred.

- Inspect for uneven flooring, thresholds, debris, or slippery surfaces.
- Confirm that the bed is not contacting nearby equipment.
- Check that attached accessories are not dragging.
- Verify that the bed is in the appropriate transport configuration.
- Move to a clear, level testing area when safe.

Expected outcome: No environmental condition interferes with transport movement.

### 7. Perform a Controlled Transport Test

With the bed unoccupied, follow the approved transport test procedure.

- Confirm adequate clearance.
- Engage powered transport using normal controls.
- Observe initial movement and directional stability.
- Check for uneven pulling, wheel dragging, vibration, or unusual noise.
- Stop immediately if the bed moves unexpectedly.

Expected outcome: Powered transport engages and moves predictably without directional instability.

### 8. Verify Transport Disengagement and Braking

After transport testing:

- Confirm that powered transport disengages correctly.
- Verify that the bed stops as expected.
- Engage the brakes.
- Check that the bed remains stationary.
- Complete required manufacturer transport and brake verification.

Expected outcome: Powered movement, disengagement, steering, and braking operate reliably.

Troubleshooting can stop after successful verification and documentation.

## If the Problem Persists

If power, controls, brakes, casters, floor conditions, and external obstructions have been checked, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible service-level categories include the transport drive, motor control, engagement mechanism, steering components, battery system, or associated sensing and wiring.

The bed should be:

- Removed from service and labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for qualified repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using Hillrom service documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Returned to service only after successful transport, directional control, disengagement, and brake testing.

Do not continue using powered transport when its behavior is unpredictable.

## Clinical Use Tip

A powered transport malfunction can create a collision or uncontrolled movement hazard. Arrange an approved alternative before transporting a patient.

## Final Thought

Safe powered transport depends on reliable movement, steering, and braking. Check power, controls, wheels, and environmental conditions before assuming a drive failure. Remove unreliable equipment from service, verify corrective actions, and document the findings.

That is successful troubleshooting.
