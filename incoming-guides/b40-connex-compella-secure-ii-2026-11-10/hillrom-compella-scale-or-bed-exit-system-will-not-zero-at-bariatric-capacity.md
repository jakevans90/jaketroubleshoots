## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot scale zeroing or bed-exit failures associated with bed loading, accessories, positioning, controls, or sensing conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Establish an Alternate Monitoring Method

Confirm whether the problem affects patient weighing, bed-exit monitoring, or both.

- Do not rely on inaccurate weight measurements for clinical decisions.
- Establish an alternate approved weighing method.
- If bed-exit monitoring is unreliable, arrange appropriate alternate observation or fall-prevention measures.
- Do not remove a patient solely to perform a zeroing operation without clinical coordination.
- Confirm that the patient and total equipment load remain within the manufacturer's applicable limits.

Expected outcome: Patient safety is maintained without depending on an unreliable scale or bed-exit system.

### 2. Identify the Exact Scale or Bed-Exit Failure

Ask clinical staff to describe the malfunction.

- Determine whether zeroing fails, weight readings fluctuate, or bed-exit detection does not respond.
- Record any displayed error or status message.
- Identify whether the issue began after moving the bed, changing the mattress, or adding equipment.
- Determine whether the problem occurs only with heavier loading.
- Record the displayed weight or status before testing.

Expected outcome: The affected function and operating conditions are clearly identified.

### 3. Verify Power and Control Operation

Inspect the bed's power condition.

- Confirm AC power availability.
- Check the power cord and plug.
- Observe battery and system indicators.
- Confirm that the scale or bed-exit controls respond.
- Verify that the display is functioning normally.

Expected outcome: The bed has stable power and the relevant controls are responsive.

### 4. Check Bed Position and Floor Contact

Inspect the bed's physical setup.

- Confirm that the bed is on a stable, level surface.
- Check for contact with walls, furniture, or nearby equipment.
- Ensure that no external object is supporting part of the bed.
- Verify that casters and the bed frame are positioned as required for the manufacturer's weighing procedure.
- Check that cables or hoses are not pulling on the bed.

Expected outcome: The bed is positioned without external forces that could interfere with weight measurement.

### 5. Inspect Accessories and Load Conditions

Identify anything that could affect the scale.

- Check mattress and surface positioning.
- Inspect attached equipment, support devices, and accessories.
- Identify items resting against the floor or nearby structures.
- Confirm that the bed configuration matches the intended weighing setup.
- Do not remove patient-support equipment without clinical authorization.

Expected outcome: The weighing configuration is understood and external load interference has been eliminated.

### 6. Verify the Approved Zeroing Conditions

Determine whether the manufacturer-required conditions for zeroing are satisfied.

- Confirm whether the bed must be unoccupied for the intended zeroing operation.
- Follow the approved procedure for accounting for accessories and bedding.
- Do not zero the scale with a patient present unless the manufacturer specifically permits that operation.
- Do not use zeroing to compensate for an unexplained weight error.
- Record any unsuccessful zeroing attempt.

Expected outcome: Zeroing is performed only under approved conditions and completes successfully.

### 7. Check Bed-Exit Configuration and Response

If the bed-exit function is affected:

- Verify the selected monitoring mode.
- Check applicable activation and status indicators.
- Confirm that the system is armed according to the approved procedure.
- Inspect accessible controls for damage.
- Perform a manufacturer-approved bed-exit functional test without placing a patient at risk.

Expected outcome: Bed-exit monitoring arms correctly and produces the expected local alarm response.

### 8. Verify Scale Accuracy and Alarm Operation

Use approved equipment and procedures.

- Perform the manufacturer-specified scale verification using suitable test loads.
- Confirm that results meet the applicable manufacturer requirements.
- Verify bed-exit alarm operation separately.
- Check that the alarm is audible and correctly indicated.
- Verify any required remote notification path.

Expected outcome: The scale passes accuracy verification and the bed-exit system responds correctly.

Troubleshooting can stop when all affected functions pass their required checks.

## If the Problem Persists

If power, positioning, loading, accessories, zeroing conditions, and controls have been verified, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible service-level causes include load sensing, scale electronics, bed-exit detection, configuration, or associated wiring.

The bed should be:

- Removed from service and labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for qualified repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using Hillrom documentation and approved test equipment.
- Calibrated, repaired, or configured only by qualified authorized personnel.
- Returned to service after successful scale accuracy and bed-exit alarm verification.

An unsuccessful scale or bed-exit test must not be accepted as normal operation.

## Clinical Use Tip

A displayed weight is not automatically a verified weight. Confirm scale accuracy before using measurements for medication dosing or other weight-dependent clinical decisions.

## Final Thought

Reliable weighing and bed-exit monitoring require correct positioning, load conditions, and functional verification. Protect the patient, eliminate external interference, verify accuracy before suspecting internal failure, and escalate unresolved faults with complete documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
