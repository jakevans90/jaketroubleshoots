## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot degas-cycle startup or completion problems caused by power, tank conditions, controls, settings, loading, or external operating conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Workflow and Confirm the Device Is Safe to Test
Remove the UC1800 from active instrument-processing use while troubleshooting. Do not rely on a cleaner with an incomplete or unreliable degas function for clinical workflow.

Check for:
- Visible fluid leakage
- Damaged power cord or plug
- Burning odor
- Excessive heat
- Abnormal electrical noise
- Physical tank damage

If any unsafe condition is present, disconnect power if safe to do so, label the unit Out of Service, and stop troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The cleaner is safe for controlled troubleshooting and clinical processing has been redirected to another verified method or device.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Degas Problem
Ask staff whether:
- The degas cycle does not start at all
- The cycle begins and stops early
- The display or timer freezes
- Ultrasonic activity appears absent
- The problem occurs every cycle or intermittently

Attempt the function only under appropriate test conditions and according to approved operating procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The exact failure condition is reproduced or clearly defined.

### 3. Verify Incoming Power and Normal Startup
Inspect the power cord and plug for damage or looseness. Confirm the cleaner is connected directly to a known-good approved receptacle when practical.

Power-cycle the unit using normal controls and verify that the display and basic controls initialize normally.

If normal operation returns and the degas cycle completes successfully during repeated testing, troubleshooting can stop.

**Expected outcome:** The unit powers normally without resets, flickering, or intermittent loss of operation.

### 4. Check Tank Fill and Solution Condition
Verify the tank contains the appropriate cleaning solution at the operating level required for normal use. Look for:
- Tank underfill
- Excessive fill
- Heavy contamination
- Excessive foam
- Recently changed solution containing trapped air

Correct obvious external tank-condition problems using facility-approved cleaning chemistry and procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The tank contains an appropriate quantity of correctly prepared solution without obvious conditions that would interfere with ultrasonic operation.

### 5. Remove Excessive or Improper Loading
Verify the degas function is not being attempted with an inappropriate load, improperly positioned basket, or accessories contacting the tank in an abnormal manner.

When permitted, test the degas function with the tank configured normally and without unnecessary loading.

**Expected outcome:** The degas cycle starts and operates normally when loading or accessory interference is eliminated.

### 6. Verify Controls and Cycle Selection
Inspect the keypad, timer, and display for:
- Unresponsive buttons
- Stuck keys
- Incorrect cycle selection
- Incomplete command entry
- Display indications suggesting another active function

Use only normal operator-accessible controls. Do not enter restricted service menus or alter undocumented configuration.

**Expected outcome:** The degas function can be selected and commanded normally.

### 7. Observe Degas Operation
Run an appropriate test cycle and observe:
- Whether ultrasonic activity begins
- Whether the timer advances
- Whether the cycle runs consistently
- Whether the unit stops unexpectedly
- Whether abnormal noise or vibration develops

If the cycle completes normally on repeated testing, the issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop.

**Expected outcome:** The complete degas cycle operates consistently without interruption or abnormal behavior.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification
After correcting an external cause, verify normal cleaner operation through the applicable facility or manufacturer-approved functional checks.

Do not return the unit to service based only on a single successful button press.

**Expected outcome:** Degas operation and normal cleaner functions are repeatable and suitable for return to service.

## If the Problem Persists

If power, tank conditions, loading, accessories, and normal controls have been ruled out, the problem may involve the ultrasonic generation system, internal controls, sensing, wiring, or another service-level fault.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not proceed into internal ultrasonic-generator, power-control, or board-level troubleshooting without approved service procedures. After repair, complete the required functional and safety verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not release instruments from a cleaning process that depends on an ultrasonic cleaner whose cycle performance is uncertain.

## Final Thought

Safe troubleshooting starts by protecting instrument-processing continuity, then ruling out power, tank conditions, loading, accessories, and controls before assuming an internal ultrasonic-system failure. Verify the correction, escalate appropriately, and document the complete CCR.

That is successful troubleshooting.
