## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot accessory or interlock problems caused by positioning, obstruction, incorrect components, damage, contamination, or alignment.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Processing Workflow
If an interlock or accessory condition prevents normal operation, remove the cleaner from active use until reliable operation is restored.

Do not bypass, defeat, tape, jumper, or otherwise override any safety interlock.

**Expected outcome:** The device is isolated from processing and all safety features remain intact.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Condition
Determine whether the problem involves:
- Lid not recognized
- Basket not seating correctly
- Accessory preventing cycle start
- Interlock indication remaining active
- Intermittent operation when components are moved

Observe the condition using normal controls.

**Expected outcome:** The exact component and symptom are identified.

### 3. Inspect the Lid
Check for:
- Warping
- Cracks
- Loose hardware
- Obstructions
- Incorrect seating
- Foreign material around contact or alignment areas

Clean accessible surfaces according to approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The lid sits normally without obstruction or visible damage.

### 4. Inspect the Basket
Verify the basket is:
- The appropriate accessory for the cleaner
- Not bent or distorted
- Properly oriented
- Fully seated
- Free of debris preventing normal placement

Remove unnecessary items and retest.

**Expected outcome:** The basket is correctly positioned without interfering with the cleaner.

### 5. Inspect Other Accessories
Check removable racks, holders, covers, adapters, or other accessories associated with the reported problem.

Compare with a known-good compatible accessory when available.

Do not force an accessory into place.

**Expected outcome:** The correct accessory fits normally and does not create an interlock condition.

### 6. Inspect Accessible Interlock Areas
Without disassembly, look for:
- Debris
- Misalignment
- Damaged external actuating features
- Loose accessible components
- Fluid contamination

Do not manually actuate hidden switches with tools or bypass the normal mechanical relationship between components.

**Expected outcome:** No external obstruction or visible damage prevents normal interlock operation.

### 7. Retest Normal Operation
Reinstall the lid, basket, and accessories in their proper positions and attempt a normal test cycle.

If the interlock clears and operation remains stable through repeated testing, troubleshooting can stop.

**Expected outcome:** The cleaner recognizes the normal accessory condition and operates consistently.

### 8. Perform Final Verification
Confirm all original safety features remain functional and no temporary workaround has been left in place.

**Expected outcome:** The unit operates only in its intended configuration and is safe for return to service.

## If the Problem Persists

If alignment, debris, accessory compatibility, and visible mechanical condition have been ruled out, the problem may involve an interlock switch, sensor, wiring, mechanical linkage, or internal control circuit.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Never return equipment to service with an interlock bypassed or unreliable. Verify correct safety-interlock behavior after repair.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never defeat an interlock to keep instrument processing moving; transfer the workload to another verified cleaning method instead.

## Final Thought

Interlock problems require careful inspection of positioning, alignment, accessories, and contamination without defeating safety features. When external causes are ruled out, remove the cleaner from service and escalate rather than bypassing the protection.

That is successful troubleshooting.
