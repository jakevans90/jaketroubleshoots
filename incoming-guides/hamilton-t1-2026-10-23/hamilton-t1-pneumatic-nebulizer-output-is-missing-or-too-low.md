## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots absent or weak pneumatic nebulizer output caused by tubing, nebulizer cup, connections, settings, gas supply, or external flow restrictions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Therapy

Do not interrupt ventilation or essential medication delivery while troubleshooting. If nebulized medication is urgently required and the ventilator's nebulizer function is unreliable, provide therapy through an approved alternate method.

Do not dismantle a breathing circuit supporting a patient solely for troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** Ventilation and required medication delivery are maintained safely. Continue troubleshooting only when the patient is protected.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Nebulizer Complaint

Determine whether:

- No pneumatic nebulizer flow is present
- Flow is present but appears weak
- The nebulizer does not produce visible aerosol
- The nebulizer starts and stops unexpectedly
- The issue occurs with one particular nebulizer cup or tubing set
- The ventilator indicates nebulizer operation but output is absent

**Expected outcome:** The failure is clearly characterized and reproducible under safe test conditions.

### 3. Verify the Nebulizer Function Is Activated

Confirm the nebulizer function has been enabled using normal operator controls and that the ventilator is in a mode or state where the function is expected to be available.

Do not alter clinical ventilation settings simply to force nebulizer operation.

**Expected outcome:** The nebulizer is commanded on and the ventilator indicates expected operation. If output returns after proper activation, verify function and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Inspect the Nebulizer Tubing

Trace the pneumatic nebulizer tubing from the ventilator outlet to the nebulizer.

Check for:

- Kinks
- Pinching
- Disconnection
- Cracked tubing
- Loose fittings
- Fluid contamination
- Blockage
- Tubing trapped under equipment

**Expected outcome:** Tubing is open, intact, and securely connected. If correcting a restriction restores normal output, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 5. Inspect the Nebulizer Cup or Assembly

Inspect the nebulizer assembly for:

- Incorrect assembly
- Blocked jet or inlet
- Cracks
- Missing components
- Excessive residue
- Improperly seated medication cup
- Incompatible accessory

Use a clean compatible nebulizer intended for the system.

**Expected outcome:** The nebulizer assembly is intact, correctly assembled, and unobstructed. If replacing the nebulizer restores output, the issue is resolved.

### 6. Verify the Pneumatic Connection at the Ventilator

Inspect the external nebulizer outlet and mating connector for:

- Incomplete insertion
- Damage
- Contamination
- Loose fit
- Obvious obstruction

Do not insert probes or tools into pneumatic ports.

**Expected outcome:** The connection is clean, undamaged, and secure. If reseating the tubing restores expected output, troubleshooting can stop.

### 7. Test With a Known-Good Nebulizer and Tubing Set

Connect a known-good compatible nebulizer and tubing set under appropriate bench or non-patient test conditions.

This separates disposable/accessory problems from ventilator-side output problems.

**Expected outcome:** The known-good setup produces expected nebulizer activity. If so, replace the original tubing or nebulizer assembly.

### 8. Verify Ventilator Gas Supply and General Pneumatic Operation

Confirm the ventilator has the required gas supply and is not experiencing other pneumatic alarms or performance problems.

Check accessible supply hoses, connectors, and indicators for abnormalities.

Do not assume the nebulizer valve or internal pneumatic component has failed before general gas supply conditions are ruled out.

**Expected outcome:** Gas supply and ventilator pneumatic operation are otherwise normal. If a supply connection problem is corrected and nebulizer output returns, verify the ventilator fully before return to service.

### 9. Check for Environmental or Mechanical Restrictions

Confirm that:

- Tubing is not compressed by bed rails or transport equipment
- Condensation has not entered the nebulizer tubing
- The nebulizer is positioned appropriately
- The assembly is not unusually tilted or obstructed
- Accessories have not been incorrectly connected during transport setup

**Expected outcome:** No external restriction or positioning problem remains. Correcting one restores consistent nebulizer output.

### 10. Evaluate Output With Approved Test Methods

If output still appears low, evaluate the nebulizer function using the appropriate Hamilton service documentation and approved test equipment.

Do not estimate performance solely by visual appearance of aerosol.

**Expected outcome:** Nebulizer output either passes the approved functional test or is confirmed to be outside acceptable performance.

### 11. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correction:

- Activate the pneumatic nebulizer
- Confirm consistent output with a known-good compatible nebulizer
- Verify no related ventilator alarms occur
- Confirm ventilation remains stable
- Inspect tubing connections once more

**Expected outcome:** Nebulizer output is present, consistent, and does not interfere with normal ventilator operation. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 12. Escalate if Output Remains Missing or Low

If a known-good nebulizer and tubing set do not restore output and external gas supply is normal, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator is removed from service for appropriate bench evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External causes involving tubing, nebulizer assembly, outlet connection, accessory compatibility, positioning, and gas supply have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve an internal pneumatic control, valve, pressure-regulation function, control signal, or other service-level condition.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Hamilton documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not perform internal pneumatic disassembly or board-level troubleshooting without appropriate authorization and manufacturer procedures.

Complete full functional and applicable alarm testing before return to clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

If prescribed aerosol therapy cannot be delivered reliably through the ventilator, use an approved alternate medication-delivery method without compromising ventilation.

## Final Thought

Protect ventilation and medication delivery first, then check the complete external pneumatic path before suspecting an internal problem. Verify the repair using an appropriate functional test, remove the ventilator from service when output remains unreliable, and document the findings clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
