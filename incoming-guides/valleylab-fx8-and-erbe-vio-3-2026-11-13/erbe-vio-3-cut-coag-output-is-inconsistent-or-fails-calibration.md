## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots unstable or unacceptable CUT/COAG output by separating accessory, setup, activation, analyzer, connection, and generator-related causes.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Remove Questionable Output From Clinical Use
If CUT or COAG performance is unreliable, discontinue use of that generator for patient treatment. Move the procedure to another verified ESU or approved alternative before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is not exposed to uncertain electrosurgical output.

### 2. Clarify the Failure
Determine whether the complaint involves CUT, COAG, monopolar, bipolar, one instrument, one mode, one socket, or all output. Record the selected settings and whether the complaint came from clinical performance or an analyzer test.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is narrowed to a specific output path or identified as generator-wide.

### 3. Inspect the Complete Accessory Path
Check the active instrument, cable, adapter, connector, socket, and neutral-electrode path when monopolar operation is involved. Look for damaged insulation, loose plugs, contamination, bent contacts, or incompatible accessories.

**Expected outcome:** The complete external energy path is compatible, intact, and securely connected. Correcting an obvious accessory fault may resolve the complaint.

### 4. Confirm the Selected Program and Mode
Verify that the displayed socket, CUT/COAG mode, effect, and user-selectable settings match the intended clinical setup. Compare with the expected approved configuration rather than changing settings simply to make the analyzer reading pass.

**Expected outcome:** The generator is being tested in the correct mode and configuration.

### 5. Verify the Electrosurgical Analyzer Setup
Inspect the analyzer leads, load connections, adapters, test cable routing, and analyzer configuration. Confirm that the analyzer and accessories are suitable for testing HF electrosurgical output.

A poor test connection can mimic unstable generator output.

**Expected outcome:** The test setup is repeatable and appropriate. If correcting the analyzer setup produces stable acceptable results, troubleshooting can stop after repeat testing.

### 6. Repeat the Test With Known-Good Test Accessories
Use known-good analyzer cables, adapters, and compatible ESU accessories. Repeat the same measurement several times without changing multiple variables simultaneously.

**Expected outcome:** Results are repeatable. A failure that disappears with replacement test accessories identifies an external test-path problem.

### 7. Compare Output Paths
When appropriate, compare CUT versus COAG and available compatible output sockets using the same controlled test method.

Do not interpret different modes as though they should produce identical readings.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is isolated to one mode/socket or confirmed across multiple output paths.

### 8. Check for Activation Instability
Confirm that hand-control or footswitch activation remains continuously detected during the test. An intermittent switch, cable, or assignment can produce output that appears to drop out.

**Expected outcome:** Activation indication remains stable throughout the commanded energy delivery. Replace a defective external activation accessory if identified.

### 9. Perform the Approved Output Verification
Use the current ERBE procedure, approved electrosurgical analyzer, specified test loads, and applicable limits for the exact VIO 3 configuration.

Do not invent limits or attempt unauthorized calibration adjustments.

**Expected outcome:** Output meets the applicable manufacturer requirements. If it does, document the results and return the unit to service according to policy.

### 10. Escalate Failed or Unstable Output
If output remains inconsistent or fails manufacturer verification with known-good accessories and a verified analyzer setup, stop troubleshooting externally.

**Expected outcome:** The generator is removed from service for qualified service-level evaluation rather than adjusted without approved procedures.

## If the Problem Persists

Accessory, connection, activation, selected mode, and analyzer causes have been ruled out. Remaining categories can include internal HF generation, switching, measurement, control circuitry, calibration data, or other service-level faults.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Tested using current ERBE service documentation and approved electrosurgical test equipment
- Calibrated or repaired only by appropriately qualified personnel

After repair or calibration, complete the full required output verification, alarm checks, and electrical-safety testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never compensate for suspected low output by arbitrarily increasing energy when the generator, cable, electrode, or instrument has not been verified.

## Final Thought

Output complaints require controlled testing rather than assumptions. Eliminate accessories, setup, and activation problems first, verify performance with approved equipment, and escalate any generator that cannot reliably meet manufacturer requirements.

That is successful troubleshooting.
