## What This Guide Helps With

Touchscreen or front-panel inputs do not respond reliably because of contamination, protective material, startup state, external influence, software, or control hardware problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Maintain Patient Safety
If a control becomes unresponsive during clinical use, replace the generator with another verified ESU before troubleshooting.

Do not continue using a generator if required settings or energy functions cannot be reliably selected or stopped.

**Expected outcome:** Clinical care no longer depends on unreliable controls.

### 2. Confirm Which Controls Are Affected
Determine whether the entire touchscreen is unresponsive, only one area is affected, or a specific physical control does not respond.

Note whether the issue occurs at startup, after warm-up, or intermittently.

**Expected outcome:** The control failure is reproduced and localized.

### 3. Check for Surface Contamination or Obstruction
Inspect the touchscreen and controls for fluid, cleaning residue, adhesive material, protective film, gloves or objects resting against controls, or other contamination.

Clean only using approved methods and products.

**Expected outcome:** The control surface is clean and unobstructed. If normal response returns after cleaning, continue through final verification.

### 4. Restart Under Controlled Conditions
With the generator removed from patient use, perform a normal power cycle and allow startup to complete fully.

Do not repeatedly interrupt startup or force power cycles.

**Expected outcome:** The user interface initializes normally. If control response remains stable after restart, continue testing before return to service.

### 5. Disconnect Nonessential External Accessories
Remove external accessories not required for the test and restart the generator.

This helps determine whether an attached device or connection is contributing to abnormal behavior.

**Expected outcome:** Controls remain responsive with the generator in a simplified configuration.

### 6. Evaluate Touch and Physical Controls Separately
Check all accessible user controls systematically. Verify that touching one area does not trigger another function and that physical controls respond consistently without sticking.

Do not attempt unauthorized touchscreen calibration or protected service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** Each control produces the intended response. Incorrect, delayed, or intermittent responses require further evaluation.

### 7. Inspect for Physical Damage
Examine the display, bezel, front panel, and controls for cracks, impact damage, separation, fluid intrusion, or damaged knobs or keys.

**Expected outcome:** The user interface has no visible mechanical damage. Physical damage warrants removal from service.

### 8. Perform Functional Verification
If an external cause was corrected, verify settings selection, output-mode controls, alarm controls, accessory recognition, and applicable ESU outputs using approved test equipment.

**Expected outcome:** The interface remains consistently responsive and all selected functions operate correctly. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Escalate Persistent Control Problems
If the touchscreen or front-panel controls continue to freeze, misregister, or fail after external causes and normal restart are ruled out, stop troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The generator remains out of service for qualified software or hardware evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External contamination, obstruction, accessories, and normal restart have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve touchscreen hardware, control electronics, software, display assemblies, or service-level configuration.

The Valleylab FX8 should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Medtronic documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

All affected controls and associated electrosurgical functions must pass return-to-service testing.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

An ESU with unreliable controls should be exchanged immediately because correct output selection and immediate response to user input are essential to safe use.

## Final Thought

Treat unreliable ESU controls as a safety issue, eliminate simple surface and external causes first, and require consistent interface and output verification before return to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
