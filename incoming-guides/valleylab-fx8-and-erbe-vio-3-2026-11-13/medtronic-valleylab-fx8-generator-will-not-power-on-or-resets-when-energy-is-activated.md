## What This Guide Helps With

The generator is completely dead or restarts during energy activation because of AC power, cord, connection, accessory, load, or internal power-system problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Procedure
If the generator powers off or resets during energy activation, discontinue its clinical use immediately and provide another verified ESU.

An intermittent power generator should not remain connected to a patient during troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The procedure is supported by reliable equipment and the affected generator is isolated for bench evaluation.

### 2. Confirm the Failure
Determine whether the generator is completely dead, powers on intermittently, or restarts specifically when RF energy is activated.

Record whether the display extinguishes completely, startup restarts, or only the output terminates.

**Expected outcome:** The power symptom is reproduced and clearly categorized.

### 3. Verify the AC Outlet
Test the outlet using an appropriate approved method or connect the generator to a known-good hospital-grade outlet.

Avoid power strips or extension arrangements during troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** A stable AC source is confirmed. If changing outlets resolves the problem, investigate the original branch power or outlet rather than the generator.

### 4. Inspect the Power Cord and Generator Inlet
Check the full accessible power cord, plug, strain relief, and generator inlet for looseness, cuts, bent contacts, discoloration, overheating, or intermittent connection.

Gently assess for obvious mechanical looseness without stressing the connection.

**Expected outcome:** The AC connection is secure and undamaged. A defective cord is replaced with an approved equivalent and retested.

### 5. Check for Intermittent Power Connections
With the generator on the bench and not connected to a patient, observe whether normal movement of the external power cord or connector causes the unit to reset.

Do not continue testing if arcing, heat, odor, or severe looseness is present.

**Expected outcome:** External power remains stable during normal cord positioning.

### 6. Disconnect Nonessential Accessories
Power the generator off and remove nonessential footswitches, energy instruments, communication cables, and other external accessories.

Restart and verify basic operation.

**Expected outcome:** The generator powers normally in a simplified configuration. If an attached accessory was causing the reset, reconnect accessories individually to identify it.

### 7. Inspect Energy Accessories
If resets occur only during energy activation, inspect the instrument, cable, connector, return-electrode system, and relevant receptacles for damage, contamination, or incorrect connection.

Substitute known-good compatible accessories when appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** An accessory-related fault is either identified or ruled out before the generator is considered defective.

### 8. Verify With Approved ESU Test Equipment
Connect the generator to an approved ESU analyzer using the correct test arrangement and perform applicable controlled output testing.

Observe whether the generator remains powered during activation.

**Expected outcome:** The generator delivers the required output without resetting. If a known-good accessory and correct analyzer setup eliminate the reset, troubleshooting can stop after full verification.

### 9. Check for Heat or Ventilation Problems
Inspect external airflow paths and confirm the generator is not already overheated before testing.

A power reset associated with excessive heat should not be dismissed as a simple AC issue.

**Expected outcome:** Cooling and environmental conditions are normal.

### 10. Perform Return-to-Service Verification
If an external cause was corrected, verify repeated startup, controls, alarms, REM function, accessory recognition, and applicable monopolar and bipolar outputs.

**Expected outcome:** The generator remains stable during required testing and passes all manufacturer-defined return-to-service checks. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 11. Escalate Persistent Power Resets
If the generator remains dead on known-good AC or resets during controlled energy activation with known-good accessories and correct analyzer setup, remove it from service.

**Expected outcome:** Further investigation is performed at the service level rather than through repeated high-energy testing.

## If the Problem Persists

Known-good AC power, the external power cord, accessories, analyzer setup, and ventilation have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal power conversion, power distribution, RF loading, control, thermal protection, or related service-level faults.

The Valleylab FX8 should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Medtronic documentation and approved ESU test equipment.
- Repaired only by qualified personnel.

After repair, complete applicable electrical-safety, startup, alarm, REM, activation-control, and output-performance tests before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any ESU that resets during energy activation should be exchanged immediately because interruption during active electrosurgery is itself a clinical reliability concern.
