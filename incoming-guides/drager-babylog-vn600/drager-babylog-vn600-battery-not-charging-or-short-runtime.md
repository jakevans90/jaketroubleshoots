## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses charging failures or reduced battery runtime caused by AC power, connections, charging conditions, battery condition, or external power problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Reliable Power

Do not allow a patient to depend on a ventilator with unreliable backup power during transport or any situation where AC power could be interrupted.

If battery reliability is uncertain during active use, move the patient to another verified ventilator or maintain the device on a verified power source while clinical staff arrange safe continuity of care.

**Expected outcome:** Patient ventilation is not dependent on the questionable battery. If reliable power cannot be assured, remove the ventilator from clinical use.

### 2. Confirm the Battery Complaint

Determine whether the issue is:

- Battery not charging
- Charge level not increasing
- Battery discharges rapidly
- Ventilator shuts down sooner than expected
- Battery is not recognized
- Problem occurs only during transport

Record the displayed battery status and indications.

**Expected outcome:** The exact battery-related symptom is identified and reproducible.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source

Connect the ventilator to a known-good AC outlet.

Inspect the power cord for damage and confirm it is fully seated at both ends.

If appropriate, test the outlet using approved electrical test equipment.

**Expected outcome:** Reliable AC power reaches the ventilator. If charging begins normally after correcting the outlet or power connection, troubleshooting can stop after battery verification.

### 4. Observe Charging Indications

With the ventilator connected to verified AC power, observe whether the device indicates external power and battery charging.

Allow sufficient normal charging opportunity before evaluating runtime.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator recognizes AC operation and shows expected charging behavior. If charging proceeds normally, continue to verify battery performance.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Battery Components

Where the battery is user-accessible and removal is permitted, inspect it and its external contacts for:

- Damage
- Swelling
- Cracks
- Contamination
- Corrosion
- Improper seating

Do not use a visibly damaged or swollen battery.

**Expected outcome:** The battery is intact and correctly installed. A damaged battery is removed from service immediately.

### 6. Reseat or Substitute the Battery Where Applicable

If permitted by the device configuration and facility procedure, reseat the battery.

When available, substitute a known-good approved battery.

**Expected outcome:** If charging and runtime normalize with the known-good battery, the original battery is the likely cause and should be replaced according to facility policy.

### 7. Evaluate Battery Runtime Using an Approved Procedure

After the battery has been appropriately charged, perform battery testing using the applicable manufacturer or facility-approved method.

Do not estimate battery condition solely from the displayed percentage.

**Expected outcome:** Battery performance meets the required return-to-service criteria. If it does, troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Check for Intermittent External Power Loss

Gently inspect the external power connection for looseness without stressing the cable or connector.

Confirm that normal equipment movement does not cause unintended switching between AC and battery operation.

**Expected outcome:** External power remains stable during normal positioning. If a damaged cord or loose external connection is identified and corrected, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correction, verify AC operation, battery charging, transfer between AC and battery as permitted, battery indications, alarms, and required operational checks.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator operates reliably on its intended power sources and battery performance meets applicable requirements.

## If the Problem Persists

If the outlet, power cord, battery seating, and known-good battery have been verified but charging remains abnormal or runtime remains inadequate, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve the internal charging system, power-management electronics, battery interface, power supply, or another service-level condition.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Drager documentation and approved electrical test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not return a ventilator to service if required backup-power capability cannot be verified.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Confirm adequate verified battery capability before transport; a charging icon alone does not prove acceptable runtime.
