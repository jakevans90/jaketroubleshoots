## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses oxygen calibration failures or incorrect FiO2 caused by gas supply, connections, calibration conditions, external setup, or measurement problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Oxygen Delivery

If delivered or displayed oxygen concentration is questionable while a patient is connected, immediately provide ventilation and oxygen using another verified device or approved backup method.

Do not continue troubleshooting while a patient depends on an unreliable oxygen measurement or delivery system.

**Expected outcome:** Patient oxygen delivery is maintained independently. If safe oxygen delivery cannot be assured, remove the ventilator from clinical use immediately.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Determine whether the complaint is:

- Oxygen calibration will not complete
- Displayed FiO2 is higher or lower than expected
- FiO2 fluctuates
- The ventilator reports an oxygen measurement problem

Repeat the condition only under controlled bench conditions.

**Expected outcome:** The exact oxygen-related symptom is identified and reproducible. If the problem cannot be reproduced and verification testing passes, troubleshooting can stop.

### 3. Verify Oxygen and Air Supplies

Confirm oxygen and air sources are available and connected to the correct gas inlets.

Inspect external hoses, fittings, and wall connections for:

- Loose connections
- Damage
- Kinks
- Incorrect gas connection
- Signs of contamination

If practical, compare operation using another verified outlet or known-good approved hose.

**Expected outcome:** Correct gas sources are securely connected and available. If correcting a supply problem restores proper FiO2 and calibration, troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Confirm Calibration Conditions

Perform oxygen calibration only under the operating conditions required by the applicable manufacturer procedure.

Avoid attempting to compensate for an incorrect gas supply by repeatedly recalibrating the ventilator.

**Expected outcome:** Calibration is attempted under appropriate stable conditions. If calibration succeeds and oxygen output verifies normally, troubleshooting can stop.

### 5. Inspect the Patient Circuit and External Components

Verify the circuit, filters, humidification accessories, adapters, and gas-related external components are installed correctly and are not leaking or obstructed.

Check for connections that could allow room air entrainment or otherwise affect measured oxygen concentration.

**Expected outcome:** The breathing system is correctly assembled without an external leak or configuration problem affecting FiO2. If correction restores proper readings, troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Compare Displayed FiO2 With Approved Test Equipment

Using appropriate calibrated oxygen-analysis equipment, compare delivered oxygen concentration with the ventilator's displayed value during controlled bench operation.

Do not use an unverified analyzer to condemn the ventilator.

**Expected outcome:** Delivered and displayed oxygen behavior is consistent with the applicable verification procedure. If measurements are acceptable, the original complaint may have been setup-related and troubleshooting can stop after documentation.

### 7. Check for Pattern Changes Across FiO2 Settings

Using approved test methods, verify whether the discrepancy occurs consistently or only under specific operating conditions.

Do not make unauthorized calibration adjustments or enter restricted service modes.

**Expected outcome:** Testing determines whether the problem is external and configuration-related or remains present across controlled conditions.

### 8. Verify External Sensor-Related Connections Where Accessible

Inspect externally accessible oxygen measurement connections or related components for damage, looseness, or contamination where applicable.

Do not open the ventilator or replace internal oxygen measurement components without authorized service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible connections are secure and undamaged. If correcting an external connection resolves calibration or accuracy, troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correction, complete the appropriate system checks and verify oxygen delivery across representative operating conditions using approved test equipment.

Confirm alarms and ventilation functions operate normally.

**Expected outcome:** Oxygen calibration completes and delivered oxygen concentration verifies appropriately. The ventilator may be returned to service only after all required testing passes.

## If the Problem Persists

If oxygen calibration continues to fail or delivered FiO2 remains incorrect after gas supplies, hoses, patient circuit, calibration conditions, and external connections have been verified, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve an internal oxygen measurement system, gas-mixing system, pneumatic component, electronic control, or service-level calibration issue.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Drager documentation and approved oxygen-analysis equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not return a ventilator to service when oxygen delivery or measurement remains unreliable.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

When FiO2 accuracy is questioned, verify actual delivered oxygen with approved independent test equipment before considering the ventilator safe.
