## What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide for repeated blood-leak or air-detection alarms caused by disposables, fluid conditions, positioning, contamination, or detector-related faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Immediately

Repeated blood-leak or air-detection alarms involve critical dialysis safety systems. Do not bypass, silence without correction, or defeat these protections.

If a patient is connected, follow clinical procedures to maintain patient safety and treatment continuity. Use another verified machine if the cause cannot be safely corrected through normal clinical workflow.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is protected and no critical detector is being bypassed.

### 2. Identify Which Alarm Is Occurring

Determine whether the machine is reporting:

- Blood leak
- Air detection
- Both at different times
- An intermittent detector warning
- A persistent alarm immediately after setup

Record the exact message and when it occurs.

**Expected outcome:** The specific detector path and operating condition associated with the alarm are known.

### 3. Inspect the Extracorporeal Circuit for Air

For air-related alarms, have qualified clinical staff inspect the blood tubing and chamber arrangement according to facility procedure.

Check externally for:

- Visible air
- Incorrect chamber fill level
- Loose bloodline connection
- Cracked or damaged tubing
- Tubing improperly positioned in the detector
- Improperly seated disposable components

Do not bypass the detector to continue therapy.

**Expected outcome:** The blood circuit is correctly assembled, properly filled, and positioned without visible air or damaged tubing.

### 4. Inspect the Blood Leak Detection Area

For blood-leak alarms, check the accessible detector area and dialysate path for visible contamination, moisture, residue, or improperly seated external components.

A detector area contaminated during prior handling or disinfection may create false indications.

Clean only using approved procedures and materials.

**Expected outcome:** The detector area is clean, dry, and unobstructed.

### 5. Verify the Disposable Set and Placement

Confirm that the approved disposable set is appropriate for the machine and has been installed according to normal clinical setup.

Inspect for:

- Twisted tubing
- Flattened tubing
- Misrouting
- Poor detector alignment
- Damaged segments
- Incorrectly installed chambers or holders

If appropriate and permitted, use a new approved disposable set to rule out a defective disposable.

**Expected outcome:** The disposable circuit is compatible, undamaged, and correctly positioned. If the alarm disappears with a known-good disposable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 6. Check for Fluid or Optical Interference

Inspect accessible sensor windows and surrounding surfaces for residue, condensation, fingerprints, fluid splash, or other material that could interfere with detection.

Do not insert tools into sensor assemblies or alter detector alignment.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible sensing surfaces are clean and free of external interference.

### 7. Reproduce the Alarm Without a Patient

After correcting obvious external conditions, perform the approved setup and machine checks without a patient attached.

Observe whether the alarm returns under controlled conditions.

**Expected outcome:** The detector passes normal startup or preparation checks without repeated false alarms.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Before clinical use, confirm that:

- Required detector checks pass
- No repeated false alarm occurs
- Disposable positioning remains correct
- Relevant alarms remain functional
- Applicable pre-treatment tests complete successfully

Never return the machine to service based solely on temporarily clearing the alarm.

**Expected outcome:** Detection systems operate normally and required checks pass. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If disposable condition, tubing position, detector cleanliness, fluid condition, and approved setup have been verified but blood-leak or air alarms continue, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible remaining categories include detector sensing, alignment, calibration, signal processing, or other internal service-level faults. These safety systems must not be bypassed or adjusted outside approved service procedures.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Nipro documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete all required detector and alarm verification before return to clinical use. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never defeat an air or blood-leak alarm to keep dialysis running; these alarms protect against potentially serious patient harm.

## Final Thought

Treat repeated air and blood-leak alarms as safety-critical until proven otherwise. Verify the disposable circuit, positioning, and detector area before considering an internal fault, and escalate rather than bypassing an unresolved protection system.

That is successful troubleshooting.
