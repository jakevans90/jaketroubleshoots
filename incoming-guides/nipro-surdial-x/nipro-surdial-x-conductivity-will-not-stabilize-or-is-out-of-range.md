## What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide for unstable or abnormal conductivity associated with concentrate supply, water, connections, configuration, or another externally verifiable condition.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Stop Use of Unreliable Dialysate

Do not continue patient treatment when dialysate conductivity is unstable, outside the machine's acceptable condition, or cannot be independently verified as appropriate.

Move the patient to another verified machine or follow the facility's dialysis contingency process.

**Expected outcome:** No patient is dependent on a machine with questionable dialysate composition.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Determine when the conductivity issue occurs:

- During preparation
- After concentrate connection
- During treatment setup
- Intermittently during operation
- Only with one concentrate source
- After a room, water, or supply change

Record any machine message exactly.

**Expected outcome:** The timing and repeatability of the conductivity problem are established.

### 3. Verify the Water Supply Condition

Confirm that the machine has its normal approved water supply and that facility water-treatment systems are operating normally.

Check for:

- Interrupted water flow
- Kinked or restricted external water connection
- Recent water-system maintenance
- Multiple dialysis machines showing similar symptoms

Do not adjust facility water-treatment settings unless authorized.

**Expected outcome:** The dialysis machine is receiving its expected external water supply. A facility-wide problem is identified and escalated if multiple machines are affected.

### 4. Inspect Concentrate Containers and Connections

Verify the correct concentrate products intended for the prescribed treatment are present and connected to the correct external connection points.

Inspect for:

- Empty or nearly empty containers
- Loose connections
- Kinked tubing
- Cracked tubing or fittings
- Incorrect concentrate selection
- Obvious contamination
- Improperly seated pickup connections

Do not substitute unapproved fluids.

**Expected outcome:** Correct concentrate sources are available and securely connected without visible restriction or leakage.

### 5. Check for Concentrate Supply or Batch Isolation

If permitted by facility procedure, compare the issue using another approved container or source of the same required concentrate.

If multiple machines show the same conductivity problem from a common supply or batch, stop using that source and escalate appropriately.

**Expected outcome:** The concentrate source is either ruled out or identified as the external cause. If conductivity stabilizes with an approved known-good source, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 6. Verify Treatment Configuration

Confirm the treatment setup and concentrate selection match the intended prescription and facility workflow.

Do not alter clinical prescription values merely to force conductivity into an acceptable state.

If a configuration appears incorrect, have qualified clinical personnel verify the prescription and setup before continuing.

**Expected outcome:** The machine configuration corresponds to the intended treatment and no incorrect selection is producing the conductivity issue.

### 7. Inspect Accessible External Fluid Connections

Inspect accessible dialysate-related connections for leakage, incomplete seating, crossed connections, or restriction.

Do not disconnect or manipulate internal fluid-path components beyond approved external service actions.

**Expected outcome:** External fluid connections are secure, correctly routed, and free of visible leakage or obstruction.

### 8. Reprepare and Observe Conductivity

After correcting any external supply or connection problem, perform the approved preparation sequence and allow the system to reach its normal ready condition.

Observe whether conductivity becomes stable without repeated alarms or abnormal fluctuation.

**Expected outcome:** Conductivity stabilizes during preparation and remains acceptable to the machine's normal monitoring system. If successful, proceed to final verification.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Before return to service, complete applicable manufacturer-approved checks. Where facility procedure requires independent dialysate verification, use approved calibrated test equipment.

Do not return the machine to service simply because an alarm cleared temporarily.

**Expected outcome:** Conductivity remains stable and required dialysate verification passes. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If the approved water source, concentrate, connections, setup, and external fluid supply have been verified and conductivity still does not stabilize, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible remaining categories include internal mixing, proportioning, sensing, fluid-control, calibration, or service-level configuration problems. Do not adjust internal calibration or bypass conductivity protections without approved service procedures and authorization.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Nipro documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return the machine to service only after conductivity and applicable dialysate safety checks pass. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never compensate for an unresolved conductivity problem by changing the treatment prescription solely to make the displayed value appear acceptable.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient from uncertain dialysate, verify water and concentrate supplies before assuming an internal problem, and require stable verified operation before return to service. Escalate unresolved conductivity faults and document the complete troubleshooting path.

That is successful troubleshooting.
