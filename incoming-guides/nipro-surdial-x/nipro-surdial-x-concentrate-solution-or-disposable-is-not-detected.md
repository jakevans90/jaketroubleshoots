## What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when an approved concentrate, solution, or disposable is not recognized because of selection, connection, compatibility, positioning, or detection issues.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient

Do not begin or continue dialysis if the required concentrate, solution, or disposable cannot be reliably recognized.

Use another verified machine or correct the setup before treatment proceeds.

**Expected outcome:** Patient treatment does not proceed with an unverified fluid or disposable configuration.

### 2. Confirm What Is Not Being Detected

Identify the specific item involved and determine whether:

- It is never recognized
- Recognition is intermittent
- Recognition is lost when moved
- The issue occurs with one item only
- Multiple approved items fail
- The machine displays a specific message

Record the exact message.

**Expected outcome:** The affected item and pattern of failure are clearly identified.

### 3. Verify the Correct Approved Item

Confirm that the concentrate, solution, or disposable is appropriate for the intended SURDIAL X setup and treatment.

Check labeling and packaging for:

- Correct product
- Correct intended use
- Obvious damage
- Contamination
- Improper storage evidence
- Expired or otherwise unsuitable product according to facility policy

Do not substitute unapproved products.

**Expected outcome:** The correct approved item is being used.

### 4. Inspect Physical Installation and Connections

Verify that the item is fully installed in its normal operating position.

Check for:

- Loose or incomplete connection
- Incorrect orientation
- Twisted or kinked tubing
- Misaligned disposable component
- Connector damage
- Fluid or residue on accessible sensing surfaces

**Expected outcome:** The item is correctly seated and connected without visible damage.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Detection Areas

Examine accessible sensor, holder, connector, or detection surfaces for contamination, moisture, residue, or obstruction.

Clean only according to approved procedures.

Do not modify sensors, magnets, tags, switches, or interlocks to force recognition.

**Expected outcome:** The detection area is clean, dry, and unobstructed.

### 6. Test a Known-Good Approved Item

When clinically and technically appropriate, substitute another known-good approved concentrate container, solution source, or disposable of the correct type.

Observe whether recognition is restored.

**Expected outcome:** If the replacement is recognized normally, the original item is isolated as the cause and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 7. Check Configuration and Selection

Verify that machine setup selections match the installed item and intended therapy.

Do not enter unauthorized configuration menus or alter service-level settings.

If a prescription-related selection is involved, have qualified clinical staff verify it.

**Expected outcome:** The selected machine configuration matches the installed approved product.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Repeat the normal preparation sequence and confirm:

- The item remains recognized
- Required fluid preparation proceeds normally
- No related alarm recurs
- Applicable pre-treatment tests pass

**Expected outcome:** Recognition is stable through preparation and verification. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If compatibility, installation, connectors, sensing areas, approved substitutions, and configuration have been verified but recognition still fails, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible remaining categories include internal sensing, connector circuitry, detection hardware, software, or service-level configuration. Do not bypass recognition systems or simulate the presence of a disposable or solution.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Nipro documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Confirm normal recognition and all applicable preparation checks before returning the machine to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never defeat a recognition feature just to keep treatment moving; first verify that the correct approved concentrate, solution, or disposable is actually installed.

## Final Thought

Recognition failures are often caused by the item, connection, or installation rather than internal electronics. Verify compatibility and physical setup first, never bypass detection systems, and escalate unresolved faults with clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
