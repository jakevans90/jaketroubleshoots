## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting absent or intermittent power caused by outlets, cords, plugs, circuit loading, switches, overheating, or internal electrical faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Load and Clinical Operations

A sterilizer that loses power during a cycle cannot provide a verified sterile load.
Quarantine any affected load.
Reprocess instruments using another verified sterilizer.
Allow the chamber to cool and depressurize.
Do not open the door while pressure remains.
Remove the unit from immediate clinical use until power stability is confirmed.

**Expected outcome:** No interrupted load is released for patient care.

### 2. Clarify the Power Failure

Determine whether:
- The unit is completely dead.
- The display flashes and turns off.
- Shutdown occurs during heat-up.
- Power returns automatically.
- The outlet or circuit breaker trips.
- The failure follows movement of the cord.
- An odor, unusual heat, or sound occurred.

**Expected outcome:** The condition is documented as no-power, intermittent power, or load-related shutdown.

### 3. Inspect the Power Cord and Plug

Disconnect power before inspection.
Check for:
- Cuts.
- Crushing.
- Exposed conductors.
- Bent or loose plug blades.
- Heat discoloration.
- A loose strain relief.
- Evidence of liquid exposure.
- Do not tape, splice, or field-repair a damaged power cord unless permitted by approved service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The cord and plug are intact. Any damage requires removal from service.

### 4. Verify the Facility Receptacle

Confirm that:
- The outlet has power.
- The plug fits securely.
- The outlet is not damaged or overheated.
- The branch circuit is suitable for the sterilizer.
- Other high-load equipment is not causing circuit interruption.
- The unit is not connected through an extension cord or power strip.
- Use approved electrical test methods.

**Expected outcome:** Stable facility power is available. If correcting the outlet or circuit resolves the issue, stop troubleshooting after testing.

### 5. Check the Main Power Switch

Inspect the accessible switch for:
- Loose mechanical feel.
- Failure to remain in position.
- Heat damage.
- Intermittent operation when lightly operated.
- Contamination from cleaning solution.
- Do not disassemble or bypass the switch.

**Expected outcome:** The switch operates normally and consistently. Any physical damage requires service.

### 6. Evaluate Ventilation and Overheating

Check that:
- Ventilation openings are not blocked.
- The sterilizer has adequate clearance.
- Dust or stored items do not restrict airflow.
- The room is not excessively hot.
- The unit is not next to another major heat source.
- Shutdown occurs only after prolonged heating.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer can cool normally. If improving ventilation prevents shutdown and all tests pass, stop troubleshooting.

### 7. Check for External Electrical Loading

Determine whether:
- The circuit powers other high-current equipment.
- Shutdown coincides with another device starting.
- The breaker or receptacle becomes warm.
- Facilities has documented voltage problems.
- A dedicated electrical supply is required by local installation requirements.

**Expected outcome:** External electrical loading is ruled out or corrected by Facilities.

### 8. Perform One Controlled Startup Test

Using a verified outlet and with the chamber cool:
- Power the unit on.
- Observe startup.
- Check for flickering, reset, odor, heat, or abnormal noise.
- Do not proceed if the plug, cord, or switch heats abnormally.
- Confirm controls remain stable before selecting a test cycle.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer remains powered and stable. If not, remove it from service.

### 9. Run an Approved Functional Cycle

After correcting an external cause:
- Run an approved test cycle.
- Monitor for shutdown during heat-up, pressure hold, exhaust, and drying.
- Verify the branch circuit remains stable.
- Review the completed cycle record.
- Complete required quality-control testing.

**Expected outcome:** The unit remains powered through the complete cycle. Troubleshooting can stop after all tests pass.

### 10. Remove From Service for Any Unexplained Shutdown

Immediately remove the unit from service when:
- It shuts down again.
- The plug, cord, switch, or receptacle becomes hot.
- Burning odor, smoke, arcing, or discoloration is present.
- The breaker repeatedly trips.
- Power loss occurs during a cycle without an external cause.
- The chamber cannot safely depressurize.

**Expected outcome:** An electrical or thermal hazard is prevented from returning to clinical use.

## If the Problem Persists

External power, cord condition, outlet stability, circuit loading, switch operation, and ventilation have been ruled out. Remaining categories may include internal power distribution, heater load, thermal protection, wiring, control power, internal switching, or control circuitry.
The sterilizer should be:
- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Tuttnauer documentation and approved electrical safety test equipment.
- Repaired only by qualified personnel.
- Return to service requires electrical safety testing, stable startup, successful complete cycles, and all required sterilization-quality checks. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any load interrupted by power loss must be treated as nonsterile and reprocessed through a complete verified cycle.

## Final Thought

Power failures invalidate the cycle and may indicate an electrical hazard. Verify the cord, outlet, circuit, switch, and ventilation before assuming internal failure, and escalate any unexplained shutdown with complete safety and cycle documentation.
