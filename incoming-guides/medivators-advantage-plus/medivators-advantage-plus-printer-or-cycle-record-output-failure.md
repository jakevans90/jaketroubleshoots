## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses missing, incomplete, faded, or failed cycle records caused by paper, printer power, connections, queue, settings, or external reporting problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Traceability and Reprocessing Records

Do not discard or release required cycle documentation without following the facility’s approved downtime process. Confirm whether the AER itself shows a successfully completed cycle before addressing only the output problem.

Do not interpret a missing printout as proof that the cycle failed or succeeded.

**Expected outcome:** Endoscope traceability is preserved while record output is restored.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Output Failure

Determine whether the issue involves:

- No printout
- Blank paper
- Faded print
- Partial record
- Paper jam
- Repeated records
- Wrong cycle information
- A report that exists electronically but does not print
- Failure affecting one or all cycles

Record any displayed printer or export message.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is separated into printer hardware, media, queue, configuration, or data-generation categories.

### 3. Verify Cycle Completion and Stored Record

Check whether the completed cycle appears in the AER’s normal record or history view. Confirm the correct endoscope, operator, date, time, and cycle status.

Do not recreate or alter the clinical record merely to produce a printout.

**Expected outcome:** The source cycle record is confirmed as present and accurate, or a broader record-generation problem is identified.

### 4. Check Printer Power and Status

Verify that the printer is powered, online, and free of obvious fault indicators. Check its approved power source, power cord, and accessible connection to the AER.

If the printer uses a separate outlet, verify that the outlet supports another low-risk test device according to facility practice.

**Expected outcome:** The printer has stable power and is in a ready state.

### 5. Inspect Paper and Media Loading

Check that the correct approved paper or media is installed with the proper orientation. Inspect for:

- Empty roll or tray
- Incorrect paper type
- Reversed thermal paper
- Wrinkled or damp paper
- Misaligned roll
- Open cover
- Paper scraps
- Visible jam

Do not insert tools into the printer mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** Approved media is installed correctly and feeds freely. If proper loading restores printing, continue to verification.

### 6. Inspect External Printer Connections

Inspect and reseat accessible printer cables only while the devices are safely idle. Look for:

- Loose connectors
- Damaged cable insulation
- Bent pins
- Strain at the connector
- Liquid contamination
- Connection to the wrong port

Use a known-good approved cable when available.

**Expected outcome:** The printer connection is secure and undamaged. If a known-good cable restores output, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 7. Check Output Selection and Queue

Verify that the correct printer or output destination is selected through authorized user settings. Check for a paused printer, accumulated queue, or a record waiting for manual release.

Do not change protected network, system, or service configuration without authorization.

**Expected outcome:** The intended output destination is active and able to receive the cycle record.

### 8. Perform a Controlled Test Print or Reprint

Use an approved test-print, stored-record reprint, or nonclinical verification function. Confirm that the printout is complete, legible, and matches the selected record.

Avoid creating duplicate clinical documentation without marking or managing it according to policy.

**Expected outcome:** The printer produces a complete and accurate record. The issue is resolved, and troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Verify the Full Cycle Record Workflow

Run the approved verification cycle and confirm:

- Successful cycle completion
- Electronic record creation
- Correct date and time
- Correct identifiers
- Complete printed or exported record
- No printer or communication alarm

**Expected outcome:** The complete documentation pathway functions correctly.

### 10. Stop and Escalate When Record Output Remains Unreliable

If paper, power, connections, queue, and output settings are correct but records remain missing or inaccurate, remove the printer function or AER from service as required by facility traceability policy.

Do not disassemble the printer or alter protected system configuration without approved documentation.

**Expected outcome:** Reprocessing is not continued without a reliable documentation method.

## If the Problem Persists

Common power, media, connection, queue, and destination causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve the printer mechanism, print interface, local software, data-generation process, networked report destination, or configuration requiring authorized service.

The device should be:

- Removed from service when required by traceability policy
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or qualified evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return to service requires verification that complete and accurate cycle records are created and delivered through the required documentation pathway. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Confirm the electronic cycle result before treating a missing printout as either a successful or failed reprocessing cycle.

## Final Thought

Preserve traceability, verify the stored cycle record before assuming data loss, check simple printer and media causes first, validate the complete output pathway, escalate appropriately, and document the final record status.
