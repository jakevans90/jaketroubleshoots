Unresponsive or Software Freeze

Plus

## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses frozen screens, delayed touch response, or software lockups caused by contamination, power interruption, peripheral faults, environmental conditions, or software problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patients and Reprocessing Workflow

If the touchscreen freezes during a cycle, treat the cycle as incomplete unless the AER clearly completes and records it according to the validated process. Quarantine the endoscope and move work to another verified AER.

Do not disconnect power while chemical movement, draining, or an active cycle may still be occurring unless an emergency requires it.

**Expected outcome:** The affected endoscope and staff remain protected while the equipment state is assessed.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Condition

Determine whether:

- The display is visible but touch does not respond
- Only one screen area is unresponsive
- The image is frozen
- The system is slow
- Physical controls also fail
- A cycle continues in the background
- The unit restarts repeatedly
- The problem occurs after a specific screen or action

Record all displayed messages before attempting a restart.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is characterized as a touch-input, display, software, or broader power condition.

### 3. Check Whether a Cycle Is Active

Observe status indicators, pump sounds, fluid movement, alarms, and any connected printer or record output. Do not open the chamber or interrupt utilities solely because the display appears frozen.

Follow the approved emergency or interrupted-cycle procedure if the AER state cannot be determined.

**Expected outcome:** The unit’s operating state is identified without creating a chemical or reprocessing hazard.

### 4. Inspect and Clean the Touchscreen Surface

Check for:

- Water or chemical residue
- Gloves or covers interfering with touch
- Adhesive film
- Cracks
- Impact damage
- Heavy contamination
- An object pressing against the display

Clean and dry the screen using an approved method.

**Expected outcome:** The touchscreen surface is clean, dry, undamaged, and free of physical interference. If touch response returns, continue to verification.

### 5. Check External Peripherals

Disconnect only approved removable peripherals that are not required for safe operation, such as an external barcode scanner or printer, after the unit is safely idle. A failed peripheral or cable can sometimes affect software response.

Do not disconnect network, chemical, water, or internal connections as a diagnostic shortcut.

**Expected outcome:** A faulty external peripheral is isolated, or the screen remains frozen with peripherals removed.

### 6. Verify Stable Power

Inspect the power cord, plug, strain relief, outlet, and any approved power-distribution device. Check for evidence of recent power interruption, loose plugs, tripped circuits, or facility electrical work.

Do not continue using an AER that loses power unpredictably.

**Expected outcome:** The AER has a secure and stable power source.

### 7. Perform an Authorized Controlled Restart

Once the cycle state is safe and the affected endoscope remains quarantined, use the normal shutdown or restart sequence. Avoid repeated forced power cycles.

Observe startup for error messages, unusual delays, or recurrence of the freeze.

**Expected outcome:** The AER starts normally and the touchscreen responds across the full display. If it freezes again, continue troubleshooting.

### 8. Check Environment and Ventilation

Verify that external vents are unobstructed and the room is not unusually hot, cold, or humid. Look for condensation, nearby steam sources, blocked airflow, or heat-producing equipment.

Do not open service panels to cool the unit.

**Expected outcome:** The AER operates in a stable environment with unobstructed ventilation.

### 9. Perform a Full Functional Verification

Test the touchscreen across normal authorized screens and run the approved empty or verification cycle. Confirm:

- Reliable touch response
- Normal screen transitions
- No software freeze
- Correct alarm acknowledgment
- Complete cycle progression
- Accurate cycle record

**Expected outcome:** The touchscreen and software remain stable through the complete test. The issue is resolved, and troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Stop and Escalate When Freezing Recurs

If the touchscreen is clean, power is stable, peripherals are ruled out, and environmental conditions are acceptable but the freeze returns, remove the AER from service. Do not access internal computing hardware, reinstall software, or use unauthorized service tools.

**Expected outcome:** An AER with unreliable controls is prevented from processing endoscopes.

## If the Problem Persists

Common screen contamination, peripheral, power, and environmental causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve the touchscreen assembly, internal computer, storage device, application software, operating system, internal connection, or control electronics.

The AER should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or qualified bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return to service requires reliable control response, successful startup, alarm verification, complete cycle testing, and confirmation that cycle records are accurate. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A frozen display during reprocessing requires the endoscope to remain quarantined until cycle completion can be positively verified.

## Final Thought

Protect the endoscope and determine the AER’s actual cycle state first, rule out screen contamination, peripherals, power, and environment before assuming internal failure, verify stable operation, escalate recurring freezes, and document the event precisely.

That is successful troubleshooting.
