Due, Filter Not Recognized, or Filter Life Error

Plus

## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses filter-life warnings, overdue filter messages, or filter recognition problems caused by installation, identification, configuration, or service-record issues.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patients and Reprocessing Workflow

Do not bypass a filter warning or assume water quality remains acceptable. Stop using the affected AER when the warning prevents a validated cycle or when filter status cannot be confirmed.

Quarantine any endoscope from an incomplete cycle and use another verified AER when available.

**Expected outcome:** Endoscopes are not processed with an unverified or improperly installed filtration system.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Filter Message

Record the complete on-screen message and determine whether it indicates:

- Filter replacement due
- Filter life expired
- Filter not detected
- Filter identification failure
- Filter installation incomplete
- A service or reset requirement

Ask whether the message appeared after recent filter replacement or maintenance.

**Expected outcome:** The issue is identified as a scheduled-life warning, recognition problem, or post-service condition.

### 3. Review Filter Service History

Check the equipment maintenance record, filter replacement label, service log, or department documentation. Verify the last replacement date and whether the correct filter stage was serviced.

Do not reset filter life based on an estimate or verbal assumption.

**Expected outcome:** The actual filter service status is established from documented information.

### 4. Verify the Correct Approved Filter Was Installed

Confirm that the installed filter matches the approved type for the AER and water-treatment configuration. Check packaging or service documentation rather than relying only on physical similarity.

Do not substitute an unapproved filter.

**Expected outcome:** The installed filter is the correct approved component for the system.

### 5. Inspect External Filter Installation

Where accessible without disassembly, inspect the filter housing and connections for:

- Incorrect orientation
- Incomplete seating
- Loose housing
- Cross-threading
- Visible leakage
- Protective caps left in place
- Pinched tubing
- A closed isolation valve

Do not open a pressurized housing or disturb chemical and water connections without following approved service precautions.

**Expected outcome:** The filter is installed securely, oriented correctly, and free of visible leaks or restrictions.

### 6. Check Filter Identification Features

If the approved filter uses a label, tag, chip, barcode, or other identification feature, inspect it for damage, contamination, incorrect orientation, or obstruction. Clean only with an approved method.

Verify that no packaging label or replacement sticker is covering the identification area.

**Expected outcome:** The filter identification feature is present, clean, and positioned for recognition.

### 7. Confirm Required Post-Replacement Actions

Review the manufacturer-approved replacement procedure and service record to determine whether a documented acknowledgment, priming step, leak check, or authorized filter-life reset was omitted.

Do not enter unauthorized service menus or change filter-life settings without proper access and documentation.

**Expected outcome:** All required post-replacement steps have been completed by qualified personnel.

### 8. Restart the AER and Recheck Status

When safe and authorized, restart the AER normally. Observe whether the filter message clears and whether the filter status screen displays a valid condition.

Do not repeatedly reset or power-cycle the unit to suppress a recurring warning.

**Expected outcome:** The AER recognizes the filter and shows an acceptable documented status. If so, continue to functional verification.

### 9. Perform the Required Functional Verification

Run the approved post-filter or empty-cycle verification. Inspect for:

- Normal water flow
- No filter-related alarm
- No leakage at the housing or connections
- Successful cycle progression
- Acceptable water-system status

**Expected outcome:** The verification passes without filter alarms or leaks. The issue is resolved, and troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Stop and Escalate When Recognition Remains Unresolved

If the correct filter is properly installed and documented but the AER still reports a recognition or life error, remove it from service. Do not bypass the warning, alter hidden counters, or replace internal readers or control components without approved service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The AER remains unavailable until filtration status can be reliably verified.

## If the Problem Persists

Common installation, documentation, identification, and post-replacement causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve an internal reader, sensor, software record, configuration mismatch, water-system control fault, or incomplete authorized service procedure.

The AER should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or qualified bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return to service requires verified filter recognition, leak-free operation, acceptable water flow, and successful completion of required cycle testing. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never bypass a filter-life warning when the AER’s validated water quality or cycle completion depends on that filter.

## Final Thought

Protect the validated reprocessing process, verify filter type and installation before assuming an internal fault, confirm recognition through approved testing, escalate unresolved errors, and document the filter history and corrective action accurately.

That is successful troubleshooting.
