## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses charging failures or reduced battery runtime caused by AC power, connections, battery condition, charging state, storage, or service-level power problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Reliable Power
Do not rely on questionable battery operation during transport or patient support.

If the ventilator is in clinical use and battery reliability is uncertain, keep it connected to verified AC power when safe and arrange another verified ventilator before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** Patient ventilation is not dependent on an unreliable battery.

### 2. Confirm the Battery Complaint
Determine whether the issue is:

- Battery not charging
- Charge indicator not changing
- Battery not recognized
- Unexpected shutdown on battery
- Shorter-than-expected runtime
- Intermittent transfer between AC and battery

Record any displayed messages.

**Expected outcome:** The battery-related symptom is clearly defined.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source
Connect the ventilator to a known-good outlet.

Confirm the facility outlet works using an approved tester or known-good equipment, and inspect the ventilator power cord and plug for damage.

**Expected outcome:** Reliable AC power reaches the ventilator. If charging resumes after changing outlets or correcting the power connection, continue monitoring and final verification.

### 4. Verify AC Power Recognition
Observe whether the ventilator indicates that external power is present and whether charging status changes appropriately.

A unit connected to AC but not recognizing external power may have a problem unrelated to the battery itself.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator consistently recognizes AC power.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Battery Components
If the battery is user- or service-accessible without disassembly beyond approved procedures, inspect for:

- Swelling
- Cracks
- Leakage
- Damaged contacts
- Loose installation
- Contamination
- Physical damage

Do not continue using a physically damaged battery.

**Expected outcome:** The battery is physically intact and correctly installed.

### 6. Reseat the Battery When Permitted
If allowed by approved procedure, remove and reinstall the accessible battery to ensure proper contact.

Do not open the battery pack or attempt cell-level repair.

**Expected outcome:** The battery is recognized and charging normally. If reseating resolves the issue, perform final verification.

### 7. Compare With a Known-Good Compatible Battery
If available, install a compatible known-good battery and observe recognition, charging, and controlled battery operation.

**Expected outcome:** If the known-good battery operates normally, the original battery is likely degraded or defective and should be replaced according to facility procedure.

### 8. Consider Battery Condition and Usage History
Review available battery age, replacement history, storage conditions, and repeated deep-discharge history when known.

Short runtime can reflect battery degradation even when charging appears normal.

**Expected outcome:** Battery condition or usage history provides reasonable context without assuming an internal charging failure.

### 9. Perform Controlled Battery Verification
After adequate charging, perform the facility-approved battery performance or operational check in a controlled environment.

Do not intentionally deplete a ventilator battery while the device is supporting a patient.

**Expected outcome:** Battery operation meets the applicable approved acceptance criteria. If it does, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate if Charging Remains Abnormal
If a known-good battery does not charge or the ventilator does not reliably recognize AC power, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator is removed from service for evaluation of the internal charging or power system.

## If the Problem Persists

Facility power, power cord, battery installation, battery condition, and known-good battery substitution have been evaluated. Remaining causes may involve internal charging, power conversion, power-management, battery-interface, or control systems.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Drager documentation and approved electrical test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify AC operation, charging, battery transfer, battery status indication, alarm behavior, and required battery performance before return to service.

Knowing when the issue follows the ventilator rather than the battery is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Confirm adequate verified battery capacity before any transport where continuous AC power may not be available.
