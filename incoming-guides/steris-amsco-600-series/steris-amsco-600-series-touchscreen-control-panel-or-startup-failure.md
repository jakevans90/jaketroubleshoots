## What This Guide Helps With
Use this guide when the sterilizer display, touchscreen, controls, or startup sequence is unavailable, frozen, unresponsive, or unreliable.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Safety and Processing Continuity
Do not attempt to process a load on a sterilizer whose controls or display are unreliable. If a cycle is already active and the interface becomes abnormal, follow facility procedures and avoid actions that could compromise chamber safety.

Move required processing to another verified sterilizer.

**Expected outcome:** Clinical operations do not depend on an unreliable control system.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Startup or Control Symptom
Determine whether the sterilizer:
- Is completely dark.
- Powers on but does not complete startup.
- Displays a frozen screen.
- Responds to some controls but not others.
- Has an inaccurate or intermittently responsive touchscreen.
- Restarts unexpectedly.
- Displays a specific message.

Record what is visible before restarting the unit.

**Expected outcome:** The issue is defined as power loss, startup failure, display failure, touch-input failure, or intermittent control behavior.

### 3. Verify Facility Electrical Power
Confirm the sterilizer's expected facility electrical supply is available. Check for reported outages, tripped upstream infrastructure, or ongoing electrical work through appropriate facility channels.

Do not repeatedly reset unexplained protective devices.

**Expected outcome:** Stable facility power is available. If external power was lost, restore it through appropriate facility support before further evaluation.

### 4. Inspect Accessible External Power and Connections
Check accessible cords, disconnects, connectors, and external control-panel connections where applicable for:
- Damage.
- Loose connections.
- Heat.
- Discoloration.
- Moisture.
- Physical impact.

Do not open energized electrical enclosures for a basic startup complaint.

**Expected outcome:** No obvious external electrical hazard or disconnected accessible connection is present. Damage, overheating, or moisture requires removal from service.

### 5. Inspect the Touchscreen and Control Surface
Check the screen and controls for:
- Liquid contamination.
- Cleaning residue.
- Protective film interference.
- Cracks.
- Impact damage.
- Objects contacting the screen.
- Gloves or environmental conditions interfering with normal touch response.

Clean only according to approved facility/manufacturer methods.

**Expected outcome:** The control surface is clean, dry, and physically intact. If contamination was causing false or missed touches and normal operation returns, proceed to verification.

### 6. Verify Normal Control Response
Using normal user controls only, check whether the display updates consistently and whether buttons or touchscreen selections correspond to the intended command.

Do not enter unauthorized service menus or change configuration values.

**Expected outcome:** Control inputs are accurate and repeatable. If the interface remains erratic, remove the unit from production use.

### 7. Perform an Approved Normal Restart
If the sterilizer is idle and it is safe to do so, shut down and restart the unit using the normal approved procedure.

Avoid cycling facility power repeatedly as a substitute for diagnosis.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer completes startup and remains stable. If a single approved restart restores stable operation, continue with functional verification before deciding on return to service.

### 8. Observe for Repeatability
Allow the sterilizer to remain powered and verify that the control panel does not freeze, reboot, blank, or begin generating unintended input.

Intermittent control problems should not be dismissed solely because the screen works temporarily after restarting.

**Expected outcome:** The interface remains stable and predictable. Any recurrence requires escalation.

### 9. Perform Functional Verification
With no production load at risk, verify normal cycle selection, door-status indication, control response, alarm/status display, and initiation of an approved test cycle.

Confirm the display remains stable through the test.

**Expected outcome:** Startup, controls, and display function reliably through normal operation. If all required checks pass without recurrence, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent or Intermittent Control Failure
If facility power, accessible connections, touchscreen condition, normal restart, and control operation have been checked but the fault remains or recurs, stop external troubleshooting.

Potential service-level categories include the display assembly, touchscreen interface, internal power supply, controller, communication, software, or configuration.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer is removed from production until the control system is reliably repaired and verified.

## If the Problem Persists
Common external power, contamination, and connection causes have been ruled out. **Remove the sterilizer from service**, **label it Out of Service**, and arrange qualified service or repair evaluation.

Use current STERIS documentation and approved test equipment. Internal electronics, power supplies, display assemblies, controller hardware, software, and service configuration should be evaluated or repaired only by qualified personnel.

After repair, complete startup, interface, safety, cycle-control, and applicable sterilization-performance verification before return to service. An intermittently functioning control panel is not acceptable simply because a reboot temporarily restores operation.

## Clinical Use Tip
If the display or touchscreen cannot be trusted to show or accept the intended cycle and status information, move processing to another verified sterilizer.

## Final Thought
Keep unreliable controls out of production, verify power and simple external causes first, never mistake a temporary reboot recovery for a confirmed repair, and escalate recurring control failures with clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
