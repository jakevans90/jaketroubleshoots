## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoot unstable Lumify USB connections caused by connector seating, port damage, cable strain, adapters, or mobile-device connection problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Protect the Patient and Stop Unreliable Scanning
If the transducer disconnects during patient scanning, discontinue use of the affected setup and move the examination to another verified system.

Repeated reconnection during an active procedure should not substitute for reliable operation.

Expected outcome: Patient care no longer depends on an intermittent USB connection.

### 2. Confirm the Failure Pattern
Determine whether the connection is physically loose, disconnects with normal movement, fails only at certain cable positions, or has visible damage.

Observe whether the application loses transducer detection at the same time.

Expected outcome: The mechanical or intermittent connection problem is reproducible away from the patient.

### 3. Inspect the Transducer USB Connector
Disconnect the transducer and visually inspect the plug for deformation, contamination, damaged contacts, cracks, or looseness.

Do not straighten damaged contacts or perform unauthorized connector repair.

Expected outcome: The connector is clean and mechanically intact. Visible connector damage requires removal from service.

### 4. Inspect the Mobile Device USB Port
Using adequate lighting, inspect the mobile device port for debris, physical damage, looseness, or signs that the connector cannot seat fully.

Do not insert metal tools into the port or perform invasive cleaning.

Expected outcome: The port is clean, intact, and accepts the connector securely.

### 5. Inspect Adapters and Accessories
If an approved adapter, dock, strain-relief accessory, or other external connection component is used, inspect it independently.

Remove unnecessary connection points when the approved configuration allows.

Expected outcome: Each connection component is secure. If removing or replacing a defective external accessory restores stability, verify operation and troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Reconnect Without Cable Stress
Reconnect the transducer fully and arrange the mobile device and cable so their weight does not pull sideways on the USB connection.

Confirm that routine positioning does not place excessive strain on the connector.

Expected outcome: The connector stays seated during normal use. If proper cable positioning resolves the problem, verify scanning stability before returning the system to service.

### 7. Perform a Known-Good Comparison
When available, test the transducer on another compatible mobile device or test another known-good Lumify transducer on the original device.

Expected outcome: The comparison identifies whether the looseness or disconnection follows the transducer connector or the mobile device port.

### 8. Check Connection Stability During Normal Movement
With the system off the patient, perform a test scan and move the cable only through normal operating positions.

Do not intentionally stress or sharply bend the connector.

Expected outcome: The system maintains uninterrupted transducer detection and imaging. Any normal movement that repeatedly causes disconnects indicates an unreliable connection.

### 9. Verify Full System Operation
After any corrective action, confirm the Lumify application recognizes the transducer, scanning remains stable, and no connection interruption occurs through normal handling.

Expected outcome: The connection remains stable throughout functional testing. Troubleshooting can stop after applicable return-to-service checks.

### 10. Escalate Mechanical Connection Damage
If physical looseness, repeated disconnection, or connector damage remains, stop troubleshooting and remove the affected component from service.

Expected outcome: Damaged connection hardware is not returned to patient use.

## If the Problem Persists
External connection causes have been evaluated. Remaining possibilities include internal cable damage, failure within the transducer connector assembly, mobile device port damage, or another hardware interface fault.

Remove the affected transducer or mobile device from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Philips documentation and approved test equipment. Do not perform internal connector repair or deep disassembly unless specifically authorized and qualified.

Return to service only after the connection remains secure during normal handling and full imaging operation has been verified. Stopping use of an intermittent connector before total failure is appropriate troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
Support the mobile device and transducer cable so the USB connection is not carrying the weight or movement of the scanning setup.

## Final Thought
Intermittent connectors should be treated as reliability problems, not inconveniences. Protect the patient, inspect the entire external connection path, isolate the faulty side, and remove damaged hardware from service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
