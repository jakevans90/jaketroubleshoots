## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting loss of AC power, power-fail alarm problems, battery-related concerns, loose connections, outlet issues, or unexpected incubator shutdown.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Infant and Restore Thermal Support Immediately

An unexpected shutdown can quickly compromise the incubator's thermal environment. Move the infant to another verified warming device or establish appropriate alternate thermal support before troubleshooting.

Do not repeatedly power-cycle the incubator while an infant depends on it.

**Expected outcome:** The infant remains safely supported and the affected incubator is removed from clinical dependence.

### 2. Clarify the Reported Power Event

Determine whether the unit shut down completely, restarted, lost power when moved, generated a power-fail alarm, or failed to respond correctly during loss of AC power.

Ask whether the event occurred during a facility outage, plug movement, transport, or normal stationary use.

**Expected outcome:** The exact power-related symptom and circumstances are documented.

### 3. Inspect the Power Cord and Plug

Inspect the full accessible power cord, plug, strain relief, and connection at the incubator for cuts, crushed areas, looseness, heat damage, bent contacts, or other visible defects.

Do not use a visibly damaged power cord.

**Expected outcome:** The cord and plug are intact and securely connected, or a damaged external power component is identified and removed from use.

### 4. Verify the AC Outlet

Confirm that the wall receptacle or approved power source is functional using an appropriate outlet tester or known-good equipment according to facility practice.

If the incubator was connected through a power strip or extension arrangement, eliminate unauthorized intermediate connections.

**Expected outcome:** A verified AC source is available and facility power is ruled in or out as the cause.

### 5. Test the Incubator on a Known-Good AC Source

Connect the incubator directly to a verified appropriate AC receptacle.

Observe whether it powers normally and remains operating without interruption.

**Expected outcome:** Stable operation on a known-good source indicates the original outlet or external power path may have caused the problem. If stable, resolve the infrastructure issue and continue verification.

### 6. Check for Intermittency at External Connections

With the device unloaded and under controlled conditions, gently inspect the cord and accessible connection points for looseness while observing operation.

Do not aggressively flex damaged cords or recreate unsafe arcing conditions.

**Expected outcome:** The device remains powered without interruption during normal handling of intact external connections.

### 7. Verify Power-Fail Alarm Behavior

Using an approved functional test and without a patient, verify the incubator's response to loss of AC power as permitted by facility procedure and applicable manufacturer documentation.

Confirm whether the expected power-fail indication or alarm occurs.

**Expected outcome:** Power-loss annunciation operates as intended. If it does not, the device remains out of service.

### 8. Evaluate Battery-Related Symptoms Externally

If the reported problem references a battery, review the observed battery status or related indication and determine whether the complaint is poor backup operation, inability to support the intended alarm function, or another power-related symptom.

Do not open battery packs, bypass protective circuits, or attempt unauthorized internal battery testing.

**Expected outcome:** External battery indications and behavior are documented sufficiently to determine whether further service evaluation is required.

### 9. Check for Signs of Electrical or Thermal Damage

Inspect for unusual odor, discoloration, melted plastic, excessive heat, sparking evidence, or fluid intrusion around accessible power areas.

If any are present, disconnect the device from AC and stop troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** No signs of unsafe electrical damage are present, or the unit is immediately isolated for repair.

### 10. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correcting any external power issue, verify stable startup and operation on AC, applicable power-status indicators, power-fail alarm behavior, and any battery-related function required by the manufacturer's design and facility procedure.

Complete applicable electrical safety testing before return to service.

**Expected outcome:** The incubator operates reliably with no unexpected shutdown and all relevant power-loss alarms or indicators function correctly.

### 11. Escalate Recurrent Shutdown or Power-Fail Problems

If the incubator shuts down again on a verified AC source, does not provide the expected power-fail alarm, or exhibits unreliable battery-related behavior, stop troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The device remains removed from service for qualified bench evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External AC supply, wall receptacle, power cord, connections, and obvious environmental causes have been ruled out.

The remaining issue may involve the internal power supply, battery or charging system where applicable, power switching, alarm circuitry, internal wiring, or another service-level electrical condition.

The device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved electrical test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Tested for power stability, applicable backup or power-fail functions, alarm performance, thermal operation, and electrical safety before return to service.

A device that has unexpectedly shut down should not be returned to patient care until the cause has been corrected or the failure has been shown not to recur under appropriate testing.

## Clinical Use Tip

After any unexpected incubator shutdown, prioritize the infant's thermal support first and treat the incubator as unreliable until power stability is verified.

## Final Thought

Maintain infant thermal support first, verify the AC source and external power path before assuming internal failure, test power-fail functions carefully, escalate recurrent shutdowns, and document the event and verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
