## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting humidity-system problems involving water supply, reservoir installation, filling, connections, setup, or suspected humidifier heating failure.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Infant and Maintain Required Humidity or Thermal Support

If prescribed incubator humidity cannot be maintained, follow the clinical plan for the infant and use another verified incubator if continued controlled humidity is required.

Do not troubleshoot the humidification system while an infant depends on it.

**Expected outcome:** The infant has appropriate alternate support and the affected incubator can be safely tested.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Humidity Complaint

Determine whether the reservoir is simply empty, will not accept water, leaks, fails to seat, humidity does not increase, or the system appears not to heat.

Review any displayed humidity indication or alarm without assuming the heater itself has failed.

**Expected outcome:** The specific failure mode is identified and documented.

### 3. Verify Reservoir Water Level and Correct Supply

Inspect the reservoir externally and confirm that it contains the appropriate facility-approved water supply for the device.

Do not substitute unapproved fluids or additives.

**Expected outcome:** The reservoir contains an appropriate water supply at a usable level, or an empty-reservoir condition is identified and corrected.

### 4. Inspect and Reseat the Reservoir

Remove and reinstall the reservoir using normal user-accessible handling. Inspect it for cracks, deformation, contamination, damaged mating surfaces, or visible obstruction.

Confirm that it seats fully and securely.

**Expected outcome:** The reservoir is intact, correctly installed, and able to interface normally with the incubator.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Fill and Connection Areas

Check accessible water-fill ports, interfaces, tubing, or connectors for kinks, blockage, improper assembly, or signs of leakage.

Do not probe deeply into internal water pathways or disassemble the humidifier assembly.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible water pathways are unobstructed and properly connected with no visible leaks.

### 6. Verify Humidity Controls and Configuration

Confirm that humidity is enabled when appropriate and that the requested operating mode or control setting has not been inadvertently disabled or changed.

Do not enter unauthorized service menus or alter protected calibration values.

**Expected outcome:** The humidity function is enabled and configured for normal operation.

### 7. Observe Humidity Response During Unloaded Operation

With the device empty of patients and correctly filled, operate the incubator and observe whether the humidity indication responds over time and whether any abnormal odor, heat, leakage, or alarm occurs.

Do not touch internal heater surfaces.

**Expected outcome:** Humidity begins responding appropriately without leakage, overheating, or persistent alarms. If normal operation is restored, proceed to final verification.

### 8. Check for External Evidence of a Heater Problem

If humidity does not respond despite correct filling, installation, and settings, inspect only for external signs such as persistent lack of humidity response, abnormal odor, visible damage, or repeated alarm behavior.

Do not bypass heater protections or apply power directly to components.

**Expected outcome:** Either the humidification system functions normally, or sufficient evidence exists to escalate for service-level evaluation.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify reservoir retention, absence of leakage, humidity response, temperature regulation, and applicable alarms using approved facility procedures.

**Expected outcome:** Humidification operates reliably and the incubator completes required return-to-service verification.

### 10. Escalate Unresolved Humidifier Problems

If the reservoir will not fill or interface correctly, leakage persists, or humidity fails to respond after external checks, remove the unit from service.

**Expected outcome:** The incubator is referred for qualified bench evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Water availability, reservoir installation, accessible connections, and humidity controls have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve the humidifier heater, water-level sensing, internal fluid path, control electronics, temperature sensing, or another service-level condition.

The device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Tested for humidity performance, leakage, temperature control, alarms, and electrical safety before return to service.

Knowing when not to bypass a humidity or heater safety function is an important part of proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

If controlled humidity is clinically required, provide another verified incubator rather than continuing care with uncertain humidification performance.

## Final Thought

Maintain the infant's required environment, verify the water supply and reservoir interface before suspecting the heater, avoid bypassing safety protections, confirm performance after correction, and escalate unresolved faults appropriately.
