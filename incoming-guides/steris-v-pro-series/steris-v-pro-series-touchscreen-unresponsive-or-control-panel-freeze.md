## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting frozen displays, missed touch input, delayed controls, contamination, accessory interference, startup problems, or suspected control-system failure.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Ensure Safe Sterilizer Operation

Do not rely on an unresponsive control panel during an active cycle.

Do not attempt to open the chamber or interrupt power solely because the screen is frozen.

Observe physical indicators, audible alarms, and cycle status when available.

Prevent staff from starting additional loads.

Redirect urgent processing to another verified sterilizer.

Treat the current load as unavailable until cycle completion is confirmed by a valid record.

**Expected outcome:** Staff and loads remain protected while the sterilizer state is determined.

### 2. Determine Whether a Cycle Is Active

Before touching power controls:

Listen for normal operating sounds.

Observe status lamps, printers, remote indicators, or tracking-system status when available.

Check whether the door is locked.

Ask staff when the cycle started and what phase was displayed before the freeze.

Do not assume the sterilizer has stopped because the screen is static.

**Expected outcome:** The unit’s likely operating state is identified without disrupting a potentially active sterilization process.

### 3. Confirm the Exact Interface Failure

Determine whether:

- The display is blank.
- The display is visible but touch input is ignored.
- Only part of the screen is unresponsive.
- Controls respond slowly.
- The image is frozen on one screen.
- Physical buttons or peripheral controls still respond.
- The issue occurs only while wearing certain gloves or using a stylus.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is narrowed to display power, touch response, software freeze, or user-interface conditions.

### 4. Inspect and Clean the Touchscreen Surface

When the unit is idle and safe:

Inspect for moisture, cleaning residue, adhesive, tape, labels, gloves, protective film, or physical damage.

Clean only with an approved method and material.

Dry the screen completely.

Remove any unauthorized screen cover.

Do not spray liquid directly onto the panel.

**Expected outcome:** The screen is clean, dry, unobstructed, and undamaged. If touch response returns, verify all screen areas before stopping.

### 5. Check for Incorrect Touch Technique or Accessory Interference

Verify operation with normal direct touch.

Test using a clean, dry finger as permitted.

Remove thick gloves only when infection-control and safety practices allow.

Avoid leaning objects, trays, paperwork, or cables against the screen.

Confirm no cleaning cloth or accessory is continuously activating the panel.

Compare response in several operator-accessible areas.

**Expected outcome:** The panel responds consistently to deliberate touch. If an external obstruction caused the issue, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 6. Check External Power and Startup Status

For a blank or unstable display:

Confirm the sterilizer has facility power.

Inspect the approved power connection and disconnect status.

Check whether other panel indicators are illuminated.

Determine whether the unit is still starting, shutting down, or recovering from a power interruption.

Do not cycle facility breakers repeatedly.

**Expected outcome:** Stable power is present and the control panel completes normal initialization.

### 7. Allow the Active Cycle or Recovery to Finish

If evidence indicates the cycle remains active:

Do not restart or remove power.

Maintain the unit out of further use.

Wait for normal cycle completion or automatic recovery.

Confirm the door remains locked as expected.

Review the final cycle record after the unit reaches a safe state.

**Expected outcome:** The cycle ends safely and the load status can be determined before any restart is attempted.

### 8. Perform a Controlled Restart When Safe

Only restart when no cycle or recovery is active.

Remove or segregate the load.

Use the normal shutdown control if available.

If the interface cannot perform a normal shutdown, follow approved facility and manufacturer procedures for powering down an idle unit.

Allow complete shutdown before restoring power.

Observe all startup screens and record any error.

**Expected outcome:** The panel initializes, accepts input, and remains stable. If normal operation returns, continue with full functional verification.

### 9. Verify Touchscreen Function Across the Interface

After restart:

Test several operator-accessible screen areas.

Open and close common menus.

Enter and delete a nonclinical test value when appropriate.

Confirm controls do not activate without touch.

Verify required prompts, cycle selection, and navigation work normally.

Do not enter protected service menus.

**Expected outcome:** Touch input is accurate, repeatable, and stable throughout normal operator functions.

### 10. Check Connected External Devices

Disconnect only approved nonessential peripherals while the unit is idle.

Inspect barcode scanners, USB devices, printers, network cables, and tracking interfaces for damage or abnormal behavior.

Remove a recently connected nonessential peripheral and restart the idle sterilizer when appropriate.

Reconnect devices one at a time.

Do not disconnect safety-critical internal connections.

**Expected outcome:** The interface remains stable. If one external peripheral consistently causes freezing, remove that peripheral from use and document the finding.

### 11. Perform Final Functional Verification

Before return to service:

Confirm stable display brightness and image.

Verify touch response throughout normal controls.

Start an approved test cycle.

Confirm the panel updates through each phase.

Verify alarms, prompts, door status, and final cycle result display correctly.

Confirm required print or electronic records are produced.

**Expected outcome:** The control panel remains responsive throughout a complete verified cycle.

## If the Problem Persists

If cleaning, power verification, removal of external interference, safe restart, and peripheral checks do not restore stable control-panel operation, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve the touchscreen assembly, display electronics, control computer, software, communication link, protected configuration, or internal power supply.

The sterilizer should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for qualified repair or field evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate STERIS documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not continue clinical operation with intermittent controls, use unauthorized calibration screens, or disassemble the control panel. Complete full operational and process verification after repair.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A completed-looking screen is not proof of sterilization; verify the official cycle record before releasing any load affected by a panel freeze.

## Final Thought

Determine whether the sterilizer is still processing before restarting it. Rule out screen contamination, external pressure, power, startup state, and peripheral interference, then escalate persistent or intermittent controls and clearly document the final verification.

That is successful troubleshooting.
