## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoots wireless fetal transducer communication caused by pairing, power, charging, range, interference, accessory, or infrastructure problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Protect the Patient and Restore Reliable Monitoring
If wireless transducers are not communicating reliably, switch to another verified monitoring method or compatible wired monitoring arrangement when clinically appropriate before troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: Continuous fetal monitoring is maintained independently of the failed wireless connection.

### 2. Confirm the Wireless Failure
Determine whether the telemetry transducer will not pair, pairs but disconnects, fails only at distance, affects one transducer, or affects multiple wireless accessories.

Expected outcome: The failure is characterized as pairing, individual accessory, range, or broader infrastructure behavior.

### 3. Verify the Avalon FM20 Is Operating Normally
Confirm the monitor is stable, powered normally, and capable of recognizing other connected monitoring accessories.

Expected outcome: General monitor failure is ruled out before focusing on wireless communication.

### 4. Check Wireless Transducer Power and Condition
Inspect the wireless transducer and associated charging components for physical damage, contamination, poor contact, or inadequate charge indication.

Expected outcome: The wireless accessory is powered and physically suitable for testing.

### 5. Verify Intended Pairing
Using only approved user-accessible pairing procedures, confirm the transducer is paired with the intended monitor or compatible wireless system and has not been associated with another nearby device.

Expected outcome: The correct transducer is associated with the intended monitoring system.

### 6. Test at Close Range
Test wireless operation with the transducer near the intended receiver or monitor location to minimize range and environmental effects.

Expected outcome: Stable communication at close range suggests the accessory and basic pairing are functional.

### 7. Evaluate Environment and Range
Check whether failures correlate with location, distance, room boundaries, nearby equipment, or movement. Compare behavior in a known-good clinical area if practical.

Expected outcome: A site-specific wireless or interference problem can be distinguished from a device-specific fault.

### 8. Compare With Known-Good Wireless Accessories
Test a known-good compatible telemetry transducer in the same location and, if practical, test the suspect transducer with another compatible verified system.

Expected outcome: The problem is isolated to the accessory, monitor/receiver, or wireless environment.

### 9. Perform Functional Verification
Verify stable pairing, continued signal reception during expected movement and range, correct channel identification, alarms related to signal loss, and restoration after a brief controlled interruption where approved.

Expected outcome: Wireless monitoring remains stable throughout the intended clinical use area. If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Wireless Failure
If multiple known-good wireless transducers fail with the same monitor or the issue appears infrastructure-related, remove the affected equipment from service as appropriate and escalate to the responsible Clinical Engineering, network, or Philips support pathway.

Expected outcome: Unreliable wireless monitoring is not returned to clinical use until the device or infrastructure issue is resolved.

## If the Problem Persists
Accessory charge, pairing, physical condition, distance, and basic environmental factors have been checked. Remaining causes may involve the wireless receiver, system configuration, radio-frequency environment, infrastructure, or internal communication hardware.

Remove affected equipment from service when device reliability is in question, label it Out of Service, and perform bench evaluation using appropriate Philips documentation and approved test equipment. Infrastructure concerns should be escalated to the responsible technical team. Repairs or protected configuration changes should be completed only by qualified personnel.

Complete wireless communication, alarm, range, channel-identification, and monitoring verification before return to service. Knowing when the problem has moved beyond external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
Confirm the complete wireless monitoring path before relying on telemetry during patient movement or transport within the care area.

Wireless failures should be isolated among the accessory, monitor, and environment before an internal failure is assumed, with dependable alternate monitoring maintained throughout the process.
