## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots absent or excessive humidification, heating problems, condensation, or heated-tube concerns involving the tank, tubing, settings, connections, or environment.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Therapy
If humidifier malfunction is affecting safe or tolerable therapy, provide another verified therapy setup or follow the clinical plan before removing the DreamStation 2 for testing.

Disconnect power before handling components when liquid leakage is present.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is maintained safely and liquid-related electrical risk is controlled.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Humidification Complaint
Determine whether there is no heat, excessive heat, no apparent water use, excessive condensation, water entering the tubing, a heated-tube problem, or a displayed humidification-related message.

**Expected outcome:** The exact symptom is identified and reproducible.

### 3. Inspect the Water Tank
Remove and inspect the humidifier tank for cracks, contamination, damaged accessible sealing surfaces, improper assembly, overfilling, or incorrect seating.

Check the surrounding device area for evidence of leakage.

**Expected outcome:** The tank is intact and properly seated with no evidence of uncontrolled liquid leakage. If replacing a damaged approved tank corrects the problem, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Verify Water and Setup
Confirm the tank contains the appropriate water and is assembled according to the approved clinical setup. Do not infer a humidifier failure solely from slow water consumption.

**Expected outcome:** The humidifier is correctly prepared for operation.

### 5. Inspect Standard or Heated Tubing
Inspect tubing for damage, loose connections, excessive condensation, kinks, or blocked airflow.

If heated tubing is used, inspect its accessible electrical/contact interface for contamination or physical damage without probing or modifying contacts.

**Expected outcome:** Tubing is intact, securely connected, and appropriately recognized or usable for the configured setup.

### 6. Verify Humidification Settings
Review accessible humidification and heated-tube settings for unintended changes. Confirm the configuration is appropriate for the connected accessories.

Do not alter prescribed therapy parameters while troubleshooting comfort settings.

**Expected outcome:** Humidification configuration matches the installed setup.

### 7. Consider Environmental Conditions
Evaluate room temperature, placement near HVAC outlets, very cool tubing, and other environmental conditions that may contribute to condensation or apparent heating problems.

Ensure the device is positioned appropriately and vents remain unobstructed.

**Expected outcome:** Environmental conditions are not creating the reported humidification symptom.

### 8. Use Known-Good External Components
If available, substitute a known-good compatible water tank or tubing component one at a time.

**Expected outcome:** Normal humidification returns if the external component was defective. Replace that component and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
Operate the device in a controlled non-patient test and confirm there is no leakage, abnormal odor, overheating, unexpected message, or recurrent humidification problem.

**Expected outcome:** Humidification functions normally with the approved setup. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Heating or Humidification Failure
If the tank, tubing, settings, connections, and environment have been ruled out but heating remains absent, excessive, or unreliable, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The unit remains out of service pending service-level evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external tank, tubing, configuration, and environmental causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include the humidifier heating system, temperature sensing, heated-tube interface, control electronics, software, or another internal service-level issue.

Remove the DreamStation 2 from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Philips documentation and approved test equipment. Internal heating-system repair or calibration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Verify therapy and humidification functions before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any humidifier leak that allows water into or around electrical portions of the DreamStation 2 requires removal from service until the device is evaluated.

## Final Thought

Humidification problems often begin with the water tank, tubing, settings, or environment. Protect the patient and electrical equipment from leaks, verify external causes first, and escalate persistent heating failures without unnecessary disassembly.
