## What This Guide Helps With
TRS components that do not power on, initialize normally, or become ready for use due to battery, connection, control, or external equipment problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Protect the Patient and Surgical Workflow
Do not troubleshoot an unreliable surgical power system while it is required for an active procedure. Remove the affected TRS component from the sterile field as appropriate and provide a verified backup system so the procedure can continue safely.

Inspect for obvious damage, contamination, fluid intrusion, overheating, unusual odor, or evidence that the equipment was dropped.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is supported by reliable equipment and the affected TRS component can be evaluated without affecting the procedure. Stop clinical troubleshooting if physical damage, fluid intrusion, overheating, or another unsafe condition is present.

### 2. Confirm the Exact No-Power or Startup Condition
Determine which component is affected and what staff observed. Establish whether the system is completely unresponsive, powers briefly and stops, or begins startup but does not become ready.

Ask whether the problem began after a battery change, accessory installation, cleaning, sterilization, storage, transport, or an equipment exchange.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is reproduced or clearly characterized sufficiently to determine whether it follows the handpiece, power source, accessory, or setup.

### 3. Inspect the Power Source
Verify that the correct TRS power source intended for the device configuration is installed correctly and fully seated. Inspect accessible contacts and mating surfaces for contamination, moisture, bent components, corrosion, or visible damage.

Do not use a power source that is swollen, cracked, excessively hot, leaking, or otherwise damaged.

**Expected outcome:** The power source is correctly installed and its accessible interfaces are clean, dry, and undamaged. If correcting installation restores normal operation, verify function and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Substitute a Known-Good Power Source
When permitted by the equipment configuration and facility procedure, install a known-good, compatible, adequately charged power source.

If the original power source works normally in another compatible unit, document that result rather than assuming the battery is defective.

**Expected outcome:** The TRS system powers on and becomes ready with the known-good power source, identifying the original power source or its charging history as the likely cause. If normal startup is restored, complete functional verification and stop.

### 5. Remove Nonessential Accessories
Disconnect or remove external accessories that are not required for a basic power-up check, following approved handling procedures. Inspect attachments and interfaces for incomplete seating, mechanical binding, contamination, or damage.

Attempt basic operation with the simplest valid configuration.

**Expected outcome:** The device starts normally without a problematic accessory, or the failure remains present with external accessories eliminated as the cause.

### 6. Check Accessible Controls and Mechanical Interfaces
Inspect power controls, triggers, selectors, safety mechanisms, and accessible mechanical interfaces for damage, sticking, obstruction, or incomplete movement. Confirm that controls are in a normal state for startup and have not been left between positions.

Do not force a control that does not move normally.

**Expected outcome:** Controls move normally and the system is not being prevented from starting by an obvious external control or mechanical condition.

### 7. Compare With a Known-Good TRS Setup
When available, compare the affected component with a known-good TRS setup using compatible accessories and power sources. Change only one item at a time so the failure can be isolated.

**Expected outcome:** The problem consistently follows one component, or the affected component remains unable to start despite known-good external conditions.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification
After any external cause is corrected, operate the system without a patient according to approved facility and manufacturer requirements. Confirm normal startup, control response, accessory engagement, and expected operation through the intended operating modes.

**Expected outcome:** The TRS system repeatedly powers on and operates normally. If the condition is reproducibly corrected, document the findings and return the equipment to service according to facility policy.

### 9. Escalate an Unresolved Startup Failure
If the system remains unresponsive or cannot complete normal startup with a verified power source and valid configuration, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** An unresolved or intermittent power/startup failure is removed from clinical service for controlled bench evaluation rather than returned to use.

## If the Problem Persists
Common external causes such as power-source condition, installation, accessories, control position, contamination, and setup have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve an internal electrical, control, mechanical, or power-distribution condition that requires service-level evaluation.

The affected device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate DePuy Synthes documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not proceed into internal board-level troubleshooting or deep disassembly without the appropriate manufacturer service information and authorization. After repair, complete required functional and safety testing before return to clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
Have a verified backup surgical power system available before removing an unreliable TRS component from an active procedure.

## Final Thought
Protect the patient first, then isolate the power source, accessories, controls, and other external causes before assuming an internal TRS failure. Verify the correction under controlled conditions, escalate unresolved faults appropriately, and document exactly what was found and tested.

That is successful troubleshooting.
