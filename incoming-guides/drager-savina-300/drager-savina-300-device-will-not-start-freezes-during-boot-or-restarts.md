## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots startup failure, incomplete booting, freezing, or unexpected restarts caused by external power, accessories, connections, configuration, or service-level faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ventilation
Do not troubleshoot a Savina 300 that is providing unreliable ventilation while a patient depends on it. Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or approved ventilation method before troubleshooting.

Inspect for smoke, unusual odor, excessive heat, fluid intrusion, or obvious physical damage. If any are present, disconnect AC power when safe and remove the ventilator from service.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is safely supported by alternate equipment and the ventilator can be evaluated without clinical dependence. If abnormal heat, odor, fluid intrusion, or damage is present, stop troubleshooting and remove the device from service.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Startup Complaint
Determine whether the ventilator:
- Shows no signs of power
- Begins booting but freezes
- Completes booting and then restarts
- Restarts only when running from battery
- Restarts when accessories are connected or controls are operated

Attempt a controlled startup without a patient connected and document the observed behavior.

**Expected outcome:** The failure pattern is reproducible or the unit starts normally. If the ventilator boots normally and passes appropriate operational checks repeatedly, continue through final verification before returning it to service.

### 3. Verify AC Power
Inspect the power cord, plug, strain relief, and accessible power connection for damage or looseness. Confirm the outlet is functional using appropriate test equipment or a known-good source.

Avoid relying only on other equipment plugged into the same area as proof of outlet integrity.

**Expected outcome:** Reliable AC power is available and the ventilator receives power without interruption. If correcting the outlet, cord connection, or external power source resolves the problem, proceed to final functional verification and stop further troubleshooting.

### 4. Check Power Cord and External Connections
Verify the power cord is fully seated and not intermittently disconnecting with normal movement. Disconnect nonessential external accessories, communication cables, and peripheral connections before restarting the ventilator.

Reconnect accessories individually if the unit then starts normally.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator starts consistently with essential connections only. If a specific external cable or accessory causes the restart or freeze, remove that item from use and verify the ventilator independently.

### 5. Compare AC and Battery Operation
With the device off-patient, compare startup behavior while connected to AC power with its behavior during an approved battery-operational check.

A unit that operates normally on AC but fails during battery operation requires battery-system evaluation rather than assuming a general startup failure.

**Expected outcome:** Startup behavior is consistent and stable under the tested power source. If the problem occurs only during battery operation, continue troubleshooting under the battery-specific failure category.

### 6. Inspect Controls for Stuck or Damaged Inputs
Inspect the rotary control, buttons, touchscreen or touch-sensitive controls, and front-panel surfaces for damage, contamination, moisture, or a control that appears continuously activated.

Do not disassemble the control panel.

**Expected outcome:** Controls are intact and do not appear to interfere with startup. If cleaning an externally contaminated control according to approved procedures restores normal operation, perform final verification.

### 7. Observe the Startup Sequence
Restart the ventilator under controlled conditions and note where the boot process stops, whether alarms or messages appear, and whether the restart occurs at a repeatable point.

Record exact displayed messages without attempting unauthorized service-menu changes.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator completes startup and reaches a stable ready condition. If it repeatedly freezes or restarts at the same stage despite verified power and external connections, escalation is appropriate.

### 8. Perform Off-Patient Functional Verification
If the ventilator successfully starts, connect an appropriate test lung and verify:
- Stable operation
- Control responsiveness
- Alarm annunciation
- Gas delivery
- Display operation
- No unexpected restart during an extended functional check

Use approved test equipment and applicable manufacturer procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator remains continuously operational and performs normally throughout the functional check. If so, troubleshooting can stop and normal return-to-service documentation may be completed.

### 9. Remove From Service if Startup Remains Unreliable
A ventilator that intermittently freezes, fails startup, or restarts without an identified and corrected external cause is not reliable for patient support.

**Expected outcome:** The unit is either verified stable after correction or removed from clinical use for further evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external power, connection, accessory, and control causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities may include the internal power system, battery interface, control electronics, software, internal communication, or another service-level fault.

The ventilator should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Drager service documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not perform board-level troubleshooting or unauthorized configuration changes. Following any repair, complete applicable operational, alarm, electrical-safety, and ventilator performance testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never leave a patient dependent on a ventilator that has demonstrated an unexplained restart, even if it subsequently boots normally.

## Final Thought

Reliable startup is essential for a ventilator. Protect the patient first, verify external power and connections before assuming an internal fault, demonstrate stable operation after correction, escalate unresolved failures, and document the complaint, cause, and resolution clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
