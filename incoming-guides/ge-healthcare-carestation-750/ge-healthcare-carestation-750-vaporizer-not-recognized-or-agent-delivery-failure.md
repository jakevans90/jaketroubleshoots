## What This Guide Helps With
A vaporizer is not recognized or agent delivery is unavailable because of installation, locking, compatibility, filling, configuration, or interface problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Anesthetic Delivery
Do not troubleshoot an unreliable vaporizer or agent-delivery system while a patient depends on it.

If the problem occurs during a case, maintain anesthesia using an appropriate verified alternative and follow clinical escalation procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The patient continues to receive appropriate anesthesia without reliance on the questionable vaporizer.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Condition
Determine whether the issue is:

- Vaporizer not recognized
- Vaporizer cannot be enabled
- Agent concentration unavailable
- Expected agent not detected
- Agent delivery appears inconsistent
- Vaporizer installation or interlock problem

Record the exact behavior before disturbing the setup.

**Expected outcome:** The specific failure mode is identified and reproducible.

### 3. Verify Correct Vaporizer Installation
Confirm the vaporizer is compatible with the Carestation 750 configuration and is installed in the proper mounting position.

Check that it is fully seated and locked according to normal approved operation.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer is correctly mounted and secured. If recognition returns after proper installation, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Inspect the Mounting Interface
Remove the vaporizer only when safe and authorized for routine handling.

Inspect accessible mounting surfaces, locking features, and connection interfaces for contamination, obstruction, visible damage, or incomplete engagement.

Do not open or repair the vaporizer internally.

**Expected outcome:** The mounting interface is clean, unobstructed, and physically intact.

### 5. Verify Vaporizer Controls and Filling Condition
Confirm the vaporizer control is in the expected position and that the unit has an appropriate agent supply for testing.

Look for obvious external evidence of incorrect filling, leakage, physical damage, or a cap/closure problem.

Do not perform unauthorized draining, internal adjustment, or calibration.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer is externally intact, adequately filled for testing, and properly configured for use.

### 6. Check for Interlock or Multiple-Vaporizer Conditions
If more than one vaporizer position is available, verify another installed vaporizer is not preventing activation through the machine's normal interlock behavior.

Ensure each installed vaporizer is securely mounted and in an appropriate state.

**Expected outcome:** No external installation or interlock condition prevents the intended vaporizer from operating.

### 7. Reinstall and Recheck Recognition
Reinstall the vaporizer carefully and allow the system to reassess it.

If recognition returns, verify that the correct agent identity and status are displayed where applicable.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer is recognized correctly. If so, continue with agent-delivery verification.

### 8. Compare With a Compatible Known-Good Vaporizer
When approved and available, install a compatible known-good vaporizer to determine whether the problem follows the vaporizer or remains with the machine.

**Expected outcome:** If the known-good vaporizer is recognized and operates normally, remove the original vaporizer from service for evaluation. If the problem remains at the same machine position, escalate system-side evaluation.

### 9. Verify Agent Delivery With Approved Test Equipment
Before return to service, use an appropriate calibrated gas analyzer and controlled test setup to verify agent delivery and monitoring.

Do not rely solely on dial position or machine recognition.

**Expected outcome:** Agent delivery and measurement respond appropriately under test conditions. Troubleshooting can stop after checkout passes.

### 10. Escalate Unresolved Recognition or Delivery Problems
If recognition or reliable agent delivery cannot be restored through installation, interface, interlock, and approved substitution checks, stop troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The affected vaporizer and/or Carestation 750 is removed from service for qualified evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists
Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining categories may include the vaporizer itself, mounting/interface detection, internal agent-delivery control, system communications, or another service-level problem.

The affected equipment should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare and vaporizer manufacturer documentation with approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Agent concentration should be independently verified after repair and before clinical use.

Knowing when not to trust agent delivery and when to escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
Never assume an installed or recognized vaporizer is delivering the intended anesthetic concentration without appropriate verification.

## Final Thought
Vaporizer problems should be approached as installation and interface issues before assuming an internal failure. Maintain alternate anesthesia, inspect external mounting and control conditions, independently verify agent delivery, and remove questionable equipment from service when reliability cannot be proven.

That is successful troubleshooting.
