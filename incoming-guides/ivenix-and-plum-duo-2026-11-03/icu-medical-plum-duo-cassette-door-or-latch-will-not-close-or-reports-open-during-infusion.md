## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots door and latch problems caused by cassette seating, tubing interference, contamination, physical damage, disposable deformation, or door-sensing faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Stop Using an Unsecured Cassette
A cassette door or latch that will not remain securely closed can compromise infusion reliability. Transfer therapy to another verified pump before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is no longer dependent on the affected pump.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Door Condition
Determine whether the door physically will not close, will not latch, reopens, feels obstructed, or closes normally but the pump reports it as open. Record any displayed message.

**Expected outcome:** Mechanical closure and electronic door detection are distinguished from each other.

### 3. Inspect the Cassette
Remove the administration set and inspect the cassette for warping, swelling, cracked components, contamination, or other deformation.

**Expected outcome:** The cassette is intact and correctly shaped.

If a damaged cassette is found and a replacement resolves the issue, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 4. Inspect Tubing Placement
Verify tubing is not crossing the door edge, latch, hinge area, cassette pocket, or any external interface where it could prevent closure.

**Expected outcome:** No tubing interferes with door movement.

### 5. Inspect the Door and Latch Externally
With the cassette removed, visually inspect the door, latch, hinge area, and accessible cassette compartment for debris, dried residue, damage, or loose external parts. Do not disassemble the mechanism beyond approved external access.

**Expected outcome:** The door moves freely and no visible obstruction is present.

If debris can be removed using approved cleaning methods and normal closure returns, repeat testing with a cassette.

### 6. Test Door Operation Without Forcing It
Operate the door gently according to normal use. Never force a resistant latch.

**Expected outcome:** The door opens and closes smoothly without abnormal resistance.

Persistent mechanical resistance with no disposable installed requires removal from service.

### 7. Install a Known-Good Cassette
Use a verified compatible administration set and confirm it seats fully before closing the door.

**Expected outcome:** The door closes and latches normally with the known-good cassette.

If the original cassette alone caused the issue, remove that disposable from use.

### 8. Verify Door-Open Detection
With a controlled test setup, confirm the pump correctly recognizes the door as closed and responds appropriately when it is opened.

**Expected outcome:** The displayed door state matches the physical door condition.

If the door is mechanically secure but still reports open, remove the pump from service.

### 9. Observe During Simulated Infusion
Run an appropriate bench infusion and monitor for intermittent door-open indications caused by vibration, movement, or latch instability.

**Expected outcome:** The door remains securely latched and no false open indication occurs.

### 10. Complete Functional Verification
Verify door operation, cassette recognition, both channels, alarms, and basic infusion function before return to service.

**Expected outcome:** The door and latch operate consistently and the device correctly detects their state.

## If the Problem Persists

If cassette condition, tubing placement, accessible debris, known-good disposables, and mechanical closure have been checked but the door will not latch or the pump incorrectly reports it as open, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible remaining categories include latch wear, door alignment, door-position sensing, internal mechanical damage, or another service-level issue.

The pump should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using manufacturer documentation and approved service procedures
- Repaired only by qualified personnel
- Fully tested for cassette retention, door detection, alarms, and infusion performance before clinical use

Do not force, bend, shim, or defeat the door or latch mechanism.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never begin or continue an infusion with a cassette door that is difficult to latch or intermittently reports open.

## Final Thought

Door and latch problems should be approached as both mechanical and sensing issues. Verify cassette seating and tubing interference first, avoid forcing the mechanism, and escalate any repeatable latch or door-detection failure before returning the pump to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
