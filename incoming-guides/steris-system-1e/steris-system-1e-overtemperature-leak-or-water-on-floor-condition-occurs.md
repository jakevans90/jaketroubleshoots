## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoots overheating, visible leakage, or water around the SYSTEM 1E while prioritizing electrical safety, containment, external plumbing, loading, and escalation.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Stop Use and Protect Staff Immediately
If water is actively leaking, the floor is wet, the unit is unusually hot, or an overtemperature condition is reported, stop the cycle when safe and remove the equipment from clinical use. Keep personnel away from wet electrical areas.

**Expected outcome:** Exposure to electrical, slip, thermal, and chemical hazards is minimized. Active unsafe conditions are contained before troubleshooting continues.

### 2. Preserve the Load as Potentially Incompletely Processed
Do not release a load from an interrupted or abnormal cycle. Follow facility procedure for handling and reprocessing affected devices.

**Expected outcome:** No questionable load enters patient use.

### 3. Determine the Source and Timing
Ask whether the leak occurred during filling, processing, draining, or while idle. Determine whether water came from the door area, an external hose, a facility connection, a drain backup, or an unknown location. Record any overtemperature or leak message.

**Expected outcome:** The event is characterized without unnecessary operation of the equipment.

### 4. Inspect the Area Before Re-Energizing
With the equipment in a safe state, inspect the power cord, floor, external hoses, fittings, drain connection, door area, and nearby plumbing. Do not energize equipment standing in water or showing signs of electrical contamination.

**Expected outcome:** No immediate electrical hazard is present. If water has contacted electrical components or the source cannot be safely isolated, leave the unit out of service.

### 5. Check External Water Connections
Inspect accessible water supply hoses, fittings, valves, and connections for looseness, cracks, damage, or obvious leakage.

**Expected outcome:** External supply connections are dry and secure. If an authorized external connection correction eliminates the leak, continue with controlled verification.

### 6. Check the Drain Path
Inspect accessible drain hoses and the facility drain for kinks, blockage, backup, or overflow. Determine whether another nearby fixture indicates a facility drainage problem.

**Expected outcome:** Drainage is unobstructed. If a facility drain problem caused the water-on-floor event, correct the infrastructure issue before testing the reprocessor.

### 7. Inspect Door, Seal, and Load Position
Check for debris, accessories, tubing, or load components interfering with door closure or sealing. Do not adjust or dismantle the door or seal mechanism without approved service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The door closes without obstruction and no visible damage is present. If correcting load placement resolves leakage, proceed with cautious verification.

### 8. Assess Ventilation and Heat Conditions
For an overtemperature complaint, inspect accessible ventilation openings for blockage and confirm the surrounding environment is not unusually hot. Look for abnormal fan noise, odor, discoloration, or excessive external heat.

**Expected outcome:** Ventilation is unobstructed and there are no signs of continuing overheating. Persistent overheating requires removal from service.

### 9. Perform Controlled Functional Verification Only if Safe
If the source was an external, clearly corrected condition and there is no electrical or chemical hazard, perform an approved test cycle while observing for leaks, abnormal temperature, drainage problems, and alarms.

**Expected outcome:** The cycle completes with no leakage, overheating, abnormal odor, or water accumulation. Troubleshooting can stop after required return-to-service checks.

### 10. Escalate Any Unexplained or Recurrent Leak or Overtemperature Condition
Do not continue operating a SYSTEM 1E that repeatedly leaks, overheats, or places water near electrical components.

**Expected outcome:** The equipment remains removed from service until qualified evaluation and corrective action are completed.

## If the Problem Persists
External supply hoses, drain conditions, door obstruction, loading, ventilation, and facility plumbing have been evaluated. The remaining problem may involve internal plumbing, pumps, valves, seals, temperature regulation, heating components, internal drainage, leak detection, or control circuitry.

Remove the SYSTEM 1E from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate STERIS documentation and approved test equipment. Internal leak repair, heating-system work, calibration, or electrical repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify leak-free operation, proper fill and drain behavior, normal process temperature control, alarms, electrical safety as appropriate, and complete cycle performance.

Stopping operation when water, heat, or electrical safety becomes uncertain is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
A water-on-floor condition near powered equipment should be treated as both a processing problem and an electrical and slip hazard until the source is identified.

## Final Thought
Treat heat and water events as safety issues first, identify external plumbing and loading causes before assuming internal failure, verify the correction under controlled conditions, and escalate immediately when leakage or overheating remains unexplained.

That is successful troubleshooting.
