## What This Guide Helps With

Helps isolate blurred images caused by focus, coupler, telescope seating, contamination, fogging, optical damage, or camera-path problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Confirm Adequate Visualization

Do not continue using a blurred image when procedural visualization is inadequate. Exchange to a verified scope or camera setup before troubleshooting if the system is in clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** The clinical team has a clear and reliable image.

### 2. Confirm the Nature of the Blur

Determine whether the entire image is blurred, only the edges are affected, focus changes with movement, or the image cannot be focused at all.

Confirm whether the issue occurs with one telescope or all tested telescopes.

**Expected outcome:** The blur is characterized well enough to guide optical troubleshooting.

If the image becomes clear and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 3. Inspect Accessible Optical Surfaces

Inspect the camera window, coupler lenses, and telescope eyepiece or accessible surfaces for fingerprints, residue, fogging, moisture, or debris.

Clean only according to approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** Optical surfaces are clean and free of visible contamination.

If cleaning restores image clarity, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Verify Telescope and Coupler Seating

Confirm the telescope is fully seated and locked into the coupler and that the coupler is properly attached to the camera head.

Inspect locking mechanisms for looseness or visible damage.

**Expected outcome:** The optical components are securely connected without excessive play.

If reseating restores a sharp image, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 5. Check Focus and Zoom Controls

Operate external focus or zoom controls through their normal range while viewing an appropriate test target.

Do not force a control that binds or stops unexpectedly.

**Expected outcome:** Focus and zoom move smoothly and a sharp image can be obtained.

If normal adjustment restores image clarity, verify operation throughout the intended range.

### 6. Check for Fogging or Temperature-Related Changes

Observe whether the image is initially clear and then becomes hazy or whether condensation is visible on optical surfaces.

Allow components to reach appropriate conditions using approved clinical processes rather than improvised heating.

**Expected outcome:** Optical surfaces remain clear without recurrent fogging.

If resolving condensation restores the image, proceed to final verification.

### 7. Substitute Known-Good Optical Components

When available, substitute a known-good compatible telescope, coupler, or camera head one component at a time.

**Expected outcome:** The comparison identifies whether blur follows the telescope, coupler, camera head, or remains with the system.

If one component is isolated, remove it from service and replace it.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify image sharpness across the expected focus range, secure telescope connection, stable zoom operation if applicable, and clear images in required visualization modes.

**Expected outcome:** The image remains sharp, stable, and suitable for intended clinical use.

If these checks pass, the system may be returned to service.

## If the Problem Persists

External optical surfaces, component seating, focus controls, fogging, and known-good substitutions have been checked.

Remove the affected camera head, coupler, telescope, or system from service as appropriate, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Persistent blur may involve internal optical alignment, mechanical damage, camera imaging hardware, or another service-level fault.

Use appropriate Karl Storz documentation and approved test equipment. Do not disassemble optical assemblies beyond authorized maintenance.

Return equipment to service only after image quality has been verified.

## Clinical Use Tip

A technically functioning camera system is not clinically acceptable if the optical path cannot provide a consistently sharp surgical image.

## Final Thought

Start blurred-image troubleshooting with the optical path, not internal electronics. Verify cleanliness, seating, focus, and known-good components before escalating, and document the final image-quality check.
