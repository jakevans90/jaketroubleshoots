## What This Guide Helps With

Helps isolate white-balance failures or unexpected color shifts caused by lighting, optics, camera connections, contamination, controls, or component problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Confirm Image Reliability

Do not rely on an image with abnormal color representation when tissue identification could be affected. Transition to a verified alternate camera system if necessary.

**Expected outcome:** Clinical visualization remains reliable while the affected equipment is evaluated.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Color Problem

Determine whether white balance fails completely, produces an obvious color cast, or whether color changes only when switching between visualization modes.

Confirm whether the problem occurs with all telescopes or only one setup.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is identified as a white-balance failure, optical issue, or mode-specific color concern.

If normal color is restored and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 3. Inspect the Optical Path

Inspect the telescope, coupler, camera window, and accessible optical surfaces for contamination, fogging, residue, scratches, or poor seating.

Ensure components are cleaned according to approved departmental procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The optical path is clean, secure, and produces a clear image.

If cleaning or reseating restores normal color, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Verify Light-Source and Light-Cable Operation

Confirm the Rubina light source is operating normally and the light cable is securely connected.

Inspect the light cable for obvious damage or degraded connection quality.

**Expected outcome:** Illumination is stable and appropriate for normal camera operation.

If restoring stable illumination resolves white balance, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 5. Perform White Balance Under Appropriate Conditions

Using normal approved system controls, repeat white balance with the camera, scope, and light source assembled for intended use.

Ensure the target is evenly illuminated and the optical path is not obstructed.

**Expected outcome:** White balance completes normally and produces consistent natural color in standard imaging.

If white balance completes and color remains stable, proceed to final verification.

### 6. Compare Visualization Modes

Switch only through normal user-accessible visualization modes and observe whether color changes are expected mode behavior or an abnormal persistent shift.

Return to standard imaging and verify that normal color returns.

**Expected outcome:** Standard imaging displays consistent color, and mode changes do not cause persistent unintended color distortion.

If the issue was caused by an incorrect mode selection, normal operation can be confirmed and troubleshooting can stop.

### 7. Substitute Known-Good External Components

When available, test with a verified compatible telescope, coupler, camera head, or light cable one component at a time.

**Expected outcome:** The substitution identifies whether the color or white-balance problem follows an external component.

If one component causes the problem, remove it from service and replace it with a verified component.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Repeat white balance and verify image clarity, natural color, mode switching, and stable operation for the intended clinical setup.

**Expected outcome:** White balance completes successfully and color remains appropriate in each required visualization mode.

If these checks pass, the system may be returned to service.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as contamination, optics, illumination, camera connections, and mode selection have been ruled out.

Remove the system from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Persistent color or white-balance problems may involve camera imaging electronics, processing, firmware, internal communication, or configuration.

Use appropriate Karl Storz documentation and approved test equipment. Service-level calibration or configuration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Do not return the system to clinical use until image color and all required visualization modes have been verified.

## Clinical Use Tip

Verify white balance and acceptable color reproduction before the scope is introduced when accurate tissue visualization is clinically important.
