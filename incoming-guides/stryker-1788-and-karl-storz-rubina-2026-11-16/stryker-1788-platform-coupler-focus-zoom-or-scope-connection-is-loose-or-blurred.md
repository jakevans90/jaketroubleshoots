## What This Guide Helps With

Blurred or unstable images caused by coupler attachment, scope connection, focus or zoom position, contamination, optical damage, or incompatible components.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Usable Visualization

If focus cannot be maintained during a procedure, notify the clinical team. Do not repeatedly manipulate a loose scope or coupler while visualization is clinically required. Exchange the affected component or use a verified backup setup.

**Expected outcome:** Stable visualization is available before technical troubleshooting proceeds.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Optical Complaint

Determine whether the image is:

- Continuously blurred
- Sharp only at certain positions
- Losing focus when the scope is moved
- Changing unexpectedly with zoom adjustment
- Associated with a physically loose scope connection

**Expected outcome:** The optical failure pattern is clearly identified.

### 3. Inspect the Coupler and Scope Connection

With the system removed from patient use, verify that the coupler and scope are correctly seated and secured using their normal attachment method.

Inspect for damaged locking features, loose components, or evidence that the scope can move independently after attachment.

Do not force damaged components together.

**Expected outcome:** The scope remains securely attached. If proper seating restores a stable image, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 4. Inspect Accessible Optical Surfaces

Check the camera-side, coupler, and scope optical surfaces that are intended to be externally inspected for:

- Smudges
- Residue
- Moisture
- Fogging
- Debris
- Visible scratches or damage

Use approved cleaning practices only.

**Expected outcome:** Optical surfaces are clean and clear.

### 5. Verify Focus and Zoom Through Normal Controls

Adjust focus and zoom through their intended normal range while viewing a suitable test target.

Check whether focus can be achieved and maintained without excessive mechanical play.

**Expected outcome:** A sharp image can be obtained and remains stable. If normal adjustment restores image quality, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 6. Substitute a Known-Good Scope

Connect a known-good compatible scope.

**Expected outcome:** If the image becomes sharp and stable, the original scope is the likely external cause and should be evaluated separately.

### 7. Substitute a Known-Good Coupler

When the coupler is independently replaceable and an approved compatible substitute is available, test with a known-good coupler.

**Expected outcome:** If focus and scope retention return to normal, remove the suspect coupler from service.

### 8. Compare With a Known-Good Camera Head

If the problem remains after scope and coupler substitution, compare with a known-good compatible camera head.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is isolated to the optical accessory chain or remains associated with the original camera head/system.

### 9. Check for Mechanical Damage

Inspect the external camera-head mount and coupler interface for deformation, looseness, impact damage, or abnormal movement.

Do not disassemble the optical assembly.

**Expected outcome:** The mounting interface remains mechanically secure.

### 10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify:

- Scope locks securely
- Focus remains stable
- Zoom operates normally
- Image remains sharp during normal handling
- No excessive mechanical play is present
- Image remains stable with the intended scope

**Expected outcome:** The complete camera, coupler, and scope assembly maintains a clear image. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

If attachment, cleaning, focus, zoom, scope substitution, coupler substitution, and camera-head comparison have been completed, the remaining cause may involve an internal optical or mechanical problem requiring service.

The affected component should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel

Do not perform internal optical alignment or deep disassembly. Verify focus, retention, and image quality following repair.

Knowing when mechanical looseness or optical instability requires service rather than continued adjustment is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A scope or coupler that cannot remain mechanically secure should not remain in procedural use even when the image can temporarily be refocused.

## Final Thought

Treat blurred video as an optical-chain problem until the scope, coupler, mounting, cleanliness, and focus adjustments have been systematically verified. Escalate unstable mechanical or optical components rather than repeatedly readjusting them.
