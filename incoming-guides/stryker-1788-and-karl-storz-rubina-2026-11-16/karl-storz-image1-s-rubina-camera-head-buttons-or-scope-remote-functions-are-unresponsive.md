## What This Guide Helps With

Helps isolate unresponsive camera-head or remote controls caused by recognition, cable, button, connection, assignment, or approved configuration problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Essential Control

Do not rely on unresponsive camera controls if loss of function affects safe visualization during a procedure. Use alternate verified controls or exchange the camera system as clinically appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** Essential imaging functions remain safely controllable.

### 2. Confirm Which Controls Are Affected

Determine whether all camera-head buttons are unresponsive, only one button fails, or scope remote functions are affected.

Confirm whether the same action works from another normal system control.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is narrowed to a specific control, camera head, or system-wide function.

If the function works normally and consistently, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 3. Confirm Camera Recognition and Stable Video

Verify that the camera head is recognized and live video is stable.

If recognition is intermittent, address the recognition or cable problem before evaluating button assignments.

**Expected outcome:** The camera remains continuously recognized with stable video.

If stable recognition restores normal button operation, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Inspect the Camera Cable and Connector

Inspect the camera-head cable, connector, and strain relief for damage, contamination, or looseness.

Gently reposition the cable during off-patient testing while observing button response.

**Expected outcome:** Button operation and recognition remain stable with normal cable movement.

If control response changes with cable movement, remove the affected camera head from service.

### 5. Check for Mechanical Button Problems

Inspect accessible buttons for sticking, physical damage, contamination, or inability to return normally after being pressed.

Do not disassemble the camera head.

**Expected outcome:** Buttons move normally and are not mechanically obstructed.

If approved external cleaning resolves a sticking control, verify all functions before return to service.

### 6. Verify Normal Function Assignment

Using only normal user-accessible controls, confirm the affected button or remote function is assigned to the expected action.

Do not enter restricted configuration menus or make undocumented changes.

**Expected outcome:** The control is assigned to the intended function and responds correctly.

If correcting an approved assignment resolves the issue, perform final verification.

### 7. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Camera Head

When available, connect a verified compatible camera head and test the same functions.

If applicable, compare the same scope remote configuration.

**Expected outcome:** The comparison determines whether the fault follows the camera head or remains with the controller or system configuration.

If the problem follows the camera head, remove that component from service.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify each required camera-head button, remote function, mode selection, image capture control, and live video function used by the department.

**Expected outcome:** All required controls respond consistently and activate the intended functions.

If all controls pass, the system may be returned to service.

## If the Problem Persists

Recognition, external connections, mechanical controls, normal assignments, and known-good substitutions have been checked.

Remove the affected system or camera head from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Persistent control failures may involve internal switch assemblies, camera electronics, communication, firmware, or controller configuration.

Use appropriate Karl Storz service documentation and approved test equipment. Do not open sealed camera assemblies or perform unauthorized internal repairs.

Return to service only after all required controls have been functionally verified.

## Clinical Use Tip

Verify camera-head controls before the procedure so the surgical team is not dependent on a failed button during active visualization.

## Final Thought

Confirm that the problem is truly with the control before assuming a camera-head failure. Verify recognition, cables, assignments, and known-good components, then escalate unresolved control problems and document the final test results.

That is successful troubleshooting.
