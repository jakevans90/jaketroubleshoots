## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots unavailable cart, monitor, control-panel, or positioning movement associated with mechanical obstruction, locks, adjustment controls, or physical damage.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Prevent Unintended Movement

Do not troubleshoot positioning hardware while a patient, operator, or other equipment could be struck, pinched, or destabilized.

Stabilize the V8 and move clinical work to another verified system if positioning is necessary for the examination.

**Expected outcome:** The system can be evaluated without creating a movement or pinch hazard.

### 2. Clarify Which Motion Is Unavailable

Identify the exact reported positioning problem. The V8 is a mobile ultrasound platform rather than a gantry-based imaging system, so determine whether the complaint involves:

- Cart movement
- Wheel or caster operation
- Wheel locking
- Monitor positioning
- Control-panel positioning
- Height or articulation adjustment
- Probe-holder or accessory positioning

Attempt the affected movement gently without forcing it.

**Expected outcome:** The specific mechanical function and failure condition are clearly identified.

### 3. Check for Physical Obstruction

Inspect around the cart, wheels, monitor supports, control area, cables, probe cords, storage areas, and attached accessories.

Look for:

- Cables trapped around wheels
- Objects blocking caster travel
- Accessories interfering with articulation
- Probe cords stretched across moving joints
- Items stored where they prevent normal movement

Remove only safe external obstructions.

**Expected outcome:** The affected component moves freely once external obstruction is removed. If normal movement is restored, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Verify Wheel and Caster Lock Status

Check accessible wheel and caster controls. Confirm that locks are in the intended position before attempting cart movement.

Do not force the system if a wheel remains locked or a caster appears mechanically jammed.

**Expected outcome:** The cart moves and locks normally when controls are operated correctly.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Mechanical Components

Visually inspect the affected positioning assembly for:

- Bent external components
- Loose covers
- Cracked housings
- Damaged handles
- Misaligned external pieces
- Signs of collision or transport damage
- Abnormal looseness

Do not disassemble structural or counterbalanced positioning assemblies unless authorized and trained.

**Expected outcome:** No visible damage prevents safe operation. Visible structural damage results in removal from service.

### 6. Check Positioning Controls or Releases

Operate the normal user-accessible positioning release, adjustment, or lock control associated with the affected component.

Verify the control engages and releases normally. Do not bypass interlocks, defeat locks, or apply excessive force.

**Expected outcome:** The positioning mechanism releases, moves smoothly, and holds its selected position.

### 7. Compare With Normal Mechanical Behavior

If another equivalent V8 is available, compare external movement, resistance, lock operation, and positioning range without assuming that small subjective differences indicate failure.

Use the comparison only to identify obviously abnormal mechanical behavior.

**Expected outcome:** The affected movement behaves consistently with a known-good system or is clearly identified as abnormal.

### 8. Verify Safe Final Positioning

Move the system through normal external positioning functions that can be safely tested. Confirm:

- Cart rolls normally
- Wheel locks hold appropriately
- Monitor and control-panel positioning remain stable
- Cables remain clear during motion
- No unusual grinding, binding, or dropping occurs

**Expected outcome:** Positioning hardware moves and holds normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Escalate Unsafe or Unresolved Motion Problems

If a mechanism binds, drops, will not lock, remains unstable, or requires abnormal force, discontinue external troubleshooting.

Do not open spring-loaded, counterbalanced, structural, or powered positioning mechanisms without appropriate manufacturer procedures.

**Expected outcome:** Unsafe positioning equipment is removed from service for qualified repair.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as wheel locks, cable obstruction, attached accessories, positioning controls, and visible mechanical interference have been ruled out.

The remaining issue may involve a mechanical linkage, caster assembly, structural support, adjustment mechanism, sensor, powered actuator, or other service-level component.

The Samsung Healthcare V8 should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Samsung Healthcare documentation and approved tools
- Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify stable positioning, safe movement, locks, and any affected electrical functions before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never continue using an ultrasound cart with unreliable wheels, locks, or monitor positioning where unintended movement could contact the patient or operator.

## Final Thought

Treat positioning complaints as patient and operator safety concerns first. Rule out external obstruction, locks, cable routing, and visible damage before assuming an internal mechanical failure, and escalate any unstable or unsafe movement promptly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
