## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoots no-power and unexpected shutdown complaints caused by AC power, external connections, battery condition, docking, or environmental issues.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Monitoring
If the MX750 is actively monitoring a patient and repeatedly shuts down or will not power on, transfer monitoring to another verified monitor before troubleshooting. Do not rely on equipment with unstable power.

**Expected outcome:** The patient remains continuously monitored by reliable equipment. If alternate monitoring is established, continue troubleshooting the affected MX750.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Power Problem
Determine whether the monitor is completely dead, begins startup and shuts down, operates only on AC power, operates only on battery power, or shuts down intermittently during use. Ask whether movement, docking, or cable handling affects the problem.

**Expected outcome:** The failure pattern is clearly identified. If the monitor now starts normally and remains stable, verify operation before ending troubleshooting.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source
Confirm that the power cord and associated external power components are fully connected and undamaged. Test the receptacle with an appropriate outlet tester or another approved device. Avoid power strips or questionable extension arrangements during testing.

**Expected outcome:** A verified AC source is available and the monitor shows normal external-power operation. If restoring reliable AC power resolves the problem, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 4. Inspect the Power Cord and External Connections
Inspect the power cord, plug, strain relief, power connection, and accessible docking or mounting connections for looseness, bent contacts, contamination, scorching, or physical damage. Reconnect all removable external connections securely.

**Expected outcome:** Connections are secure and free of visible damage. If reseating an external connection restores stable operation, proceed to final verification.

### 5. Compare AC and Battery Operation
With the monitor removed from patient use, observe operation on verified AC power and, when appropriate, battery power. Note whether shutdown occurs only on one power source.

**Expected outcome:** The MX750 remains powered without unexpected shutdown on the tested power source. A repeatable failure limited to battery operation or AC operation helps isolate the affected power path.

### 6. Evaluate Battery Condition Externally
Check whether the monitor recognizes the installed battery and whether its displayed charge status behaves normally. Inspect accessible battery seating and contacts without opening the monitor. If approved and available, compare operation using a known-good compatible battery.

**Expected outcome:** A properly recognized known-good battery supports stable operation. If replacing a defective battery corrects the shutdown complaint, continue to final verification.

### 7. Check Docking, Mounting, and Connected Accessories
If the monitor is installed in a docking arrangement or connected to external modules, displays, or accessories, determine whether the shutdown occurs only when a particular external component is attached. Disconnect nonessential external accessories while the monitor is out of clinical use and retest.

**Expected outcome:** The monitor remains stable with normal required connections. If one external accessory or connection consistently causes the shutdown, remove that item from use and correct or replace it as appropriate.

### 8. Inspect Environmental Conditions
Check for blocked ventilation, excessive dust around accessible vents, liquid contamination, unusual heat, odor, or evidence that the monitor has been dropped or physically damaged.

**Expected outcome:** No environmental or physical condition explains the shutdown. Any liquid intrusion, overheating, burning odor, or significant damage requires immediate removal from service.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
After correcting an external cause, power-cycle the monitor several times as appropriate, verify stable operation on the intended power source, confirm battery indication if applicable, and verify normal monitoring and alarm functionality using appropriate test equipment.

**Expected outcome:** The MX750 starts consistently, remains powered, and passes applicable return-to-service checks. If so, troubleshooting is complete.

### 10. Escalate an Unresolved Power Failure
If verified AC power, external connections, battery condition, accessories, and environmental causes have been ruled out and the monitor still fails to power or shuts down unexpectedly, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The monitor is removed from clinical use and referred for manufacturer-level or qualified bench evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists
Common external power and connection causes have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve an internal power subsystem, battery charging circuitry, docking interface, internal connection, thermal protection condition, or other service-level fault.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Philips service documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, perform applicable electrical safety, power, battery, monitoring, and alarm verification before returning the MX750 to clinical use. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
Never leave a patient dependent on a monitor that has demonstrated unexplained power loss, even if it restarts successfully.

## Final Thought
Protect the patient first, verify the external power path before assuming an internal failure, reproduce intermittent problems whenever safely possible, and document both the cause and final verification clearly. Escalate unstable power problems that cannot be resolved externally.

That is successful troubleshooting.
