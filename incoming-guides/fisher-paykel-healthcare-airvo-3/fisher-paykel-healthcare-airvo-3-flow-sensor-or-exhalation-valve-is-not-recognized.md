## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots sensor or gas-path recognition complaints by verifying the actual Airvo 3 configuration, compatible accessories, connections, and displayed fault information.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Therapy
If the Airvo 3 cannot reliably recognize a required component or establish normal airflow, transfer the patient to an appropriate verified respiratory support system before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** Patient support remains uninterrupted.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Message
Record the exact device message or alarm rather than relying only on the description "flow sensor" or "exhalation valve."

The Airvo 3 is a high-flow oxygen system and should not be assumed to use the same external flow-sensor or exhalation-valve arrangement as a conventional ventilator. Verify which component the device is actually identifying.

**Expected outcome:** The complaint is tied to an actual installed component, connector, or displayed message.

### 3. Verify the Intended Patient-Circuit Configuration
Compare the installed breathing tube, humidifier chamber, patient interface, and adapters with the intended approved Airvo 3 setup.

Do not add, bypass, or substitute a ventilator-style exhalation valve unless it is specifically part of the approved configuration being serviced.

**Expected outcome:** The respiratory system is assembled with the correct compatible components.

### 4. Inspect External Connections
Inspect accessible breathing-tube and accessory connections for incomplete insertion, contamination, moisture, damage, bent contacts where applicable, or poor seating.

**Expected outcome:** All externally accessible connections are clean, intact, and fully seated.

### 5. Inspect the Breathing Tube and Accessories
Check the heated breathing tube and other applicable accessories for physical damage, incorrect type, contamination, or signs that could prevent proper detection or operation.

**Expected outcome:** Installed accessories are appropriate and undamaged.

### 6. Reseat the Applicable Accessories
With the device safely removed from patient use, disconnect and reinstall the affected external component according to its normal connection method.

Do not force connectors or bypass recognition features.

**Expected outcome:** The component is recognized after correct reconnection. If so, continue to final functional verification.

### 7. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Accessory
When an external accessory is identified by the device or suspected from inspection, substitute a known-good approved compatible accessory.

**Expected outcome:** Normal recognition with the known-good accessory identifies the original accessory as defective.

### 8. Restart and Observe Normal Initialization
Restart the Airvo 3 using standard controls and observe whether the device recognizes the installed system and reaches its ready state.

**Expected outcome:** The recognition error clears and the device completes startup normally.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
Verify airflow, alarms, accessory recognition, humidification, controls, and overall system performance with the complete intended configuration.

**Expected outcome:** The Airvo 3 consistently recognizes required components and operates normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Recognition Errors
If the correct known-good accessories and connections have been verified but the device continues to report a component or sensing problem, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The Airvo 3 is removed from service for qualified evaluation of sensing, connector, control, or internal gas-path functions.

## If the Problem Persists

Incorrect accessories, poor connections, visible damage, and externally replaceable component problems have been ruled out. Persistent recognition errors may involve device-side sensing, connector interfaces, internal wiring, electronics, configuration, or software.

Remove the Airvo 3 from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for qualified bench evaluation. Use appropriate Fisher & Paykel Healthcare documentation and approved test equipment. Do not disassemble internal gas-path or sensing assemblies without appropriate authorization and training.

Following repair, verify normal accessory recognition, flow, alarms, humidification, and complete functional performance before return to service.

## Clinical Use Tip

Always identify the exact component named by the Airvo 3 before troubleshooting; terminology borrowed from conventional ventilators can lead to checking components that are not part of the Airvo configuration.

Component-recognition problems should be based on the actual Airvo 3 configuration and displayed information, not assumptions from other respiratory devices. Verify external accessories first, escalate persistent device-side failures, and document exactly what was observed and corrected.
