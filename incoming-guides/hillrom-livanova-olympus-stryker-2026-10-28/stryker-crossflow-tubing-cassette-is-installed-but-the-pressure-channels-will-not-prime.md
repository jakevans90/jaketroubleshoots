## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots failure to prime after cassette installation, including tubing setup, fluid supply, cassette seating, pressure sensing, clamps, and flow-path problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain the Procedure Safely

Do not troubleshoot an unreliable fluid-management system while the surgical team depends on it for joint distention, visualization, or controlled irrigation.

If the condition occurs during a procedure, notify the surgical team and transition to an approved alternate fluid-management method or verified replacement system before extended troubleshooting. Do not continue using the CrossFlow if pressure control or fluid delivery cannot be trusted.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is supported by a reliable fluid-management method before troubleshooting continues.

If continuity of care is established, proceed.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Priming Failure

Determine what staff observed:

- Cassette recognized but priming will not begin
- Pump operates but fluid does not advance
- Inflow primes partially and stops
- Pressure indication remains absent or implausible
- One tubing configuration works while another does not
- Problem began after cassette or tubing replacement

Record any displayed message exactly rather than interpreting it.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is reproduced and identified as a priming, fluid-path, cassette-recognition, or pressure-sensing problem.

If the system primes normally and pressure responds appropriately during controlled testing, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 3. Verify Basic Power and Startup

Confirm:

- AC power connection is secure
- Power cord and plug are undamaged
- Unit completes normal startup
- Display and controls respond normally
- No unrelated startup condition is preventing operation

A partial startup or abnormal control condition can make a tubing problem appear to be a cassette problem.

**Expected outcome:** CrossFlow starts normally and is ready to accept the tubing cassette.

If startup itself is abnormal, remove the device from service and troubleshoot the power or console condition separately.

### 4. Inspect the Cassette and Tubing Before Reinstalling It

Inspect the applicable CrossFlow tubing set for:

- Correct tubing type
- Packaging or tubing damage
- Kinks or collapsed tubing
- Closed clamps
- Misrouted tubing
- Pinched tubing near the cassette
- Contamination or fluid on cassette contact surfaces
- Damaged pressure-sensing membrane area
- Obvious deformation of the cassette

CrossFlow inflow cassettes incorporate a pressure-sensing membrane used by the pump to determine pressure. [Stryker](https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sports-medicine/products/crossflow-tubing.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Do not attempt to repair or modify disposable tubing.

**Expected outcome:** The cassette and tubing are intact, correctly routed, and free of obvious restrictions.

If replacement of damaged or improperly installed tubing restores priming, the issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 5. Remove and Correctly Reseat the Cassette

With the system in a safe non-patient test condition:

1. Remove the cassette.
2. Inspect the cassette bay for debris, dried fluid, obstruction, or damage.
3. Confirm the cassette is oriented correctly.
4. Reinsert it completely without forcing it.
5. Confirm the system recognizes the installed tubing.

CrossFlow uses cassette-style tubing, and cassette recognition is part of normal system operation. [Stryker](https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sports-medicine/products/crossflow-integrated-arthroscopy-pump.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Do not manipulate internal sensors or mechanisms in the cassette bay.

**Expected outcome:** The cassette seats fully and is recognized consistently.

If reseating restores normal priming and pressure response, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 6. Verify the Fluid Source and Inflow Path

Confirm:

- Irrigation fluid source is available
- Spike or connection is fully inserted
- Supply tubing is not kinked
- Required clamps are open
- Fluid can reach the cassette
- Tubing has not been trapped under equipment or a cart wheel
- Bag placement and setup are appropriate for the approved configuration

Do not assume a pump failure when fluid cannot physically reach the inflow cassette.

**Expected outcome:** An unrestricted fluid path exists from the supply to the CrossFlow cassette.

If correcting the supply path allows normal priming, the issue is resolved.

### 7. Check for Air or Improper Tubing Preparation

Inspect the external tubing path for excessive air, incomplete fluid filling, or an installation condition preventing the priming sequence from progressing.

Follow the approved tubing preparation method for the installed tubing set. Do not bypass sensors or defeat cassette monitoring to force a prime.

**Expected outcome:** Tubing fills appropriately and the system progresses through normal priming.

If priming completes and pressure sensing becomes stable, troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Tubing Set

If allowed by facility policy and the system is being tested off-patient, install a known-good compatible CrossFlow tubing set.

This comparison can separate:

- Disposable tubing/cassette problems
- Cassette-recognition problems
- Console-side pressure-sensing problems

Do not repeatedly consume disposables if evidence clearly points to a console-side fault.

**Expected outcome:** A known-good cassette primes normally.

If the replacement tubing works, remove the suspect tubing from use and document the finding.

If multiple verified tubing sets fail the same way, continue.

### 9. Inspect the Cassette Bay Externally

With the device powered down as appropriate, visually inspect the accessible cassette interface for:

- Residual fluid
- Debris
- Physical damage
- Bent or obstructed external mechanisms
- Signs that the cassette cannot contact the sensing area correctly

Do not disassemble the cassette mechanism or adjust pressure-sensing hardware without authorized service documentation.

**Expected outcome:** The cassette interface is clean, dry, unobstructed, and mechanically intact.

If visible damage or fluid intrusion is present, remove the unit from service.

### 10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Using approved tubing, fluid, manufacturer documentation, and appropriate test conditions:

- Confirm cassette recognition
- Complete the normal prime
- Verify fluid advances without restriction
- Confirm pressure indication responds plausibly
- Confirm no leakage is present
- Verify inflow/outflow operation as applicable
- Confirm controls and alarms needed for normal operation

**Expected outcome:** The CrossFlow primes normally and provides stable, repeatable fluid and pressure operation.

If all required functional checks pass, the device may be returned to service according to facility policy.

### 11. Escalate if Pressure Channels Still Will Not Prime

If multiple correctly installed, known-good tubing sets fail and the cassette bay is clean and undamaged externally, stop external troubleshooting.

Possible service-level categories include:

- Cassette-detection problem
- Pressure-sensing interface problem
- Pump or drive-system problem
- Internal fluid-management control fault
- Console hardware or software issue

Do not assume or replace an internal component without supporting manufacturer procedures and test results.

**Expected outcome:** An unresolved device-side condition is recognized and appropriately escalated.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out.

The CrossFlow should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Stryker documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, perform the required functional and safety testing, including cassette recognition, priming, pressure response, fluid delivery, leakage inspection, and any other manufacturer-required return-to-service checks.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate a suspected sensing or pump-control problem is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never allow a case to depend on a CrossFlow system that cannot reliably establish and monitor its fluid-management pressure path.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, verify the entire external fluid path before assuming an internal failure, use known-good tubing when appropriate, and escalate when cassette recognition or pressure sensing remains unreliable. Document both the cause and the successful verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
