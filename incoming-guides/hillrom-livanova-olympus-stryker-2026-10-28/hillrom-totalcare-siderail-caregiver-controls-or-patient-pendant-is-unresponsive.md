## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot unresponsive siderail or pendant controls by checking patient safety, power, lockouts, connectors, individual control stations, accessories, and visible damage.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Essential Bed Functions

Do not troubleshoot unreliable bed controls while a patient depends on them for positioning, emergency movement, or safe transfer. If essential controls are unavailable, move the patient to another verified bed or ensure staff have an immediately available safe alternative.

Maintain appropriate bed height, brakes, siderail position, and fall precautions.

**Expected outcome:** The patient remains safely supported without depending on the failed control interface. If safe care cannot be maintained, remove the bed from service immediately.

### 2. Confirm Which Controls Are Unresponsive

Determine whether the complaint involves:

- One siderail control panel.
- Multiple siderail controls.
- Patient controls only.
- Caregiver controls only.
- The patient pendant only.
- One specific function.
- All powered bed functions.

Test only safe, non-patient-dependent movements under controlled conditions.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is narrowed to a specific control station, group of controls, or system-wide condition. If all controls operate normally during repeated testing, proceed to final verification.

### 3. Verify Bed Power

Confirm the bed powers normally and is connected to a known-good approved AC outlet when appropriate.

Inspect:

- Power cord.
- Plug.
- Visible cord strain relief.
- Bed power indicators.

If the bed has available battery-supported functions, compare operation on AC and battery without unnecessarily cycling power while a patient is dependent on the bed.

**Expected outcome:** The bed has stable operating power. If restored AC power corrects the control problem, verify all affected controls and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Check Control Lockouts and Authorized Settings

Inspect the caregiver control area for active lockouts or disabled patient functions. Confirm whether the affected control has intentionally been restricted.

Do not defeat safety lockouts or make unauthorized configuration changes.

**Expected outcome:** The controls required for testing are enabled. If an intentional lockout caused the complaint, restore the appropriate authorized setting, verify operation, and stop troubleshooting.

### 5. Compare Control Stations

Operate the same bed function from another available caregiver control location.

For example, if a siderail button is unresponsive, determine whether the corresponding function responds from another siderail or central caregiver control.

**Expected outcome:** Either the function operates from another location, isolating the issue to one interface, or it fails from all control locations, indicating a broader problem.

If only one control station is affected and the remaining bed functions are reliable, continue inspection of that control interface.

### 6. Inspect the Patient Pendant and Cable

If the pendant is involved, inspect the entire accessible cable and connector for:

- Loose connection.
- Bent or damaged connector.
- Pinched cable.
- Cuts or crushed insulation.
- Fluid contamination.
- Strain damage.
- Physical impact.

Reseat an accessible external connector only when designed for routine connection and disconnection.

**Expected outcome:** The pendant and cable are securely connected and free of visible damage. If reseating the connector restores reliable operation, perform repeated functional testing and stop troubleshooting.

### 7. Inspect Siderail Controls for External Damage or Contamination

Examine the affected siderail control surface for:

- Cracked membrane or buttons.
- Fluid intrusion.
- Sticky controls.
- Impact damage.
- Loose housing.
- A button that remains mechanically depressed.
- Damage following cleaning or collision.

Do not open sealed control assemblies unless specifically authorized by approved service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The control panel is externally intact and individual controls move normally. Visible damage or contamination affecting reliability requires removal from service or approved replacement.

### 8. Use a Known-Good Accessory When Appropriate

If an approved compatible known-good patient pendant is available, substitute it according to facility procedure and retest the functions.

Do not substitute unverified or incompatible accessories.

**Expected outcome:** If the known-good pendant restores operation, the original pendant or its cable is the likely cause and may be replaced according to approved procedures. If the problem remains, continue evaluation of the bed.

### 9. Check for Position-Dependent Intermittency

With the bed unloaded and secured, gently move the accessible pendant cable and siderail through normal non-forceful positions while observing control response.

Do not deliberately flex damaged wiring or create additional stress.

**Expected outcome:** Controls remain stable regardless of normal cable or siderail position. Intermittent operation associated with movement indicates an unreliable connection or wiring condition requiring service escalation.

### 10. Perform Final Functional Verification

After any correction, test every affected control and compare it with other control locations.

Verify:

- Commands activate the intended function.
- Buttons release normally.
- Controls are not intermittent.
- Lockouts behave as expected.
- Pendant cable remains secure.
- No unexpected movement occurs.

**Expected outcome:** All required controls respond consistently and safely. If so, troubleshooting can stop after applicable return-to-service testing.

### 11. Escalate an Unresolved Control Failure

If power, lockouts, external connectors, pendant condition, and individual control stations have been evaluated without resolving the fault, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The bed is removed from clinical use if control reliability could affect patient safety and routed for qualified technical evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve the control interface, internal wiring, siderail electronics, bed communication circuitry, power distribution, or configuration.

The bed should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Hillrom documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Complete appropriate operational and electrical safety verification after repair before returning the bed to service.

Knowing when to stop rather than relying on intermittent patient or caregiver controls is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

If essential positioning controls are unreliable, move the patient to another verified bed before further testing rather than depending on alternate controls as a permanent workaround.

## Final Thought

Maintain safe patient support first, then isolate whether the failure involves power, settings, one control station, an accessory, or the bed itself. Verify reliable response before return to use, escalate intermittent or unresolved controls, and document the findings clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
