## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots failed white balance or unstable automatic brightness caused by scope connections, illumination, accessories, settings, contamination, or incompatible components.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Visualization
If the system is being used during a procedure and image color or brightness is unreliable, maintain visualization using another verified system or follow the clinical team's approved backup plan before troubleshooting.

Do not disconnect scopes, light sources, or video equipment while the patient depends on the affected system.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is supported by reliable visualization before technical troubleshooting begins. If dependable visualization is restored using another verified system, remove the affected equipment from clinical use until evaluated.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition
Determine whether the problem involves:
- White balance failing to complete
- Incorrect color after white balance
- Image remaining too dark or too bright
- Automatic brightness repeatedly hunting or changing
- The issue occurring with one scope or all scopes
- The issue beginning after an accessory, scope, or light-source change

Observe the displayed image without changing configuration unnecessarily.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is clearly identified as a white-balance, brightness-control, or component-specific problem.

### 3. Verify System Power and Normal Startup
Confirm the video processor, light source, display, and any required supporting components are fully powered and have completed normal startup.

Inspect external power cords and connections for looseness or damage.

If equipment was recently restarted or moved, verify every required component has returned to its normal operating state.

**Expected outcome:** All required system components power normally without obvious startup or power-related abnormalities. If normal operation returns after correcting an external power issue, verify image performance and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Inspect Scope and Video Connections
Inspect the scope connector, video connector, light-guide connection, and any camera or adapter connections applicable to the installed configuration.

Look for:
- Incomplete seating
- Bent, contaminated, wet, or damaged external contacts
- Damaged cable jackets
- Excessive strain at connectors
- Incorrect adapter or accessory installation

Reconnect only according to approved handling practices.

**Expected outcome:** All external connections are secure, clean, dry, undamaged, and correctly installed. If white balance and brightness then operate normally, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 5. Check the Distal End and Optical Path
Inspect accessible optical surfaces for contamination, condensation, debris, residue, or obvious damage.

Confirm the scope tip and light output are not obstructed by caps, packaging, protective accessories, or improperly fitted components.

Do not perform invasive scope repair or disassembly.

**Expected outcome:** The optical path is unobstructed and external optical surfaces are clean and intact. If correcting contamination restores normal image control, verify function and stop troubleshooting.

### 6. Repeat White Balance Under Proper Conditions
Using the facility's approved procedure and compatible white-reference method, attempt white balance again.

Make sure the reference fills the field of view appropriately and is evenly illuminated. Avoid unusual ambient light or reflective surfaces that could interfere with the process.

Do not bypass manufacturer-required white-balance procedures.

**Expected outcome:** White balance completes and the displayed colors appear stable and reasonable. If successful, verify brightness control and stop troubleshooting.

### 7. Verify Illumination and Automatic Brightness Conditions
Confirm that the light source is operating normally and that illumination is not being restricted by a poorly connected light guide, damaged accessory, obstructed optical path, or incompatible component.

Observe whether automatic brightness responds when viewing targets at different distances under controlled bench conditions.

**Expected outcome:** Illumination changes appropriately without persistent overexposure, underexposure, or uncontrolled brightness hunting.

### 8. Review Accessible Image and Brightness Settings
Verify that user-accessible image settings, brightness modes, exposure-related selections, and procedure profiles have not been unintentionally changed.

Compare the affected system with a known-good unit of the same configuration when available.

Do not enter restricted service menus or alter undocumented configuration values.

**Expected outcome:** User-accessible settings are appropriate for the installed equipment. If correcting an accidental setting change restores normal operation, document the change and stop troubleshooting.

### 9. Test With a Known-Good Compatible Scope or Accessory
When available, substitute a known-good compatible scope, light-guide component, or other external accessory one item at a time.

Avoid introducing multiple substitutions simultaneously because this can obscure the actual cause.

**Expected outcome:** If the problem follows one external component, remove that component from service. If the problem remains with multiple known-good components, continue escalation.

### 10. Perform Final Functional Verification
After correction, verify:
- White balance completes normally
- Colors remain stable
- Automatic brightness responds appropriately
- The image is neither persistently dark nor overexposed
- No new alarms or image abnormalities appear
- All disturbed connections remain secure

**Expected outcome:** Normal image performance is consistently restored. The device may return to service when required institutional and manufacturer verification is complete.

## If the Problem Persists

If white balance or automatic brightness continues to fail after external connections, optical surfaces, illumination, compatible scopes, accessories, and accessible settings have been checked, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve internal video processing, light-source control, scope communication, configuration, or another service-level condition.

The affected equipment should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not proceed into internal board-level troubleshooting or unauthorized service adjustments. Return the equipment to clinical use only after the required functional and safety verification is successfully completed.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not continue a procedure with unreliable visualization when another verified imaging system or approved clinical alternative is available.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, then work from illumination, connections, optics, accessories, and settings before assuming an internal imaging fault. Verify the correction, escalate appropriately when external checks are exhausted, and document the complaint, cause, and resolution clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
