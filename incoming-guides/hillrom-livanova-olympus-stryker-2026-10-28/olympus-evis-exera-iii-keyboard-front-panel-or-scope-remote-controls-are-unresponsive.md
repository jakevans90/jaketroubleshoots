## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots unresponsive controls caused by loose connections, damaged accessories, contamination, control assignments, configuration, or communication problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Before Testing Controls
If unresponsive controls affect image capture, visualization, air/water delivery, freeze functions, or another function required during a procedure, provide a verified alternate control method or imaging system before troubleshooting.

Do not repeatedly operate unreliable controls while clinical staff depend on them.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care continues using a reliable method and the affected controls can be evaluated safely.

### 2. Confirm Which Controls Are Affected
Determine whether the failure involves:
- External keyboard
- Processor front-panel controls
- Scope remote buttons
- One individual key or button
- All controls of one type
- Multiple control interfaces simultaneously

Determine whether the controls are completely dead, intermittent, delayed, or performing the wrong function.

**Expected outcome:** The affected control interface and symptom are clearly defined.

### 3. Verify Normal System Operation
Confirm the video processor and related components have powered up normally and are not frozen, restarting, or displaying another system-level abnormality.

Check whether image processing continues normally even though controls are unresponsive.

**Expected outcome:** The problem is isolated to control input rather than a complete system power or startup failure.

### 4. Inspect Keyboard and External Control Connections
If an external keyboard or other control accessory is affected, inspect its cable and connector.

Check for:
- Loose or partially seated plugs
- Bent or damaged connector hardware
- Cable strain
- Cuts or crushed areas
- Contamination or fluid intrusion
- Incorrect port connection

Reconnect only to the appropriate labeled connection.

**Expected outcome:** The keyboard or accessory is securely connected with no obvious external damage. If operation returns, verify all affected keys and stop troubleshooting.

### 5. Inspect Front-Panel Controls
Check the front panel for:
- Stuck buttons
- Residue or contamination
- Damaged key surfaces
- Physical binding
- Evidence of fluid exposure
- Obvious impact damage

Do not disassemble the front panel.

**Expected outcome:** Controls move and respond normally without visible damage or contamination. If a simple external obstruction is safely corrected and normal response returns, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

### 6. Inspect Scope Remote Buttons and Scope Connection
For unresponsive scope controls, inspect the scope connector and accessible remote buttons.

Confirm the scope is fully connected and that buttons are not physically stuck or contaminated.

If the scope was recently reprocessed or exchanged, verify the correct compatible scope is installed.

**Expected outcome:** The scope is securely connected and its external controls are physically intact.

### 7. Check Control Assignments and User-Accessible Settings
Determine whether the affected scope buttons or programmable controls have been assigned different functions through user-accessible settings.

Compare the observed assignments with an approved configuration or a known-good system when available.

Do not change service-level configuration or use restricted menus.

**Expected outcome:** Button assignments and accessible control configuration match the intended clinical workflow. If restoring the correct assignment resolves the complaint, document it and stop troubleshooting.

### 8. Test a Known-Good Compatible Keyboard or Scope
When available, substitute a known-good compatible keyboard, scope, or external control accessory.

Change only one component at a time.

**Expected outcome:** If the problem follows the substituted accessory, remove the faulty accessory from service. If multiple known-good accessories show the same failure, continue escalation.

### 9. Restart the System When Clinically Appropriate
When the system is not supporting a patient, perform an approved normal shutdown and restart.

Do not cycle power during an active procedure unless directed by the clinical team and an alternate visualization method is already established.

After restart, retest the affected controls.

**Expected outcome:** Controls initialize and respond normally. If the issue is cleared and remains stable through repeated testing, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 10. Verify All Required Control Functions
Test the affected interface under controlled conditions.

Verify:
- Keyboard entry responds correctly
- Front-panel buttons operate the intended functions
- Scope remote buttons perform their assigned actions
- No button is intermittent or stuck
- No unintended function activates

**Expected outcome:** All tested controls operate consistently and correctly. The system may return to service after required verification.

## If the Problem Persists

If controls remain unresponsive after checking power state, physical condition, connectors, scope seating, button assignments, known-good accessories, and a normal restart, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve control-interface electronics, internal communication, processor configuration, scope electronics, or another service-level condition.

The affected equipment should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not disassemble front panels, scope controls, or internal input circuitry without appropriate authorization and documentation. Complete functional verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A remote button that activates the wrong function can be as unsafe as a completely unresponsive button; verify both response and correct assignment.

## Final Thought

Treat control failures as a patient-safety concern when they affect visualization or procedural functions. Verify physical connections, accessories, assignments, and system response before assuming internal failure, then escalate and document clearly when external troubleshooting does not resolve the issue.

That is successful troubleshooting.
