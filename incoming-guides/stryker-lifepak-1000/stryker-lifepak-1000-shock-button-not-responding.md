## What This Guide Helps With
Helps troubleshoot shock button issues caused by control conditions, physical damage, or external operational factors.
## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Confirm Patient Safety and Equipment Availability
If the device is needed for emergency use, provide another verified defibrillator before troubleshooting.
Expected outcome: Emergency care remains supported.
### 2. Confirm the Shock Button Complaint
Verify that the shock button does not respond when a shock is advised. Document whether the issue is intermittent or consistent.
Expected outcome: Exact control problem is identified.
### 3. Inspect the Shock Button Area
Check for visible damage, contamination, sticking, or obstruction around the button.
Expected outcome: External mechanical issues are identified or ruled out.
### 4. Verify Device State During Test
Confirm the device reaches the appropriate condition where shock delivery would normally be available during approved testing.
Expected outcome: The control issue is evaluated under the correct operating condition.
### 5. Perform Approved Functional Verification
Use appropriate test equipment to verify shock control operation.
Expected outcome: Shock control functions correctly or failure is confirmed.
### 6. Remove From Service if Failure Continues
If the shock button remains unreliable, remove the device from clinical use.
Expected outcome: Unsafe equipment is prevented from use.
## If the Problem Persists
Common external causes such as physical obstruction, contamination, and operating conditions have been ruled out.
The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel
Complete functional verification is required before return to service.
## Clinical Use Tip
Do not rely on a defibrillator with uncertain shock control operation during emergency response.
