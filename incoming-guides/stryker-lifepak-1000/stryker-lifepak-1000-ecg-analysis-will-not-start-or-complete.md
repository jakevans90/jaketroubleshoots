## What This Guide Helps With
Helps troubleshoot ECG analysis problems caused by electrode contact, patient signal quality, connections, or external conditions.
## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Confirm Patient Safety and Equipment Availability
If the device is needed for emergency care, use a verified alternate defibrillator before troubleshooting.
Expected outcome: Patient care capability remains available.
### 2. Confirm the ECG Analysis Problem
Verify whether ECG analysis will not begin, stops during analysis, or does not provide a result. Document the exact behavior.
Expected outcome: The reported condition is confirmed.
### 3. Verify Electrode Placement and Connection
Inspect electrode placement and ensure the electrode connector is fully seated. Confirm electrodes are properly attached and in usable condition.
Expected outcome: Proper electrode connection and patient signal path are confirmed.
### 4. Check ECG Signal Quality
Verify that electrode contact is adequate and that excessive motion, poor contact, or environmental interference is not affecting signal quality.
Expected outcome: A usable ECG signal is present.
### 5. Substitute Known-Good Electrodes
If available, use verified compatible electrodes to determine whether the original electrodes are causing the issue.
Expected outcome: ECG analysis begins normally if electrodes were the cause.
### 6. Verify Normal Device Function
Perform an operational check to confirm the device can acquire ECG information and complete analysis under appropriate test conditions.
Expected outcome: ECG analysis completes normally and troubleshooting can stop.
### 7. Escalate Continued ECG Analysis Failure
If the device cannot complete ECG analysis after external checks, remove it from service for evaluation.
Expected outcome: Device is safely routed for qualified service review.
## If the Problem Persists
Common external causes such as electrode condition, connections, patient signal quality, and accessories have been ruled out.
The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel
Return-to-service testing should verify proper ECG acquisition and defibrillator functionality.
## Clinical Use Tip
Do not delay emergency care while troubleshooting ECG analysis; use another verified defibrillator if needed.
