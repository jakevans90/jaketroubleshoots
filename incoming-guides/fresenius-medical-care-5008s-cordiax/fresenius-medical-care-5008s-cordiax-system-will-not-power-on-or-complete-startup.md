## What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when the system is unresponsive, loses power, or stops during startup after basic external power and connection problems are suspected.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Continuity of Care

Do not troubleshoot a dialysis machine while a patient depends on unreliable operation. If treatment is underway and the machine loses power, repeatedly restarts, or cannot complete startup, follow the facility's clinical contingency process and move treatment to another verified dialysis machine as appropriate.

Inspect for smoke, unusual odor, excessive heat, liquid intrusion, or obvious physical damage. If any are present, disconnect the machine from AC power when safe, label it Out of Service, and stop troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is supported by safe alternate equipment and the affected machine can be evaluated without clinical dependence. If a clear unsafe condition is identified, troubleshooting stops and the device is removed from service.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Startup Failure

Ask staff what occurred immediately before the problem and reproduce the condition only when safe. Determine whether the machine is completely dead, powers briefly and shuts down, freezes during startup, restarts repeatedly, or displays a startup message.

Record any displayed message exactly rather than interpreting or clearing it prematurely.

**Expected outcome:** The failure pattern is clearly defined and repeatable enough to guide further checks. If the machine now starts normally and remains stable, continue through final verification before returning it to service.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source

Confirm the power cord is fully seated and inspect the cord and plug for damage, looseness, discoloration, or overheating. Verify the receptacle has power using an approved tester or known-good load in accordance with facility practice.

Avoid extension cords, damaged adapters, or questionable power strips.

**Expected outcome:** A verified AC source is available and the machine receives power through an undamaged connection. If restoring a valid power source corrects the problem and startup completes normally, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Check External Power Controls and Connections

Verify the machine's accessible power controls are in the correct operating position and that no externally accessible emergency disconnect or facility power control is preventing startup.

Inspect accessible peripheral and communication connections for anything obviously loose, damaged, or improperly connected that could interfere with initialization.

**Expected outcome:** All external power controls and accessible connections are correctly positioned and secure. If correcting an external connection restores normal startup, no further troubleshooting is needed beyond verification.

### 5. Allow a Controlled Restart

With the machine disconnected from patient use, perform a normal power-down and restart using approved operating controls. Do not rapidly cycle power or repeatedly restart a machine that shuts itself down.

Observe whether startup progresses farther and note exactly where it stops.

**Expected outcome:** The machine completes startup without freezing, restarting, or presenting unresolved startup faults. If it does, continue to functional verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 6. Check Environmental Conditions

Confirm ventilation openings are unobstructed and the machine has not recently been exposed to liquid, condensation, unusual heat, or an unstable electrical environment.

If the machine was moved, verify no cables, hoses, or accessories were pinched or disturbed during transport.

**Expected outcome:** The operating environment and external setup are suitable for normal startup. Any correctable environmental cause is removed.

### 7. Compare With a Known-Good Setup

When practical, compare the machine's external power arrangement and connected accessories with another functioning 5008S CorDiax in the same area. Substitute only approved external accessories when doing so will not alter clinical configuration.

**Expected outcome:** An external accessory or setup difference is either identified and corrected or ruled out.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

After startup completes, verify the machine remains powered, displays and controls respond normally, required self-checks complete, and no new alarms or abnormal behavior appears.

Do not return the machine to service solely because it powers on once after an unexplained shutdown or restart.

**Expected outcome:** The machine completes a stable startup and required operational checks without recurrence. If successful, troubleshooting can stop and the device may proceed through normal return-to-service requirements.

## If the Problem Persists

If the 5008S CorDiax still will not power on or complete startup after verified AC power, external connections, controls, accessories, and environmental conditions have been ruled out, the problem may involve an internal power, control, software, interlock, or startup subsystem requiring service-level evaluation.

The device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Fresenius Medical Care documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Subjected to applicable functional, safety, and return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate an unresolved startup failure is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A dialysis machine that unexpectedly shuts down or restarts should not remain connected to a patient while Clinical Engineering attempts to reproduce the problem.
