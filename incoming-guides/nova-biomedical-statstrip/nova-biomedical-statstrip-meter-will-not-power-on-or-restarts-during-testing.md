## What This Guide Helps With
Power failures caused by depleted battery, charging problems, poor contacts, dock issues, contamination, physical damage, or service-level electrical faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Care

Immediately replace a meter that shuts down or restarts during patient testing with another verified device.

Do not continue troubleshooting an unreliable meter while a patient result is needed.

**Expected outcome:** Patient testing continues without dependence on unstable equipment.

### 2. Confirm the Power Symptom

Determine whether the meter is completely dead, powers on only while docked, restarts randomly, restarts when a strip is inserted, or shuts down during testing.

**Expected outcome:** The failure pattern is clearly defined.

### 3. Inspect for Physical or Liquid Damage

Check the housing, display, battery area, charging contacts, and strip-port area for cracks, impact damage, moisture, corrosion, residue, swelling, heat damage, or odor.

**Expected outcome:** No obvious unsafe physical condition is present.

If liquid intrusion, swelling, overheating, or significant damage is found, stop troubleshooting and remove the meter from service.

### 4. Check Battery Condition and Installation

Verify that the battery is correctly installed and that accessible battery contacts are clean and undamaged.

Do not open sealed battery assemblies.

**Expected outcome:** The battery is correctly seated with no visible damage.

### 5. Test With a Known-Good Charging Location

Place the meter on a known-good compatible StatStrip docking station or charging location.

Observe whether the meter shows expected charging or power behavior.

**Expected outcome:** The meter recognizes external charging power.

### 6. Compare With Another Meter

Place another known-good StatStrip on the same dock.

If the second meter also fails to charge or power correctly, investigate the dock or its power source.

**Expected outcome:** The fault is isolated to either the meter or charging infrastructure.

### 7. Allow Adequate Charging

If the battery was depleted, allow charging according to approved facility and manufacturer practice before retesting.

Do not repeatedly attempt patient testing on a meter with unstable power.

**Expected outcome:** The meter powers on and remains stable after charging.

### 8. Perform Controlled Operational Testing

Once adequately charged, power the meter on and navigate normal user-accessible functions.

Insert an approved strip and perform permitted functional or control testing.

**Expected outcome:** The meter remains powered throughout normal operation.

### 9. Verify Restart Behavior

Observe the meter during repeated approved tests and while transitioning on and off the dock.

**Expected outcome:** No unexpected shutdowns, resets, or boot cycles occur.

If stable operation is demonstrated, troubleshooting may stop.

### 10. Remove From Service for Continued Instability

If the meter will not power on or restarts despite known-good charging infrastructure and adequate battery charge, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** An electrically unreliable meter is kept out of patient care.

## If the Problem Persists

External charging, docking, battery seating, and visible contamination causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include battery failure, charging-circuit problems, internal power faults, software instability, or other service-level defects.

The meter should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Nova Biomedical documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired only by qualified personnel.
- Fully tested for stable operation before return to service.

Repeated restarting during glucose testing is sufficient reason to stop and escalate.

## Clinical Use Tip

A meter that resets during a test cannot be trusted to complete patient identification, testing, or result transmission reliably.

## Final Thought

Maintain testing continuity, rule out charging and battery-related causes first, never ignore unexplained resets, and return the meter to service only after stable operation is demonstrated and documented.

That is successful troubleshooting.
