## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots cooling and temperature faults caused by blocked airflow, room conditions, facility cooling, visible fan issues, or other external thermal conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Stop Scanning if Thermal Reliability Is Questionable
Do not continue scanning through an overtemperature, cooling, or significant fan fault.

Safely end the examination and remove the patient from dependence on the affected system.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care is transferred safely and the MRI system is no longer operating under questionable thermal conditions.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Thermal Condition
Identify whether the system reports:
- Overtemperature
- Cooling unavailable
- Fan fault
- Temperature warning
- Automatic scan inhibition
- Unexpected thermal shutdown

Record the displayed wording and when it occurred.

**Expected outcome:** The exact thermal symptom is documented.

### 3. Check Scanner and Equipment-Room Environment
Inspect for:
- Excessive room temperature
- Failed room HVAC
- Closed or blocked ventilation
- Recent building-system problem
- Water or condensation
- Unusual heat around equipment

Coordinate with Facilities when room or equipment cooling may be involved.

**Expected outcome:** The environment is within its normal controlled condition with no obvious facility failure. If a facility problem exists, keep the MRI out of service until corrected.

### 4. Inspect Accessible Ventilation Areas
Check externally accessible vents and airflow openings for blockage from:
- Stored items
- Dust buildup visible from outside
- Covers
- Furniture
- Temporary equipment

Do not remove internal panels solely to access fans.

**Expected outcome:** External airflow paths are unobstructed. If clearing an external blockage resolves the warning, continue monitoring and verify stable operation.

### 5. Listen for Abnormal Fan Behavior
From a safe external location, note:
- Missing expected airflow
- Grinding
- Rattling
- Cycling
- Unusual mechanical noise

Do not reach into fans or operate equipment with exposed internal components.

**Expected outcome:** No obvious external indication of fan failure is present. Significant abnormal noise warrants removal from service.

### 6. Check for Recent Power or Cooling Events
Determine whether the fault followed:
- Power interruption
- HVAC failure
- Chiller or building-service interruption
- Maintenance work
- Equipment-room access or changes

**Expected outcome:** Any external event correlated with the thermal fault is identified.

### 7. Allow Recovery Only When Appropriate
If the cause was a verified external environmental condition and manufacturer/site procedures permit, allow the system to return to a normal stable condition before restarting.

Do not repeatedly restart a system that immediately returns to an overtemperature state.

**Expected outcome:** The scanner returns to normal thermal status without recurring warning.

### 8. Verify Stable Operation
After correction, confirm:
- No cooling warning remains
- No overtemperature message returns
- Fans and airflow appear normal externally
- System reaches normal ready state
- A controlled operational check does not recreate the fault

**Expected outcome:** Thermal operation remains stable. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Escalate Recurrent Thermal Faults
If room conditions, external airflow, and facility support are normal but the cooling or temperature fault returns, discontinue use.

**Expected outcome:** The system is kept out of service for qualified cooling-system or MRI service evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external environmental and airflow causes have been ruled out. Remaining categories may include internal fans, cooling circuits, sensors, heat exchangers, power electronics cooling, refrigeration or facility interfaces, or service-level control problems.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or service evaluation
- Evaluated using United Imaging documentation and approved diagnostic equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Any unusual heat, smoke, burning odor, liquid leak, or repeated protective shutdown requires immediate removal from service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting. Return the MRI to use only after thermal stability and required operational checks have been verified.

## Clinical Use Tip

An MRI cooling fault should not be treated as a nuisance alarm; thermal problems can affect system reliability and may worsen quickly under repeated scanning.

## Final Thought

Cooling faults demand conservative troubleshooting: protect the patient, verify room conditions and airflow first, and stop if the system continues to overheat. Do not bypass thermal protections, and document both the environmental findings and final stability check.

That is successful troubleshooting.
