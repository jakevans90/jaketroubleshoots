## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots poor battery charging or runtime caused by power source, docking, charging opportunity, connection, battery condition, or environmental factors.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Monitoring
Do not evaluate questionable battery performance while a patient depends on the CARESCAPE ONE for uninterrupted monitoring. Keep the unit connected to a verified power source or transfer monitoring to another verified device.

Remove the monitor from service if the battery is swollen, leaking, unusually hot, physically damaged, or associated with odor or enclosure deformation.

**Expected outcome:** The patient remains continuously monitored and the battery complaint can be evaluated safely.

### 2. Confirm the Battery Complaint
Determine whether the reported issue is:
- No charging indication.
- Battery percentage not increasing.
- Rapid discharge.
- Immediate shutdown after undocking.
- Runtime shorter than previously observed.
- Intermittent charging when docked.

Document whether the complaint occurs with one host or in multiple locations.

**Expected outcome:** The battery symptom is clearly defined and can be reproduced or objectively evaluated.

### 3. Verify the Charging Power Source
Confirm the host monitor, dock, or approved charging arrangement supplying the CARESCAPE ONE is powered and operating normally.

Inspect associated external power connections and verify that the host itself does not have a power or battery problem.

**Expected outcome:** A valid charging source is available. If restoring host or external power allows normal charging, verify battery operation and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Inspect and Reseat the Docking Connection
Remove the CARESCAPE ONE from clinical use and inspect accessible docking contacts and mating surfaces for contamination, damage, obstruction, or incomplete seating.

Reseat the device securely.

**Expected outcome:** The device docks correctly and displays appropriate charging behavior. If reseating restores charging, continue long enough to verify battery operation and then stop.

### 5. Allow Adequate Charging Opportunity
If the battery is deeply discharged, maintain the device on an appropriate charging source before evaluating runtime.

Avoid declaring battery failure based on a brief charging period after prolonged storage or heavy battery use.

**Expected outcome:** Battery indication increases appropriately. If the battery charges and subsequently supports normal intended use, troubleshooting is complete.

### 6. Compare Charging on a Known-Good Compatible Setup
When available, test the CARESCAPE ONE using another known-good compatible host or charging location.

Conversely, compare a known-good CARESCAPE ONE on the original host if this can be done safely and within local workflow.

**Expected outcome:** The complaint follows either the monitor or the charging environment. A host-specific problem should be addressed at the host or docking system rather than misdiagnosed as a battery failure.

### 7. Evaluate Runtime Under Controlled Conditions
After charging, operate the monitor from battery without patient dependence and observe whether it remains stable during representative parameter use.

Do not invent a runtime requirement. Compare performance with manufacturer criteria, facility expectations, battery age/history, or a known-good unit as appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The battery supports acceptable operation based on approved criteria. If it does, troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Inspect for Battery or Device Condition Concerns
Inspect the device for signs of heat, swelling, damage, corrosion, fluid exposure, or other conditions that could affect charging.

Do not open the device or attempt battery-cell repair.

**Expected outcome:** No unsafe physical condition is present. Any abnormal battery condition requires removal from service and qualified replacement or service.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
After correction, verify:
- Charging indication.
- Battery level progression.
- Stable operation after undocking.
- Appropriate redocking behavior.
- Parameter monitoring.
- Alarm functionality.

**Expected outcome:** Charging and battery operation are reliable. If all checks pass, the device may proceed through normal return-to-service processes.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Charging or Runtime Failure
If charging remains absent or runtime remains unacceptable after verifying the power source, docking connection, adequate charge time, and compatible host, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** A monitor with unreliable battery performance is removed from service for appropriate evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve battery degradation, charging circuitry, power management, internal connections, firmware, or the host/docking charging interface.

The CARESCAPE ONE should be:
- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired, configured, or fitted with a replacement battery only by qualified personnel.

After corrective service, verify charging, battery operation, docking, monitoring parameters, alarms, and any required electrical or functional safety checks before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not transport or monitor a patient on a CARESCAPE ONE with uncertain battery capacity unless a reliable alternate power and monitoring plan is immediately available.

## Final Thought

Battery complaints should be separated into charging-source, docking, battery-condition, and actual runtime problems before parts are replaced. Patient monitoring must remain continuous, and unresolved battery reliability concerns require removal from service and clear CCR documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
