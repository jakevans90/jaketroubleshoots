## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot visible steam leakage, unexpected safety-valve release, or abnormal exhaust behavior while identifying external causes and recognizing when pressure-system service is required.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Personnel and Remove the Sterilizer From Clinical Use

Steam and pressurized water can cause serious burns.

If the safety valve is leaking, releasing unexpectedly, or the sterilizer is exhausting abnormally, do not continue routine processing to reproduce the condition repeatedly.

Allow the sterilizer to cool and depressurize fully before inspection. Do not touch, tighten, remove, or manipulate pressurized components while the chamber is hot or under pressure.

Move required instrument processing to another verified sterilizer.

**Expected outcome:** Personnel are protected from steam and pressure hazards, and the suspect sterilizer is not used for clinical loads.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Complaint

Determine whether staff observed:

- Continuous dripping or steam leakage.
- Safety valve release during heat-up.
- Release during the sterilization portion of the cycle.
- Release during exhaust.
- Unusually slow chamber depressurization.
- Steam escaping from another location that may have been mistaken for the safety valve.
- A single event or repeated condition.

Inspect only when the sterilizer is cool and depressurized.

**Expected outcome:** The location and timing of the abnormal exhaust or leakage are clearly identified.

### 3. Verify the Source of the Leak

Visually inspect accessible areas and distinguish the safety-valve area from:

- Door leakage.
- Drain or exhaust outlet.
- Reservoir venting.
- Tubing connections.
- Condensation from normal steam handling.

Do not remove covers or disassemble pressure components simply to identify the source.

**Expected outcome:** The actual leak location is identified. If the reported condition came from a normal exhaust location and controlled testing verifies normal operation, troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Inspect Door and Gasket Condition

With the chamber cool and depressurized, inspect the door and accessible gasket surfaces for:

- Debris.
- Damage.
- Deformation.
- Improper seating.
- Residue.
- Evidence of steam tracks or leakage.

A chamber-sealing problem may alter pressure behavior and can be mistaken for another exhaust problem.

**Expected outcome:** The door and gasket are clean, intact, and properly seated. If an approved external correction eliminates the abnormal leakage and the cycle verifies normally, troubleshooting can stop.

### 5. Check Chamber Loading and Obstructions

Inspect the chamber for conditions that could affect normal steam circulation or exhaust.

Check for:

- Improperly positioned trays.
- Packaging contacting openings.
- Debris.
- Obstructed accessible drain areas.
- Excessive load density.
- Containers that can trap water.

Do not insert tools into internal pressure or exhaust passages.

**Expected outcome:** The chamber and accessible drainage areas are unobstructed. If correcting an external obstruction restores normal exhaust behavior, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 6. Inspect Accessible Exhaust and Drain Areas

When the sterilizer is cool, inspect externally accessible exhaust and drain areas for obvious blockage, contamination, or damage.

Check for:

- Debris.
- Mineral buildup.
- Kinked accessible tubing.
- Restricted external drain routing where applicable.
- Leakage at accessible fittings.

Do not disassemble internal valves, pressure-control assemblies, or safety devices.

**Expected outcome:** No external restriction or leakage condition is present. If an approved accessible correction restores normal exhaust, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

### 7. Check Installation and Environmental Conditions

Verify that the sterilizer is installed correctly on a stable surface and that external exhaust or drainage arrangements have not been altered.

Look for recent changes such as:

- Unit relocation.
- Drain routing changes.
- Items placed against ventilation or exhaust areas.
- External tubing being pinched or redirected.
- Unusual room airflow or heat conditions.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer installation does not create an external restriction or abnormal operating condition. If correcting installation resolves the complaint, troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Observe a Controlled Cycle Only if Safe

If no active leak or hazardous condition is present and controlled testing is appropriate, perform a nonclinical cycle while observing from a safe position.

Monitor for:

- Unexpected steam release.
- Location of leakage.
- Timing of any release.
- Abnormally slow pressure reduction.
- Abnormal sounds or odors.
- Repeated discharge from the safety-valve area.

Do not approach or manipulate the valve during operation.

If the safety valve releases unexpectedly or leakage becomes significant, terminate testing according to safe procedures and remove the sterilizer from service.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer completes heat-up, sterilization, and exhaust without unintended safety-valve discharge or abnormal leakage. If verified, proceed with required return-to-service testing.

### 9. Do Not Adjust the Safety Valve

Do not attempt to adjust, tighten, recalibrate, clean internally, or defeat the safety valve as part of routine external troubleshooting.

The safety valve is a pressure-protection component. Unexpected activation may indicate either a valve problem or another pressure-control problem requiring qualified evaluation.

**Expected outcome:** No unauthorized adjustment is made to a critical pressure-safety component.

### 10. Escalate Any Persistent Safety-Valve or Exhaust Abnormality

If external loading, drainage, installation, door sealing, and obvious obstructions have been ruled out, stop troubleshooting.

Possible remaining categories include:

- Safety-valve malfunction.
- Pressure-control problem.
- Exhaust-valve malfunction.
- Internal plumbing restriction.
- Pressure or temperature sensing issue.
- Control-system problem affecting pressure regulation.

Do not assume which component has failed without appropriate service testing.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer remains out of service until pressure-system integrity and safe operation are verified.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remove the sterilizer from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation.

Pressure and safety-system evaluation should be performed using appropriate Midmark documentation and approved test equipment. Safety-valve service, pressure-system adjustment, internal plumbing work, or calibration should only be performed by qualified personnel.

After repair, complete all applicable pressure, leakage, safety-device, cycle-performance, and sterilization verification required before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting on a pressurized sterilizer is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never continue processing loads in a sterilizer with unexplained steam leakage or abnormal pressure release; move instrument processing to another verified unit.

## Final Thought

Treat abnormal pressure release as a safety issue, rule out accessible external causes without manipulating pressure-protection components, stop when service-level pressure evaluation is required, and document the event and verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
