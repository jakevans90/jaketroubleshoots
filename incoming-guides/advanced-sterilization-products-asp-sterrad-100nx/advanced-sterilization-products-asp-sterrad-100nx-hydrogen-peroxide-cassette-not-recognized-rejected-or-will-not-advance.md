## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot cassette recognition, rejection, seating, or advance problems caused by cassette condition, installation, obstruction, or sterilizer-side detection issues.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Staff and Maintain Sterilization Availability

Do not continue attempting to use a cassette that is damaged, leaking, or otherwise unsafe to handle. Follow facility procedures for hydrogen peroxide sterilant exposure and cassette handling.

Move urgent instrument processing to another verified sterilization pathway when necessary.

**Expected outcome:** Staff are protected from sterilant exposure and clinical processing can continue safely.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Cassette Complaint

Determine whether the cassette:

- Is not detected
- Is immediately rejected
- Is accepted but will not advance
- Stops during use
- Produces a displayed message
- Works intermittently
- Fails only with one cassette

Record the exact on-screen message.

**Expected outcome:** The complaint is narrowed to recognition, acceptance, positioning, or movement.

### 3. Inspect the Cassette Before Reinstallation

Using appropriate handling precautions, visually inspect the cassette for:

- Physical damage
- Leakage
- Deformation
- Contamination
- Incorrect storage or handling condition
- Packaging material that should have been removed
- Any obvious reason it should not be used

Do not use a visibly damaged or leaking cassette.

**Expected outcome:** Only a cassette that appears suitable for use is installed.

If the original cassette is clearly defective and another approved cassette is accepted normally, the issue is resolved after functional verification.

### 4. Verify Correct Cassette Installation

Confirm the cassette is inserted in the correct orientation and fully seated according to the intended operator loading method.

Do not force the cassette or manually manipulate internal advancement mechanisms.

**Expected outcome:** The cassette seats normally without excessive resistance or misalignment.

If reseating restores recognition and the system proceeds normally, continue to a complete cycle verification.

### 5. Inspect the Accessible Cassette Area

With the unit in a safe condition, inspect the accessible cassette loading area for:

- Debris
- Packaging fragments
- Foreign material
- Residue
- Visible mechanical obstruction
- Obvious physical damage

Do not reach into inaccessible mechanisms or disassemble the cassette transport assembly.

**Expected outcome:** The cassette path is externally clean and unobstructed.

If removal of accessible debris restores normal cassette movement, verify operation before returning the unit to service.

### 6. Compare With a Known-Good Approved Cassette

If permitted by facility procedures, test using another approved cassette that is known to be within acceptable use conditions.

This comparison helps separate a cassette-specific problem from a sterilizer recognition or transport problem.

**Expected outcome:** A known-good cassette is recognized and handled normally.

If only one cassette fails, remove that cassette from use and manage it according to facility and manufacturer requirements.

### 7. Verify Door and System Readiness

Confirm the sterilizer is otherwise in a normal ready state, the chamber door is properly closed when required, and there are no unrelated startup, door, or system faults preventing cassette processing.

**Expected outcome:** No separate system condition is blocking cassette recognition or advancement.

### 8. Observe for Repeated Transport or Recognition Failure

If multiple known-good cassettes are rejected, fail to advance, or are intermittently detected, stop repeated insertion attempts.

Do not bypass sensors, manually advance the cassette, or access internal sterilant-delivery components.

**Expected outcome:** A probable sterilizer-side problem is identified without creating additional risk or damage.

### 9. Perform Functional Verification After Correction

If an external cause was corrected, run an appropriate verification cycle using approved procedures.

Confirm the cassette remains recognized and advances as expected throughout the process.

**Expected outcome:** The cassette is accepted and the sterilizer completes the verification cycle without cassette-related interruption.

If successful, troubleshooting can stop after documenting the result.

### 10. Remove From Service for Persistent Cassette Handling Problems

If multiple approved cassettes cannot be recognized or advanced, remove the sterilizer from service.

Persistent failure may involve cassette detection, transport, sterilant delivery, control, or another internal service-level subsystem.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer is prevented from entering clinical use with unreliable sterilant delivery.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as cassette condition, installation, obstruction, and system readiness have been ruled out.

The sterilizer should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench/service evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not defeat cassette sensors or service internal hydrogen peroxide delivery components without appropriate authorization and manufacturer guidance.

After repair, verify cassette recognition, cycle completion, cycle documentation, and all required return-to-service performance checks.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Treat any damaged or leaking hydrogen peroxide cassette as a sterilant exposure concern and follow the facility’s chemical-safety process.

## Final Thought

Cassette problems should be approached by protecting staff, inspecting the cassette, verifying correct installation, and comparing with a known-good approved cassette before assuming an internal failure. Persistent recognition or transport problems require proper escalation and clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
