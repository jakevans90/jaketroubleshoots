## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots rotating touchscreen display, touch-response, or position-detection failures caused by power, positioning, contamination, obstruction, or external connection issues.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Clinical Workflow
If the touchscreen is required to control imaging during patient care, transfer the examination to another verified system rather than continuing with unreliable controls.

Expected outcome: Patient imaging continues using dependable controls while the affected TE X is evaluated.

### 2. Confirm Which Touchscreen Function Has Failed
Determine whether the screen is completely blank, displays an image but does not respond to touch, responds incorrectly, or fails to recognize its physical orientation or position.

Expected outcome: The issue is categorized as display, touch-input, position-sensing, or combined failure.

### 3. Verify Overall System Operation
Confirm the TE X is powered normally and determine whether other displays, controls, indicators, or imaging functions remain operational. Perform a normal restart when clinically appropriate.

Expected outcome: The system completes startup normally. If a normal restart fully restores stable touchscreen operation, verify function and stop.

### 4. Inspect the Screen and Rotation Area
Inspect the touchscreen surface, hinges, rotating mechanism, surrounding structure, and accessible cable paths for contamination, liquid, impact damage, pinching, obstruction, or anything preventing normal movement.

Expected outcome: The screen is clean, undamaged, and rotates through its normal usable range without obstruction.

### 5. Reposition the Display
Move the display gently through normal supported positions without forcing it. Observe whether the image, touch response, or orientation changes when the screen is moved.

Expected outcome: Display and touch operation remain stable throughout normal positioning. Intermittent operation related to movement requires removal from service.

### 6. Check for External Touch Interference
Clean and dry the touchscreen using approved methods. Remove unintended objects pressing on the display edge and ensure gloves, covers, or environmental contamination are not causing continuous or false input.

Expected outcome: Touch commands register correctly and consistently. If cleaning or removing interference restores function, verify multiple controls and stop.

### 7. Verify Normal Display and Control Settings
Using available normal controls, confirm brightness, display mode, screen lock, and other user-accessible settings are appropriate. Do not alter restricted calibration or service settings.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen displays the expected interface and accepts normal input.

### 8. Compare Touch and Non-Touch Controls
Where the TE X provides other accessible controls, determine whether the same functions can be operated normally through them. This helps separate touchscreen input failure from a broader system freeze.

Expected outcome: Other controls operate normally if the failure is isolated to the touchscreen.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
Verify a stable image, reliable touch response across the required interface, correct screen-position behavior, and normal operation while the touchscreen is placed in typical clinical positions.

Expected outcome: Display, touch, and position functions remain stable. If any function remains intermittent, remove the system from service.

## If the Problem Persists

External obstruction, contamination, positioning, normal settings, and basic system-operation causes have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve display hardware, touch sensing, position sensing, internal cabling, system software, or service-level configuration.

Remove the TE X from service if required controls cannot be trusted. Label it **Out of Service** and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Mindray documentation and approved test equipment. Do not disassemble the rotating display or perform internal cable or board repairs outside authorized procedures.

Return to service only after all display, touch, position-detection, and applicable functional checks pass.

## Clinical Use Tip

A touchscreen that responds intermittently can cause unintended control selections; use another verified system until operation is consistently reliable.

## Final Thought

Maintain patient safety, separate display problems from touch and position-sensing problems, eliminate simple external causes first, and escalate any unreliable control system rather than assuming a specific internal failure.

That is successful troubleshooting.
