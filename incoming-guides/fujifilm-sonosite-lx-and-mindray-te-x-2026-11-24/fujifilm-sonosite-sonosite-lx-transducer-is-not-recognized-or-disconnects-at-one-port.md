## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots a transducer that is missing, intermittently disconnects, or works on one connection but not another.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Imaging Availability
If the transducer is required for an active procedure, move clinical imaging to another verified transducer or ultrasound system before troubleshooting. Do not troubleshoot intermittent imaging equipment while clinicians depend on it.

Expected outcome: Patient care continues using reliable equipment.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition
Ask which transducer and connection were involved, whether the failure is constant or intermittent, and whether movement of the connector or cable affects recognition. Observe the reported condition when practical.

Expected outcome: The failure is confirmed as transducer-specific, port-specific, intermittent, or system-wide.

### 3. Inspect the Transducer and Cable
Disconnect the transducer according to normal handling practices. Inspect the housing, cable, strain relief, connector, and accessible contact area for contamination, bent or damaged features, fluid intrusion, or physical damage.

Do not use a transducer with damaged insulation, exposed conductors, cracked housing, or questionable fluid integrity.

Expected outcome: The transducer and cable are clean, dry, and free of visible damage. If damage is found, remove the transducer from service and troubleshooting can stop.

### 4. Reseat the Connection
Reconnect the transducer fully and verify that the connector is properly seated and secured without excessive force. Restart normal imaging operation and verify whether the transducer remains recognized.

Expected outcome: The transducer is consistently identified and remains connected. If recognition is restored and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 5. Compare With a Known-Good Transducer
Connect a compatible known-good transducer to the suspect connection and verify recognition and imaging.

Expected outcome: A known-good transducer operates normally. If only the original transducer fails, remove that transducer from service for evaluation.

### 6. Compare Available Connections
If the system provides another appropriate transducer connection, test the suspect transducer and a known-good transducer on the alternate connection without forcing incompatible connectors.

Expected outcome: Testing identifies whether the condition follows the transducer or remains associated with one connection. A repeatable port-specific failure requires service evaluation.

### 7. Check for Mechanical Sensitivity
With no patient dependent on the system, observe whether normal gentle repositioning of the transducer cable or connector causes recognition to appear and disappear. Do not flex, twist, or stress the cable excessively.

Expected outcome: Recognition remains stable during normal handling. Any repeatable dropout indicates an unreliable transducer or connection that should not be returned to clinical use.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification
Verify that the transducer is recognized, produces a stable image, responds to normal imaging controls, and remains connected through normal positioning.

Expected outcome: Stable recognition and imaging are confirmed. Troubleshooting can stop and the system may be returned to service if all applicable safety and functional checks pass.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as incomplete connection, visible contamination, cable damage, and a defective transducer have been ruled out. A persistent port-specific problem may involve the connector assembly, internal interface electronics, configuration, or another service-level condition.

Remove the affected system or connection from service when reliable imaging cannot be assured. Label the equipment **Out of Service** as appropriate and send it for repair or bench evaluation using Fujifilm Sonosite service documentation and approved test equipment. Repairs or configuration changes should be performed only by qualified personnel.

After corrective work, complete appropriate functional and electrical-safety testing before return to clinical service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not allow a procedure to continue with a transducer that intermittently disappears or loses imaging when its cable is moved.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, establish whether the failure follows the transducer or the connection, verify external causes before assuming internal failure, and document the evidence supporting repair or return to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
