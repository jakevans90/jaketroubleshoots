## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots missing external video, monitor, or recording connections caused by cables, input selection, device power, configuration, or compatibility.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Clinical Workflow
If an external display or recorder is required for a procedure, provide another verified display or recording path before troubleshooting the failed connection.

Expected outcome: The procedure does not depend on an unreliable external video path.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Failure
Determine whether the external device is not detected, displays no image, intermittently drops video, or fails only to record while video remains visible.

Expected outcome: The failure is clearly defined as detection, display, signal stability, or recording.

### 3. Verify External Device Power
Confirm the monitor or recording device is powered on and functioning normally.

Expected outcome: The external device starts normally and is ready to accept an input signal.

### 4. Verify Input Selection
Confirm the external monitor or recorder is set to the input corresponding to the connected ultrasound output.

Expected outcome: The correct input is selected. If video appears and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 5. Inspect and Reseat the Video Cable
Inspect the cable and accessible connectors for bent contacts, looseness, damage, or strain. Reseat both ends securely without forcing connectors.

Expected outcome: Connections are secure and the cable shows no visible damage.

### 6. Substitute a Known-Good Cable
Replace the suspect cable with a compatible known-good cable.

Expected outcome: Normal video with the known-good cable identifies the original cable as defective.

### 7. Test the External Device With Another Source
When practical, verify the monitor or recorder with another compatible known-good video source.

Expected outcome: The external device correctly displays or records another source, ruling out the external device itself.

### 8. Test the Sonosite LX With Another Known-Good Display
Connect the ultrasound system to a compatible known-good monitor or recording device using an appropriate cable.

Expected outcome: Successful output identifies the original external device or interface path as the problem. Failure with multiple known-good devices indicates a system-side concern.

### 9. Verify Normal Output Settings
Check authorized user-accessible display or output settings and compare them with a known-working configuration. Do not enter service menus or make unsupported output changes.

Expected outcome: Appropriate output configuration is confirmed.

### 10. Perform Final Functional Verification
Verify a stable image on the external monitor and, when applicable, make a controlled test recording and confirm playback.

Expected outcome: External video remains stable and recording functions correctly. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

Power, input selection, external cables, known-good displays or recorders, and accessible output configuration have been checked. Persistent failure may involve the ultrasound video-output interface, software, output configuration, internal electronics, or compatibility at the receiving device.

Remove the affected video function or Sonosite LX from service when the external display or recording path is required for the intended clinical use. Label equipment **Out of Service** as appropriate and send it for repair or bench evaluation using Fujifilm Sonosite documentation and approved test equipment.

After corrective action, verify the entire video and recording path before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Verify the complete video path before a procedure when the external monitor or recorder is essential to the clinical workflow.

## Final Thought

External video failures should be isolated one connection at a time. Verify power, input selection, cables, receiving equipment, and authorized settings before assuming an internal output failure, then document and escalate appropriately.

That is successful troubleshooting.
