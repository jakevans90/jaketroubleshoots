## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots image artifacts limited to one transducer by checking technique, coupling, cable condition, connections, environment, and transducer integrity.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Diagnostic Reliability
Do not continue clinical imaging with a transducer producing unexplained artifacts that could affect interpretation. Move the examination to another verified transducer or ultrasound system.

Expected outcome: Patient imaging continues using equipment with reliable image quality.

### 2. Confirm the Artifact
Determine whether the issue is dropout, repeated echoes, electrical noise, nonuniform brightness, or another reproducible image defect. Confirm whether it appears only with one transducer.

Expected outcome: The artifact is reproduced and its relationship to the transducer is established.

### 3. Verify Coupling and Scan Conditions
Use appropriate ultrasound coupling and a suitable test object or phantom when available. Eliminate air gaps and obvious technique-related artifact.

Expected outcome: Artifacts caused by inadequate coupling or positioning disappear. If image quality becomes normal and remains reproducible, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 4. Inspect the Transducer
Inspect the acoustic surface, housing, cable, strain relief, and connector for cracks, cuts, dents, delamination, fluid intrusion, contamination, or other visible damage.

Expected outcome: No visible defect is present. If significant damage is found, remove the transducer from service.

### 5. Reseat the Transducer
Disconnect and reconnect the transducer carefully, confirming proper seating and secure attachment.

Expected outcome: The image remains stable with no connector-related artifact.

### 6. Compare With a Known-Good Transducer
Using comparable imaging conditions, test a compatible known-good transducer on the same system.

Expected outcome: The known-good transducer produces a normal image. If the artifact follows only the original transducer, remove that transducer from service for evaluation.

### 7. Check for Environmental Interference
Evaluate whether the noise changes when nearby nonessential electrical equipment is moved, disconnected where appropriate, or when the ultrasound system is tested in another known-good location.

Expected outcome: Environmental electrical interference is ruled out or identified as the cause.

### 8. Evaluate Normal Cable Positioning
Observe the image while the transducer cable is placed through normal operating positions without excessive bending or twisting.

Expected outcome: The image remains stable. Reproducible artifact caused by ordinary cable movement indicates an unreliable transducer assembly.

### 9. Perform Final Image Verification
Using a suitable test object or approved quality-assurance method, verify uniformity and absence of the reported artifact.

Expected outcome: Image quality is stable and clinically appropriate. Troubleshooting can stop when the corrected condition is reproducible.

## If the Problem Persists

Coupling, positioning, environmental effects, connections, and comparison with a known-good transducer have been evaluated. Persistent artifact may involve transducer elements, cable integrity, connector electronics, the transducer interface, processing electronics, calibration, or another internal condition.

Remove the affected transducer or system from service as appropriate and label it **Out of Service**. Send it for repair or bench evaluation using Fujifilm Sonosite documentation and approved ultrasound test equipment. Repairs and calibration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Complete appropriate image-quality and functional testing before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

When an artifact appears on only one transducer, compare against a known-good transducer before changing system-wide settings.
