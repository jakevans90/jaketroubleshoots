## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot repeat cycle cancellations during vacuum, sterilant delivery, diffusion, or plasma phases caused by load, packaging, consumables, chamber, or utility conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Care and Sterility Assurance

Treat every canceled sterilization cycle as unsuccessful unless the system and facility procedures explicitly establish otherwise. Do not release the load for patient use.

Route instruments through another validated sterilization process or verified sterilizer.

**Expected outcome:** Instruments from the canceled cycle remain segregated and patient care continues through an approved alternative.

### 2. Capture the Exact Cancellation Point

Record:

- Displayed message
- Phase when cancellation occurred
- Whether the cancellation happens at the same phase repeatedly
- Load contents
- Cycle selected
- Whether the unit previously completed cycles normally

Do not clear useful information before documenting it.

**Expected outcome:** The cancellation is characterized well enough to determine whether it follows the load, consumables, or sterilizer.

### 3. Inspect the Load and Packaging

Check for externally verifiable load problems such as:

- Excessive load density
- Items positioned against the chamber
- Incorrectly arranged trays
- Damaged packaging
- Restricted openings or channels
- Visible moisture
- Materials or packaging not approved for the selected process

Clinical Engineering should coordinate questionable load preparation with Sterile Processing rather than altering validated processing practices.

**Expected outcome:** The load is dry, correctly prepared, appropriately positioned, and compatible with the intended process.

### 4. Verify the Chamber and Door Area

With the system safe and idle, inspect accessible areas for:

- Debris
- Packaging trapped near the door
- Visible contamination
- Obstruction around the chamber opening
- Obvious seal damage visible without disassembly

**Expected outcome:** The chamber opening and accessible sealing surfaces are clean and unobstructed.

### 5. Verify Sterilant Cassette Condition

Confirm that an approved cassette is recognized, properly installed, undamaged, and shows usable capacity.

Do not attempt to reuse, refill, repair, or modify a questionable cassette.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilant source is recognized and available for the requested cycle.

### 6. Compare With a Controlled Load

If facility procedures allow, evaluate the sterilizer using a properly prepared controlled or routine load known to process successfully.

This helps distinguish a load-related cancellation from a sterilizer-related problem.

**Expected outcome:** A controlled load proceeds normally. If the original load configuration caused the cancellation, correct the processing issue and stop troubleshooting after successful verification.

### 7. Check Environmental and Utility Conditions

Verify that the sterilizer has stable electrical power and that required facility utilities and environmental conditions have not been interrupted or altered.

Check for recent:

- Power disturbances
- Facility work
- Utility interruptions
- Room environmental changes
- Relocation or service activity

Do not alter facility utilities or manufacturer configurations without authorization.

**Expected outcome:** No external facility condition explains the phase cancellation.

### 8. Observe for Abnormal External Behavior

During an approved test cycle, observe externally for:

- Unusual pump noise
- Repeated pauses
- Unexpected restart
- Door or chamber abnormalities
- Recurrent cancellation at the same phase

Do not bypass interlocks or operate with covers removed.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer progresses normally without abnormal external behavior.

### 9. Perform Final Cycle Verification

If the suspected external cause is corrected, complete the facility-required test or verification process before releasing the sterilizer for routine use.

**Expected outcome:** A complete cycle finishes normally with the expected cycle record and required quality indicators. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Repeat Phase Cancellations

If cancellations recur with correctly prepared loads, recognized consumables, stable utilities, and no accessible obstruction, remove the sterilizer from service.

**Expected outcome:** Further investigation is transferred to qualified service personnel.

## If the Problem Persists

External load, packaging, cassette, chamber-access, power, and environmental causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include the vacuum system, valves, sterilant-delivery system, pressure sensing, plasma-generation system, control system, or another service-level condition.

The sterilizer should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or qualified service evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete required post-repair functional and sterilization-process verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any load from a canceled cycle must remain considered nonsterile until it completes an approved sterilization process.

## Final Thought

A canceled sterilization cycle requires both patient-safety control of the load and logical isolation of load, consumable, chamber, utility, and equipment causes before service-level escalation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
