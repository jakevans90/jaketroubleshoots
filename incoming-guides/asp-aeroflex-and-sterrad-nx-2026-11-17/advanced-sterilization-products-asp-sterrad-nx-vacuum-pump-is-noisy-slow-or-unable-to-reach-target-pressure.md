## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot abnormal vacuum performance by checking load, chamber, door sealing, environment, power, external airflow, and repeatability before service-level evaluation.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Sterility Assurance

If vacuum performance is unreliable or cycles are canceling, stop routine instrument processing on the affected sterilizer and redirect loads to verified equipment.

**Expected outcome:** No load is released from a cycle with questionable vacuum performance.

### 2. Characterize the Vacuum Complaint

Determine whether the sterilizer:

- Sounds louder than normal
- Takes unusually long during vacuum phases
- Cancels during vacuum
- Operates intermittently
- Shows the problem only with certain loads

Record any displayed message without assuming the pump itself is defective.

**Expected outcome:** The vacuum symptom is documented objectively.

### 3. Inspect the Load

Check for:

- Overloading
- Incorrect placement
- Packaging against the chamber
- Moisture
- Items that may interfere with evacuation
- Loads that differ significantly from those previously processed successfully

**Expected outcome:** The load is correctly prepared and arranged.

### 4. Inspect Chamber and Door Areas

With the unit safe and idle, inspect accessible areas for:

- Debris
- Packaging fragments
- Visible contamination
- Material interfering with the door seal
- Obvious seal abnormalities

**Expected outcome:** The chamber opening is clean and unobstructed.

### 5. Verify Stable Electrical Power

Confirm that the sterilizer is connected to its intended power source and that there is no evidence of intermittent power, loose external connection, or recent electrical disturbance.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer has stable facility power.

### 6. Check Accessible Ventilation Areas

Inspect external ventilation openings for obvious blockage, accumulated material, or objects restricting airflow.

Do not remove panels or access internal pump components.

**Expected outcome:** External ventilation is unobstructed.

### 7. Compare Empty or Controlled Testing

When approved by facility procedures, compare vacuum behavior using an appropriate controlled verification condition rather than the suspect clinical load.

Listen for major changes in sound and observe whether the cancellation repeats.

**Expected outcome:** Normal behavior with a controlled test points toward load-related conditions. Persistent abnormal behavior points toward the sterilizer.

### 8. Stop for Significant Mechanical Symptoms

Immediately discontinue testing if there is:

- Grinding
- Severe vibration
- Smoke
- Burning odor
- Fluid leakage
- Excessive heat
- Repeated electrical reset

**Expected outcome:** Potential equipment damage or safety risk is not worsened through repeated operation.

### 9. Verify Operation After External Correction

If an obstruction, load issue, or external environmental cause is corrected, perform the required verification cycle.

**Expected outcome:** Vacuum phases proceed normally without abnormal noise or cancellation. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Vacuum Problems

If abnormal noise, slow evacuation, or vacuum-phase cancellation continues with proper loads and no external obstruction, remove the sterilizer from service.

**Expected outcome:** Qualified service evaluates the vacuum system using manufacturer-approved procedures.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as load condition, chamber obstruction, door-area contamination, power problems, and ventilation restriction have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include the vacuum pump, valves, pressure sensing, internal leaks, tubing, control circuitry, or another service-level condition.

The sterilizer should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or qualified service evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired only by qualified personnel

Complete all required post-repair cycle and sterilization-process verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A cycle that cannot establish the required vacuum condition must not be treated as a successful sterilization cycle.

Abnormal vacuum behavior should be verified without assuming a failed pump. Rule out external causes first, but stop testing promptly when persistent mechanical symptoms point to service-level repair.
