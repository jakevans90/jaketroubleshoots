## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses handpiece heating or shutdown related to irrigation, installation, prolonged operation, cable condition, disposables, ventilation, or handpiece malfunction.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Immediately

Stop using a handpiece that becomes unusually hot, repeatedly stops, smells abnormal, or behaves unpredictably. Move to an approved replacement handpiece or alternate surgical method.

Do not continue operation to see whether the problem clears during patient use.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is no longer exposed to an overheating or unreliable handpiece.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Ask whether the handpiece felt hot externally, stopped producing vibration, shut down after a period of use, or resumed after resting.

Inspect the handpiece for discoloration, odor, damage, contamination, or evidence of fluid-related problems.

**Expected outcome:** The failure pattern and any visible signs of overheating are documented.

### 3. Verify Irrigation Delivery

Confirm adequate irrigation is reaching the handpiece. Check the fluid source, tubing, cartridge installation, clamps, kinks, and handpiece-side connections.

Poor or absent irrigation must be corrected before further functional testing.

**Expected outcome:** Irrigation is stable and appropriate for controlled testing. If restoring irrigation prevents heating and operation remains stable, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Inspect Tip, Flue, and Cartridge Installation

Verify the disposable components are compatible and correctly installed. Look for misalignment, damaged components, obstruction, or incorrect seating that could contribute to abnormal handpiece operation.

**Expected outcome:** The handpiece assembly is complete, correctly installed, and free of visible obstruction.

### 5. Inspect the Handpiece Cable and Connector

Check the cable, strain relief, and connector for damage, excessive bending, loose engagement, contamination, or heat-related discoloration.

Do not use a handpiece with damaged insulation or connector components.

**Expected outcome:** The cable and connector are intact and securely connected.

### 6. Check the Operating Environment

Confirm the handpiece cable and console ventilation are not obstructed and that the handpiece is not being placed against heat-retaining materials during testing.

Allow a handpiece that has become abnormally warm to cool before any bench evaluation.

**Expected outcome:** Environmental factors are not contributing to excessive heat.

### 7. Substitute Known-Good Disposables

Install a verified compatible disposable setup and repeat the nonclinical functional test while monitoring for abnormal warming or shutdown.

**Expected outcome:** The problem disappears with replacement disposables or persists with the handpiece itself.

### 8. Compare With a Known-Good Handpiece

If available, connect and tune a known-good compatible handpiece using the same verified console setup.

Do not continue testing a handpiece that rapidly overheats.

**Expected outcome:** A known-good handpiece operates normally, isolating the original handpiece, or the same behavior occurs and points toward a console-side problem.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

For any setup considered resolved, verify successful tuning, stable irrigation, aspiration, vibration output, and sustained nonclinical operation without abnormal warming, stopping, odor, or alarms.

**Expected outcome:** The system remains stable during functional testing. Troubleshooting can stop only when abnormal heating does not recur.

### 10. Escalate Any Persistent Overheating

A handpiece that repeatedly overheats, shuts down, or shows heat damage should not be returned to service based on temporary recovery.

**Expected outcome:** The affected handpiece or console is removed from service for qualified evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as irrigation, disposable installation, cable condition, setup, and environment have been ruled out. The remaining condition may involve the handpiece transducer, internal handpiece components, console drive system, thermal sensing, or another service-level fault.

The affected equipment should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Integra LifeSciences documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired only by qualified personnel.
- Functionally tested for sustained operation before return to service.

Knowing when to stop testing an overheating component is an important part of safe troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Treat unexpected handpiece heat as a patient-safety issue even if the system continues to operate or the condition temporarily clears.

## Final Thought

An overheating handpiece should be treated conservatively. Verify cooling-related external conditions such as irrigation and assembly first, compare with known-good components, and escalate any repeated heat or shutdown condition rather than risking recurrence in surgery.

That is successful troubleshooting.
