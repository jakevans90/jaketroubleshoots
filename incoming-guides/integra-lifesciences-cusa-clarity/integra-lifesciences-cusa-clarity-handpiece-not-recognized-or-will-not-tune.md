## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses handpiece detection or tuning failures caused by connections, installation, accessories, contamination, handpiece condition, or incompatible setup.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Stop Clinical Dependence on the Handpiece

If the handpiece cannot be recognized or tuned during a procedure, stop using the affected handpiece. Maintain surgical continuity with an approved alternate method or verified replacement system.

Do not repeatedly manipulate connectors while the system is being relied upon clinically.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care continues safely while the affected handpiece and console are isolated for troubleshooting.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Failure

Determine whether the handpiece is not detected at all, is detected intermittently, begins tuning but fails, or had been functioning before becoming unavailable.

Observe the console response during a controlled nonclinical connection and tuning attempt.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is clearly categorized as recognition, intermittent connection, or tuning failure.

### 3. Inspect the Handpiece Cable and Connector

Power down or place the system in the appropriate nonactive state before handling connections. Inspect the handpiece cable, connector body, strain relief, and accessible contacts for damage, contamination, bending, moisture, or incomplete seating.

Do not probe connector contacts with tools or attempt internal connector repair.

**Expected outcome:** The cable and connector are clean, dry, undamaged, and fully seated. If reseating restores stable recognition and tuning, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Verify Correct Handpiece and Accessory Combination

Confirm the connected handpiece, tip, flue, cartridge, and other required disposable components are appropriate for the intended setup and installed in the correct configuration.

Do not substitute components that are not approved for the system.

**Expected outcome:** The handpiece and accessories form a compatible, complete setup.

### 5. Inspect Tip and Disposable Installation

Examine the installed tip, flue, cartridge, and related external components for incorrect seating, looseness, visible deformation, contamination, or assembly errors that could interfere with tuning.

Correct installation only using approved normal assembly methods.

**Expected outcome:** All required disposable components are correctly installed and securely seated. If tuning succeeds afterward, perform functional verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 6. Check for Moisture or Contamination at Connections

Inspect accessible handpiece and console connection areas for fluid, residue, or contamination. Follow approved cleaning and drying practices before retesting.

Do not connect wet components.

**Expected outcome:** All connection surfaces are clean and dry, ruling out contamination as the cause.

### 7. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Handpiece When Available

Using an approved known-good handpiece and appropriate accessories, repeat recognition and tuning.

Compare the result without changing unrelated system configuration.

**Expected outcome:** A known-good handpiece tunes successfully, identifying the original handpiece or its associated setup as the likely problem, or the failure follows the console and requires further evaluation.

### 8. Compare With Known-Good Disposable Components

If recognition occurs but tuning still fails, replace the applicable disposable components with verified compatible components when appropriate.

Do not reuse suspect damaged disposables for repeated testing.

**Expected outcome:** Tuning succeeds with known-good disposables, or the failure persists despite verified external components.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Once the handpiece is recognized and tuning completes, verify stable recognition, normal control response, appropriate irrigation and aspiration setup, and consistent nonclinical operation.

Perform any required system checks before return to clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** The handpiece remains recognized, tuning completes successfully, and the system functions normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Recognition or Tuning Failure

If verified connections, correct assembly, known-good disposables, and an appropriate comparison handpiece do not resolve the problem, remove the affected component or console from service as indicated.

**Expected outcome:** The unresolved fault is referred for qualified service-level evaluation without unnecessary internal repair attempts.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. The remaining condition may involve the handpiece transducer assembly, cable, connector interface, console detection circuitry, tuning electronics, internal communication, or service-level configuration.

The affected equipment should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Integra LifeSciences documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Functionally tested with approved accessories before return to service.

Knowing when to stop after external handpiece, connection, and disposable checks is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not return a handpiece to surgery merely because recognition briefly returns; verify repeatable tuning and stable operation before clinical use.

## Final Thought

Patient safety comes first. Verify the entire external handpiece setup before assuming an internal tuning failure, use known-good comparisons to isolate the problem, and escalate when the fault remains beyond safe external troubleshooting.

That is successful troubleshooting.
