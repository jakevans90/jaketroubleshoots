## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses vaporizer mounting, interlock, or agent-delivery problems caused by seating, compatibility, control position, filling, damage, or external vaporizer faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient From Unreliable Agent Delivery
Do not troubleshoot an anesthesia-agent delivery problem while a patient relies on the affected vaporizer. Maintain anesthesia using another verified delivery method or machine.

Turn the affected vaporizer off before inspection unless a specific controlled test requires otherwise.

**Expected outcome:** Patient anesthesia is maintained independently and the vaporizer can be safely evaluated.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Problem
Determine whether the vaporizer will not mount, will not lock, cannot be selected because of the interlock, turns normally but produces no measured agent, or produces unstable delivery.

Record which vaporizer and mounting position are involved.

**Expected outcome:** The symptom is clearly identified and reproducible under controlled conditions.

### 3. Verify Vaporizer Compatibility
Confirm that the vaporizer, mounting system, and agent type are approved for use with the Atlan A100 configuration.

Do not force an incompatible vaporizer onto the mount.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer is appropriate for the installed mounting system. An incompatible unit should be removed and replaced with the correct approved device.

### 4. Inspect the Vaporizer and Mount
With the vaporizer off, inspect the mounting interface for contamination, bent or damaged external features, loose hardware, foreign material, or a vaporizer that is not fully seated.

Remove and reinstall the vaporizer using its normal approved mounting method.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer seats fully and locks securely without force. If correct seating resolves the problem, continue to agent verification.

### 5. Check the Interlock Mechanism Externally
Verify that adjacent vaporizers are fully off and that only one vaporizer can be selected at a time as intended.

Inspect for an improperly seated neighboring vaporizer or external obstruction preventing normal interlock movement.

**Expected outcome:** The interlock operates normally and prevents unintended simultaneous activation. If not, remove the machine from service rather than bypassing or forcing the interlock.

### 6. Check Agent Level and Filling Condition
Confirm the vaporizer contains the correct anesthetic agent and that the fill connection or cap is properly secured.

Look for external leakage, overfill evidence, contamination, or damage. Do not continue using a vaporizer with suspected wrong-agent filling or leakage.

**Expected outcome:** Correct agent is present, the fill system is secured, and there is no visible leak or contamination.

### 7. Compare With a Known-Good Vaporizer
If available, install a compatible known-good vaporizer on the same mounting position and repeat the controlled test.

Alternatively, test the suspect vaporizer on an approved compatible system according to facility procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The comparison isolates the problem to either the vaporizer or the anesthesia-machine mounting position. If the known-good vaporizer operates correctly, remove the suspect vaporizer from service.

### 8. Verify Agent Delivery With Approved Analysis Equipment
Under controlled bench conditions, use an approved gas analyzer to confirm that agent output responds appropriately when the vaporizer is operated.

Verify analyzer calibration, sampling setup, and water-trap condition first.

**Expected outcome:** Measured agent delivery changes predictably with vaporizer operation. If not, do not adjust internal vaporizer calibration without proper authorization and service procedures.

### 9. Complete System and Functional Testing
After any external correction or vaporizer substitution, complete the appropriate anesthesia-machine system test and verify fresh-gas delivery, agent measurement, interlock function, and alarms.

**Expected outcome:** Vaporizer mounting, interlock, and measured agent delivery all function normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Unresolved Mount or Delivery Problems
If mounting, interlock, compatibility, agent fill, and known-good comparisons do not resolve the issue, remove the affected vaporizer or anesthesia machine from service as appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The defective component is clearly isolated or referred for qualified service evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes including incorrect seating, incompatible vaporizer, adjacent-vaporizer position, fill condition, and analyzer setup have been ruled out. The problem may involve the vaporizer itself, mounting interface, interlock mechanism, internal gas path, or another service-level component.

The affected equipment should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Drager and vaporizer manufacturer documentation.
- Repaired or calibrated only by qualified personnel using approved test equipment.
- Fully tested for mounting security, interlock operation, agent output, leaks, and anesthesia-machine function before return to service.

Never defeat or bypass a vaporizer interlock to keep the machine operational.

## Clinical Use Tip

When agent delivery is uncertain, use another verified vaporizer or anesthesia machine rather than attempting troubleshooting during a case.

## Final Thought

Agent delivery must be verified, not assumed. Start with compatibility, mounting, interlock position, fill condition, and known-good substitution before considering internal failure, and escalate any unresolved or unsafe vaporizer condition.

That is successful troubleshooting.
