## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots touchscreen response problems involving gloves, contamination, protective films, positioning, external damage, or a persistent touch-interface fault.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Defibrillation Capability

If the DEFIGARD Touch 7 is connected to or supporting a patient and touchscreen control is unreliable, transfer monitoring and emergency-response readiness to another verified defibrillator before troubleshooting.

Do not continue using a defibrillator whose controls cannot be operated predictably.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care continues on reliable equipment and the affected unit is available for safe evaluation.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Touchscreen Problem

Determine whether the screen:

- Does not respond anywhere.
- Responds only in certain areas.
- Selects an adjacent control.
- Requires excessive pressure.
- Works with bare fingers but not while wearing gloves.
- Responds intermittently.

Restart the unit only when it is not being relied upon for patient care and observe whether normal touch operation returns.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is reproduced consistently or is shown to have been temporary. If normal operation returns and remains reliable through functional testing, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 3. Inspect the Screen Surface

Check the display for:

- Moisture.
- Gel.
- Cleaning residue.
- Blood or other contamination.
- Cracked glass.
- Deep scratches.
- Impact damage.
- A loose or lifting protective film.

Clean and dry the display using approved facility and manufacturer-compatible methods.

**Expected outcome:** The screen is clean, dry, undamaged, and free of anything that could interfere with touch detection. If cleaning restores reliable operation, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Compare Gloved and Ungloved Operation

Using clean hands and appropriate gloves, compare touchscreen response without gloves and with the glove type normally used by clinical staff.

Do not change clinical glove requirements simply to make the device usable.

**Expected outcome:** The touchscreen responds reliably during normal intended operation. If the problem occurs only with a specific glove type, document the finding and verify whether an approved operating method or configuration applies before returning the device to service.

### 5. Check for External Screen Accessories

Inspect for unauthorized screen protectors, overlays, adhesive labels, transport covers, or other materials contacting the touchscreen.

Remove only accessories that are clearly nonessential and approved for removal.

**Expected outcome:** Nothing external is interfering with the touchscreen. If removing an inappropriate overlay restores normal response, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 6. Check Control Behavior Across the Display

With the device removed from clinical use, test several normal touchscreen selections across different areas of the screen.

Also verify any available physical controls required for operation.

Do not enter restricted service or calibration menus unless authorized by manufacturer documentation.

**Expected outcome:** Touch locations correspond correctly with displayed controls and respond consistently. Persistent offset, dead areas, or unintended selections indicate the unit should remain out of service.

### 7. Check Environmental Conditions

Determine whether the problem occurs only when the screen is wet, heavily contaminated, extremely cold or warm, or immediately after cleaning.

Allow the unit to return to normal operating conditions before retesting.

**Expected outcome:** Touch performance remains reliable under normal clinical conditions.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify normal startup and accessible operational controls according to approved procedures. Confirm that touch selections are repeatable and do not activate unintended functions.

**Expected outcome:** The touchscreen responds accurately and consistently. If so, document the correction and return the device to service according to facility policy.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Persistent dead areas, inaccurate touch locations, intermittent response, or unreliable operation may indicate a touchscreen, display-interface, configuration, or other service-level problem.

The device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not attempt internal display or board-level repair without authorized service procedures. Complete appropriate operational and electrical-safety testing before return to service when required.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A defibrillator with unpredictable touchscreen control should not remain available for emergency response even if basic monitoring still works.

## Final Thought

Protect the patient first, eliminate simple screen and environmental causes, verify reliable control operation, and remove the defibrillator from service when touchscreen behavior remains unpredictable. Clear CCR documentation should identify what was found and how operation was verified.

That is successful troubleshooting.
