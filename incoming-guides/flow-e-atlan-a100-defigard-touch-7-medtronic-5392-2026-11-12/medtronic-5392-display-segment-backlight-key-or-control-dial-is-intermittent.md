## What This Guide Helps With

Intermittent display or control operation caused by contamination, mechanical damage, battery condition, stuck controls, or a device user-interface fault.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient From Unreliable Controls

Do not continue using a pacemaker on a patient if the operator cannot reliably read settings or adjust controls. Transfer pacing support to another verified device before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** Clinical care no longer depends on an unreliable display or control interface.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Interface Problem

Identify whether the problem affects a display segment, backlight, key, rotary control, or multiple interface functions. Record whether the fault occurs continuously or only during movement or adjustment.

**Expected outcome:** The failed interface function is clearly identified and reproducible.

### 3. Inspect the Exterior

Inspect the display window, controls, control shafts, key surfaces, enclosure seams, and surrounding areas for cracking, impact damage, contamination, residue, or fluid intrusion.

**Expected outcome:** No obvious external damage or contamination is affecting normal operation.

### 4. Verify Battery Condition

Install known-good approved batteries if battery condition is uncertain. Observe whether display brightness or control behavior changes.

**Expected outcome:** Interface operation remains stable with a verified power source.

### 5. Exercise Controls Normally

Operate each accessible key and dial through its normal range without forcing it. Observe for missed inputs, repeated inputs, sticking, delayed response, or changes in the displayed setting.

**Expected outcome:** Every control responds once and consistently to each intended action.

### 6. Check Display Readability

Observe all displayed information during startup and normal bench operation. Look for missing segments, flickering, partial characters, intermittent backlighting, or information that cannot be read reliably.

**Expected outcome:** Display information remains complete and clearly visible.

### 7. Check for Position-Related Intermittency

Reposition the device gently on the bench and observe the display and controls. Do not flex the enclosure or intentionally stress controls.

**Expected outcome:** Operation does not change during normal handling.

### 8. Compare Settings With Actual Function

Using approved test equipment, verify that changes made with the rate, output, sensitivity, or other applicable controls produce the expected functional response.

**Expected outcome:** Displayed settings and actual pacemaker function remain consistent.

### 9. Complete Functional Verification

Verify pacing, sensing, alarms, display, keys, dials, and startup behavior using applicable service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** All user-interface functions operate reliably. If so, troubleshooting can stop and the device may be returned to service.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Interface Problems

If a display segment, backlight, key, or control dial remains intermittent, remove the device from service even if pacing output currently appears normal.

**Expected outcome:** An unreliable user interface is referred for qualified repair.

## If the Problem Persists

External contamination, battery condition, and obvious mechanical causes have been ruled out. The remaining fault may involve an internal display, keypad, control encoder, connection, or control-processing problem.

The pacemaker should be removed from service, labeled **Out of Service**, and sent for qualified bench evaluation. Use approved Medtronic documentation and test equipment, and perform complete return-to-service testing after repair.

## Clinical Use Tip

A pacemaker is not clinically reliable if its settings cannot be confidently read or changed, even when its measured pacing output is currently acceptable.
