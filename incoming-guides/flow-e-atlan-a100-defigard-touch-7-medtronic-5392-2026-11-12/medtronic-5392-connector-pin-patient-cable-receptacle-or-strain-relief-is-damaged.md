## What This Guide Helps With

Physical damage to pacing connectors, receptacles, or cable strain relief that can cause intermittent sensing, pacing loss, poor connection, or unsafe operation.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Stop Using the Damaged Connection

Do not continue using a visibly damaged pacing cable or pacemaker receptacle on a patient. Establish alternate verified pacing support before disconnecting or exchanging equipment.

**Expected outcome:** Patient support is maintained without relying on the damaged connection.

### 2. Confirm the Location of the Damage

Determine whether damage is on the atrial cable, ventricular cable, cable connector, connector pin, device receptacle, strain relief, or more than one component.

**Expected outcome:** The damaged component is accurately identified.

### 3. Inspect the Patient Cable

Inspect the entire accessible cable length and both ends for exposed conductors, cracked insulation, bent connector components, looseness, crushed sections, contamination, or separated strain relief.

**Expected outcome:** Any unsafe cable damage is identified before further testing.

### 4. Inspect the Pacemaker Receptacle

With the unit removed from patient use, visually inspect the receptacle for bent or recessed contacts, cracks, looseness, contamination, or deformation.

Do not insert tools into the receptacle or attempt to straighten internal contacts.

**Expected outcome:** Device-side mechanical condition is established without causing additional damage.

### 5. Check Connector Fit

Using an undamaged approved cable, confirm whether the connector seats smoothly and securely without excessive force, looseness, or movement.

Do not force a connector into a damaged receptacle.

**Expected outcome:** A serviceable receptacle accepts the approved connector normally and retains it securely.

### 6. Substitute a Known-Good Cable

If the receptacle appears intact, connect a known-good approved cable and verify mechanical stability.

**Expected outcome:** The connection is secure with the known-good cable. If the original cable alone is damaged, replace that cable and continue verification.

### 7. Check for Intermittent Electrical Connection

Connect the pacemaker to appropriate test equipment and verify pacing and sensing while the cable remains in its normal position. Observe for dropouts during normal gentle handling.

Do not deliberately flex a damaged connector or strain relief.

**Expected outcome:** Pacing and sensing remain stable with no connection-related interruption.

### 8. Determine Whether the Device Receptacle Is Damaged

If multiple known-good cables are loose, intermittent, or difficult to seat in the same device receptacle, treat the pacemaker receptacle as suspect.

**Expected outcome:** The fault is isolated to either the external cable or the device connection.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After replacing an external cable or accessory, verify both pacing channels, sensing, alarms, controls, and connection stability using approved equipment.

**Expected outcome:** All functions and connections are stable. If the damage was limited to a replaceable cable and all verification passes, troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Device-Side Mechanical Damage

If a receptacle, device-side connector, or inaccessible contact is damaged, loose, deformed, or intermittent, remove the pacemaker from service.

**Expected outcome:** Device-side connector damage is sent for qualified repair rather than field manipulation.

## If the Problem Persists

External cable damage can be corrected through approved accessory replacement, but persistent problems with a device receptacle or internal connector require service-level evaluation.

The pacemaker should be removed from service, labeled **Out of Service**, and sent for qualified repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Medtronic documentation and approved test equipment. Repairs to internal connectors or receptacles should be performed only by qualified personnel, followed by complete pacing, sensing, alarm, and connector-stability verification before return to service.

Knowing when a mechanical connector problem has moved beyond safe external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A pacing cable or receptacle with mechanical damage should never be considered acceptable merely because electrical continuity is present at one moment.
