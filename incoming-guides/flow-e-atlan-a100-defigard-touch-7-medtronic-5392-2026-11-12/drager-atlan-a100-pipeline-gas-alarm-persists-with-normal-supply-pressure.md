## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses persistent pipeline alarms caused by hose connections, wrong outlets, intermittent supply, connector problems, infrastructure issues, or machine-side sensing faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Gas Supply
Do not troubleshoot a persistent pipeline-gas alarm while a patient depends on an uncertain gas source. Transfer to another verified gas source or anesthesia machine according to clinical procedure.

**Expected outcome:** Patient gas delivery remains uninterrupted and the affected machine can be safely evaluated.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Alarm
Record the exact pipeline gas indicated, when the alarm appears, whether it clears intermittently, and whether cylinders are installed or in use.

Do not assume the wall pressure is normal solely because another device appears functional.

**Expected outcome:** The affected gas source and alarm behavior are clearly identified.

### 3. Verify the Correct Pipeline Connection
Confirm the hose is connected to the correct indexed machine inlet and correct facility gas outlet.

Inspect for crossed hoses, partially engaged quick-connects, adapters, or incorrect room connections.

**Expected outcome:** The intended gas is connected to the correct inlet and outlet. If reconnection clears the alarm, continue to final verification.

### 4. Inspect the Pipeline Hose
Examine the full accessible length of the hose for kinks, crushing, cracks, damaged fittings, excessive tension, or loose threaded connections.

Replace a questionable hose with a known-good approved hose if available.

**Expected outcome:** The supply hose is intact and unrestricted. If the alarm clears with a known-good hose, remove the defective hose from service.

### 5. Compare With Another Facility Outlet
When practical, connect the machine to another verified outlet for the same gas or use an approved independent source.

This helps distinguish the anesthesia machine from a specific wall outlet or zone.

**Expected outcome:** If the alarm clears on another verified source, escalate the original outlet or facility gas system rather than the anesthesia machine.

### 6. Verify Supply With Approved Test Equipment
Use appropriate approved gas-pressure test equipment or obtain verification from the responsible facilities/medical-gas team.

Avoid relying only on a visual wall-outlet inspection.

**Expected outcome:** The actual supply is confirmed to be appropriate for normal equipment operation. If supply is abnormal, the infrastructure issue is identified.

### 7. Check Cylinder Behavior if Applicable
If the machine supports an alternate cylinder source, observe whether the alarm condition changes when using the approved backup source under controlled conditions.

Do not deplete emergency gas unnecessarily.

**Expected outcome:** The comparison helps determine whether the fault follows the pipeline source or remains machine-side.

### 8. Restart and Recheck the Alarm
After verifying connections, hose condition, and source, restart the machine normally and observe the pipeline indication.

Do not bypass alarms or alter service-level pressure thresholds.

**Expected outcome:** The correct gas source is recognized and the alarm clears. If so, proceed to system verification.

### 9. Complete Gas-System Verification
Perform the normal system test and verify gas delivery, oxygen monitoring, ventilation, and relevant alarms using approved test equipment.

**Expected outcome:** Gas supplies are recognized correctly and the machine passes the required system test. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate a Persistent Machine-Side Alarm
If the verified gas supply, hose, outlet, and alternate source are all satisfactory but the pipeline alarm remains, remove the machine from service.

**Expected outcome:** The unit is referred for service-level evaluation of the machine inlet, pressure sensing, internal pneumatic path, or associated electronics.

## If the Problem Persists

External pipeline supply, hose condition, wall outlet, connections, and alternate-source comparison have been ruled out. A persistent alarm may involve an internal inlet, regulator, pressure sensor, pneumatic pathway, or control circuit.

The device should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated with appropriate Drager documentation and approved gas-pressure test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
- Fully verified for gas recognition, gas delivery, alarms, ventilation, and system-test performance before return to service.

A normal-looking wall connection does not prove the machine is receiving a valid supply; verification is required.

## Clinical Use Tip

When a pipeline alarm persists, secure an alternate verified gas source before investigating either the machine or facility infrastructure.

## Final Thought

Persistent gas-supply alarms require verification of the entire external path before internal failure is suspected. Check the outlet, hose, connectors, alternate source, and actual supply condition, then escalate when the machine still cannot reliably recognize a verified source.

That is successful troubleshooting.
