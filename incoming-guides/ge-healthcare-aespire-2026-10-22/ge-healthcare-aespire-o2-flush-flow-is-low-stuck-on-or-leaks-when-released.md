## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoots abnormal O2 flush operation caused by gas supply, button obstruction, external damage, contamination, connections, or a service-level flush-valve problem.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Before Testing the O2 Flush

A stuck or unintended O2 flush can rapidly alter breathing-system pressure and gas composition.

If the condition occurs during a case, maintain patient support with an alternate verified anesthesia-delivery method before testing or manipulating the flush control.

**Expected outcome:** Patient ventilation and anesthesia delivery are protected before troubleshooting begins.

### 2. Confirm the Exact O2 Flush Condition

Determine whether the flush:

- Produces little or no flow
- Produces intermittent flow
- Continues flowing after the control is released
- Appears to leak continuously
- Requires abnormal force to operate
- Does not return normally after activation

Test only under controlled non-patient conditions.

**Expected outcome:** The exact failure mode is identified. If the flush operates normally and returns properly during repeated controlled tests, document the result and stop troubleshooting.

### 3. Verify the Oxygen Supply

Confirm that the machine has an appropriate oxygen supply available from the connected pipeline or cylinder source being used for testing.

Inspect:

- Oxygen hose connection
- Wall connection
- Cylinder connection when applicable
- External supply indicators
- Obvious hose damage or kinking

**Expected outcome:** An adequate external oxygen source is connected and available. If restoring the oxygen supply corrects low flush flow, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 4. Inspect the O2 Flush Control Externally

Inspect the flush button or control for:

- Physical damage
- Binding
- Debris
- Liquid residue
- Tape or labels interfering with movement
- Contact with nearby equipment
- A damaged or displaced external cover

Do not disassemble the valve assembly.

**Expected outcome:** The control moves freely and returns immediately when released. If removing an external obstruction restores normal operation, troubleshooting can stop after repeated verification.

### 5. Check for Continuous Flow After Release

Activate and release the flush control under safe test conditions.

Observe whether flow stops promptly and whether the control returns fully to its resting position.

If flow continues after the control has physically returned, remove the machine from service.

**Expected outcome:** Flow occurs only while intentionally activated and stops when released.

### 6. Check for External Leakage or Audible Gas Flow

With the flush control released, listen for unexpected continuous gas flow and inspect accessible fittings around the control area and gas-supply connections.

Do not open covers or attempt internal pneumatic repair.

**Expected outcome:** No unintended oxygen flow or external leakage is present when the flush is not activated.

### 7. Compare Operation With a Known-Good Machine When Available

If the behavior is difficult to interpret, compare control feel and basic operation with another verified Aespire or equivalent machine configured for the same function.

Do not use comparison to establish undocumented flow specifications.

**Expected outcome:** The test clarifies whether the observed flush behavior is abnormal without requiring internal adjustment.

### 8. Verify Breathing-System Configuration During the Test

Ensure the breathing system is properly assembled and the test setup is appropriate.

A disconnected circuit, open pathway, or unrelated leak can make flush performance appear weak or abnormal.

**Expected outcome:** The external breathing-system configuration does not explain the reported condition.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correcting any external issue, perform the applicable manufacturer-approved pre-use or functional checks.

Verify:

- Flush activates reliably
- Flow stops upon release
- Control returns freely
- No unintended leakage is heard or observed
- No abnormal alarms or indications remain

**Expected outcome:** O2 flush operation is repeatable and normal. If so, troubleshooting is complete.

### 10. Remove From Service if Flush Operation Is Unreliable

If the flush remains low, sticks, leaks, activates unpredictably, or fails to shut off reliably, do not return the machine to clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** The anesthesia machine is removed from service for qualified evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External oxygen supply, hose connections, breathing-system setup, control obstruction, and visible damage have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve an internal pneumatic valve, flush-control mechanism, gas pathway, internal leak, or other service-level condition.

The device should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Perform all required anesthesia-machine functional and safety testing before returning the unit to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Treat a stuck-on O2 flush as a potentially serious breathing-system pressure and anesthetic-delivery hazard and move the patient to a verified alternative before troubleshooting.

## Final Thought

An O2 flush problem should be approached as a patient-safety issue first. Verify gas supply, external controls, setup, and obvious obstruction before assuming an internal pneumatic failure, and remove the machine from service whenever flush operation remains unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
