## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoots external-power recognition problems involving adapters, rechargeable battery packs, connectors, outlets, cables, seating, or pump-side power interfaces.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient
If external power cannot be relied upon and battery capacity is uncertain, remove the pump from active patient therapy and use another verified device.

**Expected outcome:** Patient treatment does not depend on an uncertain external power connection.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Condition
Determine which external power accessory was being used and whether the pump failed to recognize it, failed to remain powered, or changed unexpectedly to internal batteries.

**Expected outcome:** The complaint is clearly separated into accessory, source, connection, or pump recognition behavior.

### 3. Inspect the External Power Accessory
Examine the AC adapter or rechargeable battery pack, cable, strain relief, connector, and housing for cuts, bent contacts, contamination, liquid exposure, looseness, or other visible damage.

**Expected outcome:** The accessory is physically intact. Damaged power accessories are removed from service rather than used for further testing.

### 4. Verify the AC Source When Applicable
For an AC adapter complaint, test the receptacle with an appropriate method or use a known-good outlet. Confirm switches, power strips, or extension equipment are not responsible for the loss of power.

**Expected outcome:** A verified AC source is available. If changing to a known-good outlet restores operation, the issue is external to the pump.

### 5. Check Connector Seating
Inspect the pump's accessible external power connection and ensure the approved accessory connects fully without excessive looseness or force. Do not pry, bend, or reshape contacts.

**Expected outcome:** The connection is secure and stable. If correct seating restores recognition, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 6. Substitute a Known-Good Approved Accessory
Use a known-good compatible AC adapter or external battery accessory as appropriate. Compare pump behavior with the originally reported accessory.

**Expected outcome:** If the pump recognizes the known-good accessory, the original adapter, pack, or cable is the likely cause. If neither is recognized, the pump requires further evaluation.

### 7. Test the Suspect Accessory on Another Approved Setup When Appropriate
If institutional procedures allow, test the suspect accessory with another compatible verified pump to determine whether the failure follows the accessory.

**Expected outcome:** A fault that follows the accessory confirms the accessory should be removed from service. A fault remaining only with one pump suggests a pump-side issue.

### 8. Check for Intermittent Connections
During nonpatient operation, observe the external-power indication while the pump and cable remain in normal positions. Do not intentionally flex damaged cables or stress connectors.

**Expected outcome:** Power recognition remains stable. Any intermittent connection requires removal of the affected pump or accessory from service.

### 9. Perform Functional Verification
With a verified external power source, check startup, power indication, stable operation, controls, alarms, and appropriate power-source transition behavior according to approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** External power is consistently recognized and the pump remains operational. Successful testing means troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Recognition Failure
If known-good approved external power accessories are not recognized, remove the pump from service. Do not disassemble the power connector or troubleshoot internal charging or power circuits without authorized service procedures.

**Expected outcome:** A pump-side power-interface fault is referred for qualified evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists
Outlet problems, damaged accessories, poor seating, loose external connections, and defective adapters or packs have been ruled out. Remaining causes may include the pump-side connector, internal power circuitry, charging circuitry, configuration, or another service-level condition.

Remove the pump from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for bench evaluation. Use manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment. Repairs should be performed only by qualified personnel, followed by complete return-to-service testing.

Knowing when to stop at the external power interface is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
Secure external power cables so movement of the patient, pole, or bed cannot repeatedly disconnect the pump.

## Final Thought
Verify the outlet, accessory, cable, and connection before assuming a pump failure, use known-good substitutions to isolate the cause, and escalate unstable power interfaces appropriately.
