## What This Guide Helps With
A scan or exposure complaint has been associated with the ESG-410 even though diagnostic exposure and scanning are not normal electrosurgical generator functions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain the Required Procedure

If an imaging exposure or scan cannot be performed reliably, do not repeatedly attempt the acquisition while a patient depends on the malfunctioning system. Follow the department's alternate equipment or workflow process.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care proceeds without reliance on an unreliable scan or exposure function.

### 2. Confirm the Equipment Producing the Exposure or Scan

Identify the imaging system, workstation, detector, scanner, or other equipment responsible for the reported function.

The Olympus ESG-410 produces electrosurgical energy and does not normally initiate diagnostic scans or imaging exposures.

**Expected outcome:** The actual equipment associated with the failed scan or exposure is identified.

### 3. Verify Whether Staff Actually Mean ESU Activation

Clarify whether “exposure” or “start” was used to describe activation of electrosurgical output rather than imaging.

Ask whether the ESG-410 powers normally but fails to activate from a hand control or footswitch.

**Expected outcome:** The complaint is classified correctly as either an imaging-system issue or an ESG-410 activation issue.

### 4. Check External Activation Accessories if the Complaint Is ESU Related

If electrosurgical activation is the true complaint, inspect the applicable handpiece, footswitch, connecting cable, plug, and receptacle for damage, contamination, incomplete insertion, or obvious incompatibility.

Substitute a compatible known-good accessory when appropriate.

**Expected outcome:** The activation accessory is securely connected and recognized. If a known-good accessory restores operation, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 5. Verify Controls and Mode Selection

Confirm that the generator is in a normal ready state and that the required externally selectable operating controls are set appropriately for the connected accessory and intended controlled test.

Do not make undocumented configuration changes.

**Expected outcome:** The generator is ready for activation under controlled test conditions.

### 6. Test Using Approved ESU Test Equipment

If permitted within the Clinical Engineering scope, perform applicable manufacturer-approved functional or output testing with an appropriate electrosurgical analyzer and compatible test accessories.

Never use a patient as the test load.

**Expected outcome:** Activation and applicable output behavior are verified using approved equipment.

### 7. Redirect True Imaging Problems

If the complaint genuinely involves a scan or exposure, stop troubleshooting the ESG-410 and open or redirect the work order to the correct imaging system.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is investigated using the proper equipment documentation.

### 8. Complete Return-to-Service Verification

For an ESG-410 activation complaint that has been corrected externally, verify startup, controls, accessories, alarms, and applicable output checks before returning the generator to service.

**Expected outcome:** The generator performs normally through the tested clinical activation path.

## If the Problem Persists
A diagnostic exposure or scan failure is outside the normal function of the Olympus ESG-410. The imaging system responsible for the failed function should be removed from service if it cannot reliably perform its intended acquisition.

If the true problem is failure of ESG-410 electrosurgical activation after external accessories, connections, controls, and approved functional testing have been checked, remove the generator from service and label it **Out of Service**. Send it for bench evaluation using Olympus documentation and approved ESU test equipment.

Internal repair or service-level configuration should be performed only by qualified personnel, followed by complete return-to-service testing.

## Clinical Use Tip
Before exchanging an ESU during a procedure, confirm whether the unavailable function is actually electrosurgical activation or belongs to another device in the room.
