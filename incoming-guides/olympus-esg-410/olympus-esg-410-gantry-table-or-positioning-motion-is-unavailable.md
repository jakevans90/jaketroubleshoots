## What This Guide Helps With
A reported positioning or motion problem has been assigned to an ESG-410, although the electrosurgical generator does not provide gantry or table motion.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Protect the Patient and Prevent Unintended Movement

If the reported problem involves an operating table, imaging gantry, patient positioner, or other powered motion system, stop using that motion function until the actual affected equipment is identified and verified.

Do not attempt to troubleshoot moving equipment while a patient depends on unsafe or uncontrolled positioning.

**Expected outcome:** Patient positioning is stable and no unintended equipment movement can occur.

### 2. Verify the Asset Associated With the Complaint

Confirm the equipment manufacturer, model, asset tag, serial number, and location listed on the work order.

The Olympus ESG-410 is an electrosurgical generator. Gantry, table, and patient-positioning motion are not normal ESG-410 functions.

**Expected outcome:** The correct affected asset is identified. If the work order was assigned to the wrong device, troubleshooting of the ESG-410 should stop and the work order should be redirected.

### 3. Confirm What Staff Mean by “Positioning”

Ask staff to demonstrate or describe the unavailable function without operating unsafe equipment. Determine whether they are referring to a surgical table, imaging system, boom, equipment cart, accessory mount, or another device located near the ESG-410.

**Expected outcome:** The physical device responsible for the motion function is identified.

### 4. Inspect the ESG-410 Only for Relevant External Conditions

If staff believe the ESG-410 somehow contributes to the problem, verify that its external cables and accessories are not entangled with, restricting, or physically obstructing other movable equipment.

Do not alter unrelated equipment connections without identifying their purpose.

**Expected outcome:** The ESG-410 and its cables are not mechanically interfering with another device.

### 5. Verify Whether the Complaint Is Actually an ESU Control Problem

Determine whether “positioning” was used incorrectly to describe an ESG-410 control, accessory placement, footswitch position, or other electrosurgical function.

Verify control response, accessory connections, and basic generator operation if the actual complaint concerns the ESU.

**Expected outcome:** The complaint is either correctly reclassified as an ESG-410 issue or confirmed to belong to another asset.

### 6. Test the Correct Equipment

If a table, gantry, boom, or other positioning device is identified, troubleshoot that device under its own manufacturer, model, and service documentation rather than applying electrosurgical troubleshooting procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The positioning problem is evaluated against the correct equipment and technical documentation.

### 7. Verify the ESG-410 Before Closing Its Work Order

If the ESG-410 was incorrectly implicated, verify that it powers on normally, controls respond, and no obvious damage or accessory problem exists before documenting it as not responsible for the motion failure.

**Expected outcome:** The ESG-410 is confirmed operational and can remain in service if all applicable checks pass.

### 8. Correct the Equipment Record or Escalate the Misidentified Complaint

Update the work order to identify the actual affected device or notify the appropriate Clinical Engineering team if the responsible equipment cannot be determined.

**Expected outcome:** The reported problem is attached to the correct asset and the ESG-410 is not subjected to unnecessary repair.

## If the Problem Persists
Gantry, table, or positioning motion failure is not a normal functional category of the Olympus ESG-410. If the actual affected device cannot be identified, keep any equipment exhibiting unsafe or unavailable motion out of service until its identity and function can be established.

The responsible device should be labeled **Out of Service** when its motion cannot be safely verified and should receive bench or field evaluation using the correct manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment. Repair or configuration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Do not replace parts or alter the ESG-410 in an attempt to correct a function the generator does not provide. Correct asset identification is part of proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
When several devices are clustered around an OR table, confirm the asset tag and exact failed function before removing equipment from service.
