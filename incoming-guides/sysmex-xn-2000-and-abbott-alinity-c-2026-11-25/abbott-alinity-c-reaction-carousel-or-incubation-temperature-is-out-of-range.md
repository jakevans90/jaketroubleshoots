## What This Guide Helps With

Investigate reaction carousel and incubation temperature faults associated with ventilation, environmental conditions, startup stabilization, or temperature-control abnormalities.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Testing
- Notify laboratory personnel of the temperature-related fault.
- Suspend affected testing when reaction temperature cannot be maintained within manufacturer requirements.
- Arrange alternate validated testing for time-sensitive specimens.
- Do not override temperature alarms or accept potentially unreliable assay results.
- Coordinate review of results produced during the affected period.

Expected outcome: Testing is contained until the required reaction temperature can be verified.

### 2. Confirm the Temperature Fault
- Record the exact Alinity c error message.
- Identify whether the alarm involves the reaction carousel, incubation system, or another temperature-controlled area.
- Record the displayed temperature and status information available through normal system functions.
- Determine whether the fault occurs during startup, after extended operation, or intermittently.
- Review the event history for related operating alarms.

Expected outcome: The affected temperature-control function and failure pattern are identified.

### 3. Check Room Environmental Conditions
- Verify the laboratory temperature is within the manufacturer's approved operating range.
- Look for recent HVAC interruptions, abnormal room heating, or direct exposure to conditioned-air discharge.
- Identify nearby equipment that may be adding excessive heat.
- Coordinate environmental corrections with facilities personnel.
- Avoid inventing or substituting temperature limits for Abbott specifications.

Expected outcome: The analyzer is operating in an appropriate laboratory environment.

### 4. Inspect External Ventilation and Clearance
- Inspect accessible ventilation openings for dust accumulation or obstruction.
- Verify that carts, boxes, supplies, or adjacent equipment are not restricting airflow.
- Listen for unusual fan noise without opening protected covers.
- Check for visible accumulation around accessible cooling areas.
- Clean only approved external surfaces using permitted methods.

Expected outcome: External airflow is unobstructed and no obvious ventilation problem is present. If an external obstruction is corrected and temperature stabilizes with accepted QC, troubleshooting can stop.

### 5. Review Startup and Stabilization Conditions
- Determine whether the analyzer was recently powered down or restarted.
- Review whether sufficient manufacturer-defined stabilization time has elapsed.
- Check whether the analyzer reports an ongoing readiness or temperature-stabilization condition.
- Do not repeatedly cycle power to force readiness.
- Do not adjust internal heaters, sensors, or temperature setpoints.

Expected outcome: The temperature status is evaluated under the correct operating and stabilization conditions.

### 6. Check for Related System Faults
- Review active instrument messages for power, cooling, fan, or initialization-related problems.
- Verify that external power remains stable.
- Check whether the problem appeared after service, relocation, or environmental changes.
- Document any correlation with abnormal operation.

Expected outcome: External power and operating conditions are verified, and associated alarms are identified.

### 7. Verify Temperature Recovery
- After an approved external correction, monitor the normal instrument temperature-status display.
- Confirm the instrument reaches and maintains its required operating condition.
- Have laboratory personnel complete applicable functional checks, calibration, and QC.
- Verify that the temperature alarm does not return during the approved observation period.

Expected outcome: Reaction temperature remains acceptable and required laboratory checks pass. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Escalate Persistent Temperature Faults
- If the instrument cannot reach or maintain its required temperature under appropriate environmental conditions, stop affected testing.
- Preserve temperature trends and event messages.
- Request Abbott service evaluation.

Expected outcome: The unresolved thermal-control problem is isolated for qualified service.

## If the Problem Persists

Once room conditions, external ventilation, power stability, and startup requirements have been ruled out, remaining causes may involve internal temperature sensors, heaters, cooling components, control circuitry, or carousel-related mechanisms.

The affected analyzer should be:

- Removed from service and labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation by qualified personnel.
- Evaluated using Abbott documentation and approved temperature-verification equipment.
- Repaired or calibrated only by authorized personnel.
- Returned to service after verified temperature stability, applicable functional testing, and acceptable QC.

Persistent temperature faults are not resolved by repeatedly clearing alarms.

## Clinical Use Tip

Reaction temperature affects assay performance. A normal-looking result does not prove it was generated under valid incubation conditions.

## Final Thought

Temperature-related faults should be investigated through environmental conditions, ventilation, and operating history before internal controls are suspected. Protect assay reliability, verify stable operation, escalate unresolved faults, and document the final result.

That is successful troubleshooting.
