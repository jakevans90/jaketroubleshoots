## What This Guide Helps With

Unexpected background results, abnormal scattergrams, or differential-channel behavior associated with reagents, contamination, sample quality, or analyzer-level faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Results
Do not release questionable patient results from a channel showing persistent abnormal background, scattergram, or differential behavior.

Follow laboratory policy for holding, repeating, or redirecting affected specimens to a verified analyzer.

Expected outcome: Potentially unreliable results are prevented from entering clinical use.

### 2. Confirm Whether the Problem Is Patient-Specific or System-Wide
Review whether the abnormality occurs with one specimen, multiple specimens, controls, background checks, or all testing on one analyzer module.

Compare the affected module with the second XN-2000 module if available.

Expected outcome: The issue is categorized as sample-specific, channel-specific, module-specific, or system-wide.

If only one specimen is affected and controls and other specimens are normal, follow laboratory specimen-review procedures rather than instrument repair.

### 3. Inspect Reagent Status
Verify required reagents are present, correctly installed, in date according to the displayed system information, and not showing obvious external leakage, empty containers, disconnected tubing, or improper placement.

Do not manually alter lot or expiration data without authorization.

Expected outcome: Required reagents are present and the analyzer recognizes them normally.

If correcting an improperly installed reagent restores normal background and channel behavior, verify controls and stop.

### 4. Check for Recent Reagent or Consumable Changes
Determine whether the problem began after a reagent replacement, lot change, container change, cleaning activity, or other routine service event.

Confirm all changed items were installed according to the approved workflow.

Expected outcome: No recent external change remains incorrectly completed.

If correcting a recent installation issue resolves the abnormal results, complete QC before returning the affected channel to use.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Tubing and Connections
Check visible reagent and waste tubing for kinks, pinching, loose external connections, obvious air gaps, leaks, or incorrect routing.

Do not open internal fluidic assemblies.

Expected outcome: Accessible fluid paths are secure and unobstructed.

If an external tubing problem is corrected and the analyzer subsequently produces normal background and QC, troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Perform Approved Background or Cleaning Procedures
Use only routine cleaning, rinse, or background procedures available to trained laboratory or service personnel and permitted by the approved documentation.

Do not introduce unapproved cleaning agents.

Expected outcome: Background testing returns to the analyzer's accepted condition and abnormal contamination-related patterns clear.

If the issue clears and QC passes, stop troubleshooting.

### 7. Compare the Two Analyzer Modules
If the XN-2000 has one module producing abnormal scattergrams or background while the other performs normally using the same approved reagents and control material, document that distinction.

Expected outcome: The fault is isolated to a specific module or measurement channel rather than the shared specimen or laboratory environment.

If both modules perform normally after external corrections, return to service.

### 8. Run Appropriate QC
Use laboratory-approved control material and review the affected measurement channel.

Clinical Engineering should not redefine QC acceptance criteria.

Expected outcome: QC performs within the laboratory's established acceptance criteria and scattergram presentation returns to expected behavior.

If QC passes following the corrective action, proceed to final verification.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
Verify background, control results, analyzer readiness, and representative channel operation before releasing the analyzer back to routine testing.

Expected outcome: The affected measurement channel demonstrates stable, acceptable performance.

If all verification passes, document and return to service.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Channel Abnormalities
If abnormal background, scattergrams, or differential behavior persists after external reagent, tubing, contamination, and routine cleaning causes are ruled out, remove the affected analyzer module or channel from clinical use.

Expected outcome: Questionable analytical performance is not used for patient reporting.

## If the Problem Persists

Remaining causes may involve internal fluidics, optical detection, measurement hardware, contamination beyond routine access, calibration, or other service-level conditions.

The device should be:

- Removed from service when reliable results cannot be assured
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Sysmex documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired, calibrated, or configured only by qualified personnel

Return-to-service testing should include the laboratory's required background checks, QC, and any applicable verification following service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A visually plausible CBC does not override abnormal analyzer background, scattergram, or QC behavior; hold affected results until analytical reliability is confirmed.

## Final Thought

Analytical abnormalities require a conservative approach. Rule out specimen, reagent, fluid-path, and contamination causes first, verify performance with approved QC, and escalate whenever reliable measurement cannot be demonstrated.

That is successful troubleshooting.
