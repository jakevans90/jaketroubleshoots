## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot processing interruptions associated with cuvette washing, inadequate water supply, wash consumables, waste drainage, or external fluid connections.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Testing and Contain Fluid Hazards
- Notify laboratory personnel that the analyzer has stopped processing.
- Redirect urgent specimens to another validated analyzer when necessary.
- Do not restart processing while water supply or waste management is unreliable.
- Use appropriate protective equipment when inspecting potentially contaminated waste connections.
- If liquid is leaking near electrical components, isolate the equipment safely and request authorized service.

Expected outcome: Patient testing continuity is maintained and fluid-related hazards are controlled.

### 2. Identify the Reported Fluid-System Fault
- Review active Alinity c alarms and event history.
- Determine whether the interruption relates to water supply, cuvette washing, wash solution, waste drainage, or liquid handling.
- Record when the alarm occurred and which process was active.
- Determine whether the failure followed maintenance, consumable replacement, plumbing work, or an unexpected facility water interruption.

Expected outcome: The affected fluid-system function and timing are identified.

### 3. Verify External Water Supply
- Confirm that the analyzer's approved water source is available.
- Check accessible supply-line connections for loose fittings, kinks, compression, or leaks.
- Verify that the water purification or supply system reports normal status.
- Coordinate with laboratory or facilities personnel to confirm water quality and availability according to Abbott requirements.
- Do not bypass water-quality safeguards.

Expected outcome: The analyzer receives an appropriate, uninterrupted external water supply.

### 4. Inspect Wash Solutions and Consumables
- Verify the presence and correct installation of applicable wash and cleaning solutions.
- Check for empty containers, incorrect products, or visible leakage.
- Confirm the installed consumables are approved for the system.
- Check whether a recent consumable replacement introduced the fault.

Expected outcome: Required wash consumables are correctly installed and available.

### 5. Inspect External Waste Connections
- Verify that the waste path is connected to the designated disposal system.
- Check accessible tubing for kinks, compression, disconnections, or visible leakage.
- Confirm that applicable waste containers are not full.
- Check for drainage restrictions that can be identified without disconnecting contaminated internal plumbing.
- Coordinate facility drain problems with the appropriate support department.

Expected outcome: Waste can leave the analyzer through the approved disposal path without visible obstruction or leakage.

### 6. Inspect the Accessible Cuvette-Wash Area
- Place the system in the approved safe state before inspection.
- Look for obvious spills, residual liquid, damaged accessible components, or foreign material.
- Do not reach into a moving reaction carousel or washing mechanism.
- Do not disassemble internal pumps, valves, wash stations, or manifolds.
- Record any visible abnormalities for Abbott service personnel.

Expected outcome: No accessible obstruction or leak is identified, or a condition requiring service is documented.

### 7. Verify Recovery After External Correction
- After correcting an approved external issue, perform the manufacturer-authorized recovery procedure.
- Observe water and waste status during system initialization.
- Confirm the cuvette-wash-related error clears.
- Have laboratory personnel perform the required instrument checks and QC.
- Check for repeated leaks or alarms during the verification process.

Expected outcome: Fluid status remains normal, processing resumes, and required QC passes. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 8. Escalate Recurring Wash or Fluid Faults
- If water and waste connections are verified but faults continue, stop affected testing.
- Preserve the event history and observations.
- Request Abbott service evaluation.

Expected outcome: An unresolved fluid-system problem is isolated for qualified service.

## If the Problem Persists

Once water availability, wash consumables, waste connections, and accessible drainage conditions have been ruled out, the remaining problem may involve internal pumps, valves, fluid sensors, wash mechanisms, or process-control components.

The analyzer should be:

- Removed from service and labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation by qualified personnel.
- Evaluated using Abbott service documentation and approved testing equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by authorized personnel.
- Returned to service after verified fluid-system operation, leak checks, required functional testing, and acceptable laboratory QC.

Repeatedly restarting a system with unresolved fluid faults is not appropriate troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A water or cuvette-wash fault may affect assay integrity before processing stops. Ask laboratory leadership to review potentially affected results.

## Final Thought

Cuvette-wash and fluid-system problems require immediate attention to testing reliability and biohazard safety. Inspect external water, consumables, and drainage before suspecting internal components. Verify the correction, escalate persistent faults, and document the final testing outcome.

That is successful troubleshooting.
