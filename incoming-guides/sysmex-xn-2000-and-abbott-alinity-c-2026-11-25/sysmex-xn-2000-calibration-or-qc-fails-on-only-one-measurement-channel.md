## What This Guide Helps With

Calibration or quality control fails on one measurement channel while other channels remain acceptable, suggesting a localized analytical problem.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Results
Do not release patient results from a measurement channel that has failed required QC or calibration verification.

Follow laboratory policy for holding results, rerouting specimens, or using another verified analyzer.

Expected outcome: Patient results from the affected channel are withheld until acceptable performance is demonstrated.

### 2. Confirm the Failure Is Limited to One Channel
Review the QC or calibration data and determine which measurement channel is affected. Confirm whether the second analyzer module or other measurement channels are performing normally.

Expected outcome: The failure is isolated to a specific channel rather than a system-wide problem.

### 3. Verify Control or Calibrator Material
Check that the correct material was used, it is within the laboratory's approved use period, was handled according to laboratory procedure, and has not been visibly compromised.

Clinical Engineering should not redefine acceptable control ranges.

Expected outcome: Appropriate control or calibration material was used correctly.

If repeating with properly prepared material produces acceptable results, stop after documenting the finding.

### 4. Confirm Reagent Status for the Affected Channel
Verify that reagents associated with the failing channel are present, properly installed, correctly recognized, and have no obvious external tubing or connection problem.

Expected outcome: Required reagents are available and externally connected correctly.

If correcting an external reagent issue restores QC, verify stability and stop.

### 5. Inspect for Recent Changes
Determine whether the failure began after reagent replacement, lot change, cleaning, service, relocation, or another recent event.

Verify the recent activity was completed correctly.

Expected outcome: No incomplete or incorrect external change remains.

If correcting a recent installation or workflow issue resolves the failure, complete required QC and stop.

### 6. Check Accessible Fluid Paths
Inspect visible tubing associated with the affected analyzer module for kinks, air gaps, leaks, or loose external connections.

Do not open internal fluidic assemblies.

Expected outcome: Accessible fluid paths are secure and unobstructed.

If correcting a visible external fluid-path issue restores QC, verify and stop.

### 7. Perform Approved Routine Cleaning
If contamination is a reasonable possibility, perform only approved routine cleaning procedures accessible to trained personnel.

Expected outcome: The affected channel returns to stable operation without contamination-related abnormality.

If QC becomes acceptable after approved cleaning, complete repeated verification as required and stop.

### 8. Compare With the Other Analyzer Module
If the same control material and appropriate reagents produce acceptable results on the other XN-2000 module but not the affected module, document the comparison.

Expected outcome: The problem is confirmed as localized to the affected analyzer module or channel.

### 9. Perform Final QC Verification
Run the laboratory-required QC after any corrective action.

Expected outcome: The affected channel meets the laboratory's established acceptance criteria.

If QC passes and remains stable, return the channel to service according to laboratory policy.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Channel Failure
If the same channel continues to fail QC or calibration despite verified controls, reagents, external fluid paths, and routine cleaning, remove the affected channel or module from clinical use.

Expected outcome: A channel with unverified analytical performance is not used for patient reporting.

## If the Problem Persists

Remaining causes may include internal fluidics, optics, detectors, calibration state, measurement hardware, or other service-level analytical components.

The device should be:

- Removed from service when the affected measurement cannot be trusted
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Sysmex documentation and approved test equipment
- Calibrated, repaired, or configured only by qualified personnel

Return-to-service testing must include the laboratory's required calibration or QC verification.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A functioning analyzer is not necessarily a verified analyzer; one failed measurement channel is enough to withhold results from that channel.

## Final Thought

When one analytical channel fails, isolate the problem methodically through material, reagent, fluid-path, and contamination checks before assuming internal failure. Do not restore clinical use until required verification passes.

That is successful troubleshooting.
