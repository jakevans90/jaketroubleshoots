## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot vaporizer recognition, mounting, locking, agent identification, and external interface problems before escalating for service-level evaluation.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Anesthesia Capability
Do not troubleshoot a vaporizer recognition or locking problem while a patient depends on the affected vaporizer for anesthetic delivery. Transfer to another verified anesthesia machine or use the facility-approved alternate anesthesia plan before continuing.

If the vaporizer is loose, cannot lock, appears damaged, leaks, or the displayed agent does not match the installed vaporizer, do not use it clinically.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is supported by verified equipment and the affected machine is available for controlled troubleshooting.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition
Determine whether the problem is:
- Vaporizer not detected
- Vaporizer detected intermittently
- Vaporizer will not fully seat or lock
- Displayed agent does not match the installed vaporizer
- Problem occurs with one vaporizer or every compatible vaporizer
- Problem began after vaporizer replacement, relocation, filling, or cleaning

Record any displayed message exactly as shown.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is clearly reproduced and narrowed to recognition, mechanical mounting, or identification.

If the reported problem cannot be reproduced and repeated checks show correct vaporizer recognition and secure locking, complete functional verification before returning the machine to service.

### 3. Inspect the Vaporizer Externally
Remove the vaporizer from clinical use and inspect it for:
- Bent or damaged mounting features
- Debris or residue on the mounting interface
- Cracks or impact damage
- Damaged locking components
- Signs of liquid contamination or leakage
- Improperly closed fill components
- Incorrect or incompatible vaporizer type

Do not attempt internal vaporizer repair.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer is externally intact, clean, compatible, and capable of mounting normally.

If obvious damage or leakage is found, remove that vaporizer from service and stop troubleshooting it.

### 4. Inspect the Machine Vaporizer Mounting Interface
With the machine safe and not connected to a patient, inspect the vaporizer mounting area for contamination, physical obstruction, damaged mating surfaces, or foreign material preventing full seating.

Verify that no protective cap, packaging material, or misplaced accessory is interfering with installation.

**Expected outcome:** The mounting interface is clean, unobstructed, and visibly undamaged.

### 5. Reseat and Lock the Vaporizer
Install the vaporizer using the approved mounting orientation and verify that it seats completely without excessive force.

Confirm the locking mechanism engages fully and that the vaporizer cannot be unintentionally lifted or shifted.

Do not force a vaporizer that will not seat normally.

**Expected outcome:** The vaporizer mounts securely and the machine recognizes the installed unit correctly.

If recognition is correct and the vaporizer remains securely locked through repeated checks, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 6. Compare With a Known-Good Compatible Vaporizer
If available, substitute another known-good, compatible vaporizer approved for the A7.

Observe whether the machine:
- Recognizes the replacement consistently
- Displays the correct agent
- Accepts and locks the vaporizer normally

Then, if appropriate, test the suspect vaporizer on another compatible verified machine using approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The comparison identifies whether the problem follows the vaporizer or remains with the anesthesia machine.

If the issue follows one vaporizer, remove that vaporizer from service. If multiple known-good vaporizers fail on the same machine, continue with machine-level evaluation.

### 7. Verify Displayed Agent and Relevant Configuration
Confirm that the agent shown by the A7 corresponds to the installed compatible vaporizer.

Review only normal user-accessible or authorized Clinical Engineering configuration relevant to vaporizer identification. Do not enter restricted service menus or alter agent-related configuration without manufacturer authorization.

**Expected outcome:** The installed vaporizer and displayed agent identification agree consistently.

If the machine continues to display an incorrect agent with known-good compatible vaporizers, remove the machine from service.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification
After correction, perform the required anesthesia machine pre-use or functional checks appropriate to the facility and manufacturer documentation.

Verify:
- Vaporizer remains securely mounted
- Correct agent is displayed
- No leakage or unusual odor is present
- Machine completes applicable checks
- No vaporizer-related warnings remain

**Expected outcome:** The machine recognizes the correct vaporizer consistently and passes required functional verification.

If all checks pass, troubleshooting is complete and the machine may be returned to service according to facility procedure.

### 9. Escalate an Unresolved Vaporizer Interface Problem
Stop external troubleshooting if:
- A known-good vaporizer is not recognized
- Multiple vaporizers will not lock correctly
- Agent identification remains incorrect
- The mounting interface appears damaged
- Recognition is intermittent
- The machine fails required functional or leak testing

**Expected outcome:** The machine is removed from clinical availability before an unreliable vaporizer interface can affect anesthetic delivery.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as improper seating, incompatible equipment, contamination, obvious vaporizer damage, and a defective individual vaporizer have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve the vaporizer interface, recognition system, mounting mechanism, configuration, or another internal service-level issue. Do not assume a specific failed component without manufacturer-supported testing.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled **Out of Service**
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Mindray documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, complete all required anesthesia-system functional, leak, vaporizer-recognition, and return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate an unreliable anesthetic-delivery interface is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never rely on a vaporizer that is loosely mounted, intermittently recognized, or identified as the wrong agent; move the patient to verified equipment first.
