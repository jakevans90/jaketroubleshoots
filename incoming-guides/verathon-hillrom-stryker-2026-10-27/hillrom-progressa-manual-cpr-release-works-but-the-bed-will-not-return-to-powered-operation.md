## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoot loss of powered bed operation after manual CPR release by checking safety status, power, controls, release position, connections, and reset conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Maintain Patient Safety and Emergency Readiness

If the manual CPR release has been used and the bed will not resume powered operation, do not leave a patient dependent on the bed for positioning, transport, or emergency functions.

Transfer the patient to another verified bed when powered positioning cannot be restored promptly and safely.

Apply the brakes and ensure the bed is mechanically stable before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care is transferred or otherwise safely maintained while the affected bed is evaluated. If safe operation cannot be assured, remove the bed from service.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Post-CPR Condition

Determine what happened immediately before the fault:

- Manual CPR release was activated.
- The head section lowered normally.
- Powered controls stopped responding afterward.
- Some functions work while others do not.
- An alert, indicator, or unusual mechanical condition remains present.

Test only normal operator controls.

**Expected outcome:** The extent of the powered-operation failure is defined. If all functions operate normally after the CPR mechanism is restored, continue with verification and stop if repeated testing passes.

### 3. Verify the Manual CPR Release Has Fully Returned to Its Normal Position

Inspect the manual CPR release handle, lever, cable, and externally visible mechanism.

Confirm that the control is not still partially pulled, mechanically hung up, obstructed, or prevented from returning by linens, accessories, or impact damage.

Do not force the mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** The manual CPR release returns fully to its normal resting position. If correcting an external obstruction restores powered operation, perform repeated functional verification and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Check AC Power and Bed Power Status

Verify the bed is connected to known-good AC power and confirm the outlet is functioning if necessary.

Inspect the power cord and plug for visible damage. Confirm normal bed power indicators and determine whether any powered functions respond.

**Expected outcome:** The bed has stable AC power and normal power indication. If reconnecting power or correcting an external supply issue restores operation, verify all required functions before return to service.

### 5. Check for a Normal Reset or Recovery Condition

Allow the bed to complete any normal startup or recovery sequence available through standard operator use.

If facility-approved procedures allow power cycling the bed through normal controls or disconnect/reconnect of AC power, perform that action without entering a service menu or bypassing safety features.

**Expected outcome:** The bed initializes normally and powered functions become available. If normal operation returns and remains reliable through repeated testing, troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Test Multiple Normal Control Locations

Check powered bed movement from the normal caregiver controls, patient controls when appropriate, and any other authorized external control location.

Determine whether the problem affects:

- All powered motion.
- Only the head section.
- Only controls associated with one panel.
- A group of related functions.

**Expected outcome:** Control response is consistent across authorized interfaces. A single nonresponsive control location narrows the problem to that control path rather than a complete bed power failure.

### 7. Inspect Accessible CPR and Control Connections

Remove the bed from patient use and inspect accessible cables, external connectors, and wiring associated with controls and moving frame sections.

Look for connections that may have been pulled, pinched, or strained during rapid CPR lowering.

Do not open internal control modules or disassemble the CPR mechanism beyond approved external inspection.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible connections are secure and undamaged. If restoring an approved connection returns normal powered operation, perform complete functional verification.

### 8. Verify Head Section and Related Motion

Once powered operation returns, raise and lower the head section using powered controls.

Then operate other major bed functions to confirm that the manual release did not leave the bed in an abnormal or unreliable operating state.

**Expected outcome:** The head section and other powered functions operate smoothly and consistently without unexpected stopping, noise, or alerts.

### 9. Verify CPR Function and Recovery Before Return to Service

Using the appropriate manufacturer-approved verification procedure for an unloaded bed, confirm that the CPR release functions correctly and that the bed can subsequently resume powered operation.

Do not intentionally place a patient at risk to test the feature.

**Expected outcome:** Manual CPR operation and subsequent powered recovery both function consistently. If they do, the bed may be returned to service after required facility testing.

## If the Problem Persists
If the CPR release returns normally but powered bed operation does not recover, or if recovery is intermittent, likely remaining categories include CPR interlock sensing, control wiring, actuator enable circuitry, power/control electronics, or another internal service-level fault.

The bed should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Hillrom documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

After repair, verify CPR release, powered head movement, all affected bed motions, controls, alarms or indicators, and other required safety functions before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
A bed that cannot reliably recover powered positioning after CPR activation should not remain available for patient care, even if the manual release itself still operates.

## Final Thought
Treat failure to recover after CPR activation as a patient-safety issue, verify the release mechanism and external power path first, test recovery carefully, and remove the bed from service if powered operation remains unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
