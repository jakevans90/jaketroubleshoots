## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot a ProCuity bed extender or mattress retainer that will not move correctly, lock securely, or remain in its intended position.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Before Testing

Do not troubleshoot an unstable bed extender or mattress-retention system while a patient depends on it for support or positioning.

If the mattress could shift, leave an unsupported gap, or create an entrapment or fall risk, transfer the patient to another verified bed before testing.

**Expected outcome:** The bed is safely available for inspection without a patient depending on the affected component. If secure mattress support cannot be maintained, remove the bed from service.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Complaint

Operate the extender or retainer normally and determine whether:

- It will not move.
- It moves but will not lock.
- It locks only in certain positions.
- It releases unexpectedly.
- The mattress retainer will not seat or remain secured.
- Movement is restricted by the mattress, linens, or accessories.

Do not force a stuck mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** The affected function and failure condition are clearly reproduced. If normal locking is restored and repeatable, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 3. Remove External Obstructions

Inspect the extender, retainer, frame interface, mattress edge, linens, mattress cover, cables, accessory straps, and stored equipment for interference.

Ensure that bedding or a mattress corner is not trapped in the mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** The extender and retainer have an unobstructed path of travel. If removing an obstruction restores normal operation, repeat the function several times and proceed to final verification.

### 4. Inspect for Visible Damage or Misalignment

Check accessible mechanical parts for:

- Bent retainers.
- Impact damage.
- Cracked or deformed plastic.
- Loose visible hardware.
- Misaligned components.
- Debris or dried contamination in locking areas.

Do not bend components back into position or perform unapproved mechanical adjustment.

**Expected outcome:** No external damage or misalignment prevents normal engagement. Significant damage requires removal from service and repair evaluation.

### 5. Verify Correct Extender Positioning

Move the bed extender through its normal external range using the intended release and locking controls. Ensure it reaches a complete stop rather than being left partially between positions.

Avoid excessive force.

**Expected outcome:** The extender moves smoothly and positively engages at its intended positions. If it remains loose, will not engage, or releases under normal handling, it is not safe for clinical use.

### 6. Check Mattress Retainer Engagement

Verify that each affected mattress retainer is properly seated and positioned to control mattress movement. Compare it with an equivalent retainer on the opposite side or end of the bed if available.

Inspect for an incorrectly installed accessory or mattress interfering with engagement.

**Expected outcome:** The mattress retainer remains securely positioned and supports the mattress without excessive movement. If the retainer cannot be secured, continue to escalation.

### 7. Verify Mattress and Accessory Compatibility

Confirm that the mattress and installed accessories are appropriate for the bed configuration and are not preventing the extender or retainer from engaging.

Do not modify the bed or attachment points to accommodate an incompatible accessory.

**Expected outcome:** The installed mattress and accessories do not interfere with the extender or retention system. If changing to an approved, known-good configuration resolves the issue, perform final verification.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

After any external correction:

- Extend and retract the bed as applicable.
- Confirm positive locking.
- Verify the retainer remains secured.
- Check that the mattress lies correctly and does not shift excessively.
- Inspect for gaps, pinch points, or interference.
- Verify nearby bed functions were not affected.

Complete required return-to-service inspection according to approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The extender and mattress-retention components operate smoothly, remain securely locked, and maintain proper mattress positioning. Troubleshooting can stop and the bed can return to service.

## If the Problem Persists

If the extender or retainer still will not lock after external interference, mattress fit, visible damage, and positioning have been checked, the problem may involve the internal locking mechanism, mechanical adjustment, worn components, or another service-level condition.

The bed should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel.

Do not use a temporary restraint, improvised latch, or modification to keep the extender or mattress retainer in place.

Following repair, verify secure locking, mattress positioning, absence of hazardous gaps, and required overall bed safety functions before clinical use.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never return a bed to use when the mattress can shift enough to create an unsupported gap or compromise patient positioning.

## Final Thought

A bed extender or mattress retainer is part of the patient-support system and must remain mechanically secure. Rule out simple interference and configuration problems first, verify positive locking, and stop troubleshooting when a service-level mechanical problem is suspected. Clear CCR documentation should show exactly what was found and how safe operation was confirmed.

That is successful troubleshooting.
