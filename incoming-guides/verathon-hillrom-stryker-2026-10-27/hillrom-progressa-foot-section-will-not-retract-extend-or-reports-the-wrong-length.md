## What This Guide Helps With
Troubleshoot foot-section movement or length indication problems caused by loading, obstruction, controls, positioning, connections, or configuration before escalating for service.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting
### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Safe Bed Support

If the foot section cannot be positioned reliably, do not continue troubleshooting while a patient depends on the affected movement. Transfer the patient to another verified bed when the fault could affect positioning, support, transfer, or fall prevention.

Secure the bed, apply the brakes, lower it when appropriate, and ensure no staff, equipment, linens, or patient extremities are near moving sections.

**Expected outcome:** The bed is in a safe condition for controlled troubleshooting without risk to the patient or staff. If safe operation cannot be maintained, remove the bed from service and stop troubleshooting.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Foot-Section Complaint

Determine whether the foot section:

- Will not extend.
- Will not retract.
- Moves only partway.
- Stops intermittently.
- Moves normally but displays an incorrect length or position.
- Produces an alert or indication during movement.

Operate the function using the normal accessible control intended for the movement.

**Expected outcome:** The exact failure condition is reproduced consistently or the bed operates normally. If operation is normal and repeated functional checks pass, troubleshooting can stop after documenting the findings.

### 3. Check for External Mechanical Obstruction

Inspect the foot section, mattress, linens, accessories, frame openings, cables, drainage tubing, and nearby equipment for anything that could restrict travel.

Confirm that no accessory is mounted in a way that interferes with telescoping or moving components.

**Expected outcome:** The foot section has an unobstructed travel path. If removing an obstruction restores full normal operation and indication, verify the function through repeated cycles and stop troubleshooting.

### 4. Verify Mattress and Foot-End Positioning

Confirm the mattress and any approved surface components are correctly seated and are not shifted, bunched, or caught around the moving foot section.

Check that removable foot-end components and accessories are fully installed and aligned.

**Expected outcome:** The bed surface and foot-end components are correctly positioned without loading or binding the mechanism. If correcting positioning restores operation, complete functional verification and stop.

### 5. Check Bed Power and Control Availability

Verify the bed is connected to known-good AC power when available and that normal power indicators are present.

Check whether other powered bed functions operate normally. Test the affected foot-section function from each normal operator control location available for that function.

Do not enter restricted service menus or alter service configuration.

**Expected outcome:** The bed has normal power and the foot function responds consistently from authorized controls. If one external control location is the only source of the problem, document the finding and evaluate that control assembly according to approved service documentation.

### 6. Inspect Accessible Cables and Connections

With the bed safely positioned and removed from patient use, inspect externally accessible wiring, connectors, handset/control cables, and foot-section harness routing for:

- Loose connections.
- Pinched cables.
- Abrasion.
- Stretching during section travel.
- Fluid contamination.
- Impact damage.

Do not disassemble internal drive assemblies or control boards.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible connections are secure and undamaged. If reseating an approved external connection restores reliable movement and indication, repeat the movement several times before returning the bed to service.

### 7. Compare Actual Movement With the Displayed Length or Position

If the foot section physically moves but the reported length appears incorrect, compare the physical section position with the displayed indication through its normal range.

Do not assume the drive mechanism is defective when movement is normal but position feedback is incorrect.

**Expected outcome:** Physical position and displayed status change logically together. A persistent mismatch indicates a feedback, calibration, configuration, or service-level problem requiring escalation.

### 8. Perform Final Functional Verification

After any external correction, operate the foot section through multiple normal extend and retract cycles with the bed unloaded or appropriately test-loaded according to facility practice.

Verify:

- Smooth movement.
- No abnormal noise or binding.
- Correct stopping behavior.
- Consistent control response.
- Logical length or position indication.
- No new bed alerts.

**Expected outcome:** The foot section operates smoothly and reports position consistently. If all checks pass, troubleshooting is complete and the bed may be returned to service following required facility testing.

## If the Problem Persists
If the foot section still will not extend or retract correctly, stops at inconsistent positions, or reports an incorrect length after external causes have been ruled out, the remaining causes may involve position feedback, actuator control, drive components, wiring, configuration, or another internal service-level condition.

The bed should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled **Out of Service**.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate Hillrom service documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired, calibrated, or configured only by qualified personnel.

After repair, verify full foot-section movement, position indication, related bed controls, and any affected safety functions before return to clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip
Do not allow a patient to remain dependent on a foot section that moves unpredictably or cannot maintain the required support position.

## Final Thought
Protect the patient first, verify simple external causes before assuming an internal failure, confirm both movement and position indication after correction, and escalate any unreliable positioning function with clear CCR documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
