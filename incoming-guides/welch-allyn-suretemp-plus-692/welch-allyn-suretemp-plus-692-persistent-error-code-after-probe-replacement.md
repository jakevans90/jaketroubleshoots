## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots a continuing error after probe replacement caused by incompatibility, connection, probe well, contamination, power, configuration, or device-side faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Remove the Thermometer From Use

Do not use the thermometer while an unresolved error remains, even if a new probe has been installed. Provide another verified device for patient temperature measurement.

Document the exact displayed code or message without interpreting it beyond available manufacturer documentation.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care continues safely, and the original error information is preserved.

### 2. Confirm the Replacement History

Determine why the probe was replaced, whether the original and replacement probes produced the same error, and whether the error began before or after replacement.

Record the replacement probe’s source, compatibility, and condition.

**Expected outcome:** The sequence of events is clear and unnecessary repeated probe replacement is avoided.

### 3. Verify the Replacement Probe Is Compatible

Confirm that the replacement probe is approved for the SureTemp Plus 692 and matches the installed probe well and intended measurement application.

Inspect for packaging damage, an incorrect connector, or a probe intended for another model.

**Expected outcome:** A correct compatible replacement probe is installed. If the probe is incompatible, install the proper accessory and retest.

### 4. Inspect the New Probe Before Use

Even a new probe may be damaged. Inspect the probe, cable, strain relief, connector, and tip for cracks, bent contacts, contamination, pinched packaging damage, or moisture.

**Expected outcome:** The replacement probe is visibly intact and dry.

### 5. Inspect and Reseat the Probe Connector

Power the thermometer off. Disconnect the probe and inspect both the plug and thermometer receptacle for debris, corrosion, moisture, damaged alignment features, or bent contacts.

Reconnect the probe securely and restart the thermometer.

**Expected outcome:** The probe is fully seated and the error clears. If so, perform repeated functional verification.

### 6. Inspect the Probe Well

Confirm that the correct probe well is installed, fully seated, clean, and undamaged. Remove accessible debris and replace a cracked or warped well.

A storage-detection or probe-well problem may remain even after the probe itself is replaced.

**Expected outcome:** Probe-well detection is normal and no storage fault contributes to the error.

### 7. Install Fresh Verified Batteries

Replace the complete battery set with fresh, matched, correct batteries and inspect the contacts. Restart the device using only the standard operating procedure.

Unstable power can preserve or retrigger an error condition.

**Expected outcome:** The thermometer starts normally with stable power. If the error clears, complete repeated measurements and verification.

### 8. Test With a Second Known-Good Compatible Probe

When available, test with a probe that is already verified on another compatible thermometer. This prevents a defective replacement probe from misleading the diagnosis.

**Expected outcome:** The known-good probe either clears the error, identifying the replacement probe as faulty, or produces the same error, indicating a device-side or probe-well issue.

### 9. Test the Replacement Probe on Another Compatible Device

Connect the replacement probe to a verified compatible SureTemp Plus 692 when safe and practical.

Follow infection-control precautions and do not use the comparison device for patient care during testing.

**Expected outcome:** The replacement probe functions normally on the comparison device or reproduces the error and is removed from use.

### 10. Check Approved Configuration and Normal Startup State

Compare the suspect thermometer with another department unit using the same approved configuration. Do not enter unauthorized service menus or clear diagnostic history through undocumented procedures.

**Expected outcome:** No approved setting or accessory mismatch is contributing to the persistent error.

### 11. Perform Approved Functional and Accuracy Testing

If the error clears, complete repeated probe insertion, removal, mode selection, measurement, and temperature-verification testing using approved equipment.

**Expected outcome:** The error does not recur and the thermometer completes stable, accurate measurements. If the error returns, remove the device from service.

### 12. Stop Replacing External Parts and Escalate

If two known-good compatible probes, a verified probe well, secure connections, and fresh batteries do not clear the error, stop external troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The thermometer is labeled Out of Service and routed for qualified service evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving probe compatibility, probe condition, connector seating, the probe well, batteries, and configuration have been ruled out. The remaining error may involve the probe receptacle, internal detection circuitry, storage-detection mechanism, internal wiring, control electronics, or another service-level fault.

Remove the device from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it using the appropriate Welch Allyn service documentation and approved test equipment. Repairs and protected configuration changes should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify the documented error is cleared, the probe is recognized repeatedly, all required modes operate, and temperature accuracy passes the approved verification process. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not assume a replacement probe fixed the problem until the thermometer completes repeated verified measurements without the original error recurring.

## Final Thought

A new probe does not rule out connection, probe-well, power, compatibility, or device-side causes. Protect the patient, preserve the exact error information, verify known-good external components, and escalate rather than continuing unsupported part replacement.

That is successful troubleshooting.
