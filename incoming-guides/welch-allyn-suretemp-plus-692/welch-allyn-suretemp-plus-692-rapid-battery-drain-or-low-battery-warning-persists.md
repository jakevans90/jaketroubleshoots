## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots short battery life or persistent low-battery indication caused by batteries, contacts, installation, contamination, storage, or abnormal power consumption.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain an Available Backup

Do not rely on a thermometer that may shut down during patient assessment. Provide another verified thermometer and remove the affected device from active clinical use during evaluation.

Inspect for heat, odor, swollen batteries, corrosion, leakage, or liquid intrusion. Use appropriate precautions when handling damaged batteries.

**Expected outcome:** Patient care is uninterrupted, and unsafe batteries or equipment are isolated.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Battery Problem

Ask staff whether batteries drain during use, while stored, or immediately after replacement. Determine whether the low-battery indication appears continuously, intermittently, or only during measurement.

Document battery brand, type, age, installation date, and estimated usage when known.

**Expected outcome:** The battery complaint is defined well enough to distinguish actual drain from an incorrect warning indication.

### 3. Verify the Correct Battery Type and Complete Set

Confirm that all installed batteries are the approved size and chemistry specified for the thermometer. Do not mix old and new batteries, brands, chemistries, or cells with different charge states.

Replace the complete set with fresh, matched batteries from a reliable supply.

**Expected outcome:** A matched set of correct batteries is installed. If the warning clears and runtime is normal, proceed to final verification.

### 4. Verify Battery Orientation and Compartment Closure

Inspect the polarity markings and confirm every battery is installed in the correct direction. Ensure the battery compartment cover is fully closed and secure.

A partially installed battery or loose cover may create intermittent power and false low-battery indications.

**Expected outcome:** All batteries are correctly oriented and held securely.

### 5. Inspect the Battery Compartment

Remove the batteries and inspect accessible contacts and the compartment for corrosion, residue, bent contacts, weakened spring tension, contamination, or signs of prior battery leakage.

Clean only according to approved procedures. Do not scrape contacts aggressively or apply unapproved chemicals.

**Expected outcome:** Contacts are clean, dry, intact, and provide firm pressure against the batteries.

### 6. Check the Replacement Batteries Independently

Use an appropriate battery tester or digital multimeter to compare the fresh cells before and after installation. Identify a weak or defective cell within the set.

Do not judge battery condition only by package age or appearance.

**Expected outcome:** Each installed battery is in comparable, serviceable condition. A weak cell is replaced as part of a complete matched set.

### 7. Observe Startup and Low-Battery Indication

Start the thermometer several times and observe whether the low-battery symbol appears immediately, only under measurement load, or after a period of operation.

Gently reposition the device without striking or shaking it and watch for power interruptions that may indicate poor contact.

**Expected outcome:** The thermometer starts consistently and the warning remains cleared with fresh batteries. If the warning persists immediately, further evaluation is required.

### 8. Check for Controls or Mechanical Conditions Keeping the Device Active

Inspect the keypad and probe-storage area for stuck buttons, a probe that does not seat correctly, or a storage-detection fault that may prevent normal standby behavior.

Verify the thermometer transitions to its expected idle state after the probe is returned to the well.

**Expected outcome:** No stuck control or storage fault keeps the device unnecessarily active.

### 9. Compare Runtime Under Controlled Conditions

Install fresh verified batteries, record the date, and perform repeated nonpatient functional cycles over a controlled observation period. Compare behavior with another verified SureTemp Plus 692 under similar use when practical.

Avoid declaring excessive drain based on an unknown prior battery history.

**Expected outcome:** Battery indication and operating time remain stable during controlled use. If the fresh batteries decline abnormally, remove the device from service.

### 10. Verify the Warning Clears After Battery Replacement

After installing known-good batteries, restart the thermometer and confirm that the low-battery warning clears. If the display retains an old indication, perform only the manufacturer-supported normal restart procedure.

Do not enter unauthorized service modes or alter internal calibration data.

**Expected outcome:** The warning accurately reflects the condition of the installed batteries.

### 11. Stop and Escalate Persistent Battery Drain

If known-good matched batteries drain rapidly or the low-battery warning remains despite clean, intact contacts, do not continue clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** The thermometer is labeled and routed for internal power-management or display-circuit evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

External causes involving battery type, condition, installation, contacts, compartment closure, keypad state, and probe storage have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve excessive internal current draw, power-management circuitry, internal contamination, a defective display indication, or another service-level fault.

Remove the thermometer from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it with appropriate Welch Allyn documentation and approved test equipment. Internal repair should be completed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify startup, low-battery indication, stable operation under repeated measurements, normal standby behavior, and temperature accuracy. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Replace the full battery set at the first sign of unreliable power and keep another verified thermometer available so patient assessment is not delayed.

## Final Thought

Confirm battery quality, installation, contact condition, and normal standby behavior before assuming an internal power problem. Remove the thermometer from service when power remains unreliable, verify the correction under load, and document the exact battery findings and final result.

That is successful troubleshooting.
