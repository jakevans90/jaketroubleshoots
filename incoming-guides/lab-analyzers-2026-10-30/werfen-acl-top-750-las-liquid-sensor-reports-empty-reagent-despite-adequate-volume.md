## What This Guide Helps With

Helps isolate false reagent-empty indications caused by container placement, bubbles, reagent condition, identification, sensing, or aspiration-related problems.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Testing and Confirm Continuity of Care

Do not rely on an analyzer that cannot reliably recognize or aspirate required reagents. Notify the laboratory and redirect testing to another validated analyzer when necessary.

Do not bypass reagent monitoring or continue patient testing using a reagent position whose status cannot be verified.

**Expected outcome:** Testing continuity is maintained independently of the questionable reagent position. Continue only after patient-impact risk is controlled.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Determine:

- Which reagent position is affected
- Whether one or multiple reagents are reported empty
- Whether the message appears immediately after loading
- Whether it occurs only during aspiration
- Whether the reagent was recently replaced
- Whether the problem follows the reagent container or remains with the analyzer position

Confirm visually that adequate reagent volume is actually present.

**Expected outcome:** The affected reagent and circumstances are clearly identified. If the container is genuinely empty, replacement resolves the issue and troubleshooting can stop.

### 3. Inspect Reagent Container Installation

Check the affected reagent container for:

- Proper seating
- Correct orientation
- Correct container type
- Secure cap or closure configuration where applicable
- Physical damage
- Warping
- Leakage
- Obstructions around the container position

Compare its installation with a functioning reagent position.

**Expected outcome:** The reagent container is correctly installed and physically intact. If reseating the container corrects the status indication, the issue is resolved.

### 4. Check Reagent Condition and Accessible Liquid Path

Inspect for conditions that can interfere with liquid detection or aspiration, including:

- Excessive bubbles or foam
- Reagent adhering unusually to container walls
- Contamination
- Precipitate or visible abnormal material
- Incorrectly prepared reagent
- Improperly handled reagent

Follow laboratory and manufacturer requirements for reagent preparation and handling. Do not manipulate chemistry beyond approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The reagent appears appropriate for use and presents a stable liquid level. If correcting an approved preparation or handling problem restores detection, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 5. Verify Reagent Identification and Position

Confirm that the correct reagent is loaded in the expected position and that any required identification information has been recognized correctly.

Check for damaged, obscured, or poorly positioned labels or identification features where applicable.

**Expected outcome:** The analyzer correctly associates the reagent container with its intended position. If correcting identification or placement restores normal recognition, the problem is resolved.

### 6. Compare With a Known-Good Reagent Container

When permitted by laboratory procedure, replace the questionable container with a known-good, properly prepared reagent container.

Observe whether the empty indication:

- Clears with the known-good container
- Follows the original container
- Remains associated with the analyzer position

This comparison helps separate a reagent/container problem from a sensing or aspiration problem.

**Expected outcome:** A known-good reagent is recognized correctly. If the problem follows the original reagent container, remove that reagent from use and troubleshooting can stop after proper replacement and verification.

### 7. Inspect Accessible Sensor and Aspiration Areas

With the analyzer in an appropriate safe state, inspect accessible reagent sensing and aspiration areas for:

- Dried reagent residue
- Spills
- Visible contamination
- Obstruction
- Misalignment of the container relative to the aspiration position

Clean only areas identified for routine external cleaning using approved methods.

Do not disassemble liquid sensors, probes, pumps, or internal fluidic assemblies.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible sensing and aspiration areas are clean and unobstructed. If approved cleaning corrects reagent detection, proceed to functional verification.

### 8. Restart or Reinitialize Normally if Appropriate

If no physical or reagent-related problem is found, perform a normal system restart or approved reagent reinitialization using standard controls.

Do not use unauthorized service menus or alter calibration or configuration settings.

**Expected outcome:** The analyzer recognizes the reagent correctly after normal initialization. If the false-empty indication returns immediately, service-level evaluation is appropriate.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Using appropriate laboratory QC or nonpatient verification material, confirm that the analyzer:

- Recognizes the reagent
- Aspirates without repeated empty indications
- Completes the required assay sequence
- Produces acceptable QC according to laboratory requirements

**Expected outcome:** Reagent status remains stable and controlled testing completes successfully. If all applicable checks pass, the analyzer may be returned to service.

## If the Problem Persists

If adequate reagent volume, correct installation, reagent condition, identification, bubbles, contamination, and known-good substitutions have been ruled out, the problem may involve a liquid-level sensing system, aspiration path, positioning mechanism, fluidics, configuration, or another service-level condition.

The analyzer should be:

- Removed from service if the affected reagent is required for clinical testing
- Labeled Out of Service when reliable testing cannot be assured
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return the analyzer to service only after reliable reagent detection, aspiration, and required laboratory QC have been demonstrated.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting instead of bypassing an unreliable reagent indication is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

A reagent that appears physically full is not sufficient evidence of availability; confirm that the analyzer can consistently recognize and aspirate it before patient testing.

## Final Thought

Confirm the reagent, container, placement, identification, and visible fluid condition before suspecting an internal liquid sensor. Verify successful aspiration and QC, escalate persistent sensing problems, and document the evidence supporting the final action.

That is successful troubleshooting.
