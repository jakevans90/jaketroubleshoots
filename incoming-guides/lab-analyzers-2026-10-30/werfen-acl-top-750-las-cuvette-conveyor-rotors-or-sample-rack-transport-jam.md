## What This Guide Helps With

Helps isolate transport jams caused by obstructed racks, cuvettes, loading errors, positioning problems, debris, or service-level mechanical faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Patient Testing and Confirm Continuity of Care

Do not continue relying on an analyzer that cannot reliably move samples, cuvettes, or reaction components. Notify the laboratory that the instrument is being evaluated and direct testing to another validated analyzer when required.

Do not attempt to clear moving mechanisms while the analyzer is actively operating.

**Expected outcome:** Patient testing is transferred or otherwise safely maintained before troubleshooting begins. If continuity of care is established, continue troubleshooting.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Jam Condition

Ask laboratory staff what happened immediately before the problem. Determine whether the fault involves:

- Sample rack loading or movement
- Cuvette transport
- Reaction rotor movement
- A specific rack or sample position
- A recurring location in the transport path
- A single event or repeated jams

Observe the analyzer from outside its normal accessible areas and note any displayed messages without assuming they identify the failed component.

**Expected outcome:** The affected transport section and circumstances are clearly identified. If the problem was caused by an obvious loading mistake that is safely corrected and normal operation returns, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 3. Check Sample Racks and Loading Conditions

Remove accessible sample racks according to normal laboratory handling practices. Inspect them for:

- Bent, cracked, or damaged rack components
- Incorrectly seated tubes
- Tubes extending unusually high or low
- Loose labels interfering with transport
- Caps or accessories contacting nearby structures
- Foreign material on rack surfaces

Compare the questionable rack with a known-good rack if available.

**Expected outcome:** Racks and tubes are correctly seated and move freely without external interference. If replacing or correcting a damaged or improperly loaded rack restores normal operation, the issue is resolved.

### 4. Inspect Accessible Cuvettes and Consumables

Check accessible cuvette supplies and transport areas for:

- Misloaded or deformed cuvettes
- Loose cuvettes outside their intended path
- Packaging fragments
- Broken plastic
- Debris
- Incorrect consumable installation

Do not reach into inaccessible moving assemblies or force a jammed mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible cuvette supplies are correctly installed and no external obstruction remains. If removing an accessible obstruction restores reliable operation, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 5. Inspect the Accessible Transport Path

With the analyzer placed in a safe state, visually inspect externally accessible transport areas for foreign material, spilled residue, broken labels, damaged racks, or components visibly out of position.

Do not manually rotate motors, force belts, move internal gears, or defeat covers or interlocks.

**Expected outcome:** The accessible transport path is clear and no obvious damage is present. Persistent binding or abnormal alignment indicates service-level evaluation is required.

### 6. Verify Covers, Drawers, and Loading Areas

Confirm that user-accessible doors, covers, drawers, and loading areas are fully closed and properly seated. A partially seated access point can prevent normal transport initialization or movement.

**Expected outcome:** All accessible covers and loading areas are correctly positioned. If reseating one restores normal transport, the issue is resolved.

### 7. Restart Only When Safe and Appropriate

If no physical obstruction or damage is present, perform a normal analyzer restart using approved operating controls. Do not repeatedly cycle power if the transport mechanism continues to bind or make abnormal sounds.

Observe initialization and determine whether the affected transport system completes its normal movement.

**Expected outcome:** The analyzer initializes without transport faults, abnormal sounds, or repeated jams. If initialization completes normally, continue to functional verification.

### 8. Test With Known-Good Racks and Normal Consumables

Using laboratory-approved test material or other appropriate nonpatient testing, run the transport system with a known-good rack and properly installed consumables.

Observe rack loading, movement, cuvette handling, and completion of the test sequence.

**Expected outcome:** Samples and consumables move through the system without hesitation, repeated positioning attempts, or transport alarms. If successful, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm that the analyzer can:

- Accept and move a known-good rack
- Handle cuvettes normally
- Complete its normal test sequence
- Produce expected results from appropriate QC or verification material
- Operate without recurring transport faults

Complete any required laboratory QC or return-to-service procedures before clinical use.

**Expected outcome:** Transport remains reliable through a complete controlled test and required QC. The analyzer may be returned to service if all applicable checks pass.

## If the Problem Persists

If the jam remains after racks, tubes, consumables, accessible obstructions, covers, and loading conditions have been ruled out, the problem may involve a transport sensor, motor, drive mechanism, alignment issue, rotor mechanism, or other internal service-level fault.

The analyzer should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not repeatedly force transport components or continue cycling the analyzer through a mechanical jam. Return to service only after the transport system operates normally and all required operational checks and laboratory QC have passed.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate a persistent mechanical transport problem is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never allow patient samples to remain dependent on an analyzer with intermittent transport because a partial jam can delay testing or create sample identification problems.

## Final Thought

Protect patient testing first, then work from loading conditions and accessible obstructions toward service-level causes. Verify reliable transport before assuming an internal failure, escalate when mechanical movement remains abnormal, and clearly document what was found and tested.

That is successful troubleshooting.
