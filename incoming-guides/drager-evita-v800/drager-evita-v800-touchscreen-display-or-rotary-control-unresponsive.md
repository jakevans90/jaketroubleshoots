## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses unresponsive touchscreen, display, or rotary controls caused by startup state, contamination, obstruction, external power, accessories, or control-system faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Control of Ventilation

If the touchscreen, display, or rotary control becomes unreliable while the ventilator is supporting a patient, do not continue depending on controls that cannot be operated reliably. Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or appropriate backup ventilation method.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is ventilated independently of the device with unreliable controls.

### 2. Confirm Which Interface Is Affected

Determine whether the problem involves:

- Touchscreen input
- Blank or frozen display
- Display image present but no touch response
- Rotary control not responding
- Intermittent control response
- All controls simultaneously unresponsive

Record what was displayed when the problem occurred.

**Expected outcome:** The specific interface failure is identified. If normal response has returned, reproduce the condition off the patient before deciding the ventilator is acceptable for use.

### 3. Check AC Power and Startup State

Verify the ventilator has stable AC power and completes normal startup. Observe whether the screen initializes normally or freezes during boot.

Inspect the external power cord and connection for looseness or damage.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator starts normally from a verified power source. If correcting power restores full control response and the problem does not recur during testing, continue to final verification.

### 4. Inspect the Screen and Controls Externally

Check for:

- Liquid contamination
- Cleaning residue
- Adhesive material
- Protective material interfering with touch
- Objects resting against the screen
- Physical impact damage
- Cracked display surfaces
- Rotary control obstruction

Clean only according to approved practices.

**Expected outcome:** The controls are clean, unobstructed, and physically intact. If removing contamination or obstruction restores consistent operation, proceed to functional testing.

### 5. Remove Nonessential External Connections

With the ventilator off the patient, disconnect nonessential external peripherals or communication accessories that are not required for basic operation, when this can be done safely and without altering protected configuration.

Restart only according to normal operator-accessible procedures.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator starts with normal control response. If removing a peripheral resolves the issue, isolate the suspect accessory or connection before returning the ventilator to service.

### 6. Verify Touchscreen and Rotary Control Response

Navigate through normal user-accessible screens and controls without changing protected service parameters. Confirm touch selections register consistently and the rotary control responds throughout ordinary interaction.

**Expected outcome:** Controls respond reliably without missed or unintended selections. If so, continue to display verification.

### 7. Verify Display Stability

Observe the screen for blanking, flickering, freezing, distorted content, delayed refresh, or intermittent loss of information during controlled operation.

**Expected outcome:** Display information remains continuously legible and responsive. If instability recurs, remove the ventilator from service.

### 8. Perform a Controlled Functional Test

Connect the ventilator to a suitable test lung or approved test equipment and operate it through normal user-accessible functions. Confirm controls remain usable while ventilation is active and alarms can be viewed and managed appropriately.

**Expected outcome:** The display and controls remain stable throughout ventilation testing. If all functions remain reliable, continue to final system verification.

### 9. Complete Required System and Alarm Checks

Perform the applicable pre-use or system check and verify representative alarm annunciation and control interaction.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator completes its required checks with reliable control and display operation. Troubleshooting can stop and the unit may be returned to service.

### 10. Escalate Any Recurring Interface Failure

An intermittent touchscreen, display, or rotary control problem is unsafe even if the device temporarily recovers. If the issue recurs or cannot be confidently resolved through external causes, stop troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator remains out of service for qualified evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

If power, contamination, physical obstruction, external accessories, and normal restart conditions have been ruled out, persistent or intermittent control failure may involve the display assembly, touch interface, control electronics, software, communication between assemblies, or another service-level problem.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify stable startup, touchscreen and rotary control response, display operation, ventilation, alarms, and required return-to-service testing. An intermittently unreliable control interface should not be returned to clinical use simply because it temporarily responds.

## Clinical Use Tip

If clinicians cannot reliably view or change ventilation information, move the patient to another verified ventilator before attempting technical troubleshooting.

## Final Thought

Unreliable controls can make an otherwise functioning ventilator unsafe. Protect the patient first, eliminate simple power, contamination, obstruction, and accessory causes, verify the interface under actual ventilation testing, and escalate any recurring control failure.

That is successful troubleshooting.
