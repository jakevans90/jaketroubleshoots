## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses failed pre-use or system checks caused by circuit setup, accessories, gas supply, connections, configuration, or other externally verifiable conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ventilation

Do not troubleshoot an Evita V800 that is providing unreliable ventilation to a patient. Transfer ventilation to another verified ventilator or an appropriate backup method before continuing. Confirm the patient is stable and continuously supported.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is safely supported independently of the ventilator being evaluated. If reliable ventilation cannot be assured, remove the device from clinical use immediately.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Check Failure

Repeat or review the reported system or pre-use check only when the ventilator is off the patient. Record which portion of the check fails, any displayed message, and whether the failure occurs consistently.

Avoid assuming that a failed overall check identifies an internal ventilator fault.

**Expected outcome:** The failing portion of the check is identified and reproducible. If the check now completes successfully without further intervention, verify normal operation and troubleshooting can stop.

### 3. Verify AC Power and Basic Startup

Confirm the AC power cord is fully seated, the approved power source is available, and the ventilator starts normally without abnormal sounds, odor, heat, visible damage, or unrelated startup faults.

If outlet integrity is questionable, verify it using an appropriate facility-approved method.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator powers normally from a verified AC source. If correcting the power connection allows the complete pre-use check to pass, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

### 4. Inspect the Breathing Circuit Setup

Inspect the complete breathing circuit for:

- Loose or incomplete connections
- Incorrectly connected inspiratory or expiratory limbs
- Kinks or occlusions
- Damaged tubing
- Improperly installed filters or humidification components
- Water accumulation
- Open ports or unintended leaks
- Incorrect test-lung connection when used for the check

Correct only externally accessible setup problems.

**Expected outcome:** The breathing circuit is complete, unobstructed, correctly connected, and appropriate for testing. If the check passes after correction, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

### 5. Inspect the Expiratory Valve and Flow-Sensing Components

Verify that the externally removable expiratory valve and applicable flow-sensing components are correctly installed, fully seated, clean, dry, and free of obvious physical damage or contamination.

Use only compatible components intended for the Evita V800.

**Expected outcome:** Required respiratory components are correctly installed and recognized. If reseating or replacing an externally removable component resolves the check failure, complete the full system check and troubleshooting can stop.

### 6. Verify Air and Oxygen Supplies

Confirm both required gas supplies are connected correctly and that the facility supply is available. Inspect hoses and external fittings for looseness, damage, crossed connections, severe kinking, or obvious leakage.

If available, compare operation using another known-good facility gas source or approved hose.

**Expected outcome:** Required gas supplies are present and stable. If restoring a gas connection permits the system check to complete successfully, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

### 7. Check External Accessories and Configuration

Verify that accessories required for the intended setup are compatible, connected correctly, and configured appropriately. Compare the current setup with a known-good Evita V800 installation when practical.

Do not enter unauthorized service menus or change protected service configuration.

**Expected outcome:** Installed accessories and user-accessible configuration match the intended ventilator setup. If correcting an external configuration issue resolves the failure, repeat the complete check and stop troubleshooting if it passes.

### 8. Substitute Known-Good External Components

When the failed check points toward a removable circuit component, sensor, valve assembly, hose, cable, or accessory, substitute a compatible known-good component one at a time.

Avoid changing multiple items simultaneously because doing so obscures the actual cause.

**Expected outcome:** A single external component substitution identifies or rules out the removable component as the cause. If the full check passes with the known-good component, replace the defective accessory and troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correction, perform the complete required pre-use or system check with the intended breathing circuit configuration. Confirm normal startup, gas availability, ventilation operation, displayed values, and applicable alarms before return to service.

**Expected outcome:** The Evita V800 completes its required check without unresolved failures and demonstrates normal operation. The ventilator may be returned to service according to facility procedure.

### 10. Stop and Escalate if the Check Still Fails

If the system check repeatedly fails after external causes have been ruled out, do not bypass the failure or place the ventilator into patient service.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator remains removed from service pending qualified bench evaluation.

## If the Problem Persists

A persistent system or pre-use check failure after power, gas supplies, breathing circuit, removable respiratory components, accessories, and accessible configuration have been verified may involve an internal pneumatic, sensing, electronic, calibration, or configuration problem.

The ventilator should be:

- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Following corrective work, complete all applicable functional, alarm, performance, and return-to-service testing before clinical use. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting rather than bypassing a failed system check is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Never place an Evita V800 on a patient when its required pre-use or system check remains unresolved.

Protect the patient first, identify the exact failed portion of the check, rule out external setup and supply problems logically, verify the complete ventilator afterward, and document the actual complaint, cause, and resolution.
