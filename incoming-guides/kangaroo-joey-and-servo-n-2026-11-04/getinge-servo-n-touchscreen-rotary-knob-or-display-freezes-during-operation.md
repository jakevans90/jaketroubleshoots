## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots frozen or unresponsive user controls caused by accessories, contamination, power conditions, software state, external connections, or service-level faults.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient Immediately
If the touchscreen, rotary control, or display becomes unreliable during ventilation, do not continue depending on the affected interface.

Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or establish appropriate backup ventilation before troubleshooting.

**Expected outcome:** Patient ventilation is no longer dependent on an unreliable control interface.

### 2. Determine What Is Actually Unresponsive
Identify whether the problem affects:
- Touchscreen only
- Rotary knob only
- Display only
- All user controls
- Display updates while ventilation continues
- Complete system operation

Record whether alarms remain active and whether the ventilator otherwise appears to continue cycling.

**Expected outcome:** The failure is accurately characterized without assuming the entire ventilator has failed.

### 3. Inspect the Screen and Controls
Check for:
- Liquid
- Cleaning residue
- Protective material interfering with touch
- Physical damage
- Stuck controls
- Debris around the rotary knob
- Evidence of impact

Clean only according to approved procedures.

**Expected outcome:** External contamination or obstruction is ruled out.

### 4. Disconnect Nonessential External Accessories
With the ventilator safely removed from patient use, disconnect nonessential external accessories or communication connections one at a time.

Observe whether a particular accessory is associated with the freeze.

**Expected outcome:** The interface remains responsive with external accessories removed, or the problematic accessory is identified.

### 5. Verify Stable Power
Connect the ventilator to a known-good AC source and confirm normal power indications.

Inspect the power cord and battery status for abnormal behavior or repeated power transitions.

**Expected outcome:** Unstable external power is ruled out.

### 6. Perform a Controlled Restart
With the ventilator removed from patient use, perform a normal controlled shutdown and restart using approved procedures.

Do not interrupt power abruptly unless specifically required by authorized service instructions.

**Expected outcome:** The ventilator completes startup normally and all controls respond. If the problem does not recur during extended verification, troubleshooting may stop after complete functional testing.

### 7. Check for Repeatability
Operate the interface through normal menus and controls without changing unauthorized configuration.

Observe for:
- Delayed response
- Repeated freezing
- Unexpected reboot
- Touch misregistration
- Rotary control dropout

**Expected outcome:** The interface remains consistently responsive.

### 8. Complete Full Functional Verification
Run required pre-use and alarm tests and verify the touchscreen, rotary control, display, and visual indications.

**Expected outcome:** All user-interface functions operate normally and consistently. Troubleshooting can stop.

## If the Problem Persists

External contamination, accessories, power conditions, and a controlled restart have been ruled out. A recurring freeze may involve display hardware, touchscreen electronics, control hardware, software, internal communication, or other service-level faults.

The Servo-n should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate Getinge service documentation
- Repaired or updated only by qualified personnel using authorized procedures

Do not return a ventilator with intermittent controls to clinical service even if ventilation hardware appears to continue operating.

After repair, verify startup, controls, display, alarms, ventilation performance, and required pre-use checks.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

An intermittent user interface is a patient-safety failure even when the ventilator appears to continue delivering breaths.

## Final Thought

A reliable control interface is essential to safe ventilation. Protect the patient first, rule out external contamination, accessories, and power conditions, and remove the ventilator from service when an interface fault remains intermittent or unexplained.

That is successful troubleshooting.
