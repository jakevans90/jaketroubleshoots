## What This Guide Helps With

Addresses cycles that will not begin, stop unexpectedly, or remain in one phase due to door, utility, load, control, or configuration conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Load and Maintain Sterilization Continuity

Do not consider an interrupted, aborted, or incomplete sterilization cycle acceptable for patient use. Quarantine the affected load according to facility policy and use another validated sterilizer when continued processing is required.

**Expected outcome:** No potentially unsterile load is released while the malfunction is investigated.

### 2. Confirm Exactly Where the Cycle Fails

Determine whether the cycle refuses to start, begins and immediately aborts, stops during heating, remains in sterilization, stalls during exhaust or drying, or produces a specific message.

Record the selected cycle and whether the problem occurs with every cycle or only a particular load or program.

**Expected outcome:** The failed phase and reproducibility of the problem are known.

### 3. Verify the Sterilizer Is Fully Ready

Confirm the T-Edge 10 has completed startup and is not showing a condition that prevents cycle initiation. Verify normal controls respond.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer is in a normal ready state before a cycle is selected.

### 4. Check Door Closure and Latching

Inspect the door opening, gasket area, and accessible sealing surfaces for debris or an obstruction. Confirm the door closes fully and the locking mechanism engages normally without forcing it.

Do not bypass a door interlock.

**Expected outcome:** The door closes, seals, and reaches its normal locked condition. If correcting an obstruction restores normal cycling, proceed to final verification.

### 5. Verify Load Placement

Check that the load is arranged appropriately and does not contact or interfere with the door, chamber opening, drain area, or other accessible components.

Compare operation with an empty chamber or approved representative test load when facility procedure permits.

**Expected outcome:** The cycle is not being prevented or disrupted by improper loading.

### 6. Verify Required Water and External Conditions

Confirm the sterilizer has the required water supply or reservoir condition for its configuration and that any accessible external connections are properly installed.

Check for low-water conditions, visible leaks, blocked accessible drainage areas, or other external conditions that could prevent cycle progression.

**Expected outcome:** Required external utilities and water conditions are available.

### 7. Review Normal Cycle Selection and Settings

Verify staff selected the intended validated cycle and did not unintentionally choose an incompatible setting for the load. Do not change protected configuration or sterilization parameters simply to make the cycle complete.

**Expected outcome:** The selected program and normal user-accessible settings are appropriate for the intended process.

### 8. Run a Controlled Test Cycle

With the chamber appropriately prepared and using approved test conditions, initiate a cycle and observe whether it progresses through normal phases.

Do not repeatedly restart a unit that consistently stalls at the same point.

**Expected outcome:** The cycle proceeds without unexpected pause or abort. If it completes normally and required process verification passes, troubleshooting can stop.

### 9. Perform Final Functional and Process Verification

Verify completion of the cycle, expected end-of-cycle status, door operation, and required physical, chemical, or biological process checks according to facility and manufacturer requirements.

**Expected outcome:** The sterilizer completes the validated process successfully and may be returned to service.

### 10. Escalate Repeated or Unexplained Cycle Failures

If the unit repeatedly aborts or stalls after door, loading, water, utility, and normal setting checks, remove it from service.

**Expected outcome:** A persistent cycle-control or process problem is escalated rather than bypassed.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving door closure, loading, utilities, water availability, and normal cycle selection have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve temperature or pressure sensing, heating, valves, pumps, control logic, internal plumbing, or another service-level condition.

Remove the sterilizer from service, label it **Out of Service**, and arrange bench or qualified service evaluation using appropriate Tuttnauer documentation and approved test equipment. Do not bypass interlocks or alter validated process parameters.

Return the sterilizer to service only after corrective work and all required functional and sterilization-process verification are complete. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Any load from an aborted or incomplete cycle must remain segregated until it is reprocessed according to facility sterilization policy.

## Final Thought

Protect the load first, identify the exact phase of failure, and work from door, loading, water, and settings toward service-level causes. Never bypass a safety interlock or accept an incomplete sterilization process simply because the unit appears otherwise functional.

That is successful troubleshooting.
