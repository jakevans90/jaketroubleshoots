## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot collimator light failure, incorrect light field, blade motion problems, control issues, obstruction, and apparent field misalignment.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient From Incorrect Field Selection
Do not use the system clinically if the collimated X-ray field cannot be reliably set or verified. Use another verified imaging system until the issue is resolved.  
Expected outcome: Patient exposure is not performed with an unreliable field indication.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Collimator Problem
Determine whether the lamp does not illuminate, one or more blades do not move, the field size indication appears incorrect, or the light field appears asymmetrical.  
Expected outcome: The specific collimator symptom is documented.

### 3. Verify Normal System Power and Collimator Controls
Confirm the DRX-Rise is operating normally and test the standard collimator lamp and blade controls.  
Expected outcome: Controls respond normally and the requested field changes are visible.

### 4. Inspect the Collimator Opening Externally
Look for attached accessories, protective covers, foreign material, tape, or physical damage obstructing the light field or apparent blade position. Do not reach into or disassemble the collimator.  
Expected outcome: Nothing external blocks the lamp or blade opening.

### 5. Check Tube-Head Position and Surface Geometry
Position the tube head and receptor in a normal test geometry. Ensure angled surfaces, raised rails, detector holders, or other structures are not making the light field appear distorted.  
Expected outcome: Apparent field shape is evaluated under controlled positioning conditions.

### 6. Observe Blade Motion
Operate field-size controls through a reasonable range while watching for smooth, symmetrical blade movement. Stop if blades bind, chatter, or fail to respond.  
Expected outcome: Blades move consistently and hold position.

### 7. Check Lamp Operation
Activate the collimator light through the normal control and confirm the field is sufficiently visible for positioning. Do not substitute an unapproved lamp or bypass controls.  
Expected outcome: The collimator light activates normally. If a temporary external obstruction or control issue was corrected and operation is stable, proceed to final verification.

### 8. Verify Light-Field and Radiation-Field Alignment When Required
If field alignment is questioned, perform an approved collimation/alignment test using appropriate radiation test equipment and manufacturer or facility procedures.  
Expected outcome: Alignment meets applicable approved acceptance criteria. Do not invent or substitute tolerances.

### 9. Perform Final Functional Verification
Confirm lamp operation, field-size adjustment, blade symmetry, indicated field behavior, and any required alignment test.  
Expected outcome: Collimation is reliable and verified. Troubleshooting can stop.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Collimator Problems
If the lamp, blade mechanism, field indication, or alignment remains unreliable, remove the system from service.  
Expected outcome: The system is prevented from clinical use until collimator performance is restored and verified.

## If the Problem Persists

External obstruction, positioning, controls, and basic operation have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include the lamp assembly, blade mechanism, position sensing, control hardware, calibration, wiring, or internal collimator components.

Remove the DRX-Rise from service, label it **Out of Service**, and arrange bench evaluation or authorized repair using appropriate Carestream documentation and approved radiation test equipment.

After correction, complete required collimator functional and alignment verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop before internal collimator disassembly is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not rely on a visibly incorrect light field to estimate the actual X-ray field.
