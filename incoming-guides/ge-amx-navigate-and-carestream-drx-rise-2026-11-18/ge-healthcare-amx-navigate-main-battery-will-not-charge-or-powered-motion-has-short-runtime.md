## What This Guide Helps With

The main battery does not appear to charge, discharges quickly, or provides insufficient runtime for powered transport and imaging workflow.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect Clinical Operations
Do not allow the unit to become stranded during patient transport or imaging. If battery operation is unreliable, move clinical work to another verified mobile X-ray system.

Expected outcome: Patient care does not depend on an unreliable battery.

### 2. Confirm the Exact Battery Complaint
Determine whether:
- The battery does not charge at all
- Charging starts but stops
- Charge indication appears inaccurate
- Runtime is short
- Powered drive fails before imaging functions
- The unit shuts down unexpectedly
- The issue occurs only when unplugged

Expected outcome: The battery-related symptom is clearly defined.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source
Inspect the power cord and plug for damage. Connect the unit to a known-good approved receptacle. Verify the receptacle according to facility practice if AC power is questionable.

Expected outcome: Reliable AC power is available to the system.

### 4. Inspect Power-Cord and Inlet Condition
Inspect accessible power connections for:
- Loose fit
- Burn marks
- Heat damage
- Bent contacts
- Cracked insulation
- Evidence of arcing
- Strain-relief damage

Remove the unit from service if unsafe electrical damage is present.

Expected outcome: The AC power path is physically intact.

### 5. Verify Charging Indication
With the unit connected to known-good AC power, observe normal charging indicators or battery status.

Expected outcome: The system recognizes AC input and indicates charging as expected.

### 6. Allow Normal Charging Opportunity
Confirm the unit has actually been connected to AC power long enough under normal operating conditions to establish whether the battery accepts a charge. Avoid judging battery condition immediately after a heavily discharged unit is plugged in.

Expected outcome: Battery state increases normally or the inability to charge is confirmed.

### 7. Check for Abnormal Heat, Odor, or Battery-Related Damage
Inspect accessible areas for unusual heat, swelling, odor, discoloration, or other signs of battery or charging-system distress.

Expected outcome: No unsafe physical condition is present. If abnormal heat, odor, or damage exists, disconnect the unit when safe and remove it from service.

### 8. Compare Stationary and Powered-Drive Runtime
When safe, observe whether battery depletion is unusually rapid during powered transport compared with stationary operation. Excessive mechanical resistance may increase drive demand.

Expected outcome: The test helps distinguish general battery weakness from excessive transport load.

### 9. Check Wheels and Casters for Excessive Resistance
Inspect for debris, binding, damaged casters, or dragging components that could force the drive system to work harder.

Expected outcome: The unit rolls freely without obvious external resistance.

### 10. Perform a Controlled Battery Functional Check
After charging, operate the system under an approved non-patient workflow and observe whether battery status remains stable during normal transport and system operation.

Expected outcome: Battery operation is stable and sufficient for normal workflow. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

### 11. Escalate Persistent Charging or Runtime Failure
If a known-good AC source, power cord, external mechanical condition, and normal charging opportunity do not correct the problem, remove the unit from service for battery and charging-system evaluation.

Expected outcome: A system with unreliable battery capacity is not returned to mobile clinical use.

## If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities may involve battery capacity, internal charging electronics, battery monitoring, high internal current draw, drive-system load, wiring, or service-level power management.

The device should be:
- Removed from service
- Labeled Out of Service
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation
- Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved test equipment
- Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify AC charging, battery indication, startup, controlled powered movement, and appropriate battery-supported operation before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Return the mobile X-ray unit to approved charging power when available so unexpected battery depletion does not interrupt bedside imaging.

## Final Thought

Verify AC supply, physical connections, charging indications, and unnecessary mechanical load before condemning the battery. Battery-supported transport must be predictable before a mobile X-ray system is returned to clinical service.
