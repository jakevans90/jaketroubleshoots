## What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot charging failure, reduced powered-drive runtime, AC supply problems, battery condition, charging connections, and abnormal battery behavior.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Transport Workflow
Do not depend on powered transport or battery operation if runtime is unpredictable. Move the unit to a safe location and use another verified system if necessary.  
Expected outcome: The system can be evaluated without risking loss of power during patient care or transport.

### 2. Confirm the Battery Complaint
Determine whether the battery does not charge, indicates charging but gains little capacity, discharges rapidly, or only provides short powered-drive runtime.  
Expected outcome: The specific charging or runtime symptom is documented.

### 3. Verify the AC Power Source
Inspect the power cord and plug for damage and confirm they are fully connected. Test the outlet using an appropriate method or known-good equipment according to facility practice.  
Expected outcome: A verified AC source is available. If restoring AC power begins normal charging, continue through verification and stop if stable.

### 4. Check Charging Indications
Observe normal system charging and battery indicators when connected to AC power. Record unexpected indications without assuming battery failure.  
Expected outcome: The system recognizes AC power and indicates the expected charging state.

### 5. Inspect Accessible Charging Connections
Check the power cord, strain relief, accessible charging contacts, docking interface if used, and surrounding area for looseness, debris, heat damage, or physical deterioration.  
Expected outcome: External charging connections are intact and secure.

### 6. Allow a Normal Charge Period and Reassess
If the system was deeply discharged, charge it according to normal facility and manufacturer-approved operation, then reassess battery indication and drive operation.  
Expected outcome: Battery state improves and runtime becomes stable. If normal performance returns and remains consistent, troubleshooting can stop.

### 7. Evaluate Powered-Drive Conditions
Test drive operation without a patient. Determine whether short runtime occurs during all operation or only during heavy transport conditions such as slopes, frequent starts, or unusually high resistance.  
Expected outcome: Battery and drive behavior are characterized under safe test conditions.

### 8. Check for Abnormal Battery Signs
Look for unusual heat, odor, swelling, leakage, damaged battery enclosures, or repeated shutdowns. Do not continue charging a battery or system showing abnormal physical signs.  
Expected outcome: No evidence of unsafe battery condition is present.

### 9. Perform an Approved Battery/Runtime Evaluation
When appropriate, use manufacturer-approved diagnostic or battery test methods available to Clinical Engineering without unauthorized disassembly.  
Expected outcome: Battery performance is acceptable or degradation is confirmed.

### 10. Escalate Persistent Charging or Runtime Problems
If the battery will not charge normally or powered movement remains unreliable, remove the system from clinical service until repair or battery evaluation is completed.  
Expected outcome: The mobile unit is not returned to use with unpredictable power availability.

## If the Problem Persists

External power, charging connections, and normal operating conditions have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include degraded battery capacity, charger circuitry, power-management hardware, drive-system loading, software, or internal electrical faults.

Remove the DRX-Rise from service, label it **Out of Service**, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Carestream documentation and approved electrical and battery test equipment. Battery replacement or internal repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify charging, battery indication, powered drive, and sufficient stable battery operation using approved procedures.

Knowing when to stop after external power checks is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

Do not begin patient transport with a mobile X-ray system that has demonstrated unpredictable battery or drive runtime.
