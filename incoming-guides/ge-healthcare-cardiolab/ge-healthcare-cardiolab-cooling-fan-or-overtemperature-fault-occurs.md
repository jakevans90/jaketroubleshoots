## What This Guide Helps With

CardioLab reports overheating, excessive fan operation, or cooling problems because of blocked airflow, environment, dust, placement, or internal hardware conditions.

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

### 1. Protect the Patient and Remove an Unstable System From Dependence

If CardioLab displays an overtemperature warning, shuts down unexpectedly, emits unusual heat or odor, or has unreliable cooling, provide another verified monitoring or recording method before troubleshooting.

Do not continue operating equipment that appears to be overheating.

**Expected outcome:** The patient is no longer dependent on equipment with uncertain thermal stability.

### 2. Confirm the Reported Thermal Condition

Determine whether staff observed a warning, fan noise, unexpected shutdown, excessive case temperature, intermittent restarting, or another thermal symptom.

Record the exact message if one is displayed.

**Expected outcome:** The reported condition and affected CardioLab component are clearly identified.

### 3. Inspect External Airflow

Check externally accessible vents and fan openings on the workstation, displays, acquisition hardware, and equipment rack.

Ensure vents are not blocked by papers, drapes, carts, walls, cables, dust accumulation, or other equipment.

Do not insert tools into energized ventilation openings.

**Expected outcome:** Cooling air can enter and exit required external vents freely.

If clearing an external obstruction resolves the condition and the equipment remains stable, continue to final verification.

### 4. Check Equipment Placement

Confirm CardioLab components have not been pushed into a confined location or placed against another heat-producing device.

Verify rack doors, panels, or external airflow paths are positioned as intended for normal operation.

**Expected outcome:** Equipment placement allows normal ventilation.

If repositioning the equipment restores stable temperature and fan behavior, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

### 5. Check Room Environment

Determine whether room temperature or ventilation has changed, HVAC is unavailable, or nearby heat-producing equipment is affecting the CardioLab installation.

Escalate facility environmental problems through the appropriate hospital pathway.

**Expected outcome:** The room environment is suitable for normal equipment operation.

If facility conditions caused the problem, verify CardioLab remains stable after the environment is corrected.

### 6. Inspect for Dust and External Contamination

With equipment safely powered down when required, inspect accessible external vents, filters, or fan openings for dust or contamination.

Clean only externally serviceable areas using approved methods. Do not disassemble equipment solely to reach internal fans.

**Expected outcome:** External cooling paths are clean enough for normal airflow.

If approved external cleaning resolves abnormal fan behavior or thermal warnings, verify under normal operation.

### 7. Observe Fan Operation

After the system is safely restarted, listen for abnormal fan noise, grinding, intermittent fan operation, or complete lack of expected airflow.

Do not defeat fan monitoring or thermal protection.

**Expected outcome:** Accessible cooling fans operate without obvious abnormal noise or interruption.

If abnormal fan behavior persists, remove the affected component from service.

### 8. Perform Functional and Stability Verification

Allow the system to operate through an approved functional check while observing for recurrence of warnings, shutdown, fan abnormalities, or communication loss.

Verify CardioLab acquisition and workstation functions remain stable.

**Expected outcome:** The system operates normally without thermal warnings or abnormal fan behavior.

If the condition does not recur and all required checks pass, troubleshooting is complete.

### 9. Escalate Persistent Thermal Problems

If overheating, fan failure, unusual odor, unexpected shutdown, or repeated thermal warnings persist after airflow and environmental causes are ruled out, stop troubleshooting.

Do not perform internal fan, power-supply, heatsink, or board-level repair unless authorized and trained.

**Expected outcome:** Equipment with unresolved thermal instability is removed from service and escalated.

## If the Problem Persists

External airflow, equipment placement, room environment, and externally serviceable contamination have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities may involve internal fans, power supplies, temperature sensing, computer hardware, acquisition electronics, or another internal service-level condition.

The CardioLab system should be:

- Removed from service.
- Labeled Out of Service.
- Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
- Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved test equipment.
- Repaired only by qualified personnel.

Verify thermal stability and all applicable CardioLab functions before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

## Clinical Use Tip

An overtemperature warning or unexplained thermal shutdown should be treated as a reliability problem even if CardioLab restarts normally afterward.

## Final Thought

Treat heat and cooling problems as potential reliability hazards. Rule out blocked airflow, poor placement, environmental conditions, and external contamination first, then remove the system from service if thermal instability remains.
