Caster/Steer Alignment Causes the Bed to Track Sideways

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

ProCuity

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot a ProCuity bed that pulls, drifts, or tracks sideways because of caster condition, steering engagement, loading, or floor-related factors.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Control Bed Movement

Do not troubleshoot unpredictable transport or steering behavior while transporting a patient.

Move the patient to another verified bed if the tracking problem creates a collision, control, or transport hazard. Test the affected bed in a clear area with adequate space.

Expected outcome: The bed can be tested without risk to the patient, staff, walls, or nearby equipment. If the bed cannot be controlled safely, remove it from service.

2. Confirm the Tracking Complaint

Push the unloaded bed slowly on a reasonably level, clear surface and determine whether it:

  • Pulls consistently to one side.
  • Wanders only when steer mode is engaged.
  • Requires excessive force.
  • Has one caster that does not swivel normally.
  • Behaves differently in neutral and steer modes.
  • Tracks normally in one area but poorly in another.

Avoid high-speed testing.

Expected outcome: The complaint is reproducible and its relationship to caster or steer mode is understood. If tracking is normal, compare with the original location and conditions before closing the work order.

3. Inspect All Casters Externally

Inspect each caster for:

  • Wrapped hair, string, tape, or debris.
  • Flat spots or damaged tread.
  • Visible bending or impact damage.
  • Loose or missing accessible hardware.
  • Foreign material around the swivel area.
  • Uneven caster contact with the floor.

Remove only accessible debris without disassembling the caster.

Expected outcome: All casters rotate and swivel freely without visible damage or obstruction. If debris removal restores normal tracking, proceed to final verification.

4. Verify Brake and Neutral Operation

Operate the bed’s brake and neutral controls through their normal positions. Confirm that no wheel remains partially braked when the system is released.

Push the bed slowly after releasing the brake.

Expected outcome: All casters roll freely when released. A caster that remains partially braked or drags requires service evaluation if no external obstruction is present.

5. Verify Steer Engagement

Engage and disengage the bed’s normal steering function while observing the steering caster or directional behavior.

Confirm that the steering function fully engages rather than remaining between modes. Compare bed behavior with steer mode engaged and disengaged.

Do not make unauthorized caster alignment adjustments.

Expected outcome: Steering engagement produces a predictable change in tracking and the bed moves in the intended direction. If steer mode does not engage consistently, further service evaluation is required.

6. Check for Uneven Loading or Attached Equipment

Remove or reposition nonessential equipment that may be creating an uneven load or dragging against the floor.

Inspect oxygen-cylinder holders, pumps, cables, power cords, accessories, or other attachments for floor contact or interference with caster rotation.

Expected outcome: Nothing external drags on the floor or restricts caster movement. If correcting an accessory or load condition restores straight tracking, proceed to final verification.

7. Compare Operation on Another Floor Surface

Test the bed on another suitable level surface if the complaint occurs only in one hallway or room.

Floor transitions, damaged flooring, thresholds, debris, or slope may cause apparent steering problems.

Expected outcome: The bed tracks consistently on a suitable surface. If the problem is isolated to the environment rather than the bed, document the finding and route the environmental concern appropriately.

8. Compare Individual Caster Behavior

With the bed safely positioned and using only approved external inspection methods, compare caster swivel and rolling behavior.

Look for a caster that consistently trails incorrectly, resists rotation, or fails to align with movement.

Do not disassemble caster assemblies or attempt internal repairs.

Expected outcome: Caster behavior is consistent across the bed. A caster with persistent mechanical resistance or alignment problems requires qualified repair.

9. Perform Final Transport Verification

After correction, push the unloaded bed forward, backward, and through gentle turns with steer mode both engaged and disengaged as appropriate.

Verify:

  • Predictable tracking.
  • Normal steering effort.
  • Proper brake release.
  • No caster drag.
  • No abnormal noise or instability.

Complete any required return-to-service safety inspection.

Expected outcome: The bed tracks predictably and can be maneuvered safely. Troubleshooting can stop and the bed may return to service.

If the Problem Persists

If the bed continues to track sideways after caster debris, brake release, steering engagement, external loading, accessories, and floor conditions are ruled out, the problem may involve caster wear, steering linkage, mechanical alignment, or another service-level component.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service if transport control is unreliable.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel.

Do not compensate for a persistent steering fault by instructing staff to push harder or manually force the bed straight.

Following repair, verify caster rolling, braking, steering engagement, straight tracking, turning, and overall transport safety before return to service.

Clinical Use Tip

A bed that pulls sideways may become difficult to control in elevators, doorways, ramps, or congested corridors even if it can still be moved on an open floor.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Transport staff reported that the ProCuity bed consistently pulled to the right when steer mode was engaged."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found tape and debris wrapped around the right-side caster, restricting free swivel movement."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the debris, inspected all casters, verified brake and steer operation, completed a transport test, and returned the bed to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Direction of pulling or drifting.
  • Whether the problem occurs in steer or neutral mode.
  • Caster condition.
  • Debris found.
  • Brake-release behavior.
  • Attached accessories or equipment.
  • Floor or room where the complaint occurred.
  • Comparison on another surface.
  • Transport test results.
  • Final bed status.

Final Thought

Poor tracking should be evaluated as a transport-safety problem, not merely an inconvenience. Check caster condition, brake release, steering engagement, loading, and the environment before assuming an internal mechanical fault. If predictable control cannot be restored, remove the bed from service and document the escalation clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
