Output Is Low, Unstable, or Inaccurate

Canon

Model

Aplio i900 CV

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting weak, unstable, or inaccurate imaging-related output caused by probe condition, connections, settings, positioning, patient interface, or system-level problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Do Not Rely on Questionable Imaging Performance

If the displayed imaging information appears unreliable or inconsistent with the clinical situation, do not continue making clinical decisions from the affected system. Move the examination to another verified echocardiography system or probe as appropriate. Expected outcome: Patient assessment does not depend on questionable system performance.

2. Confirm What Is Low, Unstable, or Inaccurate

Determine whether the complaint involves image brightness, signal strength, Doppler-related information, measurement behavior, intermittent image loss, or another observable imaging output. Identify the probe and examination conditions involved. Expected outcome: The complaint is defined well enough to reproduce and isolate safely.

3. Inspect the Probe and Cable Externally

Examine the probe housing, acoustic surface, cable, strain relief, connector, and accessible contacts for cracks, cuts, contamination, liquid intrusion, bent areas, or other damage. Remove a visibly damaged probe from service. Expected outcome: The probe is externally intact and suitable for further testing.

4. Verify the Probe Connection

Confirm the probe is fully seated and recognized by the system. Reseat it using the normal connection method if appropriate. Check whether the symptom changes when the cable is positioned normally without pulling or flexing it excessively. Expected outcome: The connection is secure and the output remains stable during normal cable positioning.

5. Check Patient Interface and Probe Positioning

Confirm appropriate acoustic coupling, correct probe positioning, and adequate approved ultrasound gel. Rule out poor contact or technique-related effects before assuming equipment failure. Expected outcome: The image improves when proper coupling and positioning are established, or the equipment-related symptom remains reproducible.

6. Review User-Accessible Imaging Settings

Verify that relevant imaging mode and adjustable settings have not been unintentionally changed. Compare with a known normal setup or facility-approved baseline when available. Do not alter protected calibration or service parameters. Expected outcome: The system is operating with appropriate user-accessible settings for the test being performed.

7. Compare With a Known-Good Compatible Probe

When available, use a known-good compatible probe under comparable test conditions. If the symptom follows the original probe, remove that probe from service. If the issue occurs with multiple known-good probes, the system becomes more suspect. Expected outcome: The problem is isolated to the probe or remains associated with the system.

8. Check the Operating Environment

Look for nearby equipment, damaged power accessories, unusual electrical interference, or room conditions associated with the complaint. If the issue occurs only in one room or location, compare operation in another suitable location when practical. Expected outcome: Environmental or infrastructure influence is either identified or reasonably ruled out.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correcting an external cause, verify stable image acquisition and the affected imaging function using an appropriate approved test method. If measurement accuracy is part of the complaint, use appropriate approved test equipment or procedures rather than visual judgment alone. Expected outcome: Imaging output is stable and appropriate functional verification passes. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Unresolved Performance Problems

If poor or inaccurate output persists with a known-good probe, correct settings, proper test conditions, and no obvious environmental cause, remove the system from service. Do not attempt internal imaging-board, transmitter, receiver, or signal-path repair. Expected outcome: A suspected internal imaging, calibration, software, or signal-processing problem is referred for qualified service.

If the Problem Persists

Common probe, connector, positioning, coupling, setting, and environmental causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve internal imaging electronics, calibration, signal processing, software, an internal connection path, or another service-level condition.

Remove the Aplio i900 CV from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for qualified repair or bench evaluation using Canon documentation and approved test equipment. Internal adjustments or repairs should be completed only by qualified personnel.

Complete appropriate imaging-performance and functional verification before return to service. Knowing when displayed clinical information cannot be trusted and removing the system from use is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

When image quality or measurement behavior appears questionable, confirm findings on another verified system rather than allowing troubleshooting to delay clinical evaluation.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported weak and intermittently degraded cardiac images when using one probe on the Aplio i900 CV."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found visible damage to the affected probe cable, while a known-good compatible probe produced stable images."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering removed the damaged probe from service, verified stable imaging with the known-good probe, and returned the system to service with the affected accessory excluded."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Specific imaging symptom
  • Probe involved
  • Probe and cable condition
  • Connector condition
  • Imaging mode and relevant settings observed
  • Coupling and positioning conditions
  • Known-good probe comparison
  • Whether the issue followed the probe or system
  • Room or environmental comparison
  • Final imaging verification
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient from unreliable imaging information, rule out probe, connection, positioning, settings, and environment before assuming internal failure, verify performance after correction, escalate persistent inaccuracies, and document exactly what was tested.

That is successful troubleshooting.
