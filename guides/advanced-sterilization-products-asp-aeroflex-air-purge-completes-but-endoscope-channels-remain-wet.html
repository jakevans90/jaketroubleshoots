Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP) AEROFLEX

Air Purge Completes but Endoscope Channels Remain Wet

Asset Type

Endoscope Reprocessor (AER)

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

AEROFLEX

What This Guide Helps With

Residual channel moisture after air purge caused by connection problems, tubing restrictions, loading, blocked channels, inadequate air supply, or reprocessor-side airflow issues.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Endoscope Handling and Storage

Do not place an inadequately dried scope into normal storage when required drying conditions have not been achieved. Follow facility policy for additional validated drying or reprocessing.

Expected outcome: A wet endoscope is not stored or released contrary to facility infection-prevention procedures.

2. Confirm the Scope and Channels Affected

Determine whether moisture remains in one channel, several channels, or every processed scope. Confirm whether the purge sequence actually completed without alarms.

Expected outcome: The problem is distinguished as scope-specific, channel-specific, or AER-wide.

3. Inspect Channel Connections

Verify all required channel tubing and adapters were connected correctly and remained secure through the cycle.

Expected outcome: Each channel has a complete, unobstructed purge pathway. Correct any loose or omitted connection and repeat the applicable verification.

4. Check Tubing for Kinks or Restrictions

Inspect external connection tubing for pinching, sharp bends, collapsed sections, fluid accumulation, or obstruction.

Expected outcome: Air can pass through the external tubing without restriction. If correcting the tubing routing restores drying, troubleshooting can stop.

5. Check Endoscope Positioning

Verify the endoscope was loaded so that tubing and channels were not compressed or positioned in a manner that could trap fluid.

Expected outcome: The scope is loaded without dependent loops or pinched tubing that could interfere with drainage or air purge.

6. Compare With a Known-Good Scope

If appropriate, process or test with another compatible known-good endoscope and approved connection set.

Expected outcome: If the wet-channel condition follows one scope, remove that scope from service for evaluation. If several scopes are affected, continue checking the AER.

7. Verify External Air Supply When Applicable

If the installation uses an external air source accessible to Clinical Engineering, confirm the source is connected, available, and not obviously restricted. Do not adjust regulated service parameters without approved procedures.

Expected outcome: The expected external air supply is present. If restoring a disconnected supply corrects the purge, troubleshooting can stop.

8. Observe Purge Performance During an Approved Test

Run an appropriate test cycle and determine whether purge sounds, flow behavior, and completion indications are consistent with normal operation.

Expected outcome: Air purge completes and channels are acceptably cleared according to the approved workflow.

9. Escalate Persistent Wet-Channel Conditions

If correct connections, known-good tubing, proper loading, and available air supply do not resolve the issue, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The AEROFLEX is removed from service for qualified airflow and pneumatic evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving channel connections, tubing, scope loading, scope-specific obstruction, and available air supply have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal pneumatic valves, airflow pathways, regulators, sensors, or control functions.

Remove the AEROFLEX from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment.

Return the device to service only after normal purge performance is demonstrated and processed scopes meet the facility's required drying condition.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not confuse a completed purge timer with successful drying; inspect the processed scope according to the facility's validated workflow.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported scopes remained visibly wet in several channels even though the AEROFLEX air-purge stage completed."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a channel-connection tube sharply kinked where it entered the basin."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Rerouted the tubing, repeated the approved processing test, and verified normal purge with no residual channel moisture."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Scope model
  • Channels affected
  • Whether purge completed normally
  • Channel tubing condition
  • Adapter condition
  • Scope loading position
  • Known-good scope comparison
  • External air availability
  • Moisture result after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Residual moisture should be approached as a complete airflow-path problem. Verify scope connections, tubing, positioning, and external air before escalating internal pneumatic concerns.

That is successful troubleshooting.
