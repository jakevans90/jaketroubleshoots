Printer Feeds but the Cycle Record Is Blank or Incomplete

Asset Type

Sterilizer

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

STERRAD NX

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot blank, faint, partial, or incomplete cycle printouts caused by paper installation, paper type, obstruction, printer condition, or data-generation problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Cycle Documentation

Do not discard electronic or other available cycle documentation while troubleshooting the printer.

If printed records are required by facility policy, establish an approved alternate documentation method before continued routine processing.

Expected outcome: Sterilization traceability is maintained despite the printer problem.

2. Confirm the Exact Printing Failure

Determine whether the printer:

  • Feeds blank paper
  • Prints only part of the record
  • Produces faint output
  • Stops before the record is complete
  • Fails only for certain cycles
  • Produces no printed information despite paper movement

Expected outcome: Paper-feed and print-generation problems are distinguished.

3. Inspect Paper Installation

Verify that the approved paper is:

  • Present
  • Loaded correctly
  • Oriented correctly
  • Not wrinkled or damaged
  • Feeding through the intended path

Do not force paper into the mechanism.

Expected outcome: Paper is correctly loaded and moves normally.

4. Verify Paper Type

Confirm the installed paper is the correct approved type for the printer.

A printer may feed normally but produce blank output if inappropriate media is installed.

Expected outcome: Correct printer media is installed. If replacing incorrect paper restores printing, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

5. Inspect the Accessible Paper Path

Check for:

  • Torn paper
  • Adhesive residue
  • Debris
  • Misaligned roll
  • Obvious obstruction

Do not disassemble the printer mechanism beyond authorized user-accessible areas.

Expected outcome: The accessible paper path is clear.

6. Compare Printed and Electronic Cycle Information

Determine whether the complete cycle record exists on the sterilizer display or electronic record system.

If complete data exists electronically but the printed copy is incomplete, the problem is localized toward printing rather than cycle-record generation.

Expected outcome: Record-generation and physical-printing functions are isolated.

7. Perform an Approved Test Print or Cycle Record Print

Use available normal printer functions to generate a record where permitted.

Observe paper movement and print quality.

Expected outcome: The printer produces a complete readable record.

8. Check for Intermittent Behavior

Print more than one approved record if necessary to determine whether the fault is repeatable.

Do not declare the problem corrected based on a single partial improvement.

Expected outcome: Printing remains consistent across repeated use.

9. Verify Documentation Workflow

Confirm that:

  • Complete cycle information is present
  • Print is readable
  • Required identifiers are included
  • Records can be retained according to facility policy

Expected outcome: Normal cycle documentation is restored and troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Persistent Printer Problems

If correct paper is installed and the paper path is clear but records remain blank or incomplete, remove the printer function or sterilizer from use as required by facility documentation policy and escalate for service.

Expected outcome: Qualified service evaluates the printer or record-generation system.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as incorrect media, improper loading, and accessible paper obstruction have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include the print mechanism, print electronics, internal connection, record-generation software, or control system.

Depending on facility documentation requirements, the sterilizer should be:

  • Removed from service when required
  • Labeled Out of Service when appropriate
  • Sent for repair or service evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Verify complete cycle documentation before unrestricted return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A successful sterilization cycle and a complete traceability record are separate requirements; protect both during printer failures.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported that the STERRAD NX printer advanced paper but every cycle record came out blank."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an incorrect paper roll installed while electronic cycle records remained complete."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the paper with the approved type, verified multiple readable cycle-record prints, and returned the printer function to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Blank, faint, or partial print
  • Paper type
  • Paper orientation
  • Paper-path condition
  • Electronic record availability
  • Test-print result
  • Whether multiple records were tested
  • Required documentation status
  • Final printer status
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Printer faults should be isolated from sterilization and record-generation functions. Check media and the accessible paper path first, then escalate persistent printing failures without compromising traceability.

That is successful troubleshooting.
