Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP) AEROFLEX

Basin, Channel Connector, or Drain Leaks During Processing

Asset Type

Endoscope Reprocessor (AER)

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

AEROFLEX

What This Guide Helps With

Visible liquid leaks caused by loose channel connections, tubing damage, loading, drain hoses, seals, basin overflow, or internal fluid-path problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Personnel, Equipment, and Processing Safety

Stop the cycle when a significant or unexplained liquid leak is observed. Keep staff away from pooled chemical or process fluid and follow facility spill procedures as appropriate. Treat the scope as incompletely processed.

Expected outcome: Exposure risk and further equipment damage are minimized.

2. Identify the Source and Fluid Location

Determine whether liquid is leaking inside the basin, from a channel connector, around the lid, underneath the AER, or from the external drain or supply connection.

Expected outcome: The leak location is narrowed before connections are disturbed.

3. Inspect Channel Connections

With the unit safely stopped, verify accessible channel tubing and adapters are fully seated and inspect them for cracks, splits, damaged fittings, or disconnected hoses.

Expected outcome: All external channel connections are intact and secure. Replace an approved damaged accessory when appropriate.

4. Inspect Scope and Tubing Loading

Check whether tubing was pinched, stretched, twisted, or trapped by the lid or other equipment during the cycle.

Expected outcome: Connections are free from mechanical strain. If correcting tubing routing resolves the leak, proceed to controlled verification.

5. Inspect Basin and Accessible Sealing Surfaces

Look for obvious debris, damaged accessible seals, foreign material, or displaced components around the basin and lid interface. Do not disassemble the fluid system.

Expected outcome: Accessible sealing areas are clean and intact. A visibly damaged seal or basin component requires removal from service.

6. Inspect External Supply and Drain Hoses

Check water and drain hoses for loose fittings, cracks, compression, deterioration, or evidence of leakage around external connections.

Expected outcome: External plumbing is secure and dry. If tightening or replacing an approved external connection resolves the leak, proceed to verification.

7. Determine Whether the Leak Is Load-Specific

If safe and permitted, compare the condition with an empty-basin operational check or a known-good connection setup.

Expected outcome: A leak caused by one scope connection set is separated from a leak inherent to the AEROFLEX.

8. Perform a Controlled Leak Check After Correction

Run an approved operational test while observing the previously affected area. Do not place the unit back into clinical workflow solely because the visible liquid was wiped up.

Expected outcome: No leakage is observed through fill, circulation, rinse, and drain portions relevant to the original complaint.

9. Remove From Service for Internal or Unexplained Leakage

If liquid appears from beneath the enclosure, inside inaccessible areas, from an internal fluid path, or continues despite correct external connections, stop troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The AEROFLEX is isolated before additional leakage can damage equipment or compromise processing.

If the Problem Persists

External causes involving scope connections, channel tubing, loading, external water connections, drain hoses, and accessible seals have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal plumbing, valves, pumps, basin seals, fittings, or drain components.

Remove the device from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment. Internal fluid-path repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify the repaired unit remains leak-free throughout required operational testing and completes normal processing functions.

Clinical Use Tip

Any scope involved in an interrupted or leaking cycle should remain quarantined until it has completed the facility's validated reprocessing workflow.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported liquid leaking from the front of the AEROFLEX basin during processing."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an external channel hose connector incompletely seated and leaking during circulation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reseated the approved connector, repeated an operational cycle test, and verified the basin and connection remained dry throughout processing."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact leak location
  • Cycle phase when leak appeared
  • Type of fluid if known
  • Channel connector condition
  • Tubing condition and routing
  • Basin and seal condition
  • External drain and supply connections
  • Empty versus loaded test result
  • Leak status after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Fluid leaks should be traced from visible external connections inward without unnecessary disassembly. Protect personnel and scopes first, verify simple connection and loading causes, and remove the AER from service for unexplained internal leakage.

That is successful troubleshooting.
