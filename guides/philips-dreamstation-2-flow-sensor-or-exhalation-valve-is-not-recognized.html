Philips DreamStation 2

Flow Sensor or Exhalation Valve Is Not Recognized

Asset Type

Sleep Therapy System

Manufacturer

Philips

Model

DreamStation 2

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses reported flow-sensor or exhalation-valve problems while distinguishing DreamStation 2 PAP circuit components from ventilator-style removable sensors and valves.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Therapy

Do not troubleshoot uncertain airflow or exhalation performance while a patient depends on the DreamStation 2. Transfer therapy to another verified system before technical evaluation.

Expected outcome: The patient continues receiving prescribed therapy safely.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Message or Symptom

Record the exact display message or staff description. Determine whether the complaint is actually poor airflow, excessive leak, blocked exhalation, accessory recognition, or an assumed flow-sensor/exhalation-valve problem.

The DreamStation 2 does not use a typical critical-care ventilator patient circuit with a removable electronic expiratory valve or external flow sensor that must be recognized by the ventilator.

Expected outcome: The actual DreamStation 2 symptom is identified without applying an incorrect ventilator troubleshooting model.

3. Inspect the Patient Interface and Exhalation Path

Inspect the mask or interface for its intended exhalation vent and verify the vent is open, clean, and not obstructed by bedding, tape, accessories, or incorrect assembly.

Never block the intentional exhaust path.

Expected outcome: The patient interface provides an unobstructed intentional exhalation path.

4. Inspect Tubing and Connections

Check the breathing tube, mask elbow, connectors, humidifier interface, and other external air-path components for damage, disconnection, restriction, or incorrect assembly.

Expected outcome: The PAP circuit is correctly assembled with no unintended restriction or leak.

5. Verify the Correct Accessories

Confirm that the installed tubing, patient interface, and related accessories are appropriate for the DreamStation 2 and that unapproved adapters or ventilator components have not been inserted into the system.

Expected outcome: Only compatible PAP therapy components are connected.

6. Substitute Known-Good Circuit Components

Where appropriate, substitute known-good compatible tubing and interface components one at a time.

Expected outcome: Normal airflow returns if an external circuit component was responsible. Replace the defective component and stop troubleshooting after verification.

7. Evaluate Flow and Pressure Performance

If a genuine airflow problem remains, use appropriate respiratory test equipment to evaluate the DreamStation 2's therapy output according to applicable Philips service information.

Do not access internal sensors or attempt internal calibration without authorized procedures.

Expected outcome: Device output is stable and appropriate during controlled testing.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Reassemble the complete approved PAP circuit and confirm stable airflow, unobstructed intentional exhalation, secure connections, and normal device operation.

Expected outcome: The therapy system functions normally. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

9. Escalate Persistent Flow-Sensing Symptoms

If the circuit and external accessories are correct but the device continues to display a relevant fault or demonstrates unstable flow performance, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The DreamStation 2 remains out of service for qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External circuit, interface, exhalation-path, and accessory causes have been ruled out. A continuing airflow-control problem may involve internal flow/pressure sensing, the blower/control system, software, or another service-level condition.

Remove the DreamStation 2 from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for bench evaluation using appropriate Philips documentation and approved respiratory test equipment. Internal sensing or pneumatic repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Complete functional verification before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never obstruct the mask's intentional exhalation vent while investigating PAP airflow or leak complaints.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported poor airflow and believed the DreamStation 2 exhalation system was malfunctioning."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the intentional exhaust openings on the patient interface obstructed by incorrectly positioned material."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the obstruction, inspected the circuit, and verified normal airflow and unobstructed exhalation through the approved interface."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact displayed message
  • Description of reported airflow problem
  • Patient-interface type
  • Intentional exhalation vent condition
  • Tubing and connector condition
  • Humidifier connection condition
  • Known-good accessory substitutions
  • Objective flow or pressure testing
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Apply the correct PAP-system architecture rather than assuming ventilator-style removable flow sensors or expiratory valves. Verify the external air path first, objectively test unresolved airflow problems, and escalate internal sensing concerns appropriately.

That is successful troubleshooting.
