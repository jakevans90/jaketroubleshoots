Physio-Control LIFEPAK 20 / 20e

Error: 9011 – Pacer Fault

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Physio-Control

Model

LIFEPAK 20 / 20e

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting Error 9011 Pacer Fault when the LIFEPAK 20 / 20e reports a pacing subsystem failure that may prevent reliable pacing therapy.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

  • Remove the LIFEPAK 20 / 20e from patient use immediately when Error 9011 is active, recurring, or associated with questionable pacing operation.
  • Provide clinical staff with another verified defibrillator capable of pacing before troubleshooting continues.
  • Do not attempt troubleshooting while the device is connected to an active patient.

Expected: Patient care continues with a known-good defibrillator/pacer.

Why it matters: A pacer fault may affect the availability or accuracy of transcutaneous pacing therapy.

2. Verify the Reported Error and Behavior

Confirm the exact Service Log error is:

9011 – Pacer Fault

Determine when the condition occurs:

  • During startup
  • During self-test or operational checkout
  • When PACER mode is selected
  • During controlled pacing performance testing
  • Intermittently during operation

Check for associated behavior such as:

  • Pacing unavailable
  • Failure to initiate pacing
  • Unexpected pacing interruption
  • Abnormal pacing output during controlled testing
  • Service indication or repeated Service Log entries

Expected: Error 9011 is clearly confirmed in the Service Log or associated with reproducible pacing behavior.

Why it matters: This distinguishes a true system-reported pacer fault from electrode, cable, setup, or operator-related problems.

3. Check External Power and Device Condition

Before assuming an internal failure:

  • Connect the device to a known-good AC power source.
  • Confirm normal power indicators.
  • Verify installed batteries are properly seated and in acceptable condition.
  • Inspect the device for impact damage, liquid intrusion, contamination, unusual heat, odor, or other physical abnormalities.
  • Power-cycle the device only as part of a controlled Clinical Engineering evaluation.

Expected: The unit has stable power and no obvious external condition explains the fault.

Why it matters: Unstable power or physical damage can complicate troubleshooting and should be identified before internal failure is concluded.

4. Inspect Pacing Accessories and Connections

With the device removed from patient use, inspect the external pacing path:

  • Therapy cable
  • Cable connectors
  • Test adapter, if used
  • Approved pacing electrodes or controlled test load
  • Connector pins and strain-relief areas

Look for:

  • Bent or damaged contacts
  • Loose connections
  • Contamination
  • Fluid intrusion
  • Cracked insulation
  • Excessive cable wear

Reseat all accessible external connections.

Expected: External pacing accessories are properly connected and physically intact.

Why it matters: A damaged therapy accessory may create pacing problems, although a persistent 9011 Service Log error more strongly suggests a device-related fault.

5. Isolate the External Accessory Path

During controlled bench evaluation, substitute known-good compatible accessories when available.

Examples include:

  • Known-good therapy cable
  • Known-good test adapter
  • Verified defibrillator/pacer analyzer setup

Repeat only the appropriate operational or pacing check.

Expected: The Clinical Engineer determines whether the symptom follows an accessory or remains with the LIFEPAK 20 / 20e.

If the error clears with a replacement accessory: Correct the accessory-related problem, complete required functional and pacing verification, document the repair, and return the device to service only after all required tests pass.

If Error 9011 remains: Continue troubleshooting. A device-related pacer fault is increasingly likely.

6. Confirm Configuration and Test Setup

Verify that the observed behavior is not caused by an incorrect controlled test setup.

Check:

  • Correct pacing mode selection
  • Appropriate pacing rate and current settings
  • Proper analyzer connection
  • Correct simulated ECG input when demand pacing is evaluated
  • Secure therapy connections
  • Compatible test accessories

Expected: Test configuration is correct and the fault is not caused by setup error.

Why it matters: Demand pacing tests can appear abnormal when the analyzer, simulated ECG, therapy connection, or pacing configuration is incorrect.

7. Review the Service Log for Recurrence

Review the Service Log and determine whether Error 9011 is:

  • A single historical event
  • Repeated across multiple startups
  • Repeated when pacing is selected
  • Associated with other therapy-related errors

Document the recurrence pattern before corrective action.

Expected: The error history supports either an isolated event or a persistent pacing subsystem fault.

Why it matters: Repeated 9011 entries strengthen the case for internal corrective action.

8. Follow the Manual-Backed Software Corrective Action

After external causes and test setup issues have been ruled out, follow the authorized service procedure to reload the device software.

  • Do not improvise the software process.
  • Use the approved Physio-Control service method, required files, and applicable service documentation.

After the reload:

  • Restart the device.
  • Recheck the Service Log.
  • Repeat appropriate operational checks.
  • Evaluate whether Error 9011 returns.

Expected: The pacer fault clears if the condition was caused by corrupted or abnormal device software operation.

If resolved: Proceed to complete pacing performance verification before considering return to service.

9. Verify Pacing Performance Before Release

A cleared error alone is not sufficient for return to clinical use.

Using approved test equipment and facility procedures, verify applicable pacing performance, including appropriate checks of:

  • Pacing rate
  • Pacing current/output
  • Demand pacing behavior
  • Pacing initiation and termination
  • Stability of pacing operation
  • Absence of recurring Error 9011 indications

Expected: Pacing performance is within required specifications and the error does not recur.

If all required tests pass: Document the corrective action and return the device to service.

10. Stop When Resolved

If the issue is corrected by:

  • External accessory replacement
  • Connection correction
  • Test setup correction
  • Authorized device software reload

and the LIFEPAK 20 / 20e passes all required operational and pacing performance checks:

  • Clear the device for clinical use according to facility procedure.
  • Document the final verified condition.
  • Return the device to service.

Do not continue replacing parts after the fault has been corrected and performance has been verified.

If the Problem Persists

If Error 9011 remains after stable power has been verified, physical condition has been checked, external pacing accessories have been inspected, known-good accessories have been substituted where appropriate, configuration and test setup have been verified, Service Log behavior has been reviewed, and the manual-backed device software reload has been completed, then external and common causes have been reasonably ruled out.

A persistent 9011 – Pacer Fault strongly indicates an internal therapy subsystem failure.

The manual-backed next corrective action is replacement of the A04 Therapy PCB.

  • Remove the device from service.
  • Label it Out of Service.
  • Send for repair or qualified bench evaluation.
  • Escalate for authorized repair and follow approved service procedures for A04 Therapy PCB replacement.
  • Complete required post-repair functional and pacing performance testing before release.

Do not return the LIFEPAK 20 / 20e to clinical use based only on disappearance of the error after repair.

Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot Error 9011 on an active patient. Move the patient to another verified defibrillator/pacer first, maintain therapy continuity, and do not rely on a LIFEPAK 20 / 20e with an unresolved pacer fault.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"LIFEPAK 20e reported Service Log Error 9011 – Pacer Fault. Staff reported pacing unavailable/intermittent during operational checkout."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Verified stable power, inspected therapy cable and external pacing connections, confirmed controlled test setup, and reproduced Error 9011. Error persisted after external causes were ruled out, indicating a device-related pacing subsystem fault."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed device from service and completed manual-backed device software reload. Error persisted, so unit was labeled Out of Service and escalated for authorized A04 Therapy PCB corrective action. Pacing performance verification required before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Alarm behavior, including whether Error 9011 occurred at startup, during pacing, intermittently, or repeatedly
  • Accessories swapped, including therapy cable, test adapter, pacing accessories, or other compatible known-good accessories
  • Power behavior, including AC outlet tested, power indicators, and battery condition
  • Environmental factors, including contamination, liquid intrusion, unusual heat, odor, impact damage, or other physical abnormalities
  • Indicator lights, including Service indication or other abnormal device indicators
  • Analyzer or test load used
  • Pacing mode and settings at failure
  • Service Log recurrence and other therapy-related codes present
  • Whether device software was reloaded
  • Whether Error 9011 returned after reload
  • Pacing performance test results
  • Final device status, including returned to service, Out of Service, or sent for repair or bench evaluation

Final Thought

Error 9011 should be treated as a genuine pacing reliability concern, not simply cleared and ignored. Patient safety comes first, followed by logical isolation of power, accessories, setup, and device-specific causes. When external causes are ruled out, the manual-backed path progresses to software reload and, if persistent, A04 Therapy PCB corrective action. Accurate CCR documentation preserves the fault history and supports safe escalation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
