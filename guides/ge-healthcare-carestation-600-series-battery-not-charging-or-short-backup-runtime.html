GE Healthcare Carestation 600 Series

Battery Not Charging or Short Backup Runtime

Asset Type

Anesthesia Machine

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

Carestation 600 Series

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting poor battery charging, missing battery indication, unexpected shutdown, or reduced backup runtime caused by AC power, charging, battery condition, or excessive load.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not test battery runtime or intentionally disconnect AC power while the Carestation 600 Series is supporting an active patient.

If a battery warning, low-battery alarm, or unexpected transfer to battery occurs during a procedure:

  • Notify the anesthesia provider immediately.
  • Confirm that ventilation, gas delivery, alarms, and monitoring remain operational.
  • Restore verified AC power when possible.
  • Prepare another verified anesthesia machine or approved backup ventilation method.
  • Transfer the patient when directed by the anesthesia provider.

Expected outcome: Patient care is no longer dependent on equipment with uncertain backup power.

Continue troubleshooting only after the machine is safely removed from patient use.

2. Confirm the Reported Battery Condition

Determine whether the reported issue involves:

  • Battery not charging
  • Battery symbol missing or showing a failure
  • Repeated low-battery alarms
  • Immediate shutdown when AC power is removed
  • Backup runtime shorter than expected
  • Battery level decreasing while connected to AC power
  • Intermittent switching between AC and battery operation

Record the exact displayed message and battery-indicator behavior.

Expected outcome: The failure is clearly identified as a charging, detection, capacity, or AC-power problem.

3. Verify the Wall Outlet

Connect an electrical safety analyzer, outlet tester, or another approved test device to the wall receptacle.

Confirm:

  • Correct line voltage
  • Proper grounding
  • No loose receptacle contact
  • No intermittent power
  • The outlet is not controlled by an unexpected wall switch

Do not assume the outlet is functional because other equipment is connected nearby.

Expected outcome: The receptacle provides stable, correctly wired AC power.

If the outlet fails testing, move the machine to a verified receptacle and report the facility electrical problem.

4. Inspect the Power Cord and Connection

Inspect the complete power cord for:

  • Cuts, crushed sections, or exposed conductors
  • Bent or damaged plug blades
  • Heat discoloration
  • Loose strain relief
  • A connection that does not seat securely at the machine
  • Evidence that the cord has been pulled or rolled over

Firmly reconnect the cord at both the wall outlet and machine inlet.

Expected outcome: The power cord is undamaged and securely connected.

Replace a damaged cord only with the correct approved replacement.

5. Confirm the Mains Indicator Is On

With the machine connected to verified AC power, check that the mains indicator is illuminated.

The mains indicator identifies that AC power is connected. The internal battery should charge while AC power remains connected unless it is already fully charged.

Expected outcome: The mains indicator remains continuously illuminated.

If the indicator is off despite verified power and a known-good cord, remove the machine from service for evaluation of the power-entry or internal charging system.

6. Eliminate External Power Interruptions

Check whether the machine is connected through:

  • A power strip
  • An extension cord
  • A switched outlet
  • An isolation-power system showing a fault
  • A loose ceiling-pendant receptacle
  • A receptacle with poor plug retention

Connect the Carestation directly to a verified hospital-grade outlet when permitted.

Observe the mains indicator while gently checking the plug and cord for intermittent contact. Do not flex damaged wiring.

Expected outcome: AC power remains stable without unexpected battery transfer.

If stable operation returns, correct the external power-source problem and stop.

7. Allow Adequate Charging Time

Leave the machine connected to verified AC power according to facility and manufacturer charging practices.

Avoid evaluating backup runtime immediately after:

  • Extended storage
  • Transportation while unplugged
  • A prolonged power outage
  • Complete battery depletion
  • Battery replacement
  • Repeated battery testing

Confirm that the mains indicator remains on throughout the charging period.

Expected outcome: The displayed battery level increases and charging-related warnings clear.

If the battery level does not increase, continue troubleshooting.

8. Check for Battery Recognition or Failure Indications

Review the screen for:

  • Missing battery indication
  • Battery failure symbol
  • Battery not installed or not detected message
  • Charging fault
  • Low-battery warning despite prolonged AC connection
  • Rapid changes in the battery-level display

A missing or failed battery indication may identify a battery connection, battery assembly, monitoring, or charging-system problem rather than simple battery depletion.

Expected outcome: The battery is recognized and its charge state is displayed normally.

If the battery remains unrecognized, remove the machine from service and escalate for bench evaluation.

9. Check Machine Age and Battery-Service History

Review the CMMS and service records for:

  • Battery installation date
  • Previous capacity-test results
  • Repeated low-runtime complaints
  • Long periods of storage without AC power
  • Prior charger or power-supply repairs
  • Whether both batteries were replaced as a matched set

The Carestation 600 Series uses internal rechargeable sealed lead-acid batteries. Available service information indicates that the system uses two series-connected batteries and recommends evaluating battery capacity as the batteries age.

Expected outcome: Battery age and service history support continued use, or aged batteries are identified as the likely cause.

Do not replace only one battery in a series-connected matched battery set unless specifically permitted by current GE service documentation.

10. Perform an Approved Battery-Capacity Test

Only trained Clinical Engineering personnel should perform the battery-capacity test using the current GE service procedure and required test setup.

Before testing:

  • Fully charge the batteries.
  • Remove the machine from clinical use.
  • Connect an appropriate test lung and required gas supplies.
  • Verify that backup ventilation equipment is available.
  • Record the starting charge indication and test conditions.

Do not judge battery condition by briefly unplugging the machine for a few seconds.

The Carestation 600 Series is designed to provide up to approximately 90 minutes of system operation and ventilation under specified fully charged conditions, but actual runtime varies with battery condition, configuration, load, and operating conditions.

Expected outcome: The batteries meet the manufacturer’s current capacity-test requirements.

If the test passes, reconnect AC power, recharge the system, complete checkout, and return it to service.

11. Evaluate Connected Electrical Loads

Determine whether optional monitors, warming equipment, pumps, or other accessories are powered from the anesthesia machine’s auxiliary outlets.

Excessive or unauthorized accessory loading may:

  • Affect power-system operation
  • Trip an outlet breaker
  • Create confusion about which components remain powered during an outage
  • Increase total current demand

Disconnect nonessential accessories and repeat the operational check if permitted.

Expected outcome: The Carestation remains stable on AC power and approved accessories operate within the outlet ratings.

Do not assume every device connected to the workstation’s outlets is supported by the anesthesia machine’s internal batteries.

12. Inspect for Heat, Odor, Swelling, or Leakage

Without opening internal assemblies, check for:

  • Unusual heat near the battery compartment
  • Electrical or sulfur-like odor
  • Case deformation
  • Fluid leakage
  • Corrosion
  • Repeated fuse or breaker operation
  • Abnormal buzzing or clicking during charging

Expected outcome: No evidence of battery damage or electrical overheating is present.

If heat, odor, swelling, leakage, or smoke is detected:

  • Disconnect AC power when safe.
  • Remove the machine from service immediately.
  • Keep it away from patient-care areas.
  • Follow facility procedures for damaged batteries and electrical equipment.
  • Escalate for qualified service.

13. Complete a Functional and Power-Transfer Check

After correcting the suspected cause:

  • Fully charge the batteries.
  • Complete the normal system checkout.
  • Confirm the mains indicator is on.
  • With the machine off patient and connected to the required test equipment, remove AC power briefly according to approved procedures.
  • Verify an orderly transfer to battery operation.
  • Reconnect AC power.
  • Confirm the machine returns to mains operation and charging resumes.
  • Verify that no battery or power-system fault remains.

Expected outcome: The machine transfers between AC and battery power correctly, maintains required operation, and shows normal charging status.

If the issue is resolved, stop troubleshooting and document the repair.

If the Problem Persists

If verified AC power, the power cord, connections, charging time, accessory loading, and battery condition have been checked, the fault is likely within the battery assembly, battery connections, charging circuit, power supply, or power-monitoring electronics.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for qualified repair or bench evaluation
  • Tested using the current GE Healthcare service documentation
  • Given a complete system checkout and battery-capacity test before return to clinical use

Do not continue repeatedly cycling or deeply discharging questionable batteries. Knowing when to stop and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never begin a surgical case with an unresolved battery warning or uncertain backup runtime. Loss of AC power must not leave the patient dependent on an anesthesia machine with unverified battery capacity.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Anesthesia staff reported that the Carestation 600 Series displayed a low-battery warning and operated for only several minutes after AC power was disconnected."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Verified AC power and the power cord were normal, but the aged internal battery set failed the approved capacity test."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the machine from service, replaced the battery set according to the current GE service procedure, fully charged the batteries, and verified normal checkout, AC-to-battery transfer, and acceptable backup capacity."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact battery or power message displayed
  • Mains indicator status
  • Outlet voltage and ground test results
  • Power cord condition
  • Whether another outlet was tested
  • Battery installation date
  • Charging time provided
  • Starting and ending battery indication
  • Battery-capacity test conditions and result
  • Connected accessory load
  • Unusual heat, odor, swelling, or leakage
  • AC-to-battery transfer behavior
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Reliable backup power is essential to anesthesia-machine safety. Begin with the outlet, cord, mains indication, and charging conditions before suspecting internal failure. Use an approved capacity test, escalate unsafe electrical conditions promptly, and document the complaint, verified cause, and final resolution clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
