Manual CPR Release Works but the Foot Section Will Not Rehome

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Hillrom

Model

TotalCare

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot a foot section that will not return to its normal reference position after manual CPR release, focusing on obstructions, power, position, and controls.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Emergency Readiness

Do not troubleshoot a bed that cannot reliably recover from CPR positioning while a patient depends on it for care.

If the bed cannot be returned to a safe, functional configuration following manual CPR operation, transfer the patient to another verified bed before continuing evaluation.

Expected outcome: The patient is supported on equipment with reliable positioning and emergency functions. If this cannot be assured, remove the TotalCare bed from service.

2. Confirm the Exact Post-CPR Condition

With the bed unloaded or under controlled service conditions, determine what occurred after the manual CPR release.

Identify whether:

  • The back section lowered correctly.
  • The foot section remained retracted or displaced.
  • Powered foot movement is completely unavailable.
  • The foot section moves but stops before reaching its normal position.
  • Other powered bed functions operate normally.
  • A warning, indicator, or message remains active.

Expected outcome: The failure is reproducible and isolated to the foot-section rehome or recovery process rather than a general bed power failure.

3. Inspect for Obstructions Around the Foot Section

Check the entire accessible foot-end travel path for:

  • Linens.
  • Mattress material.
  • Tubing.
  • Cables.
  • Patient belongings.
  • Equipment mounted to the frame.
  • Objects beneath the bed.
  • Damaged covers or retainers contacting moving parts.

Do not force the foot section into position.

Expected outcome: The foot section has an unobstructed path. If an obstruction is removed and normal rehome operation returns, perform repeated verification and stop troubleshooting.

4. Confirm Mattress and Foot-End Components Are Properly Seated

Inspect the mattress, foot-end retainer, removable components, and external support surfaces for misalignment following CPR activation.

A shifted mattress or displaced external component can interfere with the section returning to its expected position.

Expected outcome: All external components are correctly seated and free of mechanical interference. If repositioning restores normal function, complete final verification.

5. Restore Stable AC Power

Connect the bed to a known-good approved AC outlet when practical and verify normal bed power.

Inspect the cord and plug for visible damage and compare the behavior with reliable AC power available.

Expected outcome: Adequate operating power is available for powered recovery. If the foot section rehomes normally after AC power is restored, verify battery and bed operation as required before return to service.

6. Verify the CPR Release Has Fully Reset Mechanically

Inspect the accessible CPR handle or release mechanism and confirm it has returned to its normal resting position after activation.

Do not manually manipulate internal latches or bypass the CPR mechanism.

If the release handle appears stuck, damaged, or does not return normally, stop further powered testing.

Expected outcome: The manual CPR control is fully returned to its normal position and shows no visible damage. A release that remains mechanically abnormal requires service evaluation.

7. Place the Bed in a Known Starting Position

Using functioning authorized controls, return other bed sections toward a normal neutral position where possible, then retry foot-section movement.

Avoid repeated cycling if the foot section binds, produces abnormal noise, or moves unpredictably.

Expected outcome: The bed is in a consistent starting configuration. If this allows the foot section to complete its return and normal operation remains repeatable, continue to final verification.

8. Compare Available Caregiver Controls

Attempt the foot-section command from another authorized caregiver control location if available.

Determine whether:

  • All controls produce the same failure.
  • One control interface fails while another succeeds.
  • The foot section receives a command but stops during movement.
  • No movement response occurs at all.

Expected outcome: The test distinguishes a control-interface problem from a broader foot-section movement problem. If one external control is defective but the underlying movement operates normally, repair the control according to approved procedures and verify the complete bed.

9. Inspect Accessible Foot-Section Mechanisms

With the bed secured and power removed when appropriate, inspect accessible external portions of the foot-section mechanism.

Look for:

  • Bent brackets.
  • Loose mounting hardware.
  • Pinched wiring.
  • Damaged covers.
  • Visible linkage interference.
  • Signs of impact.
  • Fluid contamination.

Do not open actuator assemblies or perform internal board-level troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: No external mechanical or wiring defect is visible. Any physical damage that could impair movement requires the bed to remain out of service.

10. Perform Controlled Functional Verification

If an external cause was corrected, operate the bed through applicable normal positioning and a controlled manual CPR activation followed by recovery, according to approved facility and manufacturer practices.

Verify:

  • CPR release functions correctly.
  • Powered controls recover normally afterward.
  • Foot section returns to its expected operating position.
  • Movement is smooth and repeatable.
  • No warning remains.
  • No abnormal noise or binding occurs.

Expected outcome: The bed reliably returns to normal powered operation following CPR release. Troubleshooting can stop once repeatable function and return-to-service requirements are satisfied.

11. Escalate if the Foot Section Still Will Not Rehome

If the foot section remains unable to return after obstructions, mattress position, power, CPR handle position, controls, and accessible external mechanisms have been checked, stop troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The bed is removed from service for qualified diagnosis rather than being returned to clinical use with an unresolved post-CPR recovery failure.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. The remaining condition may involve foot-section position sensing, drive components, CPR mechanism state detection, wiring, control electronics, mechanical linkage, or another service-level issue.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Hillrom service documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

After service, verify normal bed articulation and successful recovery following manual CPR activation before return to clinical use.

A bed that performs the emergency release but cannot reliably recover afterward is not fully functional. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

After any CPR-release complaint, verify the complete recovery sequence before returning the bed to patient care; successful emergency lowering alone is not sufficient.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the manual CPR release lowered the bed correctly, but the foot section would not return to its normal position afterward."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a blanket trapped beneath the foot section and restricting its return travel after the CPR release."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the obstruction, repositioned the bedding, and verified normal foot-section movement and successful powered recovery following repeated controlled CPR-release testing."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether manual CPR release operated normally.
  • Position of the foot section after CPR.
  • Other bed functions available.
  • AC or battery operation.
  • CPR handle return position.
  • Obstructions or linens found.
  • Control locations tested.
  • Visible mechanical damage.
  • Unusual sound or binding.
  • Results of controlled CPR recovery testing.
  • Final bed status.

Final Thought

Patient safety comes first whenever emergency positioning or recovery is unreliable. Rule out obstruction, power, positioning, and external control causes before assuming an internal fault, verify the entire CPR recovery sequence, escalate unresolved failures, and document the result clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
