Belimed WD 290 Series

Spray Arm Does Not Rotate or the Rack Manifold Is Not Coupled

Asset Type

Washer-Disinfector

Manufacturer

Belimed

Model

WD 290 Series

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots poor spray-arm movement or rack water coupling caused by loading, obstruction, rack positioning, manifold alignment, or damaged external components.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patients and Processing Workflow

Do not release instruments from a cycle with questionable spray performance. Keep the affected load segregated and provide another validated washer-disinfector if processing must continue.

If inadequate washing or disinfection may have occurred, follow the facility's reprocessing policy for the affected load.

Expected outcome: Potentially inadequately processed instruments are prevented from entering clinical use.

2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Determine whether the problem involves:

  • One spray arm or multiple arms
  • One particular rack or all racks
  • A spray arm that is physically stuck
  • A rack that does not fully engage the washer connection
  • Poor cleaning results suggesting inadequate circulation

Review available cycle information for additional indications of an interrupted or abnormal process.

Expected outcome: The affected rack, spray component, or operating condition is clearly identified.

3. Inspect the Spray Arm Externally

With the unit safe and not operating, inspect the affected spray arm for:

  • Trapped instruments or packaging
  • Debris around the hub
  • Blocked visible spray openings
  • Bent or visibly damaged components
  • Contact with baskets, trays, or other load items

Gently verify that the spray arm can move freely without forcing it.

Expected outcome: The spray arm moves freely and nothing in the load or chamber interferes with its rotation. If correcting an obstruction restores normal operation, troubleshooting can stop after successful cycle verification.

4. Verify Load Placement

Confirm trays, baskets, instruments, and other items are loaded so they do not extend into the spray-arm travel path.

Compare the load with a known properly functioning configuration when practical.

Do not modify racks or accessories to obtain clearance.

Expected outcome: The load does not restrict spray-arm movement or interfere with water distribution.

5. Inspect Rack Position and Manifold Coupling

Remove and reinstall the rack while inspecting:

  • Rack seating and alignment
  • Coupling surfaces
  • External seals or connection points
  • Bent or damaged rack components
  • Foreign material preventing full engagement
  • Mispositioned baskets or accessories

Verify that the rack moves completely into its normal operating position without excessive force.

Expected outcome: The rack seats normally and the manifold connection aligns and engages as intended. If reseating the rack corrects the problem, proceed to functional verification.

6. Compare With a Known-Good Rack When Available

If the condition appears limited to one rack, use another compatible known-good rack according to facility procedures.

Do not interchange racks or accessories unless they are approved for the WD 290 Series configuration.

Expected outcome: A known-good rack couples normally and allows proper operation. If only the original rack fails, remove that rack from use for repair or replacement evaluation.

7. Check Chamber Connection Areas

Inspect accessible chamber-side connection points for:

  • Visible debris
  • Damage
  • Loose external components
  • Obstruction that could prevent rack engagement

Do not disassemble internal plumbing or valves.

Expected outcome: Accessible connection areas are clean, intact, and unobstructed.

8. Perform a Controlled Functional Cycle

Once external causes have been corrected, operate the washer-disinfector using an appropriate test or facility-approved cycle without relying on the cycle for patient-ready instruments until proper operation is confirmed.

Verify:

  • Rack remains properly seated
  • No abnormal leakage occurs
  • The cycle completes normally
  • Spray components operate without obvious interference
  • No related alarm or process failure occurs

Expected outcome: The rack remains coupled and the cycle completes normally. If confirmed, troubleshooting can stop and the unit may proceed through applicable return-to-service requirements.

9. Escalate if the Condition Remains

If the spray arm remains stationary despite being mechanically free, or the rack will not couple despite proper positioning and an undamaged rack, stop external troubleshooting.

Possible service-level categories include circulation problems, internal coupling mechanisms, plumbing restrictions, sensing problems, or other internal failures.

Expected outcome: An unresolved washer-disinfector is prevented from continued clinical processing and is referred for qualified service.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as loading interference, spray-arm obstruction, rack positioning, coupling alignment, and visible damage have been ruled out.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench/service evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Belimed documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not proceed into pumps, valves, internal manifolds, control assemblies, or other internal systems without appropriate service authorization.

Following correction, perform applicable functional and process verification before returning the washer-disinfector to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not release a processed load when spray performance or rack coupling is uncertain; repeat processing only after the equipment has been verified functional.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported that the rack was not fully coupling and the upper spray arm was not rotating during operation."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the rack was not fully seated because debris at the accessible manifold connection prevented proper engagement."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the obstruction, reseated the rack, verified free spray-arm movement, and completed a functional cycle without recurrence."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Rack type or identification
  • Which spray arm was affected
  • Whether the arm rotated freely by hand
  • Load configuration
  • Visible obstruction or damage
  • Rack seating condition
  • Manifold condition
  • Known-good rack substitution results
  • Related alarms or cycle interruptions
  • Functional cycle result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the processed load first, then work from loading and mechanical interference toward rack coupling and functional verification. Do not assume an internal circulation failure until external causes are ruled out, and document the verified cause and final status clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
