Medline BioCon 900S

Touchscreen or UI Lockup

Asset Type

Bladder Scanner

Manufacturer

Medline

Model

BioCon 900S

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting frozen screens, delayed touch response, unresponsive controls, or recurring UI lockups caused by power state, contamination, accessories, or device faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot an unresponsive or frozen BioCon 900S while its result is being relied upon for a clinical decision.

If bladder-volume assessment is immediately required, provide another verified bladder scanner or appropriate alternative assessment method before continuing.

Expected: Patient care is not dependent on a device with unreliable controls or display behavior.

Why it matters: An unresolved UI problem can prevent reliable patient selection, scan setup, acquisition, review, or confirmation of bladder-volume results.

If transferring the clinical need to another device resolves the immediate concern, continue troubleshooting only in a controlled Clinical Engineering setting.

2. Verify the Exact Failure Behavior

Power on the BioCon 900S and determine whether the problem is:

  • Entire touchscreen unresponsive
  • Specific screen areas not responding
  • Touch response delayed
  • Display frozen while the device remains powered
  • UI repeatedly returning to one screen
  • Lockup occurring only during scanning
  • Lockup occurring when reviewing or entering data
  • Device rebooting rather than simply freezing

Document any displayed message and the exact screen active when the failure occurs.

Expected: The complaint is narrowed to a reproducible touchscreen, software-response, or broader power problem.

If the issue cannot be reproduced and normal operation remains stable, document the evaluation and continue with repeated functional checks before return to service.

3. Check for a Normal Screen-Lock or Input State

Confirm the device is not waiting for completion of a prompt, menu selection, patient-data field, confirmation window, or other active screen state.

Attempt normal navigation using only the intended on-screen controls.

Do not repeatedly tap multiple areas of the screen, as this can obscure whether the interface is delayed or truly unresponsive.

Expected: Normal UI navigation resumes when the active prompt or screen state is completed.

If this resolves the issue, verify several navigation functions and stop.

4. Inspect the Touchscreen Surface

Examine the display for:

  • Ultrasound gel
  • Cleaning-fluid residue
  • Moisture
  • Heavy fingerprints or soil
  • Adhesive contamination
  • Cracked cover surface
  • Impact damage
  • Screen lifting or separation

Clean the external surface only with methods and agents approved for the device, then allow the screen to dry completely before retesting.

Expected: The touchscreen is clean, dry, intact, and free of contamination that could interfere with touch response.

If cleaning restores reliable operation, complete repeated functional checks and stop.

5. Remove External Accessories and Data Connections

Disconnect nonessential external accessories or communication connections during controlled testing, including any connected USB interface or optional peripheral that is not required for basic standalone operation.

Then retest the UI.

Expected: The BioCon 900S operates normally in a simplified standalone configuration.

If the lockup disappears, reconnect accessories one at a time to identify whether the behavior follows a specific cable, peripheral, or connection. Stop when the external cause is isolated.

6. Verify Battery and Power Condition

Check the displayed battery condition and determine whether the UI problem occurs:

  • Only at low charge
  • Immediately after removal from the charger
  • Only after extended battery operation
  • During charging
  • Regardless of power condition

Ensure the device has adequate charge before evaluating intermittent UI behavior.

Inspect the charging equipment and accessible contacts for obvious damage, contamination, or poor seating.

Expected: Testing occurs with a stable, adequately charged power source.

If reliable operation returns after correcting the external power or charging condition, verify repeated startup and UI operation and stop.

7. Perform a Controlled Power Cycle

When the device is safely removed from clinical use, shut it down using the normal power control.

Allow the shutdown to complete, wait briefly, then restart the unit.

Do not use repeated rapid power cycling as a substitute for troubleshooting.

Expected: The device completes startup and the UI responds normally.

If one controlled restart resolves the issue, continue repeated navigation and scan-function checks. A recurring freeze after restart should not be dismissed as a one-time event.

8. Check Whether the Lockup Follows a Specific Workflow

Repeat normal functions in a controlled setting and determine whether the freeze occurs during a specific action, such as:

  • Startup
  • Patient or user data entry
  • Mode selection
  • Pre-scan viewing
  • Scan initiation
  • Result review
  • Saving data
  • Accessing settings
  • Connecting an external interface

The BioCon 900S includes touchscreen-based operation and real-time bladder viewing, so identifying the exact workflow where the UI stops responding helps distinguish a general input problem from a repeatable functional lockup.

Expected: The problem either remains random or becomes reproducible at a specific operation.

If a specific workflow repeatedly triggers the lockup, document it precisely and proceed toward escalation rather than repeatedly forcing the device through the failure.

9. Check for Self-Diagnostic or Hardware Indications

Observe startup and operation for any:

  • Self-diagnostic warning
  • Hardware or maintenance message
  • Abnormal indicator behavior
  • Unexpected restart
  • Persistent error
  • Failure associated with the wireless probe

Medline describes the BioCon 900S as having built-in self-diagnostics intended to alert users to hardware or maintenance issues.

Record the exact message or behavior rather than clearing it without documentation.

Expected: No unresolved diagnostic warning accompanies the UI problem.

If a diagnostic or maintenance indication persists, stop troubleshooting and escalate for evaluation.

10. Compare With Known-Good External Components Where Practical

When compatible known-good equipment is available and substitution is permitted by facility procedure, compare external components involved in the reported failure.

For example, determine whether the problem changes with a verified charging setup or after removing a suspect external connection.

Do not open the housing or begin internal touchscreen, display, battery, or board-level replacement as part of basic troubleshooting.

Expected: The failure either follows an external component or remains with the BioCon 900S itself.

If the issue remains with the device, continue to escalation.

11. Perform a Final Controlled Functional Check

Before return to service, verify:

  • Normal startup
  • Responsive touchscreen across required controls
  • Stable menu navigation
  • No spontaneous freezing
  • Normal scan initiation
  • Normal result display
  • No recurring diagnostic warning
  • Stable operation through several repeated cycles

Use facility-approved functional verification methods and follow applicable manufacturer guidance.

Expected: The UI remains consistently responsive and the device completes intended functions without lockup.

If the issue recurs even once during repeated verification, do not return the unit to clinical service.

If the Problem Persists

If the touchscreen or UI continues to freeze after external contamination, power condition, charging setup, accessories, connections, workflow triggers, and controlled restart have been evaluated, common external causes have been ruled out.

The issue may involve an internal touchscreen assembly, display interface, embedded software, memory, power-management circuitry, or another internal hardware fault.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or controlled bench evaluation
  • Evaluated through the appropriate authorized service pathway when required

Do not continue repeated power cycling or return a device with recurring UI lockup to clinical use simply because it temporarily recovers.

Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not troubleshoot a frozen BioCon 900S while a patient assessment depends on the result. Move the clinical need to another verified bladder scanner first, then evaluate the affected unit safely.

A touchscreen that “works after rebooting” but repeatedly freezes should be treated as an unresolved intermittent fault until reliability is demonstrated.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported the BioCon 900S touchscreen froze during bladder-scan setup and would not respond to on-screen selections."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Touchscreen remained intermittently unresponsive after cleaning, adequate-charge verification, accessory isolation, and controlled restart, indicating a persistent device-side UI fault."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the BioCon 900S from service, labeled it Out of Service, documented the recurring lockup condition, and routed the unit for bench evaluation and repair."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact screen where lockup occurred
  • Entire screen or only specific touch areas affected
  • Touchscreen cleaned and dried
  • Visible cracks or impact damage
  • Battery level at time of failure
  • Charging condition checked
  • External connections removed
  • Controlled restart performed
  • Failure reproduced
  • Lockup associated with a specific workflow
  • Diagnostic or maintenance message observed
  • Unexpected reboot observed
  • Unusual heat, odor, or sound noted
  • Final device status documented

Final Thought

Patient safety comes first. Start with observable external causes, simplify the device configuration, verify stable power, reproduce the failure logically, and escalate recurring UI lockups rather than assuming a temporary restart proves reliability. Clear CCR documentation makes intermittent failures easier to repair and protects the integrity of the service record.

That is successful troubleshooting.
