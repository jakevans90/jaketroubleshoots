STERIS Reliance 444

Rack, Manifold, or Cart Connection Not Detected

Asset Type

Washer-Disinfector

Manufacturer

STERIS

Model

Reliance 444

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots rack or cart recognition problems caused by poor seating, alignment, damaged connections, contamination, or incompatible processing accessories.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Processing and Confirm the Detection Problem

Do not process a load if the washer cannot reliably recognize or connect to the required rack, manifold, or cart.

Confirm whether:

  • The washer refuses to start
  • The rack is physically seated but not recognized
  • Detection is intermittent
  • Only one rack is affected
  • A water-manifold connection does not engage

Expected outcome: The affected rack or connection and the exact symptom are identified.

2. Verify the Correct Rack or Cart Is Being Used

Confirm the rack, manifold, or cart is appropriate for the washer and intended cycle.

Do not modify or force incompatible accessories into position.

Expected outcome: The correct compatible processing accessory is present.

3. Fully Remove and Reseat the Rack

With the washer in a safe idle condition, remove the rack or cart and reinstall it carefully.

Check for:

  • Incomplete insertion
  • Skewed wheels
  • Objects on guide tracks
  • Load overhang
  • Resistance before full seating

Expected outcome: The rack reaches its normal fully seated position. If reseating restores detection, perform a verification cycle and stop.

4. Inspect the Rack Connection Area

Visually inspect accessible manifold interfaces, alignment features, seals, and connection surfaces for:

  • Debris
  • Damage
  • Bent external parts
  • Missing visible components
  • Heavy residue

Do not disassemble the internal connection mechanism.

Expected outcome: Connection surfaces are clean, intact, and aligned.

5. Inspect the Load for Interference

Confirm instruments or trays are not preventing the rack from reaching its final seated position.

Expected outcome: The load does not interfere with rack insertion or connection.

6. Compare With a Known-Good Rack

If available, test another compatible verified rack or cart.

Expected outcome: If the known-good rack is detected, the original rack or manifold is isolated as the likely source. If no compatible rack is detected, the washer requires further evaluation.

7. Check for Reproducibility

Repeat insertion several times only if safe and appropriate. Determine whether detection is consistent or dependent on rack position.

Expected outcome: A properly functioning system recognizes the rack consistently. Intermittent recognition should not be accepted for clinical processing.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correcting an external issue, run the appropriate cycle with the rack fully connected and verify normal filling, circulation, cycle completion, and rack recognition.

Expected outcome: Rack detection remains stable throughout the cycle. Troubleshooting can stop.

9. Escalate Persistent Detection Problems

If correct rack type, seating, alignment, cleanliness, and known-good comparison have been addressed and detection remains unreliable, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The washer or affected rack is removed from service as appropriate.

If the Problem Persists

The remaining issue may involve an internal rack sensor, manifold coupling, alignment component, interlock, plumbing interface, or control system.

The affected washer or rack should be:

  • Removed from service as appropriate
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or service evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate STERIS documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel

Return-to-service testing must verify repeatable rack recognition, correct manifold engagement, circulation, and full cycle completion. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A rack that is physically inside the chamber is not necessarily properly connected; confirm recognition and manifold engagement before processing a load.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported the Reliance 444 would not recognize one processing rack and would not begin the cycle."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found debris on the rack guide preventing the rack from reaching its fully seated position."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The obstruction was removed, repeatable rack detection and normal manifold engagement were verified, and the washer completed a successful test cycle."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Rack or cart identification
  • Whether one or all racks are affected
  • Seating condition
  • Manifold alignment
  • Visible debris or damage
  • Known-good rack comparison
  • Detection consistency
  • Cycle initiation result
  • Final verification
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Start with rack compatibility, seating, loading, alignment, and a known-good comparison before assuming an internal detection failure. Intermittent rack recognition is not an acceptable return-to-service condition and should be escalated appropriately.

That is successful troubleshooting.
