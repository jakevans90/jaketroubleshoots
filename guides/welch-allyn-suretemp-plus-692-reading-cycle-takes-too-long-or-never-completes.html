Reading Cycle Takes Too Long or Never Completes

Asset Type

Thermometer

Manufacturer

Welch Allyn

Model

SureTemp Plus 692

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots delayed or incomplete temperature cycles caused by technique, probe covers, positioning, mode, movement, probe condition, environment, or device faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Avoid Delayed Clinical Assessment

Do not repeatedly attempt a prolonged measurement when the patient’s condition requires timely assessment. Provide another verified thermometer and remove the affected unit from use during troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: Patient care is not delayed by an unreliable temperature cycle.

2. Confirm the Exact Cycle Behavior

Ask staff whether the measurement starts slowly, remains active indefinitely, restarts, changes modes, or ends without a result. Determine the measurement site, patient population, probe type, and whether a probe cover was used.

Reproduce the condition using an approved nonpatient test method.

Expected outcome: The delay is confirmed and associated conditions are documented.

3. Verify Probe Recognition and Ready State

Confirm that removing the probe from the well places the thermometer in the expected ready condition. Resolve any probe or probe-well detection error first.

Expected outcome: The thermometer recognizes the probe and begins a measurement cycle normally.

4. Inspect the Probe and Cable

Check the probe tip, cable, connector, and strain relief for contamination, cracks, moisture, severe bends, or intermittent damage. Clean and dry external surfaces according to approved procedures.

Expected outcome: The probe is clean, dry, intact, and securely connected.

5. Verify the Correct Probe Cover

Confirm that the probe cover is compatible, fully seated, undamaged, and used only once. Check for doubled covers, loose covers, wrinkles, trapped debris, or an incorrectly installed cover.

Do not test patients without required infection-control barriers.

Expected outcome: A single correct probe cover is installed securely. If correcting the cover restores normal cycle completion, proceed to final verification.

6. Confirm the Intended Measurement Mode

Verify that the selected oral, axillary, pediatric, or other approved mode matches the intended measurement site and patient population. Incorrect mode selection may alter measurement behavior.

Expected outcome: The correct mode is displayed and remains selected.

7. Review Positioning and Measurement Technique

For clinical observations, verify that staff are placing the probe at the intended anatomical site and maintaining stable contact according to approved practice. Identify movement, talking, poor tissue contact, recent eating or drinking, or other factors that may prevent prediction from stabilizing.

Clinical Engineering should not use a patient as the primary technical test.

Expected outcome: Technique-related causes are identified and corrected through staff education when appropriate.

8. Check Environmental Conditions

Move the thermometer away from direct airflow, temperature extremes, sunlight, or recent transition from a hot or cold storage area. Allow the device and probe to stabilize in the clinical environment.

Expected outcome: Environmental conditions no longer interfere with measurement stabilization.

9. Test With a Known-Good Compatible Probe

Install a verified compatible probe and repeat the cycle using an approved simulator or functional verification method.

Expected outcome: The known-good probe completes the cycle normally, identifying the original probe as defective, or the delay remains and points toward a device-side problem.

10. Verify Battery Condition

Install fresh, matched, correct batteries and inspect the contacts. Low or unstable power can cause slow processing, restarts, or incomplete cycles.

Expected outcome: The thermometer operates with stable power. If fresh batteries correct the delay, complete repeated measurements.

11. Perform Repeated Controlled Measurements

Complete several measurements with the same approved test setup, allowing the device to reset normally between cycles. Record whether completion time and results are consistent.

Do not invent a maximum cycle duration; compare behavior with approved documentation or a verified comparison unit.

Expected outcome: Measurements complete consistently and produce stable verified readings. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

12. Stop and Escalate an Incomplete Cycle

If the cycle remains excessively prolonged or never completes with a known-good probe, correct mode, stable environment, proper test setup, and fresh batteries, remove the thermometer from service.

Expected outcome: The unreliable thermometer is prevented from delaying or compromising patient care.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving probe recognition, probe covers, positioning, mode, movement, environment, battery power, and probe condition have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve the probe interface, prediction-processing circuitry, internal timing, calibration data, or another service-level fault.

Remove the thermometer from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Welch Allyn documentation and approved test equipment. Internal repair and configuration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify normal cycle initiation, consistent completion over repeated tests, all required modes, and temperature accuracy. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

When a temperature cycle will not complete promptly, obtain the patient’s temperature with another verified device rather than repeatedly repositioning an unreliable thermometer.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing staff reported that the SureTemp Plus 692 remained in the measurement cycle and did not produce a temperature."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found that the probe cover was not fully seated and was wrinkled around the probe tip."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering installed a new compatible probe cover correctly and verified repeated completed measurements with stable temperature results."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Measurement site and selected mode
  • Whether the cycle starts, stalls, or restarts
  • Probe and cable condition
  • Probe-cover type and installation
  • Probe-well detection
  • Battery condition
  • Environmental conditions
  • Known-good probe result
  • Controlled cycle results
  • Final accuracy-verification result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

A prolonged cycle may result from simple technique, probe-cover, mode, positioning, environmental, or accessory causes. Verify these first, test under controlled conditions, and remove the thermometer from service when measurement completion remains unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
