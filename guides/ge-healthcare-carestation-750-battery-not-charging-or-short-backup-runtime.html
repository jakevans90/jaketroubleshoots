GE Healthcare Carestation 750

Battery Not Charging or Short Backup Runtime

Asset Type

Anesthesia Machine

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

Carestation 750

What This Guide Helps With

The battery does not charge or provides short backup operation because of AC supply, cord, connection, battery condition, storage, or charging-system problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Reliable Power

Do not intentionally remove AC power to test a questionable battery while the Carestation 750 is supporting a patient.

If reliable backup power is required and cannot be confirmed, remove the machine from clinical use or provide another verified anesthesia machine.

Expected outcome: Patient care does not depend on an unverified battery.

2. Confirm the Reported Battery Problem

Determine whether the issue is:

  • Battery not charging
  • Battery indicator not increasing
  • Machine loses power immediately when AC is disconnected
  • Backup runtime is unusually short
  • Battery status is intermittent or unavailable

Record the displayed battery condition and AC status.

Expected outcome: The battery complaint is clearly defined and can be evaluated safely.

3. Verify the AC Source

Confirm the machine is plugged into a known-good hospital-grade receptacle.

Inspect the power cord and plug for:

  • Damage
  • Loose connection
  • Bent or damaged contacts
  • Signs of overheating

Test the receptacle using approved equipment or compare with a known-good outlet.

Expected outcome: Stable AC power reaches the Carestation 750. If correcting the outlet or cord connection restores charging, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Confirm the Machine Recognizes AC Power

With AC connected, verify the machine indicates mains power or charging status as expected.

If it remains in battery operation despite a known-good outlet, inspect accessible external power connections.

Expected outcome: The machine recognizes AC input. Failure to recognize known-good AC requires escalation.

5. Allow an Appropriate Charging Opportunity

If the battery was deeply discharged or the machine was stored unplugged, leave the unit connected to AC according to normal manufacturer-approved charging practices.

Do not declare the battery defective solely from a single short test immediately after storage.

Expected outcome: Battery state increases appropriately with AC connection. If charging resumes and subsequent verification is satisfactory, troubleshooting can stop.

6. Inspect Accessible Battery Installation

If battery access and replacement are approved for Clinical Engineering, verify the battery is correctly installed and that accessible connectors are fully seated.

Look for swelling, leakage, corrosion, heat damage, or other visible deterioration.

Do not continue using a physically damaged battery.

Expected outcome: The battery is correctly installed with no visible damage. Any damaged battery is removed from service and replaced according to approved procedures.

7. Evaluate Backup Operation Safely

After adequate charging, perform the approved battery-function or backup-power test with the machine off patient and prepared for controlled evaluation.

Observe whether the machine transitions to battery operation normally and remains stable during the permitted test.

Expected outcome: The Carestation 750 transitions to battery operation and maintains stable operation during the approved verification. If it does, troubleshooting can stop after reconnecting AC and completing checkout.

8. Substitute an Approved Known-Good Battery When Appropriate

If battery replacement is an authorized Clinical Engineering action, install a compatible known-good or approved replacement battery.

Repeat charging and backup-power verification.

Expected outcome: Normal operation with a known-good battery identifies the original battery as the cause. If charging remains abnormal, escalate the machine.

9. Complete Final Return-to-Service Testing

Reconnect AC and verify:

  • AC operation
  • Charging indication
  • Battery transition
  • Relevant alarms
  • Normal startup
  • Required system checkout

Expected outcome: Both mains and backup-power operation are reliable. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Persistent Charging or Runtime Problems

If a known-good outlet, power cord, and approved battery do not restore normal charging or backup operation, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The Carestation 750 is removed from service for service-level evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining categories may include the internal charging system, power-supply circuitry, battery monitoring, internal connections, or other service-level power-management faults.

The Carestation 750 should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved electrical test equipment
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify AC operation, battery charging, backup transition, relevant alarms, and complete return-to-service checkout.

Knowing when backup power is unreliable and removing the machine from service is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A battery indicator alone does not prove adequate backup capability; verify backup operation off patient using the approved test method.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"OR staff reported that the Carestation 750 showed a low battery and lost backup power shortly after AC was disconnected."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering confirmed that the installed battery no longer maintained acceptable operation during the approved backup-power test."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The battery was replaced with an approved unit, charging and backup operation were verified, and the machine passed final checkout."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Battery status displayed
  • AC indicator status
  • Outlet tested
  • Power-cord condition
  • Charging behavior
  • Storage or extended unplugged history
  • Battery physical condition
  • Known-good battery result
  • Backup-power test result
  • Relevant alarms
  • Final checkout status
  • Final device disposition

Final Thought

Battery complaints should begin with the AC source and charging conditions before the battery or internal charger is condemned. Verify safe backup operation off patient, escalate persistent power-management problems, and document both the cause and final power testing.

That is successful troubleshooting.
