Carestream DRX-Rise

Touchscreen, Console Control, or Barcode Reader Is Unresponsive

Carestream

Model

DRX-Rise

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot unresponsive user controls, touchscreen input, console functions, barcode reader operation, loose connections, contamination, and software lockup.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Preserve the Examination Workflow

Do not continue an examination if an unresponsive control prevents correct patient selection, technique setup, exposure control, or image handling. Use another verified system when necessary. Expected outcome: Patient identification and imaging controls remain reliable.

2. Confirm Which Control Is Unresponsive

Determine whether the entire touchscreen is frozen, one screen area is affected, a physical console control does not respond, or only the barcode reader has failed. Expected outcome: The failure is isolated to a specific input device or the entire user interface.

3. Inspect for Contamination or Physical Damage

Check the touchscreen, buttons, scanner window, cables, and accessible control surfaces for fluid, residue, cracked glass, impact damage, stuck controls, or contamination. Expected outcome: Controls are clean, intact, and unobstructed.

4. Verify Normal System Operation

Determine whether the rest of the DRX-Rise continues to operate normally or whether the entire application appears frozen. Observe system indicators and displayed messages. Expected outcome: The problem is distinguished as a local input failure or broader system lockup.

5. Check Accessible Barcode Reader Connections

If the barcode reader is the only failed device, inspect its accessible cable or connection and reseat it if this is part of normal approved service access. Expected outcome: The reader reconnects and resumes normal scanning. If successful, verify several scans and stop.

6. Test With a Known-Good Barcode

Use a clean, valid barcode known to scan correctly. Check scanner-window cleanliness, orientation, and normal scanning distance. Expected outcome: The reader detects a valid barcode consistently.

7. Perform a Normal System Restart if Appropriate

If the interface is frozen and patient images are safely stored, perform the normal approved shutdown and restart process. Avoid forced power removal unless required by approved service guidance. Expected outcome: User controls return to normal operation and remain responsive.

8. Verify Touchscreen and Console Functions

Test normal navigation across several screen areas and required console controls. Do not enter restricted service menus or recalibrate controls without authorization. Expected outcome: Inputs respond correctly and consistently.

9. Perform Final Workflow Verification

Confirm patient/exam selection, barcode entry if used, control response, acquisition preparation, and image handling without a patient exposure unless an approved test is required. Expected outcome: All affected controls function normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Recurrent or Unresolved Interface Problems

If touchscreen, console, or barcode-reader failures recur after basic external checks and restart, remove the system from clinical use when the failure could affect identification or imaging control. Expected outcome: Unreliable controls are not returned to patient use.

If the Problem Persists

External contamination, connections, barcode quality, and normal restart have been ruled out. Remaining causes may include touchscreen hardware, control electronics, scanner hardware, software, interface drivers, internal communication, or configuration.

Remove the DRX-Rise from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation as appropriate. Use approved Carestream documentation and qualified service personnel.

Before return to service, verify all affected controls through a complete normal workflow.

Knowing when repeated interface failure requires escalation is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

An unreliable barcode reader or touchscreen can become a patient-identification risk, not just a convenience issue.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Radiology reported that the DRX-Rise barcode reader would not scan patient wristband barcodes."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found dried residue on the scanner window that prevented consistent barcode recognition."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the scanner window using the approved cleaning method and verified repeated successful scans with known-good barcodes."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Control or device affected
  • Whether the entire interface froze
  • Physical damage or contamination
  • Barcode type tested
  • Cable or connection condition
  • Restart result
  • Touchscreen areas tested
  • Whether patient data were preserved
  • Reproduction after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Treat input failures as workflow and identification risks, check contamination and external connections first, verify normal operation after restart or correction, and escalate recurring failures.

That is successful troubleshooting.
