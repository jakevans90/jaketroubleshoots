Cooling, Fan, or Overtemperature Fault Occurs

Asset Type

Mobile X-Ray System

Manufacturer

Samsung Healthcare

Model

GM85 Fit

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses cooling or temperature faults caused by blocked airflow, environmental conditions, contamination, fan obstruction, heavy use, or internal cooling problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Stop Continued Operation

If the GM85 Fit reports an overtemperature condition, emits unusual heat or odor, or shows signs of cooling failure, stop clinical use.

Move required imaging to another verified system.

Expected outcome: The system is no longer being operated under a potentially damaging or unsafe thermal condition.

2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Determine whether the complaint involves a temperature warning, fan noise, fan not running, unexpected shutdown, hot enclosure, or repeated fault during heavy use.

Record the exact displayed message if present.

Expected outcome: The thermal symptom and operating condition are clearly defined.

3. Inspect Ventilation Openings

Inspect externally accessible air inlets and outlets for dust buildup, coverings, bedding, wall contact, or other obstructions.

Do not insert tools into ventilation openings or remove internal covers.

Expected outcome: Airflow paths are externally clear. If removing an external blockage resolves the condition, continue with controlled verification.

4. Check the Operating Environment

Confirm the unit is not positioned against heat-producing equipment, in direct sunlight, against a wall that blocks ventilation, or in an unusually hot enclosed area.

Expected outcome: The system is operating in a normal clinical environment with unrestricted external ventilation.

5. Inspect for Excessive Dust or Contamination

Check accessible vent surfaces for accumulated lint, dust, or debris. Clean only externally and using approved methods.

Expected outcome: External vent surfaces are clean and unobstructed.

6. Listen for Abnormal Fan Behavior

During a safe controlled startup, listen for grinding, rubbing, repeated cycling, or absence of expected airflow.

Do not operate the system extensively merely to determine whether the fault will recur.

Expected outcome: Cooling operation sounds and airflow appear normal. Abnormal fan behavior requires service evaluation.

7. Allow Appropriate Cooling Before Retest

If the condition followed prolonged use and no damage is present, allow the system to return to a normal temperature before performing a controlled functional check.

Do not apply external forced cooling methods not approved for the equipment.

Expected outcome: The system restarts without a thermal warning and remains stable under limited verification. Recurring faults require escalation.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm the system completes startup, remains responsive, provides normal cooling indications, and does not reproduce the fault during appropriate functional testing.

Expected outcome: The thermal condition does not recur and the system remains stable. If cooling behavior remains questionable, do not return it to service.

If the Problem Persists

If external airflow, contamination, environmental conditions, and normal cooling time have been addressed but the fault returns, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible remaining categories include internal fans, temperature sensing, airflow monitoring, power electronics, generator cooling, internal contamination, or control-system faults.

The system should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Samsung Healthcare documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel.

Do not bypass temperature protection or continue operation of equipment with abnormal heat, odor, or fan behavior. Complete full functional verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Keep ventilation openings clear when positioning the mobile X-ray unit beside beds, walls, curtains, or other equipment.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Radiology staff reported that the GM85 Fit displayed an overtemperature warning during bedside use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an external ventilation opening obstructed by material stored against the system."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering cleared the obstruction, allowed the system to cool, and verified stable operation without recurrence before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact thermal or fan message
  • Whether shutdown occurred
  • Location where fault occurred
  • Ventilation obstruction
  • Dust or contamination observed
  • Unusual fan sound
  • Heat or odor
  • Duration or intensity of prior use
  • Result after cooling
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Cooling faults require immediate attention because continued operation can damage equipment or interrupt imaging; external airflow and environmental causes should be ruled out first, followed by safe escalation when thermal stability cannot be proven.

That is successful troubleshooting.
