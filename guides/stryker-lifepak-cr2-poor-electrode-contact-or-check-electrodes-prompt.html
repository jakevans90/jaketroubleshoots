Stryker LIFEPAK CR2

Poor Electrode Contact or Check Electrodes Prompt

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

LIFEPAK CR2

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting repeated electrode-contact prompts caused by poor skin contact, patient preparation, pad condition, tray seating, or a persistent device-side connection fault.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not delay resuscitation for extended equipment troubleshooting.

During an active cardiac arrest response:

  • Continue CPR according to the emergency response protocol.
  • Follow the LIFEPAK CR2 voice prompts.
  • Correct only immediately visible pad-contact or connection issues.
  • Obtain another verified AED or defibrillator without delay if the CR2 cannot establish dependable electrode contact or deliver required therapy.

Expected: Resuscitation continues without being dependent on prolonged troubleshooting of a malfunctioning AED.

Why it matters: The LIFEPAK CR2 operating instructions identify poor pad adhesion and inadequate electrode-tray connection as causes of electrode-related prompts. The device manufacturer also directs responders to continue CPR when required shock delivery is not possible.

If the immediate clinical situation has been transferred to another functional device, continue evaluation only in a controlled Clinical Engineering setting.

2. Confirm the Exact Prompt and Failure Behavior

Determine what Clinical Engineering or staff actually observed.

Document whether the device announced:

  • “CHECK PADS FOR GOOD CONTACT TO BARE SKIN”
  • “CHECK ELECTRODE TRAY CONNECTION”
  • An intermittent contact-related prompt
  • Failure to progress normally after pad application
  • Loss of contact only when the patient or cable moved

Also determine:

  • Whether both pads were fully applied
  • Whether the prompt began immediately or during use
  • Whether the electrode tray had already been opened
  • Whether the pads were expired, damaged, or previously used

Expected: The complaint is narrowed to patient-to-pad contact, electrode condition, tray connection, or a persistent device fault.

Why it matters: Stryker’s published CR2 troubleshooting table specifically links these prompts to improper pad adhesion or inadequate connection of the electrode tray to the defibrillator.

3. Check Patient Skin Preparation During the Reported Event

For an active event, correct only what can be done immediately without delaying CPR or defibrillation.

Verify whether the pad locations were:

  • Bare
  • Clean
  • Dry
  • Free of excessive hair that prevented adhesion
  • Free of clothing, dressings, medication patches, or other material beneath the pad

If excessive hair prevents adhesion and a razor is immediately available, quickly clear the required area. Dry or clean the skin as necessary and firmly reapply the pads.

Expected: Each pad lies flat and adheres completely to prepared bare skin.

Why it matters: The LIFEPAK CR2 instructions direct users to clean, shave, and dry the skin when necessary and firmly press the pads onto the patient.

If the prompt clears, the problem was likely inadequate patient-to-electrode contact. Stop troubleshooting and continue the clinical response.

4. Inspect Pad Adhesion Across the Entire Surface

Check both electrodes for:

  • Lifted edges
  • Wrinkles
  • Air pockets
  • Partial contact
  • Folding
  • Contamination
  • Loss of adhesion caused by moisture, hair, or body contour

Press firmly across the entire pad surface without disturbing correct placement.

Expected: Both electrodes remain fully adhered with no obvious gaps or lifted areas.

Why it matters: Stryker warns that incomplete adhesion and air pockets can contribute to ineffective energy delivery and skin burns.

If full adhesion clears the prompt, stop troubleshooting.

5. Verify Correct Pad Placement

Confirm the pads are positioned according to the illustrations printed on the LIFEPAK CR2 electrodes.

Check that:

  • Adult placement matches the pad graphics
  • Pads do not overlap or touch each other
  • Pads are not directly over an implanted pacemaker or ICD
  • Pediatric placement is appropriate for the patient’s size and prevents pad-to-pad contact

Expected: Electrode placement is appropriate and the pads remain separated with full skin contact.

Stryker’s instructions state that pads should be applied as illustrated and pressed firmly to achieve complete adhesion; for small children, the pads should be positioned so they do not touch.

If correcting placement resolves the prompt, stop troubleshooting.

6. Check Electrode Tray Seating

Once the device is off-patient and available for controlled evaluation, inspect the QUIK-STEP electrode tray externally.

Verify that:

  • The tray is fully inserted
  • The front corners are seated
  • The tray is not visibly raised or partially disengaged
  • No obvious debris or physical obstruction prevents seating
  • The tray appears mechanically secure

Do not force a damaged tray or connector area.

Expected: The electrode tray is fully and evenly seated in the defibrillator.

Why it matters: Stryker identifies incomplete electrode-tray insertion as a cause of the CR2 electrode-tray connection prompt.

If proper seating resolves the issue and subsequent approved readiness checks pass, stop troubleshooting.

7. Inspect Electrode Tray Condition and Use-By Date

Check the installed tray for:

  • Broken seal
  • Evidence it was previously opened
  • Prior use
  • Passed Use By date
  • Visible tray damage
  • Evidence of dried or compromised electrodes

The CR2 electrode tray should be replaced if the seal is broken, the electrodes have been used, or the Use By date has passed.

Expected: The installed tray is sealed, unused, undamaged, and within date.

If the tray is compromised, replace it with the correct compatible electrode tray according to approved facility and manufacturer procedures, then verify readiness. Stop if the issue is resolved.

8. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Electrode Tray When Authorized

If the original tray is suspect and facility procedures permit, install a known-good, in-date, sealed, compatible LIFEPAK CR2 electrode tray.

Inspect the replacement before installation:

  • Seal intact
  • Within Use By date
  • No visible damage

After installation, confirm that the tray is fully seated.

Expected: The device recognizes the known-good electrode tray and no connection-related abnormal condition remains.

The manufacturer’s instructions describe the QUIK-STEP electrode assembly as a replaceable tray and require inspection of the seal and Use By date before installation.

If a known-good tray resolves the issue, document the original electrode tray as the probable cause and stop troubleshooting.

9. Verify Device Readiness

After restoring the AED to its normal ready configuration:

  • Close the lid
  • Observe the Readiness indicator
  • Confirm expected ready behavior
  • If readiness is abnormal, use only the manufacturer-approved readiness-status procedure and follow the resulting prompts

Stryker states that after electrode replacement and lid closure, the Readiness indicator should flash every six seconds; if it does not, the device provides a button-based status check to identify whether it is ready.

Expected: The AED returns to documented ready status with a correctly installed electrode tray.

If readiness is normal and the complaint is resolved, return the device to service only after completing required facility inspection and documentation.

10. Stop if the Prompt Persists With a Known-Good Tray

If the CR2 continues to report electrode-contact or electrode-tray connection problems after:

  • External condition checks
  • Correct tray seating
  • Inspection for damage
  • Use of a verified compatible replacement tray
  • Approved readiness verification

do not proceed into internal connector, circuit, impedance-sensing, or board-level repair unless specifically authorized and trained under the applicable service program.

Expected: A persistent problem is treated as a probable internal or device-side fault rather than repeatedly attributed to external electrodes.

If unresolved, stop troubleshooting and escalate.

If the Problem Persists

If the prompt continues after patient-contact causes, electrode condition, tray seating, and a known-good compatible tray have been ruled out, the remaining problem is likely internal or related to the device-side electrode interface.

The LIFEPAK CR2 should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Replaced with another verified AED
  • Sent for qualified repair or bench evaluation

Do not return a defibrillator to standby readiness when electrode recognition or therapy-contact reliability remains uncertain.

Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never perform extended troubleshooting on an AED during an active cardiac arrest.

Correct immediately visible pad-contact issues, continue CPR, follow device prompts, and bring another verified defibrillator into service if dependable therapy cannot be established. Full troubleshooting belongs off-patient in a controlled Clinical Engineering environment.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported the LIFEPAK CR2 repeatedly announced a poor pad-contact or electrode-tray connection prompt during use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Inspection found the QUIK-STEP electrode tray was not fully seated in the defibrillator, causing intermittent electrode connection recognition."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reseated the electrode tray, verified secure installation and normal device readiness indication, completed required functional checks, and returned the AED to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact voice prompt documented
  • Patient skin clean and dry
  • Excessive hair present or absent
  • Pad adhesion condition
  • Pad placement verified
  • Electrode tray fully seated
  • Tray seal intact or broken
  • Electrode Use By date checked
  • Known-good tray substituted
  • Readiness indicator behavior
  • Issue intermittent or constant
  • Visible connector or tray damage
  • Final device status
  • Removed from service or returned to service

Final Thought

Poor electrode contact should be approached logically: protect the patient first, verify skin preparation and pad adhesion, inspect the electrode tray, confirm seating, and substitute a known-good tray when appropriate. A persistent contact or recognition fault on a defibrillator warrants removal from service and qualified escalation. Clear CCR documentation preserves both patient-safety information and the technical reasoning behind the final disposition.

That is successful troubleshooting.
