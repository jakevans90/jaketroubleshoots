ZOLL AED Plus

Device Will Not Power On

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

ZOLL

Model

AED Plus

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting an AED Plus that remains blank or unresponsive due to battery, installation, connection, accessory, environmental, or external damage issues.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot a nonfunctional AED while it is the only defibrillator available for an active emergency.

If the failure occurs during patient care:

  • Begin or continue CPR according to facility protocol.
  • Obtain another verified AED or manual defibrillator immediately.
  • Notify the responding clinical team that the AED Plus is unavailable.
  • Do not delay resuscitation while attempting repeated power cycles or battery replacement.

Expected outcome: Patient care continues using another functional defibrillator.

Continue Clinical Engineering troubleshooting only after the AED Plus has been removed from emergency use.

2. Confirm the Reported Power Condition

Press the power button once and closely observe the device.

Check for:

  • Display activity
  • Status-indicator changes
  • Audible tones or voice prompts
  • Brief illumination followed by shutdown
  • Clicking, repeated restarting, or other sounds
  • Any displayed error message

Expected outcome: The exact failure is identified as complete loss of power, intermittent startup, or startup followed by shutdown.

If the device powers on normally, verify readiness and complete an operational check before returning it to service.

3. Inspect the Exterior for Damage or Contamination

Examine the AED Plus for:

  • Cracks, impact damage, or enclosure separation
  • Liquid intrusion or dried residue
  • Corrosion around the battery compartment
  • Unusual odor, heat, swelling, or discoloration
  • A damaged or stuck power button
  • Evidence that the unit was dropped or improperly stored

Do not continue powering the device if there is evidence of liquid intrusion, overheating, burning odor, or battery leakage.

Expected outcome: No external condition is found that makes continued testing unsafe.

If significant damage, liquid intrusion, heat, or odor is present, remove the unit from service and send it for bench evaluation.

4. Check the Status Indicator

Observe the readiness indicator before opening the battery compartment.

Determine whether the indicator shows:

  • A ready symbol
  • A non-ready condition
  • No visible indication
  • An intermittent or abnormal appearance

A non-ready or blank indicator may support a battery, installation, self-test, or internal power concern.

Expected outcome: The readiness condition is documented for the work order.

Do not return the AED to service solely because the indicator appears normal if the unit will not power on.

5. Inspect the Battery Compartment

Open the battery compartment according to facility procedures and inspect the installed batteries.

Check for:

  • Missing batteries
  • Incorrect battery type
  • Mixed battery brands or ages
  • Batteries installed with reversed polarity
  • Loose or partially seated batteries
  • Corroded, bent, contaminated, or recessed contacts
  • Battery leakage or swelling
  • A damaged battery-compartment cover

The AED Plus uses multiple individual batteries that must be installed correctly as a complete set. A single reversed, weak, missing, or poorly seated battery may prevent startup.

Expected outcome: All batteries are present, compatible, correctly oriented, and securely installed.

If installation was incorrect, correct it and retest the device. If the AED powers on and completes its self-test successfully, document the correction and stop.

6. Replace the Batteries as a Complete Matched Set

If battery condition is unknown, expired, depleted, mixed, or questionable, replace all batteries with a complete matched set of approved batteries.

Do not:

  • Replace only one or two batteries
  • Mix new and used batteries
  • Mix brands, chemistries, or expiration dates
  • Reuse leaking, corroded, or physically damaged batteries

Install each battery using the polarity markings in the compartment.

Expected outcome: The AED receives stable power from a complete, properly installed battery set.

After installation, power on the unit and allow it to complete its startup and self-test sequence.

If the device powers on normally and passes its checks, proceed with functional verification and stop.

7. Reseat the Battery Set

If the batteries appear correct but the AED remains unresponsive:

  • Remove the complete battery set.
  • Inspect the battery contacts using adequate lighting.
  • Clean only accessible external contamination using approved procedures.
  • Reinstall the full battery set carefully.
  • Confirm every battery is fully seated.
  • Secure the battery-compartment cover.
  • Attempt one controlled power-on test.

Expected outcome: Any poor battery seating or external contact issue is corrected.

If the device powers on, complete the required readiness and functional checks before return to service.

8. Check the Power Button Operation

Press and release the power button while observing its mechanical movement.

Confirm that the button:

  • Moves normally
  • Is not jammed, recessed, cracked, or unusually loose
  • Is not obstructed by dirt, adhesive, or enclosure damage
  • Produces consistent mechanical feedback

Do not pry, disassemble, or apply excessive force to the control.

Expected outcome: The external power control operates mechanically without visible damage.

A damaged or non-moving button requires repair evaluation.

9. Disconnect Nonessential External Accessories

Inspect the electrode cable and any externally connected accessories for damage, contamination, or improper seating.

When safe and permitted by facility procedures, disconnect questionable external accessories and repeat one startup attempt.

Do not repeatedly connect and disconnect accessories or perform testing that could damage the connector.

Expected outcome: An externally damaged or improperly connected accessory is ruled out as a contributing factor.

If the AED powers on after removing a damaged accessory, replace the accessory and complete operational testing.

10. Consider Environmental Conditions

Confirm the AED has not recently been exposed to:

  • Extreme cold or heat
  • Condensation
  • High humidity
  • Water
  • Direct sunlight inside a vehicle
  • Storage outside the manufacturer’s operating conditions

Allow the device to stabilize in a controlled indoor environment before testing when temperature or condensation may be involved.

Do not apply external heat or attempt to dry internal components by opening the enclosure.

Expected outcome: Temporary environmental conditions are ruled out.

If condensation or liquid exposure is suspected, remove the device from service for inspection.

11. Perform a Controlled Startup Verification

With a verified complete battery set installed:

  • Confirm the enclosure and battery compartment are secure.
  • Press the power button once.
  • Observe the display, indicator, speaker, and startup sequence.
  • Allow the AED to complete its self-test.
  • Record any prompt, warning, or error message.

Expected outcome: The AED powers on, completes its startup checks, and indicates readiness.

If the device starts but shuts down, restarts, freezes, or reports a fault, do not return it to service.

If the Problem Persists

If the ZOLL AED Plus remains unresponsive after verifying the complete battery set, polarity, battery seating, contacts, power button, accessories, environmental conditions, and visible damage, common external causes have been ruled out.

The problem is likely associated with an internal power circuit, control assembly, battery-contact assembly, display, or other internal component.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Replaced with another verified AED
  • Sent for authorized repair or Clinical Engineering bench evaluation
  • Evaluated according to facility policy and manufacturer service documentation

Do not open the enclosure or attempt board-level repair unless specifically authorized, trained, and equipped to perform manufacturer-approved service.

Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot an AED that is actively needed for a patient. Move emergency coverage to another verified defibrillator first, then evaluate the failed AED in a safe, controlled area.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the ZOLL AED Plus display remained blank and the unit did not respond when the power button was pressed."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Inspection found a mixed set of depleted batteries, including one battery installed with reversed polarity."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the mismatched batteries, installed a complete approved matched battery set, verified correct polarity, and confirmed successful startup, self-test, audio prompts, display operation, and ready status."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Patient or emergency-response involvement
  • Status-indicator condition
  • Display, tone, or voice-prompt activity
  • Battery manufacturer, type, date, and condition
  • Whether batteries were mixed or reversed
  • Battery contacts inspected
  • Evidence of corrosion or leakage
  • Power button condition
  • Electrode cable condition
  • Liquid, impact, heat, or odor observed
  • Exact startup message or error
  • Final device status
  • Replacement AED provided
  • Repair or bench-evaluation destination

Final Thought

A ZOLL AED Plus that will not power on must be treated as unavailable for emergency use. Begin with patient safety, then logically verify the batteries, contacts, controls, accessories, environment, and visible condition. When external causes have been ruled out, remove the device from service and escalate it for repair. Clear CCR documentation preserves the findings, corrective action, and final readiness status.

That is successful troubleshooting.
