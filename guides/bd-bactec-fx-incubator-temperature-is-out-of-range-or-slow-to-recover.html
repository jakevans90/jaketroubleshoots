Incubator Temperature Is Out of Range or Slow to Recover

Asset Type

Blood Culture System

Manufacturer

BD

Model

BACTEC FX

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot abnormal or slowly recovering incubation temperature caused by door access, airflow, room conditions, power, loading, ventilation, or service-level temperature-control problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Specimen Testing

Treat an unreliable incubation-temperature condition as a potential testing-quality issue. Follow laboratory procedure for specimen handling and transfer work to another verified system when required.

Do not continue relying on an analyzer whose incubation environment cannot be confirmed stable.

Expected outcome: Specimen testing continuity is maintained and questionable incubation conditions are not used without appropriate laboratory evaluation.

2. Confirm the Temperature Complaint

Determine whether the system:

  • Reports temperature outside its normal operating condition.
  • Recovers slowly after drawer or door access.
  • Fluctuates continuously.
  • Shows the problem only after heavy bottle loading.
  • Developed the issue after a power interruption, relocation, cleaning, or HVAC change.

Record the displayed condition and time of occurrence without assuming the temperature sensor is defective.

Expected outcome: The temperature problem is characterized as persistent, intermittent, or related to a specific event.

3. Check AC Power and Recent Power Events

Verify that the instrument is receiving stable facility power and that:

  • The power cord is secure.
  • The receptacle is not loose or damaged.
  • No recent breaker trip or prolonged outage occurred.
  • The instrument has completed normal recovery from any authorized restart.

Do not repeatedly power-cycle the analyzer as a troubleshooting technique.

Expected outcome: Stable AC power is available and no unresolved power interruption explains the temperature condition. If temperature recovers normally after a known temporary power event, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Check Access Panels, Doors, and Drawers

Verify all user-accessible doors, drawers, and panels are fully closed and seated.

Look for:

  • A drawer left partially open.
  • An obstruction preventing closure.
  • A misloaded bottle or rack holding an access point open.
  • Damaged external seals that are visibly preventing proper closure.

Expected outcome: The incubation enclosure is fully closed without an obvious air leak or obstruction. If proper closure restores temperature recovery, troubleshooting can stop after stabilization and verification.

5. Review Recent Loading Activity

Determine whether a large number of room-temperature bottles were loaded recently or whether drawers were repeatedly opened.

Heavy loading or repeated access can temporarily influence recovery time without proving a hardware failure.

Observe whether the system trends back toward normal operation once access is minimized.

Expected outcome: Temperature moves steadily toward normal after routine loading activity stops. If recovery completes normally and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop.

6. Inspect External Ventilation

Inspect accessible intake and exhaust areas for:

  • Blocked vents.
  • Dust accumulation.
  • Items stored against the instrument.
  • Equipment positioned too close to ventilation openings.
  • Paper, packaging, or covers restricting airflow.

Do not open internal temperature-control assemblies for routine troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: External airflow paths are clear. If clearing an external ventilation restriction restores stable temperature, proceed to final verification.

7. Evaluate Room Environment

Check whether the room has experienced:

  • HVAC failure.
  • Excessive room heat.
  • Direct sunlight.
  • Nearby heat-producing equipment.
  • Recent relocation of the analyzer.
  • Airflow from a supply vent or other environmental change.

Compare the environment with nearby laboratory equipment if appropriate.

Expected outcome: The analyzer is operating in a stable laboratory environment without an obvious external thermal influence.

8. Observe Temperature Stability

After external causes have been addressed, allow the instrument to operate without unnecessary drawer access and observe the displayed temperature condition.

Do not attempt unauthorized temperature calibration or adjustment.

Expected outcome: Temperature returns to normal operation and remains stable. If it does, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

9. Compare Displayed Condition With Approved Verification Methods

If manufacturer documentation and approved equipment allow Clinical Engineering to perform an independent temperature verification, compare the instrument condition with that approved measurement.

Do not use an unvalidated handheld reading as the sole basis for calibration or adjustment.

Expected outcome: Independent verification agrees with normal system operation or confirms that further service evaluation is required.

10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm that:

  • The analyzer reaches and maintains its normal operating temperature condition.
  • Drawers or access points close normally.
  • External ventilation remains clear.
  • No temperature-related warning reappears during normal operation.
  • Laboratory staff can resume use according to facility procedure.

Expected outcome: Stable incubation operation is verified. Troubleshooting can stop and the system can be returned to service.

11. Escalate Persistent Temperature Problems

If temperature remains abnormal, repeatedly drifts, or consistently recovers poorly after external causes have been ruled out, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: A potentially significant incubation-control problem is escalated before unreliable specimen testing occurs.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as open access points, heavy loading, environmental heat, airflow restriction, and unstable power have been ruled out.

The remaining condition may involve an internal temperature sensor, heater, circulation component, temperature-control circuit, calibration issue, or other service-level fault.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service when incubation conditions cannot be verified.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation as appropriate.
  • Evaluated using current manufacturer documentation and approved temperature test equipment.
  • Calibrated, configured, or repaired only by qualified personnel.

Return to service only after stable incubation temperature and normal system operation have been independently verified according to applicable service procedures.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting rather than adjusting an unverified temperature-control system is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not allow potentially affected blood culture bottles to remain dependent on an incubation system whose temperature stability cannot be confirmed.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Laboratory staff reported that the BACTEC FX incubator temperature recovered slowly after routine bottle loading."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found laboratory supplies stored against an external ventilation opening, restricting airflow."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleared the ventilation area and verified the analyzer returned to stable operating temperature without further temperature warnings."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Displayed temperature condition or warning
  • Time and duration of the event
  • Recent power interruption
  • Drawer or door position
  • Recent bottle loading volume
  • External ventilation condition
  • Room HVAC condition
  • Nearby heat source
  • Independent approved verification result
  • Temperature behavior after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect specimen integrity first, then evaluate power, access points, loading, ventilation, and room environment before suspecting internal temperature control. Verify stable operation, escalate unresolved conditions, and clearly document the findings and final disposition.

That is successful troubleshooting.
