Olympus UC1800

Excessive Noise, Vibration, or Cavitation Instability

Asset Type

Ultrasonic Cleaner

Manufacturer

Olympus

Model

UC1800

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot abnormal sound or unstable ultrasonic action caused by loading, tank level, accessories, positioning, solution condition, or external contact.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Remove the Cleaner From Active Processing if Performance Is Uncertain

If abnormal noise or vibration raises doubt about cleaning effectiveness, stop using the cleaner for instrument processing until evaluated.

Immediately stop testing if there is:

  • Violent vibration
  • Metal-on-metal impact
  • Burning odor
  • Visible tank damage
  • Leakage
  • Electrical arcing or smoke

Expected outcome: The unit is isolated from clinical processing and safe to evaluate.

2. Confirm the Exact Noise or Vibration

Determine whether the complaint is:

  • New buzzing
  • Rattling
  • Intermittent vibration
  • Uneven cavitation
  • Noise only with a loaded basket
  • Noise only during certain cycles

Reproduce the condition under controlled test conditions.

Expected outcome: The abnormal behavior is clearly characterized.

3. Verify Tank Fill and Solution Condition

Check for:

  • Low solution level
  • Excessive fill
  • Heavy contamination
  • Excessive foam
  • Recently changed solution that may contain trapped air

Correct obvious solution conditions and perform normal degassing when appropriate.

Expected outcome: The tank is filled correctly with suitable solution and excessive trapped air is minimized.

4. Remove the Load and Inspect Accessories

Test whether the abnormal noise remains when removable baskets or accessories are removed, when permitted.

Inspect for:

  • Bent baskets
  • Loose accessory components
  • Instruments contacting the tank
  • Basket feet or supports out of position
  • Foreign objects in the tank

If the noise disappears after correcting accessory positioning, retest with a normal representative load.

Expected outcome: Accessories and instruments are not producing mechanical rattling or abnormal tank contact.

5. Verify Unit Positioning

Confirm the cleaner is:

  • Stable
  • Level enough for normal operation
  • Not touching adjacent equipment or cabinetry
  • Not resting on loose objects
  • Not transmitting vibration into a countertop or mounting surface

Expected outcome: External structural contact is eliminated as the source of noise.

6. Observe Ultrasonic Operation

With appropriate solution and configuration, observe whether ultrasonic activity appears consistent throughout the test cycle.

Do not place hands in an operating ultrasonic tank or use unsafe improvised cavitation tests.

Use only approved verification methods.

Expected outcome: Ultrasonic action remains stable without abnormal interruption or excessive mechanical vibration.

7. Compare Loaded and Unloaded Operation

If safe and applicable, compare operation:

  • Without a basket
  • With the normal basket
  • With a representative approved load

This can distinguish load-related resonance or contact from a cleaner-level fault.

If the abnormal condition cannot be reproduced after correcting load or accessory placement, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

Expected outcome: Operation is consistent under normal loading conditions.

8. Complete Final Functional Verification

Run the unit through applicable normal functions and confirm there is no recurrent abnormal noise, vibration, leakage, or control instability.

Expected outcome: The cleaner performs consistently and is suitable for return to service.

If the Problem Persists

If tank level, solution condition, degassing, accessories, loading, and external positioning have been ruled out, persistent abnormal vibration may involve the ultrasonic transducer system, generator, tank bonding, mounting, or another internal service-level condition.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel

Do not access or energize internal ultrasonic components outside approved service procedures. Cleaning performance must be verified before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Unusual sound can indicate ineffective ultrasonic energy even when the timer is running, so do not assume the cleaning process remains valid.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported loud rattling and unstable vibration whenever the UC1800 cleaning cycle was started."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the instrument basket positioned incorrectly and contacting the side of the tank during ultrasonic operation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The basket was repositioned correctly, the cleaner was tested with and without a representative load, and normal sound and stable operation were verified."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Description of abnormal sound
  • Whether issue occurs loaded or unloaded
  • Tank level
  • Solution condition
  • Degas status
  • Basket condition
  • Accessories tested
  • Unit position and countertop contact
  • Presence of leakage or damage
  • Final operational result

Final Thought

Abnormal ultrasonic behavior should be approached logically by checking solution conditions, loading, baskets, and external vibration paths before suspecting internal transducers or generator components. Verify stable performance before returning the cleaner to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
