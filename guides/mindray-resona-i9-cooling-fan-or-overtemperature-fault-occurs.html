Cooling, Fan, or Overtemperature Fault Occurs

Asset Type

Ultrasound System

Manufacturer

Mindray

Model

Resona I9

What This Guide Helps With

Use for overheating warnings, abnormal fan behavior, excessive heat, blocked ventilation, or shutdown suspected to be related to cooling.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Stop Unsafe Operation

If the system displays an overtemperature warning, produces unusual heat or odor, or repeatedly shuts down, stop clinical use. Move the examination to another verified ultrasound system.

Expected outcome: The patient and staff are no longer dependent on potentially overheating equipment.

2. Confirm the Reported Cooling Condition

Determine whether staff observed a warning, unexpected shutdown, unusually loud fan, absent expected airflow, excessive enclosure heat, or unusual odor. Record the timing and operating conditions.

Expected outcome: The exact cooling-related symptom is identified without assuming a fan or internal component has failed.

3. Inspect External Ventilation Areas

Check accessible ventilation openings for obstruction by dust accumulation, drapes, paper, covers, carts, walls, or stored supplies. Do not open the enclosure or insert tools into ventilation openings.

Expected outcome: External airflow paths are unobstructed. If an external obstruction caused the condition, correct it and allow the system to return to normal operating temperature before verification.

4. Check Equipment Placement

Verify the system is positioned so normal ventilation is not blocked and that it has not been pushed tightly against surrounding equipment or structures.

Expected outcome: The system has an unobstructed external airflow path.

5. Evaluate the Room Environment

Determine whether the room is unusually hot or whether the system is located near a heat source. Compare with normal facility environmental conditions and involve Facilities if the room itself is abnormal.

Expected outcome: The operating environment is appropriate or an external HVAC/environmental problem is identified.

6. Inspect for Contamination or Fluid Exposure

Check external surfaces and ventilation areas for evidence of fluid intrusion, heavy dust, or contamination. If fluid entry is suspected, do not power the system for further testing.

Expected outcome: No condition requiring immediate removal from service is present.

7. Restart Only After the Condition Is Safe

If the system has cooled, ventilation is clear, and there is no evidence of damage, odor, or fluid intrusion, perform one controlled startup and observe fan noise, airflow, displayed warnings, and system stability.

Expected outcome: The system operates without recurring temperature warnings or abnormal cooling behavior.

8. Exercise the System Under Normal Test Conditions

Perform basic imaging and allow the system to operate long enough to determine whether the reported condition returns. Do not intentionally obstruct ventilation or artificially stress the system.

Expected outcome: Normal operation continues without overtemperature warnings, abnormal odor, or shutdown.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify startup, live imaging, control response, normal external airflow, and absence of cooling or temperature warnings.

Expected outcome: The system remains stable under normal operation. If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

External ventilation, placement, environmental conditions, contamination, and controlled restart have been evaluated. A recurring cooling or overtemperature problem may involve internal fans, airflow pathways, temperature sensing, power electronics, computing hardware, or another service-level condition.

Remove the system from service, label it Out of Service, and arrange repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Mindray service documentation and approved test equipment. Do not operate the system with defeated temperature protection or attempt internal cooling-system repair without proper authorization.

After repair, verify normal thermal behavior, fan operation, system stability, and basic imaging before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Unusual heat, burning odor, or repeated thermal shutdown is a stop-use condition even if the system restarts after cooling.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported the Resona I9 displayed a temperature-related warning during use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found stored material positioned against an external ventilation area and restricting airflow."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering removed the obstruction, allowed the system to cool, verified stable operation and normal imaging without recurrence, and returned the system to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact warning displayed
  • Whether shutdown occurred
  • Fan sound or airflow observation
  • Ventilation obstruction
  • Equipment placement
  • Room temperature concern
  • Dust or contamination observed
  • Unusual heat or odor
  • Fluid exposure evidence
  • Results after cooling and restart
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Treat overheating as a patient-safety and equipment-protection concern, rule out ventilation and environmental causes first, never bypass thermal protection, verify stable operation after correction, and escalate recurring faults appropriately.

That is successful troubleshooting.
