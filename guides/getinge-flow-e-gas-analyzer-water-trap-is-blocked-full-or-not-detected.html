Getinge Flow-e

Gas Analyzer Water Trap Is Blocked, Full, or Not Detected

Asset Type

Anesthesia Machine

Manufacturer

Getinge

Model

Flow-e

What This Guide Helps With

Gas monitoring is unavailable or unstable because the water trap is full, obstructed, improperly seated, contaminated, damaged, or not detected.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Gas Monitoring

If required respiratory-gas monitoring is unavailable during patient care, provide a verified alternative monitoring method or exchange the anesthesia machine before troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: Clinical monitoring continues independently while the Flow-e is evaluated.

2. Confirm the Exact Analyzer Complaint

Determine whether the water trap is reported as missing, blocked, full, or whether gas measurements simply became unavailable or unstable.

Inspect for related sampling-line conditions.

Expected outcome: The analyzer failure is clearly identified.

3. Inspect the Water Trap

Check the water trap for visible fluid accumulation, contamination, cracks, improper assembly, or damage.

Replace a disposable or serviceable trap as appropriate under approved procedures rather than attempting unauthorized repair.

Expected outcome: A clean, intact, appropriately prepared water trap is available for use.

4. Remove and Reseat the Water Trap

Remove and reinstall the water trap carefully, ensuring it is fully seated in the analyzer interface.

Inspect the accessible mounting area for contamination or obvious obstruction.

Expected outcome: The machine recognizes the trap and sampling resumes. If recognition and stable gas measurements return, proceed to final verification.

5. Inspect the Sampling Line

Check the sample line from patient connection to water trap for kinks, blockage, pooled moisture, loose connections, or physical damage.

Replace a suspect line with an approved compatible line.

Expected outcome: The sampling path is open, dry, secure, and unobstructed.

6. Check Patient-End Sampling Accessories

Inspect adapters, filters, and sampling connectors for secretions, condensation, obstruction, or incorrect installation.

Expected outcome: Gas can be sampled freely from the test circuit without external blockage.

7. Substitute a Known-Good Water Trap

If available, install an approved known-good compatible water trap.

Expected outcome: If analyzer operation returns with the known-good trap, remove the original trap from use and proceed with verification.

8. Verify Sample-Gas Measurement

Connect the machine to an appropriate controlled test setup and verify that CO2, oxygen, and agent channels respond as expected when applicable.

Expected outcome: Gas values are stable and respond logically. If the analyzer remains unable to sample after external components are replaced, escalation is required.

9. Complete Functional Checkout

Run the applicable manufacturer-approved gas-monitoring and anesthesia-machine checkout procedures.

Expected outcome: The water trap remains recognized, gas sampling is stable, and required monitoring channels function normally. The machine may be returned to service after successful verification.

10. Escalate Persistent Water-Trap Detection or Sampling Problems

If known-good traps and sampling lines do not restore operation, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The machine remains out of service pending analyzer or service-level evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External water-trap, sampling-line, connector, moisture, and patient-end blockage causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include an internal sampling pump, detection interface, analyzer pneumatic path, sensor assembly, or other service-level analyzer problem.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service if required monitoring is unavailable.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Getinge documentation and approved gas-analysis test equipment.
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel.

Do not operate clinically without required gas monitoring merely because ventilation appears normal.

Return to service only after gas sampling, gas measurement, alarms, and complete checkout are verified.

Clinical Use Tip

A blocked water trap can remove several respiratory-gas measurements at once, so verify the full sampling path rather than focusing on one displayed parameter.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Anesthesia staff reported that the Flow-e stopped displaying respiratory-gas values and indicated a water-trap problem."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the water trap full of condensate and the sampling line partially occluded."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the water trap and sampling line, verified stable gas measurements, completed checkout, and returned the machine to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact water-trap or analyzer message
  • Amount of visible fluid
  • Trap seating condition
  • Sampling-line condition
  • Patient-end adapter condition
  • Known-good trap substitution
  • Gas channels affected
  • Measurements after correction
  • Checkout result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Maintain patient monitoring first, inspect the complete external sampling path before suspecting the analyzer, verify all gas measurements after correction, and escalate when known-good consumables do not restore reliable operation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
