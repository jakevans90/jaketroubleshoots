Mindray TE X

Wireless Transducer or Charging Station Is Not Detected

Asset Type

Ultrasound System

Manufacturer

Mindray

Model

TE X

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots wireless transducer or charging-station detection problems caused by power, seating, connection, pairing, accessory, or configuration issues.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Imaging Availability

If the wireless transducer is needed for an active procedure, move imaging to another verified transducer or ultrasound system before troubleshooting. Do not troubleshoot unreliable imaging equipment while clinical care depends on it.

Expected outcome: Patient care continues with reliable imaging equipment. If alternate equipment is functioning normally, troubleshooting may proceed safely.

2. Confirm the Exact Detection Problem

Determine whether the TE X does not detect the wireless transducer at all, loses it intermittently, or recognizes the transducer but cannot establish usable imaging. Determine whether the charging station is also missing or only the transducer is affected.

Expected outcome: The failure is narrowed to the transducer, charging station, system interface, or an intermittent condition.

3. Verify System and Charging-Station Power

Confirm the TE X is operating normally and the charging station has its required external power and visible normal-status indications. Inspect the power cord, supply, connectors, and outlet for looseness or damage. Test the outlet when appropriate.

Expected outcome: The charging station remains powered without interruption. If restoring external power corrects detection or charging, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Inspect Transducer and Charging Interfaces

Remove the transducer from the charging station and inspect accessible charging contacts, seating surfaces, connectors, and housings for contamination, liquid, debris, deformation, or physical damage. Clean only using approved methods.

Expected outcome: Interfaces are clean, dry, undamaged, and the transducer seats correctly. If reseating restores normal detection, verify repeatability and stop.

5. Verify Transducer Charge State

Allow the transducer to remain correctly positioned in the charging station and confirm that its normal charging indication appears. If available, compare behavior with another compatible known-good wireless transducer.

Expected outcome: The transducer accepts a charge and remains powered. A transducer that cannot charge or power reliably should be removed from clinical use.

6. Reestablish the External Connection

Confirm the charging station or wireless interface is connected to the intended TE X system connection and that no cable, adapter, docking interface, or external accessory is loose. Disconnect and securely reconnect accessible connections with the system in an appropriate state.

Expected outcome: The TE X consistently detects the charging interface and wireless transducer. If normal operation returns, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

7. Compare With a Known-Good Compatible Device

When available, test a known-good compatible wireless transducer or charging station without changing unauthorized service settings.

Expected outcome: A known-good accessory identifies whether the problem follows the transducer/charger or remains with the TE X. Do not continue using an accessory that repeatedly fails detection.

8. Verify Normal User-Accessible Configuration

Confirm the wireless transducer is enabled and selected using normal clinical controls and that the system is not configured for a different connected probe or workflow. Do not enter restricted service menus or alter undocumented configuration.

Expected outcome: The intended wireless transducer appears and can be selected normally.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm the transducer is detected repeatedly, produces a stable image, remains connected during normal handling, and returns to a normal charging state when placed in the charging station.

Expected outcome: Detection, imaging, and charging are stable. If all functions are normal, the equipment may be returned to service.

If the Problem Persists

Common external power, seating, charging, connection, accessory, and user-accessible configuration causes have been ruled out. The remaining possibilities may involve the wireless transducer electronics, charging interface, communication hardware, system configuration, or another service-level condition.

Remove the affected equipment from service when reliable transducer detection cannot be confirmed. Label it Out of Service and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Mindray documentation and approved test equipment. Repairs or configuration changes should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Return the equipment to service only after reliable detection, charging, imaging, and required functional checks are completed. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never depend on a wireless probe that intermittently disconnects during a procedure; exchange it for a verified imaging pathway first.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the TE X would not detect the wireless transducer after it was removed from the charging station."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the charging station power connection partially disengaged, preventing reliable charging and transducer availability."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering secured the charging-station power connection, verified normal charging, confirmed repeated wireless transducer detection, and completed functional imaging verification."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether the transducer or charging station was missing
  • Transducer charge indication
  • Charging-station power indication
  • Condition of charging contacts
  • Cable and connector condition
  • Known-good transducer or charger results
  • Whether the failure was constant or intermittent
  • Imaging result after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient first, verify simple power and accessory causes before assuming an internal wireless failure, confirm reliable operation after correction, and document the complaint, cause, resolution, and final status clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
