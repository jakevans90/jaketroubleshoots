Physio-Control LIFEPAK 12

EtCO2 Sampling Failure or No Capnogram

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Physio-Control

Model

LIFEPAK 12

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting absent, intermittent, or inaccurate EtCO2 waveforms caused by disconnected, loose, kinked, obstructed, contaminated, leaking, or incompatible sampling accessories.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not perform extended EtCO2 troubleshooting while the LIFEPAK 12 is actively monitoring a patient whose ventilation status depends on capnography.

If the capnogram disappears during patient care:

  • Immediately assess the patient and verify ventilation by an alternate clinical method.
  • Check the airway, breathing circuit, and ventilator for disconnection, obstruction, or leakage.
  • Transfer EtCO2 monitoring to another verified monitor when continuous capnography is clinically required.
  • Do not assume that a missing waveform is only an equipment problem.

Expected outcome: Patient ventilation is confirmed independently, and care does not rely on an unreliable EtCO2 channel.

Continue Clinical Engineering troubleshooting only after the device has been safely removed from patient use.

2. Confirm the Exact Failure

Power on the LIFEPAK 12 in a controlled, nonclinical area and document whether the complaint involves:

  • No CO2 numeric value
  • No capnogram
  • CO2 FILTERLINE OFF
  • CO2 FILTERLINE BLOCKAGE
  • CO2 FILTERLINE PURGING
  • ALARM APNEA
  • XXX displayed instead of an EtCO2 value
  • Erratic or consistently inaccurate readings

The EtCO2 module begins initialization when compatible CO2 tubing is connected, and initialization, self-test, and warm-up may take up to approximately two-and-one-half minutes.

Expected outcome: The failure is reproduced and its displayed message or behavior is documented.

If the waveform and numerics appear normally after initialization, complete a functional verification and stop.

3. Verify the EtCO2 Option Activates

Connect a compatible Microstream FilterLine securely to the LIFEPAK 12 CO2 connector.

Confirm that:

  • The connector is inserted into the correct CO2 port.
  • The threaded connector turns clockwise and seats securely.
  • The CO2 parameter area appears on the display.
  • The module begins its initialization sequence.

A loose or disconnected FilterLine may produce CO2 FILTERLINE OFF or prevent sampling from starting.

Expected outcome: The LIFEPAK 12 recognizes the sampling line and activates the EtCO2 display.

If recognition remains absent with a verified accessory, continue troubleshooting.

4. Inspect the Sampling Line and Connector

Remove the FilterLine and inspect it externally for:

  • Kinks or sharp bends
  • Crushed or stretched tubing
  • Loose fittings
  • Visible moisture, blood, secretions, or debris
  • Damaged threads
  • A cracked airway adapter or cannula
  • Evidence that the disposable accessory was previously used

Inspect the LIFEPAK 12 CO2 inlet for visible contamination or physical damage without inserting tools or fluids into the port.

Expected outcome: The complete sampling path is open, dry, securely connected, and undamaged.

Do not clean or reuse disposable CO2 tubing. The manufacturer identifies these accessories as single-use items.

5. Replace the Disposable Sampling Assembly

Install a new, unopened, compatible FilterLine, airway adapter, or nasal sampling cannula.

Do not reuse the original line for the verification test, even when no obstruction is visible. Small amounts of moisture or internal contamination can restrict flow without being externally apparent.

Allow the EtCO2 module to complete initialization.

Expected outcome: The replacement accessory is recognized, and a stable CO2 waveform and numeric reading appear.

If this resolves the problem, discard the failed disposable accessory according to facility procedure, document the result, and stop.

6. Address FilterLine Blockage or Purging Messages

For CO2 FILTERLINE BLOCKAGE:

  • Disconnect the sampling line.
  • Check for kinks, moisture, secretions, or a clogged airway adapter.
  • Reconnect the line once.
  • Replace the entire sampling assembly if the message returns.

For CO2 FILTERLINE PURGING:

  • Inspect the tubing for water or obstruction.
  • Untwist or replace the line.
  • Reconnect it securely.
  • Allow the purge cycle to complete.

Rapid altitude or ambient-pressure changes may temporarily trigger purging. Physio-Control directs users to remove and reconnect the FilterLine when purging or blockage messages occur under these conditions.

Expected outcome: The message clears and normal sampling resumes.

If blockage or purging continues with a new line under stable environmental conditions, continue troubleshooting.

7. Check for Sampling-Path Leaks

Using an approved capnography simulator or controlled breath source, inspect the external sampling path for:

  • Loose threaded connection at the monitor
  • Loose airway-adapter connection
  • Cracked tubing or fittings
  • An improperly assembled test setup
  • A leaking breathing-circuit connection

Do not use an uncontrolled clinical patient test for bench troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The sampling path remains sealed and produces a repeatable waveform.

If reseating or replacing the accessory corrects the waveform, complete final verification and stop.

8. Verify the Displayed CO2 Waveform Channel

Confirm that the CO2 waveform is assigned to an available waveform channel and is not merely hidden by the current display configuration.

Select the CO2 parameter and verify:

  • CO2 is displayed in Channel 2 or Channel 3.
  • The scale is appropriate.
  • Autoscale is selected when suitable.
  • Display contrast is sufficient to see the waveform.

The LIFEPAK 12 provides Autoscale, 0–50 mmHg, and 0–100 mmHg CO2 scale options.

Expected outcome: The capnogram is visibly displayed at a usable scale.

If numeric EtCO2 is present but no waveform is displayed, a display-channel configuration issue may have caused the complaint.

9. Test with Known-Good Accessories and a Simulator

Connect:

  • A new compatible Microstream sampling line
  • A known-good airway adapter or cannula
  • An approved capnography or patient simulator capable of producing a repeatable CO2 waveform

Verify:

  • Sampling-line recognition
  • Module initialization
  • Waveform presence
  • Respiratory-rate indication
  • Stable EtCO2 numeric response
  • Absence of blockage, purging, or service messages

Expected outcome: A stable, repeatable capnogram and corresponding EtCO2 value are displayed.

If the device passes with known-good accessories, the original sampling accessory or external setup was the likely cause.

10. Evaluate Persistent Calibration or Module Indicators

If XXX appears instead of the EtCO2 value, or readings remain consistently inaccurate with a verified simulator and new sampling line, do not attempt correction through repeated accessory changes.

The manufacturer associates XXX with unsuccessful CO2 calibration or CO2 module failure and directs the issue to qualified service personnel. It also specifies at least annual EtCO2 calibration by qualified service personnel.

Expected outcome: The technician recognizes that external causes have been ruled out and stops before unauthorized internal repair.

If the Problem Persists

If the LIFEPAK 12 will not recognize a new compatible FilterLine, repeatedly reports blockage or purging, displays XXX, or fails to produce a waveform from an approved simulator, common external causes have been ruled out.

The problem may involve the:

  • CO2 inlet connector
  • Internal sampling pump
  • Pressure or flow detection system
  • EtCO2 module
  • Internal tubing
  • Calibration
  • Module communication circuitry

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Replaced with another verified monitor/defibrillator
  • Sent for qualified bench evaluation or authorized repair
  • Tested for full EtCO2 performance before being returned to clinical use

Knowing when to stop and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot a missing capnogram on an active patient without first confirming ventilation and providing alternate monitoring. A sampling-line failure can resemble a genuine airway or ventilation emergency.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the LIFEPAK 12 displayed CO2 FILTERLINE BLOCKAGE and would not produce an EtCO2 waveform."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Testing found the disposable Microstream FilterLine obstructed by moisture; the monitor produced normal readings with a new sampling line and capnography simulator."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the affected FilterLine, verified a stable EtCO2 waveform and respiratory rate with the simulator, and returned the LIFEPAK 12 to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Patient removed from affected monitor
  • Exact displayed CO2 message
  • CO2 option activated
  • Warm-up completed
  • Sampling-line manufacturer and type
  • FilterLine connection secured
  • Kinks, moisture, or contamination found
  • Airway adapter condition
  • New sampling line installed
  • Display channel and scale checked
  • Simulator used
  • EtCO2 and respiratory-rate test values
  • Purging or blockage behavior
  • XXX or service indicator present
  • Calibration status
  • Final device status

Final Thought

A missing capnogram must first be treated as a potential patient or airway concern. Once patient safety is secured, logical accessory, connection, obstruction, leakage, display, and simulator checks can distinguish a disposable sampling-path problem from an internal EtCO2 module failure. Clear CCR documentation records both the findings and the justification for return to service or escalation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
