Medline BioCon 900S

Charging Cradle or Battery Failure

Asset Type

Bladder Scanner

Manufacturer

Medline

Model

BioCon 900S

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting failure to charge, rapid battery depletion, intermittent cradle operation, missing charge indication, or inability to maintain reliable battery power.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot an unresolved charging or battery problem while the BioCon 900S is being relied upon for patient assessment.

Move the patient assessment to another verified bladder scanner before continuing extended troubleshooting.

Expected: Patient care is not dependent on a device with uncertain battery availability.

Why it matters: An unreliable battery or charging system may cause unexpected device shutdown or loss of scanning availability.

If transferring clinical use to another device resolves the immediate concern, continue troubleshooting only in a controlled Clinical Engineering setting.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Failure

Determine what clinical staff actually observed.

Check whether the BioCon 900S:

  • Does not show a charging response
  • Charges only when repositioned in the cradle
  • Shows charging but battery level does not increase
  • Reaches a high charge level but discharges rapidly
  • Will not power on after extended charging
  • Charges intermittently
  • Displays a battery-related warning
  • Shuts down unexpectedly during use

Document the exact behavior before replacing components.

Expected: The complaint is narrowed to charging initiation, incomplete charging, short battery runtime, or complete loss of battery power.

3. Inspect for Heat, Odor, Damage, or Contamination

Before repeated charging attempts, inspect the scanner and charging equipment for:

  • Unusual heat
  • Burning or chemical odor
  • Swelling or housing deformation
  • Cracks or impact damage
  • Liquid intrusion
  • Damaged power cords
  • Bent or damaged connectors
  • Heavy gel or cleaning residue around the cradle area

Expected: No obvious safety hazard or physical damage is present.

If unusual heat, swelling, odor, or visible battery-related deformation is present, stop troubleshooting. Remove the device from service and escalate for repair.

4. Verify the AC Outlet

Disconnect the charging system and test the wall outlet using:

  • An approved receptacle tester, or
  • Another known-good device appropriate for the outlet

Do not rely only on a report that the outlet is functional.

Expected: The outlet provides stable power.

If the outlet is defective, move the charging system to a verified outlet and retest.

If normal charging resumes, the outlet was the cause. Stop troubleshooting.

5. Inspect the External Power Path

Inspect the complete external power path from the wall outlet to the charging cradle.

Check for:

  • Loose AC connection
  • Loose power-supply connection
  • Partially inserted connector
  • Damaged cable insulation
  • Pinched or sharply bent cable
  • Strain damage near connector ends
  • Bent connector components
  • Incorrect or unapproved power supply

Reseat all accessible connections.

Expected: The external power path is secure and free of visible damage.

If reseating a loose connection restores stable charging, verify repeatability and stop troubleshooting.

6. Check the Charging Cradle for Power or Fault Indications

With the cradle connected to a verified power source, observe available indicator lights or status behavior.

Check whether:

  • Power indication is present
  • Ready indication is present
  • Charging indication appears
  • A fault or error indication is active
  • Indicators flicker when the cable is gently moved

Do not aggressively flex or manipulate the cable.

Expected: Cradle indications remain stable and consistent.

If the cradle shows no response despite verified outlet power and secure connections, continue with controlled component isolation.

7. Inspect the Cradle Seating Area

Remove the scanner from the cradle and inspect the docking or charging area.

Look for:

  • Dried ultrasound gel
  • Dust or debris
  • Cleaning residue
  • Foreign material
  • Physical obstruction
  • Cracked cradle surfaces
  • Misalignment
  • Evidence that the scanner is not seating fully

Clean only according to approved facility and manufacturer practices.

Expected: The scanner can seat fully and consistently in the cradle.

If cleaning or removing an obstruction restores reliable charging, verify repeated docking and stop troubleshooting.

8. Reseat the Scanner Correctly

Place the BioCon 900S into the charging cradle carefully and confirm it is fully seated.

Observe whether charging begins without:

  • Applying pressure
  • Holding the scanner in place
  • Tilting the scanner
  • Repeatedly repositioning it
  • Moving the cradle cable

Expected: Charging begins consistently when the scanner is properly seated.

If correct seating restores stable charging, verify the result several times and stop troubleshooting.

9. Observe the Actual Charging Behavior

Once the scanner is seated, document the charging response.

Check:

  • Whether a charging indication appears
  • Whether the indication remains stable
  • Whether charging stops unexpectedly
  • Whether slight movement interrupts charging
  • Whether battery level increases over time

Expected: Charging begins and remains stable without special positioning.

If charging occurs only at certain angles or positions, suspect a cradle alignment, charging interface, or device-side charging problem rather than immediately condemning the battery.

10. Allow Adequate Charging Time

Do not declare the battery defective after only a brief charging attempt.

Leave the scanner properly seated on a verified powered cradle for a reasonable charging period.

Then reassess:

  • Battery level
  • Ability to power on
  • Ability to remain powered
  • Whether the battery status increased
  • Whether the device becomes unusually warm

Expected: Battery state increases after sustained charging.

If the battery charges normally and remains stable, the original problem may have been caused by poor seating, incomplete charging, or an external power interruption. Stop after confirming repeatability.

11. Isolate the Cradle With Known-Good Equipment

When compatible, approved, known-good equipment is available, substitute one component at a time.

Examples:

  • Test the affected scanner on a known-good compatible cradle
  • Test a known-good compatible scanner on the suspect cradle
  • Test with a verified approved power supply when separately replaceable

Change only one variable at a time.

Expected outcomes:

  • Affected scanner charges on known-good cradle: Suspect the original cradle or its external power path
  • Known-good scanner also fails on suspect cradle: Suspect the cradle or power supply
  • Affected scanner fails on multiple known-good charging setups: Suspect scanner-side charging or battery failure
  • Failure follows one power supply: Suspect the external power supply

If substitution identifies a defective approved external component and replacement resolves the problem, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

12. Evaluate Battery Runtime Separately

A scanner may appear to charge normally while still having a degraded battery.

After obtaining a credible full-charge condition:

  • Remove the scanner from the cradle.
  • Power it on in a controlled, non-patient setting.
  • Observe battery level over time.
  • Check for rapid percentage loss.
  • Check for unexpected shutdown.
  • Compare behavior with a known-good unit when available.

Expected: The scanner maintains stable battery-powered operation without abnormal depletion.

Rapid battery decline after apparently successful charging supports battery degradation more strongly than a cradle fault.

13. Check for Intermittent Failure

Repeat the charging sequence several times:

  • Remove the scanner from the cradle.
  • Confirm cradle power remains stable.
  • Reseat the scanner.
  • Confirm charging begins.
  • Remove and repeat.

Observe whether charging initiation is consistent.

Expected: Charging begins reliably on every normal docking attempt.

If the problem is intermittent but reproducible, do not return the device to service simply because one charging attempt succeeds.

14. Perform Final Functional Verification

If the problem appears resolved:

  • Confirm the scanner powers on normally
  • Confirm battery status is reasonable
  • Confirm repeated cradle docking initiates charging
  • Confirm charging remains stable
  • Confirm no abnormal heat or odor
  • Confirm no fault indication remains
  • Confirm basic bladder-scanning operation off-patient
  • Complete any facility-required post-maintenance inspection

Expected: The BioCon 900S demonstrates repeatable charging and stable battery-powered operation.

If all checks pass, return the device to service according to facility policy and document the work performed.

If the Problem Persists

If verified outlet power, external connections, proper cradle seating, charging behavior, known-good substitution, and battery runtime checks do not resolve the issue, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining problem is more likely associated with:

  • Internal battery degradation
  • Device-side charging circuitry
  • Internal cradle electronics
  • Charging interface components
  • Internal power-management circuitry

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation

Do not proceed into deep internal disassembly solely to continue field troubleshooting.

Knowing when external causes have been ruled out and when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not troubleshoot intermittent charging or questionable battery runtime during active patient assessment.

Move the patient to another verified bladder scanner first, then evaluate the BioCon 900S in a controlled Clinical Engineering setting.

A scanner that successfully charges once but repeatedly loses power should not be considered reliable until the failure is isolated and resolved.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported the BioCon 900S would not consistently charge when placed in the charging cradle."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Found the scanner charged normally on a known-good compatible cradle, while the original cradle failed to initiate charging consistently despite verified AC power and proper scanner seating."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the defective cradle from service, replaced it with a verified functional cradle, and confirmed repeated charging initiation and stable battery-powered operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Outlet tested
  • External power connections inspected
  • Power supply inspected
  • Cradle indicator behavior documented
  • Charging indication documented
  • Fault or error indication documented
  • Scanner reseated and retested
  • Cradle checked for gel or debris
  • Known-good cradle tested
  • Known-good compatible scanner tested
  • Approved power supply substituted
  • Battery level before charging documented
  • Battery level after charging documented
  • Rapid discharge observed
  • Unexpected shutdown observed
  • Unusual heat or smell checked
  • Intermittent behavior reproduced
  • Final device status documented

Final Thought

Charging and battery failures should be approached by protecting patient care first, then separating outlet, power path, cradle, seating, and battery-runtime causes logically. Appropriate escalation prevents an unreliable bladder scanner from returning to service, while clear CCR documentation preserves what was reported, what was found, and what restored safe operation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
