Stryker LIFEPAK 35

Pads or Therapy Cable Not Detected

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

LIFEPAK 35

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting when therapy pads, connected electrodes, or the LIFEPAK 35 therapy cable are not recognized during defibrillation or pacing preparation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot an unresolved therapy-connection problem while the LIFEPAK 35 is being relied upon for active patient care.

If defibrillation, synchronized cardioversion, or pacing may be required:

  • Immediately provide another verified defibrillator
  • Transfer therapy responsibility to the known-good device
  • Remove the affected LIFEPAK 35 from active use

Expected: Patient care is supported by equipment with a verified therapy path.

Why it matters: The LIFEPAK 35 therapy cable connects compatible therapy electrode pads, simulators, and test loads to the monitor/defibrillator. Failure to recognize that path can affect therapy readiness.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Behavior

Determine what Clinical Engineering is actually reproducing:

  • Therapy cable not recognized
  • Pads not recognized after connection
  • Connection-related message remains after reseating
  • Detection changes when the cable is moved
  • Failure occurs only with one pad set
  • Failure occurs with multiple known-good pad sets
  • Failure occurs only during pacing or defibrillation setup

Record any exact on-screen message rather than documenting only “pads bad.”

Expected: The failure is narrowed to a pad-set issue, therapy-cable issue, connection issue, or device-side detection problem.

3. Verify the Therapy Cable Is Fully Connected

Power down or place the device in a safe nonclinical test condition as appropriate, then inspect and reconnect the therapy cable.

  • Disconnect by handling the connector body, not pulling on the cable
  • Inspect for contamination or obvious damage
  • Reinsert the connector fully and securely
  • Confirm the connector is properly seated

The LIFEPAK 35-specific therapy cable uses Stryker’s TRUE-LOCK Cable Connector, so incomplete engagement should be ruled out before assuming an internal fault.

Expected: The cable is firmly connected and remains mechanically secure.

If the issue resolves: Complete controlled functional verification and stop troubleshooting.

4. Inspect the External Therapy Cable

Examine the full accessible cable length for:

  • Cuts
  • Crushed sections
  • Severe kinks
  • Exposed conductors
  • Fluid contamination
  • Damaged strain reliefs
  • Bent or damaged connector features
  • Evidence of repeated pulling or impact

Gently reposition the cable during a controlled bench evaluation and observe whether detection becomes intermittent.

Expected: The cable remains recognized without intermittent connection loss.

If recognition changes with cable movement: Remove the cable from service and test with a known-good compatible therapy cable.

5. Verify the Pad-to-Cable Connection

Inspect the connection between the therapy electrode set and therapy cable.

  • Disconnect and reconnect the pad connector
  • Confirm full engagement
  • Check for damaged, contaminated, or distorted connector surfaces
  • Verify the pad lead is not under tension
  • Confirm packaging or cable routing is not preventing complete connection

Expected: The electrode set connects securely and is recognized consistently.

If the issue resolves: Stop and document the poor or incomplete connection.

6. Check Therapy Pad Compatibility and Condition

Confirm the connected therapy electrodes are:

  • Compatible with the LIFEPAK 35 therapy system
  • The correct type for the intended application
  • Within expiration date
  • Unopened before use when applicable
  • Free from obvious cable or connector damage
  • Not a training-only accessory being used for clinical verification

Stryker identifies the LIFEPAK 35 therapy cable as the interface for compatible therapy electrode pads, simulators, and test loads.

Expected: A correct, undamaged, in-date compatible accessory is installed.

If an expired, damaged, or incompatible set is found: Replace it and stop once proper detection is verified.

7. Test With a Known-Good Compatible Pad Set

Replace the suspect electrode set with a known-good compatible pad set or approved test setup.

Observe whether the LIFEPAK 35 now recognizes the therapy connection.

Expected: The known-good set is detected consistently.

  • Known-good set works: Suspect the original pad set or pad connector
  • Known-good set also fails: Continue troubleshooting

If the issue resolves: Remove the failed accessory from use and document the result.

8. Test With a Known-Good LIFEPAK 35 Therapy Cable

Where available, substitute a verified compatible therapy cable.

The Stryker LIFEPAK 35 therapy cable is specifically intended to connect compatible pads, simulators, and test loads to the device.

Expected: Therapy accessories are detected reliably with the known-good cable.

  • Known-good cable works: Remove the suspect cable from service
  • Known-good cable fails: A device-side problem becomes more likely

If the issue resolves: Replace or route the failed cable for appropriate evaluation and stop troubleshooting.

9. Inspect the Device-Side Therapy Connector Externally

With the device removed from clinical service, inspect the accessible therapy connector area for:

  • Debris
  • Fluid residue
  • Bent or damaged visible features
  • Cracked housing
  • Excessive looseness
  • Evidence of impact
  • Inability of the cable to remain securely engaged

Do not probe contacts with conductive tools and do not proceed into unnecessary internal disassembly.

Expected: The connector is clean, intact, mechanically secure, and able to retain the cable correctly.

If physical damage is present: Stop troubleshooting and escalate for repair.

10. Perform a Controlled Therapy Functional Check

After external causes have been addressed, perform an approved Clinical Engineering operational check using appropriate:

  • Defibrillator analyzer
  • Approved simulator
  • Compatible test load
  • Verified therapy cable

Do not use a patient as a troubleshooting test load.

Stryker publishes a LIFEPAK 35 Performance Inspection Procedure and product-specific performance evaluation resources, supporting formal verification rather than relying only on disappearance of a warning message.

Expected: The therapy path is recognized consistently and required functional testing passes.

If the device passes: Document the findings and return it to service only according to facility policy.

11. Review for Recurrence Before Return to Service

Power-cycle and repeat the applicable controlled connection check when appropriate.

Verify that:

  • Cable detection is stable
  • Known-good pads or test accessories remain recognized
  • No intermittent behavior occurs
  • No service indication remains
  • Required operational testing passes

Expected: The issue does not recur.

If the issue returns: Remove the device from service and escalate.

If the Problem Persists

If known-good compatible pads and a known-good therapy cable are still not detected, the common external causes have been ruled out. A device-side connector, detection circuit, therapy-path communication, or other internal fault is likely.

The LIFEPAK 35 should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or qualified bench evaluation

Do not repeatedly reconnect accessories and return the unit to clinical use when therapy detection remains intermittent or unresolved.

Stryker provides a LIFEPAK 35 service manual and formal performance inspection procedure for qualified service evaluation.

Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot pads or therapy-cable detection on an active patient. Move therapy responsibility to another verified defibrillator first, then evaluate the LIFEPAK 35 under controlled conditions.

For intermittent complaints, preserve the exact cable and pad set involved when practical. A connection problem may disappear after accessories are swapped, making the original failure difficult to reproduce.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported the LIFEPAK 35 did not detect the connected therapy pads during defibrillation preparation."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Found intermittent recognition of the therapy path with the original therapy cable; known-good compatible cable remained detected during controlled testing."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the defective therapy cable from service, installed a verified replacement, completed applicable functional testing, and returned the LIFEPAK 35 to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact on-screen message recorded
  • Original pad type and expiration date checked
  • Pad connector reseated
  • Therapy cable reseated
  • Therapy cable externally inspected
  • Known-good compatible pads tested
  • Known-good therapy cable tested
  • Device-side connector inspected
  • Intermittency during cable movement noted
  • Defibrillator analyzer or approved test load used
  • Alarm or indicator behavior documented
  • Unusual heat, smell, or physical damage noted
  • Applicable functional verification completed
  • Final device status documented

Final Thought

Therapy-accessory detection problems require disciplined troubleshooting because the symptom can originate with pads, connectors, cable damage, incomplete seating, or the device itself. Protect the patient first, prove the external path with known-good accessories, perform controlled verification, and escalate unresolved or intermittent failures. Clear CCR documentation preserves exactly what was reported, found, and done.

That is successful troubleshooting.
