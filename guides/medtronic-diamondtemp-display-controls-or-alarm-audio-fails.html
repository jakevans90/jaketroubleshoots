Display, Controls, or Alarm Audio Fails

Asset Type

Cardiac Ablation Generator

Manufacturer

Medtronic

Model

DiamondTemp

What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when the display, operator controls, indicators, or audible alarm functions are missing, unreadable, unresponsive, or unreliable.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient From an Unreliable User Interface

Do not use the generator for therapy if essential information, controls, or alarm annunciation cannot be reliably seen, heard, or operated. Move the procedure to a verified alternative if necessary.

Expected outcome: The patient is not dependent on a generator with compromised controls or alarm presentation.

2. Confirm the Exact Interface Failure

Determine whether the problem affects the entire display, part of the display, a specific control, all controls, alarm audio, or multiple functions. Record whether the problem is constant or intermittent.

Expected outcome: The failed function is precisely identified.

3. Verify Normal Power and Startup

Confirm the generator powers on and completes startup. A dark display may represent a broader startup problem rather than an isolated display failure.

Expected outcome: The system reaches the expected operating state, or the issue is redirected to startup troubleshooting.

4. Check Accessible Display and Audio Settings

Verify user-accessible brightness, display selections, and audio settings have not been unintentionally adjusted. Do not reduce required alarm audibility or alter protected settings.

Expected outcome: The affected function is not disabled or obscured by a normal setting. If restoring an appropriate setting resolves the complaint, proceed to verification.

5. Inspect External Controls and Connections

If an external activation control, display component, speaker interface, or other approved accessory is involved, inspect its cable and connector for damage or poor seating.

Expected outcome: External control interfaces are secure and undamaged.

6. Check for Contamination or Physical Obstruction

Inspect accessible controls and display surfaces for fluid, debris, damaged overlays, cracked surfaces, stuck controls, or items physically obstructing operation.

Expected outcome: Controls move and respond normally without external obstruction. Liquid intrusion or significant physical damage requires removal from service.

7. Restart the Generator

With therapy discontinued, perform a normal shutdown and restart to determine whether the interface returns to normal operation.

Expected outcome: The display, controls, indicators, and alarm audio initialize and operate normally.

8. Test All Safety-Critical Interface Functions

Using approved procedures, verify the relevant controls, visible indicators, display readability, and audible alarm annunciation. Do not intentionally create unsafe clinical conditions solely to test alarms.

Expected outcome: All safety-critical interface functions operate consistently. If they do, troubleshooting can stop after required final verification.

9. Escalate Persistent Interface Failure

If essential display, control, or alarm functions remain unavailable or intermittent, remove the generator from service.

Expected outcome: The system is prevented from clinical use until qualified service evaluation is completed.

If the Problem Persists

External settings, controls, connections, contamination, and restart-related causes have been ruled out. Remaining causes may include display hardware, control-interface electronics, audio circuitry, internal communication, software, or another service-level fault.

Remove the generator from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for bench evaluation or manufacturer-supported repair. Evaluate it with appropriate documentation and approved test equipment. Internal repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Verify all safety-critical display, control, alarm, and therapy functions before return to service. Recognizing that a partially functioning user interface is not acceptable is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A generator with inaudible alarms or unreliable controls should not remain clinically available simply because therapy output still appears functional.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the DiamondTemp generator display was operating but audible alarm annunciation could not be heard."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the accessible alarm volume had been set to an inappropriate level for the clinical environment."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering restored the approved audible setting and verified alarm annunciation and normal display and control operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Display function affected
  • Controls affected
  • Alarm audio behavior
  • Power and startup status
  • Accessible settings observed
  • Physical damage or contamination
  • External control connections
  • Restart result
  • Alarm and control verification result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Keep the patient off equipment with unreliable displays, controls, or alarms, rule out simple settings and external causes first, verify every safety-critical interface after correction, escalate unresolved faults, and document objective findings.

That is successful troubleshooting.
