Philips Lumify

Poor Image Quality, Dropout, or Intermittent Elements

Asset Type

Ultrasound System

Manufacturer

Philips

Model

Lumify

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot Lumify image degradation by checking transducer condition, connection, preset, settings, test target, positioning, and external interference first.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Confirm Imaging Reliability

Do not rely on an ultrasound system producing questionable or intermittent images for clinical decisions. Move the examination to another verified system when image reliability is uncertain.

Inspect the transducer for cracks, delamination, cable damage, connector damage, or contamination.

Expected outcome: The patient is not dependent on questionable imaging and no obvious damage requiring immediate removal from service is present.

2. Confirm the Image Complaint

Ask what clinical staff observed: poor overall image quality, a fixed dropout area, intermittent streaking, noise, or missing image elements.

Determine whether the problem occurs with every exam or only particular presets, depths, positions, or transducer movements.

Expected outcome: The image defect is reproducible and its pattern is clearly described.

3. Verify the Correct Preset and Basic Imaging Controls

Confirm that an appropriate examination preset is selected. Review normal user-accessible controls such as gain, depth, focus, and other relevant image adjustments to ensure they are not at obviously inappropriate settings.

Avoid changing system configuration unrelated to the reported problem.

Expected outcome: Appropriate imaging settings produce an expected image. If correcting an inadvertent setting resolves the complaint, verify performance and troubleshooting can stop.

4. Verify Coupling and Test Conditions

Use appropriate ultrasound coupling media and an approved phantom or other suitable nonpatient test target.

Ensure poor contact, insufficient coupling, or the test target itself is not creating an apparent dropout.

Expected outcome: A stable test setup produces a repeatable image for evaluation.

5. Inspect the Transducer Face and Cable

Clean the transducer according to approved procedures and inspect the acoustic surface for damage. Examine the accessible cable length for cuts, kinks, pinching, severe strain, or areas that cause image changes with normal movement.

Do not aggressively flex the cable.

Expected outcome: The transducer surface and cable appear intact and imaging remains stable during normal positioning. Any damaged transducer should be removed from service.

6. Inspect and Reseat the USB Connection

Disconnect and inspect the USB connector and mobile device port. Reconnect the transducer fully and observe whether image quality or dropout changes.

Expected outcome: The connection remains secure and the image is stable. If reseating corrects the image defect, complete functional verification and troubleshooting can stop.

7. Compare With a Known-Good Setup

When available, test the suspect transducer on another known-good compatible Lumify device or test a known-good compatible transducer on the original mobile device.

Keep the same test target and comparable imaging settings.

Expected outcome: The comparison identifies whether the defect follows the transducer or remains with the mobile device/software environment.

8. Check for Environmental Interference

Move the system away from obvious potential interference sources such as damaged power accessories, high-current equipment, or unusual cable routing when practical.

Observe whether image noise changes with location.

Expected outcome: Image quality remains stable regardless of normal environmental conditions. If relocating eliminates the interference, correct the environmental cause before returning the system to use.

9. Perform Final Imaging Verification

Using an approved test target, verify the image across the usable field while adjusting normal controls appropriately.

Confirm that no reproducible dropout, intermittent loss, or abnormal artifact remains.

Expected outcome: Image quality is stable and clinically usable. Troubleshooting can stop once normal return-to-service requirements are completed.

10. Escalate Persistent Image Defects

If dropout or poor image quality persists after settings, coupling, connection, environmental, and known-good comparison checks, discontinue external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The suspect transducer or system is removed from service for further evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal transducer element failure, cable or connector faults not externally visible, mobile device interface problems, or software/service-level imaging faults.

Remove the affected equipment from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it using appropriate Philips documentation and approved ultrasound test equipment. Do not attempt internal transducer repair or deep disassembly.

Before return to service, verify image uniformity, connection stability, and normal operation with appropriate testing. Knowing when an image defect requires escalation protects both the patient and the integrity of the diagnosis.

Clinical Use Tip

A repeatable dead or dropout region should be treated as a potentially unreliable transducer even when the remainder of the image appears normal.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported a persistent vertical dropout area in images produced by the Lumify transducer."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering reproduced the dropout on a test target and confirmed that the defect followed the transducer when tested on another compatible device."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering removed the defective transducer from service and provided a verified replacement after confirming normal image uniformity."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Description and location of image artifact
  • Preset and imaging settings used
  • Whether the artifact is constant or intermittent
  • Transducer face and cable condition
  • USB connector condition
  • Test target used
  • Known-good comparison results
  • Whether the defect followed the transducer
  • Environmental conditions tested
  • Final imaging result and equipment status

Final Thought

Questionable image quality is a patient-safety issue. Verify settings, test conditions, connections, and environmental causes first, isolate the problem systematically, and remove unreliable imaging hardware from service when necessary.

That is successful troubleshooting.
