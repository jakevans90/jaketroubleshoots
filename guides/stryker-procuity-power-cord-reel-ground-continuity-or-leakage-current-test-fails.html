Power-Cord Reel, Ground Continuity, or Leakage-Current Test Fails

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Stryker

ProCuity

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot ProCuity power-cord reel problems or failed electrical-safety testing involving protective grounding or leakage current before assuming an internal electrical failure.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Remove the Bed From Service for Electrical-Safety Failure

Do not troubleshoot a suspected grounding, damaged power cord, or leakage-current problem while a patient depends on the bed.

If the power cord is damaged or the bed fails an applicable electrical-safety test, disconnect it from clinical use and provide another verified bed.

Expected outcome: The affected bed is not being used until electrical safety can be confirmed. A confirmed electrical-safety failure requires the bed to remain Out of Service until corrected and retested.

2. Confirm the Exact Failure

Determine whether the reported problem is:

  • Power cord will not extend.
  • Cord will not retract.
  • Reel binds or retracts intermittently.
  • Plug or cord is physically damaged.
  • Ground continuity test fails.
  • Leakage-current measurement is unacceptable under the applicable approved procedure.
  • Test results change when the cord is moved.

Document the test equipment and test configuration used.

Expected outcome: The mechanical cord-reel complaint and/or electrical-safety test failure is clearly identified and reproducible.

3. Inspect the Power Cord and Plug

Unplug the bed and inspect the full accessible cord length, plug, strain relief, and reel exit point for:

  • Cuts or abrasions.
  • Flattening or crushing.
  • Exposed conductors.
  • Burn marks.
  • Bent or loose plug blades.
  • Damaged ground pin.
  • Discoloration or heat damage.
  • Unusual stiffness or kinking.

Do not tape over, splice, or otherwise temporarily repair a damaged medical equipment power cord.

Expected outcome: The cord and plug are intact with no visible condition that compromises safe use. Any significant damage requires continued removal from service.

4. Check Cord-Reel Movement

With the bed unplugged, extend and retract the cord using normal handling. Do not jerk, pull beyond its normal travel, or force a jammed reel.

Check whether the cord:

  • Feeds smoothly.
  • Retracts normally.
  • Stops unexpectedly.
  • Twists or bunches at the reel opening.
  • Shows damage only at certain extension lengths.

Expected outcome: The cord reel operates smoothly without binding or damaging the cord. A jammed or mechanically damaged reel requires service-level repair.

5. Inspect the AC Source and Test Setup

Before assuming the bed has failed electrically, verify the test environment.

Confirm:

  • The electrical-safety analyzer is appropriate for the test.
  • Test leads and adapters are intact.
  • The analyzer has completed required self-checks or verification.
  • The test outlet and ground path are suitable.
  • The correct approved test sequence is being used.

Use a known-good comparison or alternate verified test location if needed.

Expected outcome: The failure is confirmed to originate with the bed rather than the analyzer, test lead, adapter, or facility outlet.

6. Verify Protective Ground Continuity Using Approved Test Procedures

Perform the applicable ground-continuity test using approved electrical-safety test equipment and the current hospital or manufacturer procedure.

If the result is abnormal, repeat the measurement after checking the test-lead contact and an appropriate accessible grounding point. Do not use improvised limits or acceptance criteria.

Gently reposition the cord while observing for intermittent results only if safe and permitted by the test procedure.

Expected outcome: Ground continuity meets the applicable approved acceptance criteria and remains stable. If it does not, the bed must remain out of service.

7. Investigate Cord-Position Dependence

If continuity changes as the cord is extended, retracted, or gently repositioned, inspect the accessible cord and strain-relief areas again for intermittent damage.

Do not open the reel or bypass grounding to isolate the fault.

Expected outcome: Ground continuity remains consistent regardless of normal cord position. An intermittent result suggests a cord, reel, connection, or other service-level fault requiring repair.

8. Perform Leakage-Current Testing Using Approved Procedures

With the bed configured as required by the approved electrical-safety procedure, perform the applicable leakage-current tests.

Verify that:

  • The correct analyzer connections are used.
  • The bed is in the required operating state.
  • Accessories included in the test are appropriately connected.
  • Results are compared only with the applicable manufacturer, regulatory, or institutional acceptance criteria.

Do not invent or substitute a numerical limit when the approved requirement is not available.

Expected outcome: Leakage-current results meet the applicable approved acceptance criteria. Any confirmed failure requires the bed to remain out of service.

9. Isolate External Accessories When Appropriate

If permitted by the approved test method, disconnect nonessential externally attached accessories one at a time and repeat the relevant test to determine whether an accessory is influencing the result.

Do not disconnect protective grounding or bypass a safety feature.

Expected outcome: The source of the abnormal result is narrowed to the bed or an external accessory. A defective accessory should be removed from service separately and the bed retested in its intended configuration.

10. Perform Final Electrical and Functional Verification

After any approved corrective action, repeat the full required electrical-safety test sequence rather than relying only on the previously failed measurement.

Also verify:

  • Power cord condition.
  • Cord-reel operation.
  • AC-powered bed operation.
  • Charging behavior if applicable.
  • No abnormal odor, heat, arcing, or intermittent power.
  • Any additional return-to-service checks required by policy.

Expected outcome: The bed passes all required electrical-safety and functional tests. Only then can troubleshooting stop and the bed return to service.

If the Problem Persists

If the power-cord reel continues to bind or the bed continues to fail ground-continuity or leakage-current testing after the cord, plug, test setup, outlet, analyzer connections, and external accessories have been ruled out, the problem may involve the cord-reel assembly, protective-ground path, internal wiring, power-entry components, or another service-level electrical condition.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved electrical-safety test equipment.
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel.

Do not bypass the grounding conductor, defeat a safety device, or return the bed to service based on functional operation alone.

After repair, complete the applicable electrical-safety testing and functional return-to-service inspection before clinical use. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A bed that operates normally can still present an electrical hazard; a confirmed ground or leakage-current failure must be resolved before return to patient care.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Preventive maintenance testing found intermittent ground-continuity failure as the ProCuity power cord was moved near the reel."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found visible damage at the accessible power-cord section near the reel exit, with continuity changing as the cord was repositioned."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the bed from service for qualified power-cord/reel repair, then completed the required electrical-safety and functional tests after repair before returning it to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether the complaint involves the reel, ground continuity, leakage current, or more than one.
  • Condition of plug and ground pin.
  • Cord damage or strain-relief condition.
  • Cord position when the failure occurs.
  • Electrical-safety analyzer used.
  • Test configuration.
  • Outlet or alternate test location checked.
  • Accessories connected during testing.
  • Results before and after repair.
  • Final electrical-safety test status.
  • Final bed status.

Final Thought

Electrical-safety failures require disciplined verification. Inspect the cord and plug first, verify the analyzer and test environment, repeat testing using approved criteria, and never assume that normal bed operation means the bed is electrically safe. Escalate confirmed failures and document both the corrective action and complete return-to-service testing.

That is successful troubleshooting.
