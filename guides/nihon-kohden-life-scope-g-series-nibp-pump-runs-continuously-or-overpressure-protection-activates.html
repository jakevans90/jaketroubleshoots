NIBP Pump Runs Continuously or Overpressure Protection Activates

Asset Type

Patient Monitor

Manufacturer

Nihon Kohden

Life Scope G Series

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots prolonged NIBP inflation or overpressure protection by checking patient safety, cuff application, tubing, connections, leaks, occlusions, and monitor operation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient Immediately

Stop the NIBP measurement and remove the cuff if it remains inflated, repeatedly inflates abnormally, causes discomfort, or appears unable to release pressure normally.

Use another verified method for obtaining blood pressure until the problem is resolved.

Do not continue repeated NIBP cycling on a patient while investigating an inflation or overpressure problem.

Expected outcome: Pressure is removed from the patient's limb and blood-pressure monitoring continues by a safe alternate method. If the cuff cannot deflate promptly, remove the monitor and NIBP accessories from clinical use.

2. Confirm the Reported Behavior Off the Patient

Determine whether the pump:

  • Runs continuously
  • Runs much longer than expected
  • Repeatedly restarts
  • Causes the cuff to remain inflated
  • Triggers an overpressure or safety-protection condition
  • Fails only with a particular cuff or hose

Perform further testing off the patient using approved test equipment or an appropriate test setup.

Expected outcome: The abnormal behavior can be characterized without exposing a patient to repeated inflation. If the condition cannot be safely evaluated externally, remove the device from service.

3. Inspect the Cuff

Check the cuff for:

  • Correct assembly
  • Kinks or folds
  • Damaged bladder or fabric
  • Contamination around connectors
  • Cracked fittings
  • Improper closure
  • Obvious leakage

Confirm that the cuff is an appropriate compatible accessory for the monitor and intended application.

Expected outcome: The cuff is intact, correctly assembled, and compatible. If replacing a damaged cuff restores normal inflation and deflation, verify several measurements using approved testing and stop troubleshooting.

4. Inspect the NIBP Hose

Examine the complete external hose for:

  • Kinks
  • Compression
  • Cracks
  • Loose fittings
  • Damaged connectors
  • Occlusion
  • Signs of repeated pinching by beds, rails, or equipment

Ensure the hose is not trapped under equipment.

Expected outcome: Air can move freely through an intact hose. If correcting a kink or replacing a defective hose restores normal operation, complete functional verification and stop troubleshooting.

5. Verify All Pneumatic Connections

Disconnect and reconnect the cuff and hose at all accessible external connection points.

Ensure fittings are fully seated and not cross-connected with an incompatible accessory.

Expected outcome: The pneumatic path is connected securely without leakage or restriction. If reseating the connection restores normal NIBP cycling, verify repeated operation and stop troubleshooting.

6. Test With a Known-Good Cuff and Hose

Substitute a known-good compatible cuff and hose assembly.

Do not reuse the original suspect components during this comparison.

Expected outcome: The monitor inflates, measures, and deflates normally with the known-good accessory set. If so, remove the defective cuff or hose from service and verify normal operation before returning the monitor.

7. Check for External Pneumatic Leakage

Using approved test equipment and procedures, evaluate the accessible NIBP pneumatic path for leakage without opening the monitor.

A significant external leak can cause the pump to run excessively while attempting to achieve or maintain pressure.

Expected outcome: No abnormal external leakage is detected. If an external accessory leak is found and corrected, repeat the functional test and stop troubleshooting when normal operation is confirmed.

8. Check for External Occlusion or Restriction

Confirm that the cuff, tubing, connectors, and test setup are not blocked or restricted.

An occluded hose or improperly connected cuff can create abnormal pressure behavior even when the pump itself is functioning.

Expected outcome: The external pneumatic path is unobstructed. If clearing or replacing an obstructed accessory restores normal cycling, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

9. Observe Deflation and Pressure Release

Using an approved test setup, confirm that the cuff or test chamber releases pressure appropriately when the measurement ends or is cancelled.

Do not defeat or bypass pressure-protection features.

Expected outcome: Pressure is released normally when commanded. If pressure remains trapped despite verified external accessories, stop troubleshooting and remove the monitor from service.

10. Perform Approved NIBP Functional Verification

With known-good accessories and appropriate approved NIBP test equipment, verify that the monitor can complete normal inflation, measurement, deflation, and repeat cycles.

Do not attempt unauthorized internal calibration or adjustment.

Expected outcome: NIBP operation is repeatable, pressure is released properly, and no abnormal prolonged pumping or protection activation occurs. The device may proceed to return-to-service testing.

11. Remove From Service if the Condition Remains

If the pump continues running abnormally, pressure does not release correctly, or overpressure protection activates with known-good external accessories, stop testing.

An unreliable NIBP pneumatic system presents a patient-safety risk.

Expected outcome: The monitor is removed from service for bench evaluation rather than returned to clinical use.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as an incorrect cuff, leaking cuff, damaged hose, loose pneumatic connection, kink, and external occlusion have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve internal pneumatic components, pressure sensing, valves, pump control, safety circuitry, calibration, or another service-level fault.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or calibrated only by qualified personnel

Do not bypass overpressure protection or continue repeated inflation testing on a patient.

After corrective action, complete appropriate NIBP performance testing, pressure-release verification, safety-function verification, and overall monitor functional testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting on a potentially unsafe pneumatic system is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

If a cuff remains inflated or repeatedly reinflates abnormally, remove it from the patient promptly and obtain blood pressure with another verified method.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Life Scope G Series NIBP pump ran for an extended period and the cuff did not complete a normal measurement cycle."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a kinked and damaged NIBP hose that restricted the pneumatic path."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering replaced the hose and verified normal inflation, measurement completion, deflation, and repeated NIBP operation with approved test equipment."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact reported NIBP behavior
  • Whether the cuff remained inflated
  • Whether protection activated
  • Cuff type and condition
  • Hose and connector condition
  • Presence of kinks or occlusions
  • Known-good cuff/hose results
  • Leak-test results
  • Deflation behavior
  • Approved analyzer test results
  • Results before and after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient's limb first, then rule out the cuff, hose, connections, leakage, and restrictions before suspecting an internal pneumatic fault. Never bypass pressure protection, and verify safe inflation and deflation before returning the monitor to use.

That is successful troubleshooting.
