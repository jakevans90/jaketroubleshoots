Vaporizer Not Recognized or Interlock Fault

Asset Type

Anesthesia Machine

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Atlan A350

What This Guide Helps With

A vaporizer is not recognized or cannot be used normally because of seating, compatibility, interlock position, damage, contamination, or interface problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Anesthetic Delivery

Do not troubleshoot a vaporizer recognition or interlock problem while a patient depends on the affected vaporizer for anesthetic delivery. Use an appropriate verified alternative and maintain the required anesthetic plan. Expected outcome: The patient receives reliable anesthetic care while the machine and vaporizer interface can be inspected safely.

2. Confirm the Exact Vaporizer Condition

Record which vaporizer is involved, its installed position, the exact displayed message or interlock behavior, and whether the issue follows one particular vaporizer. Reproduce the condition only during a non-patient setup. Expected outcome: The problem is clearly associated with a particular vaporizer, mounting position, or machine condition.

3. Inspect Vaporizer Seating

Turn the vaporizer to its appropriate non-delivery condition before handling. Verify it is completely seated and mechanically secured on the intended mounting position. Do not force a vaporizer into place. Expected outcome: The vaporizer sits fully and securely on its mount. If proper reseating restores normal recognition and interlock operation, perform the required system checks before stopping.

4. Inspect the Vaporizer and Mounting Interface

Look for obvious external damage, contamination, foreign material, bent hardware, or other visible conditions at the vaporizer and mounting interface. Do not disassemble the vaporizer or machine interface. Expected outcome: The external mounting surfaces and hardware appear clean, intact, and unobstructed. If an external obstruction can be safely corrected and recognition returns, proceed to verification.

5. Verify Correct Vaporizer Compatibility and Position

Confirm the installed vaporizer is an approved compatible type for the machine configuration and that it is mounted in an appropriate position. Do not alter machine configuration merely to make an incompatible component function. Expected outcome: The vaporizer and mounting position are appropriate for the Atlan A350 setup. If an incorrect component was installed, replace it with the correct approved component and retest.

6. Check Interlock Operation Externally

With the machine out of patient use, observe whether the mechanical interlock behaves normally when compatible vaporizers are installed and positioned according to approved use. Do not defeat, bypass, restrain, or modify the interlock. Expected outcome: The interlock prevents unintended simultaneous vaporizer activation and operates without binding. If normal operation returns after correct seating, complete system testing.

7. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Vaporizer

If approved by facility practice, install a known-good compatible vaporizer to determine whether the problem follows the original vaporizer or remains with the machine mounting position. Expected outcome: The comparison isolates the issue to the original vaporizer or the machine-side interface. If the known-good vaporizer works normally, remove the suspect vaporizer from use and complete machine verification.

8. Compare Mounting Positions When Appropriate

If the machine has multiple supported vaporizer positions, compare behavior using an approved compatible vaporizer without forcing or modifying the mounts. Expected outcome: The problem either follows a vaporizer or remains associated with a particular mount. A persistent machine-side fault requires escalation.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correction, verify vaporizer recognition, interlock function, applicable system tests, gas delivery, ventilation, and alarms according to approved procedures. Expected outcome: The vaporizer is correctly recognized, the interlock functions normally, and all required tests pass. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate an Unresolved Interlock or Recognition Fault

If a compatible known-good vaporizer is not recognized or the interlock remains unreliable, remove the machine from service. Also remove a suspect vaporizer from service when the fault follows that vaporizer. Expected outcome: Unreliable anesthetic-agent delivery equipment is appropriately isolated for service evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External seating, compatibility, mounting condition, visible contamination, known-good substitution, and interlock operation have been checked. Remaining causes may involve an internal recognition interface, mounting mechanism, interlock assembly, sensing system, configuration, or other service-level condition. The affected machine or vaporizer should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Following repair, complete all applicable vaporizer recognition, interlock, agent-delivery, system, alarm, and return-to-service testing. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never defeat a vaporizer interlock to keep an anesthesia machine available; the interlock is a safety mechanism, not an inconvenience.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Anesthesia staff reported the Drager Atlan A350 did not recognize an installed vaporizer during room setup."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the vaporizer was not completely seated on its mounting position."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering correctly reseated the vaporizer, verified normal recognition and interlock operation, completed the system checks, and returned the machine to service."

Final Thought

Vaporizer recognition and interlock faults directly affect safe anesthetic-agent delivery. Check seating and compatibility first, never bypass safety mechanisms, verify the correction, and escalate unresolved interface problems. That is successful troubleshooting.
