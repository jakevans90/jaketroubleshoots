GE Healthcare AMX Navigate

Collimator Light, Field Size, or Blade Motion Is Incorrect

Asset Type

Mobile X-Ray System

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

AMX Navigate

What This Guide Helps With

The collimator light is unavailable or abnormal, blade movement is irregular, or the indicated radiation field cannot be set correctly.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient From Incorrect Field Selection

Do not perform clinical exposures if the collimator cannot reliably define the intended field. Incorrect collimation can unnecessarily irradiate anatomy outside the desired area.

Expected outcome: Clinical exposure is deferred until field control is verified.

2. Define the Exact Collimator Problem

Determine whether:

  • The light does not illuminate
  • Illumination is weak or intermittent
  • One or more blades do not move
  • The field cannot open or close normally
  • Controls bind or feel abnormal
  • Light-field geometry appears incorrect

Expected outcome: The specific collimator function at fault is identified.

3. Inspect the Collimator Externally

Check for:

  • Impact damage
  • Loose housing
  • Cracked controls
  • Contamination
  • Objects interfering with blade controls
  • Accessories blocking the light field

Expected outcome: No obvious external damage or obstruction is present.

4. Verify Normal System Power

Confirm the AMX Navigate is fully powered and otherwise operating normally.

Expected outcome: The problem is not caused by a general power or startup condition.

5. Operate the Collimator Controls Through Their Normal Range

Without exposing a patient, move the blade controls through a reasonable working range and observe for sticking, asymmetry, or delayed movement.

Expected outcome: Blade controls move smoothly and the visible field changes correspondingly.

6. Check the Light Field on a Flat Test Surface

Project the collimator light onto an appropriate flat surface. Observe whether illumination appears complete and whether the field changes logically as the blades are adjusted.

Expected outcome: The light field is visible and responds consistently to blade movement.

7. Inspect for External Light-Path Obstruction

Check whether tape, accessory hardware, protective covers, contamination, or other external material is blocking or distorting the light field.

Expected outcome: The projected field is unobstructed.

8. Compare Blade Position and Visible Field

Observe whether each control movement produces a corresponding change in the projected field. Do not make internal alignment adjustments without approved procedures and measurement equipment.

Expected outcome: Blade movement and visible-field response are consistent.

9. Perform Approved Field-Alignment Verification if Required

If the complaint concerns field accuracy rather than simple operation, use the facility's approved radiation-quality-control procedure and appropriate test equipment.

Expected outcome: Light and radiation field performance meet applicable approved acceptance criteria. Do not invent or substitute tolerances.

10. Escalate an Unresolved Collimator Problem

If the light, blades, or field alignment remain unreliable, remove the system from clinical exposure use.

Expected outcome: The system is not used when collimation accuracy cannot be assured.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities may involve the collimator lamp or illumination system, blade mechanism, control linkage, internal electronics, alignment, or service-level calibration.

The system should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or controlled evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved radiation test equipment
  • Repaired, aligned, or calibrated only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify light operation, blade movement, and required field alignment before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A functioning image receptor does not compensate for unreliable collimation; verify field control before exposing a patient.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Radiology reported the AMX Navigate collimator light field was partially blocked and difficult to visualize during positioning."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an external accessory attached near the tube head was obstructing part of the projected light field."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Repositioned the accessory, verified full light-field visibility and normal blade movement, and confirmed field operation before returning the system to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Light present or absent
  • Light intensity complaint
  • Blade motion
  • Control condition
  • Impact damage
  • External obstruction
  • Field shape
  • Test-surface observation
  • Alignment verification performed
  • Final functional result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Treat collimation problems as radiation-safety issues. Rule out simple obstruction and control problems first, then use approved measurement procedures whenever field accuracy itself is in question.

That is successful troubleshooting.
