Hillrom Progressa

Battery Not Charging or Bed Loses Functions on Backup Power

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Hillrom

Model

Progressa

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting charging-indicator problems, short battery runtime, and loss of expected bed functions when the Progressa is disconnected from AC power.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot a suspected battery or backup-power failure while the bed is required for active patient positioning, transport, pulmonary therapy, or fall prevention.

  • Notify the responsible clinical staff.
  • Transfer the patient to another appropriate bed when reliable powered functions are clinically necessary.
  • Confirm the brakes are set before testing.
  • Keep the bed connected to AC power until safe testing can be performed.
  • Do not transport a patient on a bed with unreliable backup power unless an approved contingency plan is in place.

Expected outcome: The patient is not dependent on a bed that may lose positioning or safety functions.

2. Clarify the Reported Failure

Determine whether:

  • The battery charged indicator never illuminates.
  • The low-battery indicator remains active while the bed is plugged in.
  • The bed operates normally on AC but becomes unresponsive when unplugged.
  • Bed articulation stops shortly after AC power is removed.
  • Only the touchscreen or surface functions appear unavailable on battery power.
  • The battery changes from charged to low unusually quickly.
  • A Service Required indicator or fault message is present.

Record the exact indicator behavior, audible tones, and affected functions.

Expected outcome: The complaint is separated into a charging problem, weak-battery problem, expected battery-mode limitation, or broader power-system failure.

3. Verify the Wall Outlet

Plug a known-good electrical safety analyzer, outlet tester, or approved test device into the same receptacle.

Check for:

  • Correct voltage.
  • Proper polarity.
  • Secure grounding.
  • Intermittent power.
  • A tripped breaker or wall-switch-controlled outlet.

Do not assume the bed is receiving AC power because the cord is connected.

Expected outcome: The receptacle provides stable and correctly wired AC power.

If the outlet is defective, relocate the bed to a verified outlet and notify Facilities according to hospital policy. Stop if normal charging resumes.

4. Inspect the Power Cord and Plug

Disconnect the bed from AC power and inspect the full accessible length of the power cord.

Look for:

  • Bent or damaged plug blades.
  • Cuts, crushing, or exposed conductors.
  • Loose strain relief.
  • Evidence of arcing.
  • Discoloration or melted areas.
  • Fluid contamination.
  • A loose connection where the cord enters the bed.

Do not use an extension cord or unapproved power strip during testing.

Expected outcome: The power cord and plug are intact and make a secure connection.

Remove the bed from service if the cord, plug, or strain relief is damaged.

5. Confirm That the Bed Recognizes AC Power

Connect the bed directly to a verified wall outlet.

Observe for:

  • Battery charging or charged indication.
  • Normal caregiver-control illumination.
  • Normal touchscreen operation.
  • Removal of the low-battery warning.
  • Any Service Required indicator.
  • Intermittent changes when the cord is moved gently near the plug and strain relief.

The Progressa instructions state that the battery indicator should respond after the bed is connected to AC power. A missing charging indication may suggest that AC power is not reaching the charger or that the charging system requires evaluation.

Expected outcome: The bed consistently recognizes AC power and begins or indicates charging.

If AC recognition is intermittent, stop testing and remove the bed from service.

6. Allow Adequate Charging Time

Leave the bed continuously connected to a verified AC outlet before judging battery performance.

During charging:

  • Avoid repeated unplugging.
  • Confirm the outlet remains energized.
  • Record the initial battery indication.
  • Recheck the battery status after the facility-approved charging period.
  • Confirm the charging indication does not disappear intermittently.

A severely discharged battery may not immediately support bed operation.

Expected outcome: The battery progresses toward a charged indication and maintains that status while connected to AC.

If the battery never indicates charging or charged status, continue to escalation after completing the remaining external checks.

7. Distinguish Expected Battery Behavior From a Failure

Unplug the bed only after it has had adequate time to charge. Press a bed function control to activate the battery-status indication.

On backup power, the Progressa is expected to retain bed articulation. However:

  • The integrated surface remains inflated but does not actively adjust pressures.
  • The Graphical Caregiver Interface screen does not display.
  • Battery-dependent surface or display behavior should not be mistaken for total bed failure.

Expected outcome: The frame articulation controls operate even though certain display and powered-surface functions are unavailable.

If articulation works normally and only the touchscreen or active surface adjustments stop, the bed may be operating as designed on battery power. Stop after confirming normal AC operation.

8. Test Basic Articulation on Battery Power

With the bed unloaded or with the patient safely transferred, disconnect AC power and test basic frame functions individually:

  • Bed up and down.
  • Head section up and down.
  • Knee or thigh section movement.
  • Foot section movement.
  • Trendelenburg and reverse Trendelenburg, when safely permitted.

Do not repeatedly cycle the motors or place unnecessary load on a weak battery.

Expected outcome: Basic articulation operates smoothly without immediate shutdown, dimming indicators, relay chatter, or repeated low-battery alarms.

If all articulation is lost immediately when AC is removed, reconnect the bed and remove it from service.

9. Observe Battery Discharge Behavior

Monitor how quickly the status changes after AC power is removed.

The manufacturer states that if the battery repeatedly changes from charged to low within four hours of disconnection from AC power, the battery should be replaced.

Watch for:

  • Immediate low-battery indication.
  • An intermittent low-battery tone.
  • Rapidly declining charge.
  • Bed functions slowing or stopping.
  • Battery status turning off completely.
  • The bed recovering immediately when reconnected to AC.

Expected outcome: The battery maintains adequate reserve power without an unusually rapid transition to low status.

A repeatable charged-to-low transition in less than four hours supports battery replacement or bench evaluation.

10. Check for Excessive Mechanical Load

Confirm the bed is not being tested under conditions that could produce unusually high current demand.

Check for:

  • Frame components contacting walls or equipment.
  • Linen, cables, or accessories trapped in moving sections.
  • Overloaded accessory mounts.
  • A patient or test load exceeding the bed’s approved capacity.
  • Binding or unusually slow articulation.
  • Motors that continue running after motion should stop.
  • Repeated obstruction detection.

Expected outcome: Bed movement is unobstructed and does not place an excessive load on the battery.

If a simple external obstruction is found, remove it and repeat the test. Stop if normal backup operation returns.

11. Inspect for Environmental or Physical Damage

Look for evidence that could affect the battery or charging system:

  • Fluid intrusion near the base or electrical compartments.
  • Collision damage.
  • Missing covers.
  • Corrosion.
  • Unusual heat.
  • Burning or chemical odor.
  • Swelling or deformation near the battery area.
  • Evidence of previous unauthorized repair.

Do not open the battery enclosure or continue charging a bed that is unusually hot, damaged, or producing an abnormal odor.

Expected outcome: No external evidence suggests an unsafe battery or electrical condition.

Immediately disconnect the bed from AC power and remove it from service if heat, smoke, swelling, or burning odor is present.

12. Perform a Controlled Final Verification

After external causes have been addressed:

  • Connect the bed to a verified AC outlet.
  • Confirm stable AC recognition.
  • Allow adequate charging time.
  • Disconnect AC.
  • Confirm the battery-status indication activates.
  • Test basic articulation.
  • Reconnect AC.
  • Confirm charging resumes.
  • Check for Service Required indicators.
  • Document the final operational status.

Expected outcome: The bed charges normally, retains expected articulation on battery power, and returns to charging when reconnected.

If the issue is resolved, return the bed to service only after completing the facility-required functional and electrical safety checks.

If the Problem Persists

If the Progressa still does not charge, loses basic articulation immediately when unplugged, becomes low shortly after charging, or shows a Service Required indication, common external causes have been ruled out.

The likely causes may include:

  • Degraded or failed batteries.
  • Battery connection problems.
  • AC input or charger failure.
  • Power-management electronics failure.
  • Wiring or battery-status indicator faults.
  • Excessive current draw from a frame component.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Kept connected or disconnected from AC according to the observed safety condition.
  • Sent for battery replacement, bench evaluation, or manufacturer-authorized repair.
  • Tested for proper charging, battery runtime, articulation, alarms, and electrical safety before return to service.

Do not continue cycling the bed in an attempt to revive a battery that will not hold a charge. Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not rely on a Progressa with an uncertain battery during patient transport or when powered positioning may be urgently required. Move the patient to another bed before testing whenever safe operation cannot be assured.

Remember that loss of the touchscreen or active surface pressure adjustment while unplugged can be expected battery-mode behavior. Loss of basic frame articulation is not.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Hillrom Progressa worked while plugged in but lost all bed-positioning functions shortly after AC power was disconnected."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Verified the outlet and power cord were functional; the battery indicated charged but changed to low immediately and could not support frame articulation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the bed from service, labeled it Out of Service, and sent it for battery replacement and charging-system evaluation before functional and electrical safety testing."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Wall outlet tested
  • AC voltage and ground verified
  • Power cord and plug inspected
  • Charging indicator behavior
  • Time allowed for charging
  • Charged-to-low transition time
  • Functions available on AC power
  • Functions available on battery power
  • GCI screen behavior when unplugged
  • Surface behavior when unplugged
  • Low-battery tones or indicators
  • Service Required indicator present
  • Signs of fluid damage
  • Unusual heat, smell, or sound
  • Battery replaced or referred for replacement
  • Functional test completed
  • Electrical safety test completed
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Patient safety comes first when a hospital bed cannot provide dependable backup power. Verify the outlet, cord, charging indication, expected battery-mode limitations, and external mechanical conditions before suspecting internal failure. When the battery cannot reliably support basic articulation, remove the bed from service and document the complaint, findings, and resolution clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
