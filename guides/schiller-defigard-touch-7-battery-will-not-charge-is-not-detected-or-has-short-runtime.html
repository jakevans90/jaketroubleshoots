Battery Will Not Charge, Is Not Detected, or Has Short Runtime

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Schiller

Model

DEFIGARD Touch 7

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots battery detection, charging, and runtime problems involving seating, contacts, charging source, battery condition, or power-system faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Maintain Emergency Defibrillator Availability

If battery condition is questionable, place another verified defibrillator into service before troubleshooting.

A defibrillator with unreliable battery operation should not be assigned to transport or emergency response.

Expected outcome: Patient care and emergency response remain protected.

2. Confirm the Reported Battery Symptom

Determine whether the battery:

  • Is not detected.
  • Does not charge.
  • Charges only intermittently.
  • Appears charged but discharges rapidly.
  • Causes shutdown when AC is removed.
  • Produces an unexpected battery warning.

Record the displayed status.

Expected outcome: The battery problem is clearly reproduced or characterized.

3. Verify AC and Charging Source

Confirm the unit or charging bracket is receiving reliable AC power.

Inspect:

  • Power cord.
  • External supply, if applicable.
  • Outlet operation.
  • Charging indication.

Expected outcome: A valid charging source is present. If restoring AC power resolves the problem, proceed to final verification.

4. Remove and Reinstall the Battery

With the unit safely removed from patient use, remove and reinstall the battery according to normal approved handling practices.

Check that it seats and latches correctly.

Expected outcome: The battery is securely installed and recognized.

5. Inspect Battery and Device Contacts

Inspect accessible battery contacts and mating surfaces for:

  • Dirt.
  • Bent contacts.
  • Corrosion.
  • Foreign material.
  • Cracks.
  • Swelling or physical damage.

Do not use a battery showing physical damage or abnormal heat.

Expected outcome: Contacts are clean and undamaged and the battery is physically safe for testing.

6. Substitute a Known-Good Battery

Install an approved known-good compatible battery.

Observe whether the device detects and charges it normally.

Expected outcome: A known-good battery operates normally. If the original battery alone fails, remove the original battery from service and replace it according to facility policy.

7. Evaluate Short Runtime

If the battery is detected and charges but runtime is poor, fully charge it using approved procedures and perform the applicable battery-performance evaluation.

Do not assume a runtime problem based only on an estimated display value.

Expected outcome: Battery performance meets the applicable manufacturer or facility requirement. If not, replace the battery.

8. Check Operation During AC Removal

Using an approved nonclinical test setup, verify that the defibrillator remains operational when external power is removed.

Expected outcome: The unit transfers to battery operation without unexpected shutdown or reset.

9. Perform Final Verification

Confirm battery detection, charging indication, battery operation, and return to charging when external power is restored.

Expected outcome: Battery and charging behavior are stable and repeatable. If so, return the unit to service according to policy.

If the Problem Persists

External power, battery seating, contacts, and known-good battery substitution have been ruled out. The remaining condition may involve the charging system, battery interface, power-management circuitry, or another internal service-level fault.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using approved manufacturer documentation and test equipment.
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel.

Complete battery, charging, operational, and electrical-safety verification before return to service when applicable.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Verify battery operation before transport; a defibrillator that works only while connected to AC is not suitable for mobile emergency use.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported that the DEFIGARD Touch 7 shut down shortly after being disconnected from AC power."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found that the installed battery had substantially reduced usable runtime while a known-good battery operated normally."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the battery and verified charging, AC-to-battery operation, and acceptable battery performance before returning the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Battery detected or not detected.
  • Charging indication.
  • AC source tested.
  • Battery physical condition.
  • Contact condition.
  • Known-good battery result.
  • Runtime-test result.
  • Behavior during AC removal.
  • Final charging status.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Treat battery reliability as part of defibrillator readiness. Verify power, seating, contacts, and battery condition with controlled testing before assuming an internal failure, and escalate when dependable battery operation cannot be demonstrated.

That is successful troubleshooting.
