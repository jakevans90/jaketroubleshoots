Stryker LIFEPAK 35

Printer not printing / paper feed failure

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

LIFEPAK 35

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting absent print output, failed paper advance, intermittent feeding, paper loading problems, and external causes affecting the optional LIFEPAK 35 printer.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot the printer while the LIFEPAK 35 is supporting active patient care.

If printed documentation is operationally required and unavailable, provide clinical staff with another verified device or approved documentation method before evaluation.

Expected: Patient care and required clinical documentation are not dependent on a device with an unresolved printer problem.

Why it matters: A printer fault may not prevent monitoring or therapy, but troubleshooting must never interfere with emergency readiness.

Verify the Exact Printer Complaint

Confirm what is actually occurring:

  • Printer does not start
  • Paper does not advance
  • Printer runs but produces no visible print
  • Paper feeds intermittently
  • Paper jams or feeds crooked
  • Printing stops partway through a report
  • Manual printing fails while other print behavior appears normal

Attempt a controlled print using an appropriate non-patient test condition.

Expected: The failure is reproduced consistently enough to separate a printer, paper-feed, or print-visibility problem.

If printing works normally: Document the result, complete required operational verification, and stop.

Check Whether the Device Has the Printer Option

Confirm the unit is configured with the optional printer and that the complaint applies to the installed hardware. Stryker identifies the LIFEPAK 35 printer as an optional component.

Expected: Installed printer hardware is present and available for testing.

If no printer is installed: The complaint is a configuration or expectation issue rather than a paper-feed failure. Document and stop.

Inspect the Paper Supply

Open the printer area and verify:

  • Paper is present
  • Roll is not depleted
  • Roll is not crushed or deformed
  • Paper is not wet or contaminated
  • Paper is not tightly bound or sticking to itself
  • Paper path is not visibly obstructed by torn paper

Use the correct specified printer paper for the LIFEPAK 35. Stryker documentation identifies a 100 mm printer-paper format for the platform.

Expected: A clean, compatible paper roll is available and can unwind freely.

If replacing the paper resolves the issue: Verify repeat printing and stop.

Reload the Paper Correctly

Remove and reload the paper according to the approved LIFEPAK 35 loading method.

Verify that:

  • Roll is seated correctly
  • Paper exits through the intended path
  • Paper is not folded under the roll
  • Paper is not pinched at the door
  • Sufficient paper extends into the feed path
  • Printer compartment closes fully

The LIFEPAK 35 service documentation directs technicians to the device Operating Instructions for printer-paper loading.

Expected: Paper sits squarely in the compartment and is not mechanically restricted.

If normal printing returns: Perform multiple print attempts to confirm reliable feeding, then stop.

Check Paper Orientation When Paper Feeds but Appears Blank

If the paper advances normally but no visible trace appears:

  • Remove the roll
  • Verify thermal paper orientation
  • Reload it correctly
  • Repeat the controlled print test

Expected: Printed information becomes visible when the thermal-sensitive surface is correctly presented to the print mechanism.

If visible printing returns: Confirm repeatable output and stop.

Inspect the Printer Door and Closure

Check the printer door for:

  • Incomplete closure
  • Paper trapped at the edge
  • Visible damage
  • Loose fit
  • Debris preventing full seating

Open the door, clear accessible paper or debris without disassembling the printer, reload the roll, and close the compartment securely.

Expected: The door closes fully and the paper path remains aligned.

If the printer operates normally: Repeat the print test and stop.

Inspect for an Accessible Paper Jam

With the device removed from patient use:

  • Open the printer compartment
  • Remove loose or torn paper
  • Check for folded paper at the accessible feed entrance
  • Inspect for adhesive residue or foreign material
  • Do not probe deeply into the printer mechanism with metal tools
  • Do not force rollers or gears

Expected: The externally accessible paper path is clear.

If clearing the obstruction restores operation: Reload paper, perform repeated print tests, and stop.

Test with a Known-Good Paper Roll

Install a fresh, known-good compatible roll.

This helps rule out:

  • Incorrect paper
  • Damaged roll
  • Excessive paper curl
  • Contaminated thermal stock
  • Roll deformation
  • Paper binding

Expected: Printer output and paper advance become consistent.

If the known-good roll resolves the problem: Remove the suspect paper from service, verify multiple prints, document, and stop.

Perform a Controlled Power Restart

After confirming the device is not supporting a patient:

  • Note any displayed messages or abnormal behavior
  • Power the LIFEPAK 35 down normally
  • Restart the device
  • Allow normal startup to complete
  • Repeat the printer test

Expected: A transient control condition clears and printing returns.

If printing returns: Repeat the test several times and complete required operational verification before return to service.

If the fault immediately returns: Continue troubleshooting.

Determine Whether the Failure Is Mechanical or Command-Related

Observe the printer during a controlled print request.

No sound and no paper movement:
The printer may not be receiving or executing the print command.

Motor or feed sound but no movement:
Suspect paper loading, obstruction, feed engagement, or internal printer mechanism failure.

Paper moves but output is blank:
Recheck paper orientation and compatible thermal paper before suspecting the print mechanism.

Paper feeds crooked, slips, or repeatedly jams:
Suspect alignment, door seating, paper condition, or internal feed mechanism problems.

Expected: The failure pattern is narrowed without internal disassembly.

Review Available Device Status and Service Information

Check for:

  • Printer-related messages
  • Service indication
  • Repeated startup abnormalities
  • Other concurrent device faults
  • Recent drop, impact, fluid exposure, or contamination history

Record exact messages rather than paraphrasing them.

Expected: Any broader device condition associated with the printer failure is identified.

If a Service indication or additional unresolved fault is present: Remove the device from service and escalate.

Perform Required Post-Troubleshooting Verification

After any apparent correction:

  • Repeat manual printing
  • Confirm consistent paper advance
  • Confirm visible print output
  • Check for skewing or repeated jams
  • Verify the printer door remains secure
  • Complete applicable facility inspection and operational testing

Stryker publishes a LIFEPAK 35 Performance Inspection Procedure, and its inspection documentation includes printer output as a method for waveform verification.

Expected: Printer operation is repeatable and the device passes required verification.

If printer performance remains intermittent: Do not return the unit to service based on one successful print.

If the Problem Persists

If correct paper, proper loading, paper orientation, accessible obstruction, printer-door seating, controlled restart, and known-good paper have been ruled out, the problem is likely internal to the printer assembly, feed mechanism, printer interface, wiring, or associated electronics.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation

Do not proceed into deep printer disassembly or board-level repair unless specifically authorized, trained, and working under approved Stryker service procedures.

Knowing when external causes have been eliminated and when to stop is proper troubleshooting. Stryker’s current LIFEPAK 35 service manual is the appropriate model-specific reference for authorized service work beyond these checks.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot printer feeding while the LIFEPAK 35 is actively supporting a patient. Move clinical care to another verified device first when printer availability is required for safe workflow or documentation.

A printer problem should also not distract from verifying that the defibrillator remains fully ready for emergency monitoring and therapy.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported the LIFEPAK 35 printer would not print and paper failed to advance when a print was requested."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Found the thermal paper roll incorrectly seated and binding at the printer compartment exit, preventing consistent paper feed."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reloaded compatible printer paper correctly, confirmed secure printer-door closure, and verified multiple successful print cycles with consistent paper advance and visible output."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Printer option confirmed installed
  • Exact complaint reproduced
  • Paper present
  • Correct paper type verified
  • Paper orientation checked
  • Known-good paper roll tested
  • Printer door fully seated
  • Accessible paper path inspected
  • Jam or torn paper found
  • Paper feed sound present or absent
  • Blank paper versus no paper movement documented
  • Printer-related message recorded exactly
  • Service indication present or absent
  • Recent impact or fluid exposure reported
  • Controlled restart performed
  • Repeat printing verified
  • Applicable operational inspection completed
  • Final device status documented

Final Thought

Printer troubleshooting should begin with patient safety, paper condition, loading, orientation, accessible obstructions, and repeatable testing before internal failure is assumed. Escalating an intermittent or unresolved feed problem protects device readiness, and clear CCR documentation preserves exactly what was reported, found, and verified.

That is successful troubleshooting.
