LIFEPAK 1000

What This Guide Helps With

Helps troubleshoot self-test failures related to battery, accessories, connections, and external conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Confirm Patient Safety and Equipment Availability

Remove the device from emergency availability if self-test failure prevents readiness confirmation. Provide a verified replacement defibrillator if needed. Expected outcome: Patient care capability is maintained.

2. Confirm the Self-Test Failure Condition

Verify the reported failure and document the readiness indicator or device status. Expected outcome: Specific failure condition is confirmed.

3. Check Battery Condition

Verify battery installation and condition. Replace with a known-good compatible battery if available. Expected outcome: Self-test completes if battery condition caused the failure.

4. Inspect Electrodes and External Accessories

Verify electrodes and accessories are properly installed and not expired, damaged, or improperly connected. Expected outcome: External accessory issues are corrected.

5. Inspect Device Condition

Check for physical damage, contamination, or environmental exposure. Expected outcome: External causes are ruled out.

6. Recheck Device Readiness

Allow the device to complete normal readiness checks and verify status. Expected outcome: Device passes self-test and indicates ready status. Troubleshooting can stop when readiness is restored.

7. Escalate Continued Self-Test Failure

If self-test failure remains after external checks, remove the device from service. Expected outcome: Device is routed for qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return-to-service testing should confirm defibrillator readiness before clinical use.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not place a defibrillator into emergency service if readiness status cannot be verified.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the LIFEPAK 1000 failed its automatic readiness check."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the battery connection was intermittent due to improper seating."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering corrected the battery installation, verified successful readiness status, and returned the device to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Self-test result
  • Readiness indicator status
  • Battery condition
  • Electrode status
  • Physical inspection findings
  • Final verification performed

Final Thought

Reliable defibrillator readiness requires confirming the actual cause of a self-test failure before assuming internal problems. Safe troubleshooting includes verification, escalation, and complete documentation. That is successful troubleshooting.
