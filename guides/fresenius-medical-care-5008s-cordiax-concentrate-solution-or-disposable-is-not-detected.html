Concentrate, Solution, or Disposable Is Not Detected

Asset Type

Hemodialysis (HD) Machine

Manufacturer

Fresenius Medical Care

Model

5008S CorDiax

What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when the machine does not recognize a required concentrate, solution, connector, or disposable because of setup, seating, identification, or interface problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Do Not Bypass Recognition Checks

Do not begin or continue treatment when a required concentrate, solution, or disposable is not recognized. Move the patient to a verified machine if the condition cannot be promptly corrected before treatment.

Do not bypass recognition or safety interlocks.

Expected outcome: Treatment does not proceed with an unverified fluid or disposable setup.

2. Confirm What Is Not Being Detected

Identify the specific concentrate, solution, connector, disposable, or related component named by the machine. Record the exact displayed message and whether the issue occurs every time or only with one item.

Expected outcome: The affected item and recognition point are clearly identified.

3. Verify the Correct Item Is Installed

Confirm the component is approved for the 5008S CorDiax and appropriate for the intended treatment setup. Check labeling, packaging, condition, and obvious compatibility.

Expected outcome: The correct item is being used. If the wrong component is found, replace it with the correct item and proceed to final verification.

4. Inspect Seating and Connection

Remove and reinstall the item when safe, ensuring connectors, holders, caps, pickup points, and other accessible interfaces are fully seated.

Inspect for bent, damaged, wet, dirty, or obstructed external connection surfaces.

Expected outcome: The item is fully and correctly installed. If recognition returns and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

5. Inspect the Item for Damage

Check the disposable, container, connector, or solution interface for cracks, deformation, damaged identification features, leakage, contamination, or packaging damage.

Do not attempt to modify a disposable to make it detectable.

Expected outcome: The installed item is physically intact and suitable for use.

6. Substitute a Known-Good Approved Item

Replace the questionable component with a verified compatible item. If the problem follows the original item, remove that item from use according to facility practice.

Expected outcome: If a known-good item is detected normally, the original item or its setup was the cause and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

7. Inspect the Accessible Recognition Area

Check the applicable accessible machine interface for residue, fluid, foreign material, or obvious physical damage. Clean only according to approved procedures.

Do not open the recognition assembly or alter internal sensors.

Expected outcome: The external recognition area is clean and unobstructed.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm the machine consistently recognizes the required concentrate, solution, or disposable through normal setup and preparation without recurrent messages.

Expected outcome: Recognition remains stable and normal preparation can proceed. Troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

If item compatibility, seating, physical condition, known-good substitution, and accessible interface cleanliness have been verified, the problem may involve an internal identification sensor, connector interface, switch, optical or electronic sensing function, software configuration, or control subsystem.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Fresenius Medical Care documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
  • Fully verified for component recognition and normal machine preparation before return to service.

Knowing when to stop prevents an unsafe workaround from replacing a required recognition function.

Clinical Use Tip

If a required disposable or fluid is not recognized, verify the complete setup rather than repeatedly removing and reinserting the same questionable component.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Dialysis staff reported the 5008S CorDiax would not recognize the installed concentrate connection during setup."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the concentrate connector was not fully seated in the machine interface."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The connector was correctly installed, recognition remained normal through preparation, and the machine was returned to service after functional verification."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact item not detected.
  • Exact displayed message.
  • Product or disposable identification.
  • Connection and seating condition.
  • Packaging or physical damage.
  • Signs of contamination or fluid.
  • Known-good substitution result.
  • Recognition behavior after correction.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Recognition failures are often external setup or component problems, but required safety checks should never be bypassed; verify compatibility and seating first and escalate when detection remains unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
