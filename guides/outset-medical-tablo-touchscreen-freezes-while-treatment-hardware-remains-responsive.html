Outset Medical Tablo

Touchscreen Freezes While Treatment Hardware Remains Responsive

Asset Type

Hemodialysis (HD) Machine

Manufacturer

Outset Medical

Model

Tablo

What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when the touchscreen stops responding or updating while other Tablo hardware appears powered or operational.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient Before Evaluating the User Interface

A frozen touchscreen can prevent the operator from reliably viewing treatment information, responding to alarms, or controlling therapy.

Do not continue relying on the affected Tablo solely because pumps, indicators, or other hardware still appear active. If a patient is connected, clinical personnel should maintain direct patient observation and follow the approved process for safely transitioning or discontinuing therapy as necessary before technical troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The patient is no longer dependent on an interface that cannot be reliably monitored or controlled. If normal display and touch functionality are restored and verified before clinical use, troubleshooting can stop.

2. Confirm the Exact Type of Freeze

Determine whether:

  • The image is frozen but still visible
  • The display updates but does not respond to touch
  • Only one screen or application function is stuck
  • Touch response is intermittent
  • The screen is blank but the machine remains powered
  • Alarm audio or physical indicators continue functioning
  • Other external controls respond normally

Document what remained functional during the event.

Expected outcome: The problem is characterized as a display-update, touch-input, or broader user-interface failure. If the issue cannot be reproduced after controlled testing, document the event and continue verification before return to service.

3. Check for Surface Contamination or Unintended Touch Input

Inspect the touchscreen for:

  • Water
  • Cleaning solution
  • Conductive residue
  • Gloves or objects resting against the screen
  • Protective material interfering with touch
  • Physical damage or cracking

Clean and dry the screen using approved methods.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen surface is clean, dry, unobstructed, and undamaged. If normal touch operation returns and remains stable, proceed to final verification.

4. Check External Power Stability

Verify that the machine is connected to a known-good power source and that the power cord and plug are fully seated and undamaged. Check for evidence of intermittent AC power, loose receptacle contact, damaged cord strain relief, or repeated transitions between power sources.

Do not assume a touchscreen problem is software-related until unstable external power has been excluded.

Expected outcome: Stable external power is confirmed with no evidence of interruptions. If correcting an external power problem restores stable operation, complete final verification and troubleshooting can stop.

5. Observe Whether Other System Functions Continue to Update

Without placing a patient at risk, observe accessible system indicators and hardware behavior to determine whether the freeze is limited to the touchscreen or reflects a broader control-system problem.

Do not manipulate therapy hardware simply to provoke a response.

Expected outcome: The extent of the failure is understood. A touchscreen-only freeze should still be treated as a significant control and monitoring problem requiring reliable correction before return to service.

6. Perform a Controlled Restart When Safe

Once the device is no longer supporting a patient and the treatment workflow has been safely terminated as required, perform a normal controlled shutdown and restart using the approved procedure.

Avoid removing power abruptly unless required by an authorized service procedure or immediate safety condition.

Expected outcome: The Tablo boots normally, the touchscreen responds promptly, and the interface updates correctly. If the freeze immediately returns or boot does not complete normally, remove the unit from service.

7. Test Touchscreen Operation Across Normal Screens

After restart, operate the touchscreen through normal non-treatment setup screens and verify:

  • Consistent touch response
  • Correct registration of selections
  • Normal screen transitions
  • No delayed or repeated input
  • No unexplained interface freezes
  • Normal display updates

Do not access unauthorized service functions.

Expected outcome: Touch operation remains stable across multiple normal screens. If any intermittent freezing remains, the device should not be returned to clinical use.

8. Check for Environmental Contributors

Verify that the unit is not being operated in conditions that could contribute to interface instability, including:

  • Excessive moisture
  • Recent liquid spill
  • Heavy condensation
  • Obstructed external ventilation
  • Unusual heat around the display or electronics
  • Strong mechanical impact or recent transport damage

Expected outcome: No environmental or physical condition is present that could reasonably explain the freeze. If an environmental issue is corrected, repeat the operational test before considering the issue resolved.

9. Repeat a Normal Setup Workflow

With no patient connected, perform an appropriate normal setup sequence and observe the touchscreen throughout the workflow.

Confirm that the interface continues to update and accepts commands without delay or freezing.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen remains fully responsive throughout the controlled setup sequence. Troubleshooting can stop if the behavior is stable and all required functional checks pass.

10. Complete Return-to-Service Verification

Before returning the machine to clinical use, verify normal startup, display operation, touch response, alarm annunciation where appropriate, and other required functional checks according to approved procedures.

Expected outcome: The user interface remains reliable through repeated operation with no recurrence. Troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

If touchscreen cleanliness, external power, environmental conditions, controlled restart, and normal operating workflows have been verified, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve the touchscreen assembly, display electronics, internal cabling, processor or control system, software state, power distribution, or another service-level issue.

Because the touchscreen is part of the operator's monitoring and control path, intermittent operation is not acceptable simply because treatment hardware appears to remain active.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Outset Medical documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired, updated, or configured only by qualified personnel

Any repair affecting the interface or control system should be followed by appropriate functional, alarm, and system verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop troubleshooting a control-interface failure is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A responsive pump or fluid system does not make treatment safe if the operator cannot reliably view alarms, treatment status, or control functions.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Tablo touchscreen stopped responding during setup although the machine remained powered."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found moisture and cleaning residue across the touchscreen surface that was causing unreliable touch response."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The touchscreen was cleaned and dried using the approved method, normal touch operation was restored, and repeated setup and interface testing passed before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact screen where the freeze occurred
  • Whether the image continued updating
  • Whether touch input failed completely or intermittently
  • Alarm audio behavior
  • Other hardware functions that remained responsive
  • AC power condition
  • Evidence of moisture or cleaning residue
  • Whether the device restarted normally
  • Whether the problem recurred after restart
  • Results of repeated touchscreen testing
  • Final device status

Final Thought

A touchscreen freeze on a dialysis machine is a control and monitoring concern even when other hardware appears functional. Protect the patient first, rule out simple surface, power, and environmental causes, verify stable operation, and escalate intermittent failures rather than accepting temporary recovery.

That is successful troubleshooting.
