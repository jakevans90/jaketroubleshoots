Caster Alignment Causes Pulling, Vibration, or Poor Steering

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

Secure II

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot pulling, vibration, or difficult steering caused by caster obstruction, wheel damage, floor conditions, or brake/steer mechanism problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Safe Transport Conditions

Determine whether the reported steering problem occurred during patient transport.

Do not transport a patient using a bed that pulls unexpectedly, vibrates excessively, or cannot be controlled safely.

Transfer the patient to another verified bed when necessary.

Move the affected unoccupied bed to a clear, level testing area.

Expected outcome: The bed can be inspected without exposing patients or staff to uncontrolled movement.

2. Confirm the Steering Complaint

Ask whether the bed:

  • Pulls toward one side
  • Vibrates during movement
  • Requires excessive steering force
  • Produces wheel noise
  • Behaves differently in steer and neutral modes

Determine whether the issue occurs consistently or only in specific locations.

Expected outcome: The steering behavior and operating conditions are identified.

3. Inspect Floor Conditions

Check the test area for uneven flooring, debris, thresholds, or damaged surfaces.

Move the unoccupied bed across a suitable level surface.

Compare the reported behavior with the original location.

Expected outcome: Floor-related resistance is distinguished from a bed-related problem.

4. Inspect All Accessible Casters

Examine each caster for visible damage, embedded debris, wrapped material, or uneven wheel wear.

Look for loose or damaged visible components.

Do not place hands beneath an unsupported bed or attempt unsafe lifting.

Expected outcome: Casters are free of external obstructions and obvious damage.

5. Check Brake Release

Verify that the brake control fully releases.

Carefully move the unoccupied bed and observe whether any caster appears to drag.

Inspect accessible brake/steer controls for incomplete travel or interference.

Expected outcome: All casters roll without brake drag when released.

6. Check Steering Engagement

Select the steering mode.

Move the bed a short distance in a clear area.

Observe directional tracking and whether the designated steering caster or mechanism behaves as intended.

Return to neutral mode and compare movement.

Expected outcome: Steering engagement produces predictable directional control.

7. Inspect External Loading and Accessories

Check for equipment or accessories that may contact the floor or interfere with caster movement.

Confirm that the bed is not being pulled by attached cables or hoses.

Verify that the bed is being tested under appropriate manufacturer-approved loading conditions.

Expected outcome: External equipment does not influence steering or caster operation.

8. Perform Final Rolling and Steering Verification

Move the unoccupied bed forward, backward, and through controlled turns.

Check for pulling, vibration, wheel noise, or unexpected resistance.

Verify brake application and release.

If all caster and steering functions operate normally, troubleshooting can stop.

Expected outcome: The bed rolls smoothly, tracks predictably, and brakes reliably.

If the Problem Persists

Floor conditions, caster obstructions, brake release, steering selection, and external accessories have been checked.

Remaining causes may involve caster bearings, wheel assemblies, steering alignment, brake linkage, or mechanical mounting components.

Remove the bed from service, label it Out of Service, and arrange qualified repair or bench evaluation using Stryker documentation and approved test equipment.

Do not attempt undocumented caster alignment adjustments.

Complete transport, steering, and brake verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A bed that pulls to one side can become difficult to control during turns or while passing through doorways, especially with a patient and attached equipment.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Transport staff reported that the Secure II bed pulled to the right and vibrated while being pushed."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found material wrapped around the right-side caster wheel, restricting rotation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the material and verified smooth rolling, normal steering, and reliable brake engagement."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Direction of pulling
  • Speed or conditions when vibration occurs
  • Floor condition
  • Caster inspection findings
  • Wheel damage or debris
  • Brake release results
  • Steering mode tested
  • External accessories
  • Unusual noise
  • Final rolling and brake test results

Final Thought

Safe bed transport requires smooth caster movement and reliable directional control. Begin with floor conditions, obstructions, and brake operation before suspecting internal mechanical failure. Escalate unresolved problems and document the final transport verification.

That is successful troubleshooting.
