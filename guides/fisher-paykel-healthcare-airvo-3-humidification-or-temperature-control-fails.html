Fisher & Paykel Healthcare Airvo 3

Humidification or Temperature Control Fails

Asset Type

High-Flow Oxygen System

Manufacturer

Fisher & Paykel Healthcare

Model

Airvo 3

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots inadequate heating, humidification, temperature control, chamber setup, water supply, heated-tube connections, and environmental causes.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Therapy

Do not continue patient use when required humidification or temperature control is unreliable. Transfer the patient to an appropriate verified respiratory support system if troubleshooting would interrupt therapy.

Expected outcome: Patient therapy remains safe while the affected system is evaluated.

2. Confirm the Humidification or Temperature Complaint

Determine whether the Airvo 3 fails to warm, produces a temperature-related alarm, has excessive or inadequate condensation, does not recognize the heated breathing system, or otherwise fails to reach its normal operating state.

Record exact displayed messages and settings.

Expected outcome: The specific heating or humidification failure is identified.

3. Inspect the Humidifier Chamber

Verify that the approved chamber is correctly installed and seated. Inspect it for damage, leakage, contamination, incorrect assembly, or other obvious abnormalities.

Expected outcome: The chamber is correctly installed and physically intact.

4. Verify Water Supply and Chamber Filling

Confirm that the humidification setup has an appropriate water supply and that the chamber is filling as intended for its approved configuration. Inspect external water-supply components for kinks, clamps, poor positioning, disconnection, or empty supply.

Expected outcome: Water is available to the chamber without an external supply restriction.

5. Inspect the Heated Breathing Tube

Check the tube for damage, incorrect connection, excessive condensate, kinks, or poor routing. Inspect accessible electrical/contact areas for contamination or poor seating without probing internal contacts.

Expected outcome: The heated breathing tube is correctly connected and undamaged.

6. Verify Compatible Accessories

Confirm that the chamber, heated breathing tube, patient interface, and related accessories are approved and compatible with the Airvo 3 setup.

Expected outcome: Humidification is not being compromised by an incompatible accessory.

7. Review Temperature Controls and Configuration

Confirm that user-accessible temperature settings and configuration match the intended clinical setup. Do not alter restricted parameters or use unauthorized service menus.

Expected outcome: The complaint is not caused by an incorrect accessible setting.

8. Check Environmental and Positioning Conditions

Ensure the device and circuit are positioned as intended, ventilation openings are unobstructed, the breathing tube is not exposed to unusual cooling or heat sources, and the equipment is being used in an appropriate environment.

Expected outcome: External environmental conditions are not interfering with heating or humidification.

9. Substitute Known-Good External Components

If the chamber or heated breathing tube remains suspect, replace it with a known-good approved compatible component and repeat the test.

Expected outcome: Normal heating and humidification with the replacement component identifies the original accessory as defective.

10. Verify Complete Humidification Operation

Allow the device to operate under a controlled nonpatient test condition and verify normal warm-up, temperature indication, airflow, alarms, and humidification behavior according to applicable procedures.

Expected outcome: Humidification and temperature control operate normally. Troubleshooting can stop after required return-to-service checks.

11. Escalate Persistent Heating Problems

If water supply, chamber, tubing, accessories, settings, and environmental conditions are correct but the fault persists, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The Airvo 3 is removed from service for qualified heating, sensing, control, or power-system evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External water supply, chamber installation, heated breathing tube, accessory compatibility, settings, and environmental conditions have been ruled out. Persistent problems may involve heating elements, temperature sensing, power control, electronics, or other internal service-level functions.

Remove the device from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Fisher & Paykel Healthcare documentation and approved test equipment. Internal heating-system repair or calibration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify normal warm-up, humidification, airflow, alarms, and temperature-control functions.

Clinical Use Tip

Keep the humidification chamber and breathing circuit positioned correctly during use; poor routing can contribute to water-management and temperature problems even when the device itself is functioning normally.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Airvo 3 was not humidifying properly and displayed abnormal temperature behavior."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the heated breathing tube connection was not fully seated."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering reconnected the heated breathing tube, verified normal warm-up, humidification, airflow, and alarms, and returned the unit to service after functional testing."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact temperature or humidification message
  • Chamber condition and installation
  • Water supply condition
  • Heated breathing tube condition
  • Connector condition
  • Temperature setting observed
  • Condensation noted
  • Environmental conditions
  • Known-good accessories tested
  • Final functional status

Final Thought

Humidification problems often begin with external chamber, water, tubing, connection, or environmental conditions. Verify these before assuming an internal heating failure, and remove the device from service when reliable temperature control cannot be demonstrated.

That is successful troubleshooting.
