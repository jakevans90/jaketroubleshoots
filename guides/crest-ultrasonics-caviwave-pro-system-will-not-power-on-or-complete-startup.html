System Will Not Power On or Complete Startup

What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when the CaviWave Pro is completely unresponsive, starts incompletely, or does not reach its normal ready condition.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patient Safety and Processing Continuity

Remove the ultrasonic cleaner from processing use if it cannot reliably power on or complete startup. Do not place instruments into a unit whose operating state cannot be confirmed.

Move instrument processing to another validated cleaning method or available unit according to department procedure.

Expected outcome: Processing continues on verified equipment while the affected cleaner is evaluated safely.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Ask staff what occurred and reproduce the problem without a load when safe. Determine whether the system is completely dead, briefly powers and shuts down, freezes during startup, or displays an alarm or message.

Record any visible indicators or messages exactly rather than paraphrasing them.

Expected outcome: The failure mode is clearly identified and repeatable, or the device starts normally and the original complaint can be investigated as intermittent.

3. Verify Facility Power

Inspect the power cord, plug, receptacle, disconnect, and any accessible facility power controls associated with the cleaner. Check for obvious damage, looseness, heat discoloration, or a partially disconnected plug.

Verify the receptacle or supplied branch power using an appropriate approved method.

Do not repeatedly reset a breaker or disconnect that will not remain set.

Expected outcome: Stable facility power is confirmed at the unit. If restoring an external power connection corrects startup and the system operates normally, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

4. Inspect Accessible Power Connections and Controls

Verify that the main power control is in the correct operating position and that accessible cords or connectors have not been pulled loose during cleaning, movement, or service activity.

Look for liquid intrusion, damaged insulation, burnt odor, unusual heat, or visible physical damage.

Expected outcome: External power connections are secure and no unsafe condition is present. Any sign of overheating, liquid intrusion, or electrical damage requires removal from service.

5. Check Emergency Stops or External Interlocks if Equipped

Inspect any externally accessible emergency-stop, safety switch, lid/door position, or similar interlock that can inhibit startup or operation.

Do not bypass or defeat an interlock.

Expected outcome: All external safety controls are in their normal operating state. If correcting an external interlock condition restores normal startup, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

6. Remove Nonessential External Accessories

Disconnect only nonessential external peripherals that can be safely removed, such as optional data connections or tracking accessories, then attempt a normal startup.

Do not disconnect plumbing, protective grounding, or required safety devices merely to force startup.

Expected outcome: The system completes startup with required connections intact. If one optional peripheral consistently prevents startup, leave that peripheral disconnected only if permitted and escalate the accessory separately.

7. Perform a Controlled Power Cycle

With the unit unloaded and not in clinical processing use, shut it down using the normal control method. Allow the system to fully power down, then restart it once.

Avoid repeated rapid cycling.

Expected outcome: The cleaner progresses through startup and reaches its normal ready state without freezing, resetting, or alarming. If successful, continue to functional verification.

8. Observe Startup Behavior

Watch the display, indicators, pumps, valves, and other externally observable startup activity. Note the exact point where startup stops or repeats.

Do not enter restricted service modes or alter protected configuration settings.

Expected outcome: Startup either completes normally or a consistent failure point is documented for escalation.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Once the system starts normally, verify that the user interface responds, the chamber is empty and safe, no abnormal leaks or noises are present, and an appropriate unloaded or department-approved functional check can be completed.

Expected outcome: The unit starts consistently and basic operation is normal. The issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate an Unresolved Startup Failure

If verified power is present but the cleaner remains dead, resets, freezes, or cannot complete startup, stop external troubleshooting.

Do not proceed into internal power supplies, control boards, wiring assemblies, or protected service functions without approved service documentation and authorization.

Expected outcome: The device remains removed from service and is routed for qualified bench or manufacturer-level evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external power, connection, control, and peripheral causes have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal power distribution, control electronics, startup software, safety interlocks, or other service-level functions.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After corrective work, complete appropriate operational and electrical safety testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not begin instrument processing in a cleaner that has shown intermittent startup, unexplained resets, or incomplete initialization until reliable operation is verified.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported that the CaviWave Pro would not power on at the beginning of the shift."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the unit's external power connection was loose and facility power at the receptacle was otherwise normal."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Secured the power connection, restarted the cleaner, verified normal startup and basic operation, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Completely dead versus partial startup
  • Exact startup message or alarm
  • Indicator-light behavior
  • Receptacle or facility power verification
  • Power cord and plug condition
  • Whether the issue followed cleaning, movement, or outage
  • External peripheral connections
  • Unusual odor, heat, sound, or visible damage
  • Results before and after power cycle
  • Final operational status

Final Thought

Start with patient safety and verified facility power before assuming an internal failure. Progress logically, document the exact startup behavior, verify successful operation after correction, and escalate when reliable startup cannot be established.

That is successful troubleshooting.
