Delivered Flow or Volume Is Low or Unstable

Vyaire

bellavista 1000

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot low or unstable delivered flow or volume caused by circuit leaks, obstruction, sensors, accessories, settings, gas supply, or test setup.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient Immediately

If delivered ventilation appears unreliable, transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or appropriate backup ventilation method. Do not troubleshoot unstable therapeutic output while the patient depends on the device.

Expected outcome: Reliable ventilation is maintained independently of the suspect bellavista 1000.

2. Confirm the Reported Condition With Test Equipment

Record the clinical report, displayed values, alarms, ventilation mode, and relevant settings. Under non-patient conditions, use appropriate approved ventilator test equipment or a test lung to determine whether the low or unstable delivery can be reproduced.

Do not use a patient as the test load.

Expected outcome: The complaint is either confirmed under controlled testing or identified as specific to the previous patient/circuit setup. If performance is stable and passes required tests, troubleshooting may stop after verification.

3. Inspect the Patient Circuit for Leaks

Check tubing, fittings, adapters, caps, sampling lines, humidification components, and patient-end connections. Look for disconnections, loose fittings, cracks, punctures, or poorly seated components.

Expected outcome: The circuit is complete and leak-free. If correcting a circuit leak restores stable measured delivery, complete final verification and stop troubleshooting.

4. Check the Circuit for Obstruction or Water Accumulation

Inspect externally accessible tubing and accessories for kinks, compression, accumulated condensate, occluded filters, blocked components, or improperly positioned water traps.

Replace contaminated or obstructed disposable components according to facility procedures.

Expected outcome: Gas can move freely through the circuit without unintended resistance. If removing the obstruction restores delivery, proceed to final verification.

5. Verify Flow Sensor and Exhalation Components

Inspect required external flow-sensing and exhalation components for correct orientation, seating, cleanliness, moisture, damage, and recognition by the ventilator.

Use known-good approved components when substitution is appropriate.

Expected outcome: The external sensor and exhalation pathway are correctly installed and functioning. If substitution restores stable measurements and delivery, the issue is resolved after final testing.

6. Verify Ventilator Settings and Test Conditions

Confirm the selected ventilation mode and externally accessible clinical settings match the intended test. Check that test-lung characteristics and accessory configuration are appropriate for the evaluation.

Do not change restricted configuration parameters or service settings.

Expected outcome: The physical setup, test load, and authorized ventilator settings are consistent. If an incorrect setup caused the apparent problem, correct it and repeat testing.

7. Verify Required Gas Supplies and Connections

Inspect external gas supply connections relevant to the ventilator's operation. Confirm hoses are correctly attached, undamaged, and connected to the intended source. Verify the supply is available using facility-approved methods.

Do not perform invasive gas-system service.

Expected outcome: Required external gas supplies and connections are available and stable. If restoring the supply corrects delivery, repeat performance testing.

8. Compare With Known-Good External Components

When needed, substitute a known-good compatible circuit, flow sensor, exhalation component, or other appropriate external accessory one item at a time.

This helps identify whether the observed instability follows an accessory.

Expected outcome: A defective external component is isolated or external components are ruled out. If the known-good configuration performs normally, replace the failed accessory and stop after verification.

9. Perform Ventilator Performance and Alarm Verification

Using approved test equipment, verify applicable delivered flow, volume, pressure, and alarm functions against current manufacturer requirements. Do not invent acceptance limits or use unapproved field tolerances.

Expected outcome: The ventilator delivers stable output and meets applicable manufacturer acceptance criteria. If it passes consistently, it may be returned to service.

10. Remove From Service if Output Remains Unstable

If circuit, accessories, settings, gas supplies, and test setup are correct but measured delivery remains low or unstable, stop troubleshooting at the external level.

Expected outcome: An unreliable therapeutic device is prevented from returning to clinical service and is escalated for qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External leaks, obstructions, sensors, circuit accessories, gas supplies, settings, and test setup have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve internal flow generation, pneumatic control, measurement, calibration, valves, software, or another service-level subsystem.

The ventilator should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated with appropriate Vyaire documentation and approved ventilator test equipment.
  • Repaired, calibrated, or configured only by qualified personnel.
  • Required to pass complete applicable ventilation-performance and alarm testing before return to clinical use.

Recognizing that unstable therapeutic output requires escalation is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A displayed tidal volume or flow value should not be trusted as proof of correct delivery when clinical performance is in question; verify with appropriate independent test equipment.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported unstable delivered volume from the bellavista 1000 during setup."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering reproduced the issue and found a partially disconnected circuit fitting creating an intermittent leak."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering secured the circuit connection, repeated performance testing with an appropriate ventilator analyzer, verified stable delivery and alarms, and returned the ventilator to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Ventilation mode and observed settings
  • Reported displayed versus expected behavior
  • Associated alarms
  • Patient-circuit condition
  • Leak or obstruction findings
  • Condensate or filter condition
  • Flow-sensor and exhalation-component status
  • External gas connections
  • Known-good substitutions
  • Ventilator analyzer results
  • Final functional status

Final Thought

Unstable ventilator output demands patient protection and independent verification. Work from the circuit and external accessories toward the ventilator, confirm supplies and setup, and escalate rather than assuming or attempting an internal repair when performance remains unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
