Belmont Medical Technologies Rapid Infuser RI-2

Delivered Fluid Temperature Too Low or Heater Not Available

Asset Type

Rapid Infuser

Manufacturer

Belmont Medical Technologies

Model

Rapid Infuser RI-2

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot heater availability, disposable setup, fluid flow, temperature sensing conditions, power, and environmental factors before suspecting an internal heating failure.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Appropriate Therapy

Do not rely on the RI-2 for warmed rapid infusion if delivered fluid temperature appears unreliable or the heating function is unavailable.

Move therapy to a verified alternative when warming is clinically required.

Expected outcome: Patient care continues without dependence on an unverified warming function.

2. Confirm the Exact Condition

Determine whether:

  • Heating is unavailable from startup
  • Fluid warms but remains lower than expected
  • The problem appears only at certain flow conditions
  • A temperature-related message is displayed
  • The complaint was based on device indication or independent observation

Record the exact displayed condition.

Expected outcome: The heating complaint is clearly defined before components are replaced or adjusted.

3. Verify AC Power and Basic Operation

Confirm the unit is connected to an appropriate power source when heater operation is expected.

Inspect the power cord and connection for damage or looseness and verify normal device startup.

Expected outcome: The device has stable power and no unrelated power fault is preventing heater availability.

4. Verify Disposable and Heat-Exchange Path Setup

Inspect the disposable for correct loading through all accessible warming and fluid-routing areas.

Look for:

  • Improper seating
  • Twisted tubing
  • Incomplete contact with external loading surfaces
  • Visible disposable deformation
  • Incorrect disposable type

Expected outcome: The compatible disposable is loaded correctly through the external warming pathway.

5. Confirm Adequate Priming

Verify that the disposable fluid pathway is fully primed and that fluid, rather than trapped air, occupies the intended heat-exchange pathway.

Expected outcome: The disposable is fully primed without visible air that could affect heat transfer or sensor readings.

6. Check Fluid Source and Flow Conditions

Verify that the source fluid is available and flowing normally.

Note whether the complaint occurs only during unusual operating conditions, but do not invent performance limits or temperatures not specified by approved documentation.

Expected outcome: Heating performance is evaluated under a stable, representative test condition.

7. Inspect Accessible Temperature-Sensing and Loading Areas

Inspect accessible surfaces associated with disposable positioning for contamination, residue, moisture, or physical obstruction.

Do not reposition internal sensors or open the heater assembly.

Expected outcome: No external contamination or loading problem interferes with sensing or heat transfer.

8. Test With a Known-Good Disposable

Install a new known-good compatible disposable and use an appropriate test fluid during bench evaluation.

Compare heater availability and indicated temperature behavior with the original setup.

Expected outcome: Normal heating returns with the known-good disposable, identifying the original disposable or setup as the likely cause.

9. Perform Temperature and Functional Verification

Using appropriate approved test equipment, verify that the unit warms fluid consistently and that displayed or measured behavior is reasonable according to applicable manufacturer criteria.

Do not return the device to service based solely on the heater indicator appearing active.

Expected outcome: Heating performance and temperature-related indications pass the required functional check.

10. Escalate Persistent Heating Failure

If heating remains unavailable or temperature performance remains abnormal with stable AC power, correct setup, and a known-good disposable, remove the unit from service.

Expected outcome: The device is referred for service rather than used with an unreliable warming function.

If the Problem Persists

External power, fluid flow, disposable placement, priming, contamination, and setup conditions have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve an internal heater, temperature sensor, control circuit, protective system, or service-level calibration issue.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Return-to-service testing must include appropriate heating and temperature-safety verification.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not assume fluid is being adequately warmed because the device is pumping normally; flow and warming performance must both be verified.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the RI-2 was delivering fluid but the warming function did not appear available."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the disposable incorrectly seated in the external heat-exchange loading path."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The disposable was correctly reloaded, the unit resumed normal heating operation, and temperature-related functional verification passed."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact temperature-related message
  • AC power status
  • Disposable type and condition
  • Disposable seating
  • Priming condition
  • Fluid-source condition
  • Whether heater became available with known-good disposable
  • Test equipment used
  • Results before and after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Verify power, fluid path, disposable seating, and temperature performance before assuming an internal heater failure, and remove the device from service whenever warming cannot be proven reliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
