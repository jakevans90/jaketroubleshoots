ISE Module Calibration, Reference Solution, or Electrolyte Result Is Unstable

Asset Type

Clinical Chemistry Analyzer

Manufacturer

Abbott

Model

Alinity c

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot unstable electrolyte measurements or calibration failures involving ISE-related solutions, consumables, sample quality, and operating conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Electrolyte Result Accuracy

  • Notify laboratory personnel that affected electrolyte results may be unreliable.
  • Suspend impacted assays until required calibration and QC are acceptable.
  • Redirect urgent testing to another validated analyzer.
  • Ask the laboratory to assess previously reported results if the failure may have affected patient testing.
  • Do not release results using failed or unstable ISE calibration.

Expected outcome: Affected electrolyte testing is isolated and clinical testing continuity is maintained.

2. Identify the Affected Electrolyte Measurement

  • Review the Alinity c calibration and QC history.
  • Determine whether sodium, potassium, chloride, or multiple ISE measurements are affected.
  • Record the exact error or instability pattern.
  • Compare current results with recent calibration and QC trends.
  • Determine whether instability began after maintenance, reagent replacement, or a prolonged analyzer interruption.

Expected outcome: The scope and timing of the electrolyte measurement problem are established.

3. Verify ISE-Related Consumables

  • Have laboratory personnel inspect the applicable ISE reference and other required solutions.
  • Verify correct product identification, installation, expiration, and remaining availability.
  • Check for leaks, loose accessible connections, or improperly installed consumables.
  • Confirm storage and handling requirements were met.
  • Do not substitute nonapproved solutions.

Expected outcome: Required ISE consumables are correct, available, and properly installed.

4. Review Recent Consumable Changes

  • Determine whether the problem began after an ISE-related solution or consumable was replaced.
  • Confirm that the appropriate approved replacement procedure was completed.
  • Review any system messages indicating that an associated initialization, preparation, or calibration step remains incomplete.
  • Do not manually manipulate internal electrodes or fluid paths.

Expected outcome: The consumable replacement history is verified and no required approved preparation step remains outstanding.

5. Evaluate Sample and QC Material Conditions

  • Have laboratory personnel verify the identity and suitability of calibrators and QC material.
  • Check lot information, expiration, handling, and preparation requirements.
  • Review sample conditions for hemolysis, contamination, inappropriate containers, or other known preanalytical problems.
  • Compare the affected channel's QC trend with other electrolyte channels.
  • Do not change clinical acceptance criteria to force a passing result.

Expected outcome: Sample-related and control-material causes are ruled out or identified.

6. Check External Operating Conditions

  • Review any water, waste, temperature, or related fluid-system alarms.
  • Confirm environmental conditions do not suggest overheating, freezing, leakage, or an interrupted supply.
  • Verify that the analyzer has completed its normal operating initialization.
  • Document any abnormal conditions rather than adjusting internal temperature or sensing components.

Expected outcome: No unresolved external or environmental condition is interfering with ISE operation.

7. Perform Laboratory-Approved Calibration and QC

  • After any authorized external correction, have laboratory personnel complete the applicable Abbott calibration process.
  • Review calibration acceptance status.
  • Run the required QC materials.
  • Evaluate whether results are stable and meet the laboratory's established criteria.
  • Do not repeatedly recalibrate to conceal a persistent underlying problem.

Expected outcome: Calibration is accepted and electrolyte QC is stable. Troubleshooting can stop when the laboratory approves the affected assays for patient use.

8. Escalate Unstable Measurements

  • If instability persists despite verified consumables and QC materials, remove affected testing from service.
  • Preserve calibration curves or available reports, QC trends, and event history.
  • Contact Abbott technical support for further evaluation.

Expected outcome: The unresolved electrolyte measurement issue is documented and escalated appropriately.

If the Problem Persists

After external consumables, QC materials, sample quality, and environmental conditions have been verified, remaining causes may involve internal ISE fluid handling, reference measurement components, electrodes, sensors, or calibration-related service faults.

The affected module should be:

  • Removed from service and labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation by qualified personnel.
  • Evaluated using current Abbott documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired, calibrated, or configured only by authorized personnel.
  • Returned to service only after required functional verification, acceptable calibration, and laboratory QC.

Stopping when an ISE measurement remains unstable is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Electrolyte results can influence urgent clinical treatment. Maintain access to another validated testing method until measurement stability is demonstrated.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Laboratory staff reported unstable sodium QC results and repeated ISE calibration difficulties."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering and laboratory personnel found that the installed ISE reference solution was expired."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Laboratory personnel replaced the expired solution with an approved in-date product, completed the required preparation and calibration, and verified acceptable sodium QC."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Affected electrolyte assay
  • Calibration error or status
  • QC trend
  • ISE solution identity and lot
  • Consumable expiration
  • Recent replacement history
  • Related fluid-system alarms
  • Sample quality observations
  • Calibration results before and after correction
  • Final laboratory authorization

Final Thought

ISE instability requires careful review of solutions, sample conditions, and calibration history before internal components are suspected. Protect patient-result accuracy, avoid unsupported adjustments, verify corrective actions with QC, and escalate unresolved measurement problems.

That is successful troubleshooting.
