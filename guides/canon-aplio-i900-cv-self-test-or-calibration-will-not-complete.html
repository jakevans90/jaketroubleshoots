Canon Aplio i900 CV

Self-Test or Calibration Will Not Complete

Asset Type

Echocardiography System

Manufacturer

Canon

Model

Aplio i900 CV

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting incomplete self-test or calibration caused by startup state, connected accessories, setup conditions, external interference, configuration, or service-level problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Remove the System From Clinical Dependence

Do not use the Aplio i900 CV for patient imaging when a required self-test or calibration has failed and system performance cannot be verified. Move the examination to another verified system. Expected outcome: Patient care continues without relying on unverified equipment.

2. Confirm Which Test Is Failing

Identify whether the problem occurs during startup self-test, a user-accessible calibration, or another approved verification process. Record the exact displayed message, stage, and circumstances of the failure. Do not enter restricted service menus. Expected outcome: The failed process and repeatable symptom are clearly identified.

3. Verify Normal Startup and Stable Power

Confirm the system starts normally and is connected to a verified AC source. An unstable power source or incomplete startup can prevent tests from completing reliably. Expected outcome: Stable power and normal system readiness are confirmed.

4. Check Required External Accessories and Connections

Verify that any accessory, probe, cable, or approved test item required for the particular procedure is present, compatible, correctly connected, and undamaged. Remove unrelated accessories if appropriate. Expected outcome: The test has the correct external setup and no obvious connection problem.

5. Inspect for Contamination or Physical Damage

Check relevant accessible connectors, probe surfaces, cables, accessories, and external interfaces for gel, liquid, debris, bent areas, damage, or poor seating. Correct only external issues within Clinical Engineering scope. Expected outcome: External components required for testing are clean, intact, and properly connected.

6. Verify Applicable Settings and Test Conditions

Confirm that the system is in the proper normal operating state for the approved self-test or calibration being performed. Verify relevant user-accessible settings against approved documentation. Do not change protected configuration simply to make a test pass. Expected outcome: The test is being performed under the correct supported conditions.

7. Use a Known-Good Compatible Accessory When Appropriate

If the failing test depends on a probe or external accessory, substitute a known-good compatible item when available. Repeat only the approved test. Expected outcome: A successful test with the known-good accessory isolates the original accessory as the likely cause.

8. Perform a Controlled Restart and Repeat Once

If no external cause is found, perform a normal shutdown and restart. Repeat the test under the same correct conditions. Avoid repeated test cycling when the same failure consistently returns. Expected outcome: The self-test or calibration completes normally. If it does, proceed to functional verification.

9. Verify Normal Imaging Function

After a successful test, verify basic imaging operation and any functions related to the failed process. Complete return-to-service testing required by policy. Expected outcome: The test passes and the system performs normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate a Repeatable Test Failure

If the same self-test or calibration failure remains after external setup, accessory, power, and restart checks, stop troubleshooting at the external level. Do not bypass the failure or alter unauthorized calibration data. Expected outcome: An unresolved calibration, software, hardware, or internal communication issue is referred for qualified service.

If the Problem Persists

Common external setup, accessory, power, connection, and operating-condition causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve internal calibration data, software, imaging hardware, sensors, internal communication, or another manufacturer service-level condition.

Remove the system from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it using appropriate Canon documentation and approved test equipment. Calibration adjustments, service configuration, or internal repairs should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Successful completion of the applicable test and subsequent functional verification are required before return to service. Knowing when not to bypass a failed verification is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A required self-test or calibration failure should be treated as an unresolved performance issue until the system passes the appropriate verification.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Aplio i900 CV would not complete an expected system verification after a probe was connected."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found contamination on the accessible probe connector that prevented a reliable connection."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering cleaned and reconnected the probe using the approved method, repeated the verification successfully, confirmed basic imaging operation, and returned the system to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact self-test or calibration process
  • Exact displayed message
  • Point at which the process stopped
  • AC power status
  • Probe or accessory involved
  • Connector condition
  • Known-good substitution results
  • Result after restart
  • Final test result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient from unverified equipment, confirm setup and external dependencies first, never bypass a failed test merely to restore availability, verify proper operation after correction, escalate persistent failures, and document the final test result.

That is successful troubleshooting.
