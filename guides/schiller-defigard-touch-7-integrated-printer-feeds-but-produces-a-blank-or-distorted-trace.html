Integrated Printer Feeds but Produces a Blank or Distorted Trace

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Schiller

Model

DEFIGARD Touch 7

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots printer output problems involving paper installation, paper type, contamination, door closure, feed path, or the print mechanism.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Preserve Clinical Documentation

If printed records are required and the integrated printer is unreliable, preserve ECG and event information using another approved documentation method.

Do not delay patient care solely to troubleshoot the printer.

Expected outcome: Required clinical information remains available while the printer is evaluated.

2. Confirm the Printer Symptom

Determine whether the printer:

  • Feeds blank paper.
  • Produces a faint trace.
  • Produces a distorted or compressed trace.
  • Prints intermittently.
  • Skews or wrinkles the paper.
  • Produces normal text but poor waveforms.

Expected outcome: The exact print-quality problem is reproduced.

3. Verify Correct Paper Installation

Open the printer compartment and verify that the paper is:

  • Installed in the correct orientation.
  • Loaded through the proper feed path.
  • Not folded or jammed.
  • Not excessively loose or tightly compressed in the compartment.

Expected outcome: Paper feeds smoothly and is oriented correctly. If proper loading restores printing, proceed to final verification.

4. Verify Approved Paper

Confirm that the paper is the correct approved type for the printer.

Thermal paper installed backward or inappropriate paper can feed normally while producing a blank output.

Expected outcome: Correct compatible paper is installed. If replacing the paper restores the trace, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

5. Inspect the Paper and Feed Path

Check for:

  • Damp or damaged paper.
  • Adhesive residue.
  • Torn paper.
  • Debris.
  • Misaligned roll or stack.
  • Obstruction in the feed path.

Remove accessible debris without disassembling the printer mechanism.

Expected outcome: The paper path is clean and unobstructed.

6. Inspect and Clean Accessible Print Surfaces

With the device removed from clinical use and following approved procedures, inspect accessible print-head and roller surfaces for contamination.

Clean only using methods approved for the equipment.

Expected outcome: Accessible print surfaces are clean and free of residue.

7. Check Printer Door Closure

Verify that the printer door closes fully and maintains proper paper contact.

Inspect hinges, latches, and visible alignment for damage.

Expected outcome: The printer door closes securely and paper remains properly aligned.

8. Produce a Controlled Test Print

Print an approved test strip or recorded waveform.

Compare the printed trace to the displayed waveform.

Expected outcome: The paper advances evenly and produces a clear, undistorted trace. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

Paper type, installation, feed path, contamination, and door closure have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve the thermal print head, feed drive, printer electronics, or another internal service-level fault.

The device should be removed from service if printed documentation is required for its assigned clinical workflow. Otherwise, follow facility policy for restricted use.

For repair:

  • Label the affected unit Out of Service as appropriate.
  • Send it for bench evaluation.
  • Use appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Allow internal printer repair only by qualified personnel.

Verify printer operation and overall device functionality before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A blank thermal printout can result simply from incorrect paper orientation, so verify paper type and loading before assuming printer failure.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported that the DEFIGARD Touch 7 printer fed paper normally but produced blank strips."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found that the thermal paper had been installed with the printable surface facing the wrong direction."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reloaded the approved paper correctly and verified clear ECG traces and normal paper feeding on repeated test prints."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Blank, faint, or distorted output.
  • Paper type used.
  • Paper orientation.
  • Feed-path condition.
  • Printer-door condition.
  • Contamination observed.
  • Test-print result.
  • Displayed versus printed waveform comparison.
  • Final printer status.

Final Thought

Printer problems should be approached from paper and feed-path causes first. Preserve clinical documentation through an alternate method, verify print quality after correction, and escalate when the internal printer mechanism remains unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
