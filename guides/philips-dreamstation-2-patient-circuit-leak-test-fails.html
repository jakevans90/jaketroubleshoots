Philips DreamStation 2

Patient Circuit Leak Test Fails

Asset Type

Sleep Therapy System

Manufacturer

Philips

Model

DreamStation 2

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots excessive circuit leakage or poor pressure delivery caused by tubing, mask interfaces, intentional venting, humidifier connections, or damaged accessories.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Therapy

Do not troubleshoot an unreliable PAP circuit while the patient depends on it. Provide another verified device or circuit as clinically appropriate before testing.

Expected outcome: Prescribed therapy is maintained while the suspect system is evaluated away from the patient.

2. Confirm the Exact Leak Complaint

Determine whether staff observed excessive leak, inability to maintain therapy pressure, a leak-related indication, excessive air noise, or a poor patient interface seal.

If a specific test or message was reported, document exactly what was displayed. Do not assume the DreamStation 2 performs the same formal circuit leak test used by a critical-care ventilator.

Expected outcome: The actual leak symptom is identified and reproducible.

3. Inspect the Patient Tubing

Inspect the complete breathing tube for cracks, punctures, loose cuffs, crushed sections, contamination, or incomplete connections.

Flex the tubing gently during inspection because small splits may become apparent only when the hose moves.

Expected outcome: Tubing is intact and securely connected. If replacement with known-good tubing eliminates the excessive leak, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

4. Inspect the Mask and Patient Interface

Inspect the mask cushion, frame, elbow, swivel, and associated connectors for damage, incorrect assembly, or poor fit.

Distinguish normal intentional mask vent flow from an unintended leak. Do not obstruct an intentional exhalation vent.

Expected outcome: The interface is correctly assembled and only its intended vent path remains open.

5. Inspect the Humidifier Connection

Verify that the humidifier water tank is correctly seated and that accessible seals and mating surfaces are clean, undamaged, and properly positioned.

Look for a tank that is not fully installed, damaged sealing surfaces, or visible cracks.

Expected outcome: The humidifier assembly forms a secure external connection without abnormal leakage.

6. Verify Tubing and Accessory Configuration

Confirm that the selected tubing or accessory configuration matches the connected equipment and that unnecessary adapters or incompatible components have not been introduced.

Avoid changing clinical therapy settings solely to compensate for a suspected leak.

Expected outcome: The device configuration matches the connected patient circuit and accessories.

7. Substitute Known-Good External Components

When appropriate, test with known-good compatible tubing, patient interface components, and humidifier components individually to isolate the leak source.

Change one component at a time so the cause can be identified.

Expected outcome: The excessive leak disappears when the defective external component is replaced. If so, replace that component and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Assemble the approved circuit and operate the DreamStation 2 in a controlled test. Confirm stable airflow and pressure behavior with no unexpected leak indication or abnormal air noise.

Expected outcome: The system operates consistently with the properly assembled circuit. If it does, the device may proceed through normal return-to-service requirements.

9. Escalate Persistent Leakage

If tubing, mask/interface components, humidifier seating, external seals, and configuration are correct but abnormal leakage persists, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The DreamStation 2 remains out of service pending qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external circuit and accessory causes have been ruled out. Persistent leakage may involve the device air path, blower assembly, internal sealing, pressure/flow sensing, or another service-level condition.

Remove the DreamStation 2 from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate it using appropriate Philips documentation and approved test equipment. Internal pneumatic repair or calibration should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Complete appropriate functional verification before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never block an intentional mask or circuit exhalation vent while investigating a suspected leak.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported excessive air leakage and difficulty maintaining therapy with the DreamStation 2."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a split near the cuff of the patient breathing tube."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the damaged tubing with an approved compatible tube and verified stable therapy operation without abnormal leakage."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact leak indication or symptom
  • Tubing condition
  • Mask and interface condition
  • Intentional vent condition
  • Humidifier tank seating
  • Accessory configuration
  • Known-good substitutions
  • Results before and after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Circuit leaks should be isolated from the patient outward, with intentional venting distinguished from defects and external components verified before assuming an internal pneumatic problem. Escalate persistent leakage and document the confirmed cause and verification.

That is successful troubleshooting.
