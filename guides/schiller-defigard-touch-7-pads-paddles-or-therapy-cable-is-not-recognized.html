Pads, Paddles, or Therapy Cable Is Not Recognized

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Schiller

Model

DEFIGARD Touch 7

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots therapy-accessory recognition problems involving pads, paddles, connectors, cables, accessory condition, or the device therapy interface.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Maintain Immediate Defibrillation Capability

If the therapy accessory is not recognized while a patient may require defibrillation, immediately use another verified defibrillator.

Do not continue troubleshooting a therapy-accessory fault while the patient depends on the affected device.

Expected outcome: Reliable defibrillation capability remains available.

2. Confirm Which Accessory Is Not Recognized

Identify whether the issue involves:

  • Adhesive therapy pads.
  • External paddles.
  • Therapy cable.
  • One particular accessory set.
  • All therapy accessories.

Record any displayed message.

Expected outcome: The failure is isolated to a specific accessory path or shown to affect all therapy connections.

3. Inspect the Therapy Connection

Disconnect and inspect the therapy connector for:

  • Incomplete insertion.
  • Bent or damaged contacts.
  • Debris.
  • Fluid contamination.
  • Loose connector housing.
  • Cable strain damage.

Reconnect it firmly.

Expected outcome: The therapy cable seats securely and the device recognizes the accessory.

4. Inspect Pads or Paddles

Check pads or paddles for:

  • Physical damage.
  • Contamination.
  • Expired or compromised disposable packaging.
  • Damaged connectors.
  • Broken or loose cable attachment.

Do not use damaged or compromised therapy accessories.

Expected outcome: The accessory is appropriate and physically intact.

5. Substitute Known-Good Therapy Accessories

Use approved known-good pads, paddles, or therapy cable as applicable.

Change one component at a time when possible to isolate the failed item.

Expected outcome: The known-good accessory is recognized. If the failure follows the original accessory, remove that accessory from service.

6. Verify Normal Therapy Mode Detection

Using an approved bench setup, verify that connecting the intended therapy accessory produces the expected normal device response.

Do not perform uncontrolled therapy discharge.

Expected outcome: The unit consistently detects the connected therapy accessory.

7. Test Therapy Function With an Analyzer

Connect the defibrillator to an approved defibrillator analyzer using the verified accessory path and perform applicable functional testing.

Expected outcome: The accessory remains recognized and therapy operation completes successfully. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

Known-good pads, paddles, cables, and external connections have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve the therapy connector assembly, accessory-detection circuitry, charging/therapy interface, or another internal service-level fault.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using approved manufacturer procedures and a defibrillator analyzer.
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel.

Complete therapy-output and operational testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A defibrillator that cannot consistently recognize its therapy accessory should not remain stocked for emergency use, even if monitoring functions normally.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported that the DEFIGARD Touch 7 displayed that the connected therapy pads were not recognized."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an intermittent therapy cable connection and confirmed normal recognition with a known-good cable."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the therapy cable and verified reliable pad detection and therapy operation using an approved defibrillator analyzer."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Accessory type involved.
  • Exact displayed message.
  • Pad expiration or package condition.
  • Therapy connector condition.
  • Cable damage observed.
  • Known-good pad result.
  • Known-good cable result.
  • Analyzer verification.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Treat accessory recognition as part of the complete therapy path. Verify the pads or paddles, cable, and connector before assuming an internal failure, and remove the unit from emergency service whenever accessory detection is unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
