Canon Aplio i900 CV

Unexpected Shutdown, Freeze, or Restart

Asset Type

Echocardiography System

Manufacturer

Canon

Model

Aplio i900 CV

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting intermittent shutdowns, freezes, or restarts caused by power, peripherals, environment, workload, connections, or internal hardware and software problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Preserve Clinical Continuity

If the Aplio i900 CV freezes, shuts down, or restarts during an examination, stop depending on it for patient imaging. Move the examination to another verified system when necessary and preserve recoverable study data according to department workflow. Expected outcome: Patient care continues without dependence on an unstable system.

2. Document the Exact Failure Pattern

Determine whether the system powered off completely, froze with the display still active, restarted automatically, or became unresponsive after a specific action. Record what the system was doing immediately before the event and any displayed message. Expected outcome: The symptom is characterized well enough to distinguish power interruption from software or system instability.

3. Verify Facility AC Power

Inspect the external power cord, plug, and accessible system connection. Confirm the receptacle is reliable using approved methods. Determine whether the system was operating from AC or battery when the event occurred. Expected outcome: External power loss or a loose connection is corrected or ruled out.

4. Inspect for Physical and Environmental Conditions

Check for blocked ventilation, excessive external heat, liquid contamination, recent impact, cable tension, or positioning that could affect system operation. Do not obstruct cooling areas during testing. Expected outcome: The system is appropriately positioned with no obvious external environmental cause.

5. Disconnect Nonessential Peripherals

When appropriate, disconnect nonessential USB devices, external storage, network peripherals, or other removable accessories. Operate the system under controlled conditions to determine whether instability returns. Expected outcome: An external peripheral is isolated as the trigger, or the failure persists independently.

6. Check Whether the Failure Is Function-Specific

Determine whether the freeze or restart occurs only with a particular probe, accessory, workflow, export task, or imaging function. When safe, compare operation with known-good compatible accessories. Expected outcome: A repeatable external trigger is identified or accessory involvement is ruled out.

7. Perform a Normal Restart

If the system is responsive enough to shut down normally, complete the standard shutdown and restart sequence. Avoid repeated hard power interruptions unless the system is completely nonresponsive and approved procedures permit them. Expected outcome: The system restarts normally and remains stable during basic operation.

8. Verify Basic Operation Under Observation

Check startup, display and controls, probe recognition, basic imaging, and other normal functions relevant to the reported event. Observe for recurrence without unnecessarily prolonging testing on an unstable device. Expected outcome: The system remains stable through appropriate functional verification. If the external cause was corrected and no instability returns, troubleshooting can stop.

9. Remove From Service for Recurrent Instability

If unexpected shutdowns, freezes, or restarts recur after external power, peripherals, accessories, and environmental factors have been ruled out, remove the system from service. Intermittent recovery does not establish reliability. Expected outcome: An unstable system is prevented from returning to patient care without further evaluation.

10. Escalate for Qualified Evaluation

Provide the observed pattern, circumstances, messages, and reproduction information to qualified service personnel. Do not perform internal computer, storage, power-supply, cooling, or board-level repair without appropriate authorization. Expected outcome: The likely internal hardware, software, thermal-management, or communication problem is escalated with useful diagnostic information.

If the Problem Persists

Common power, accessory, peripheral, positioning, and environmental causes have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve software instability, storage or computer hardware, internal power management, cooling, memory, internal communication, or another service-level failure.

Remove the Aplio i900 CV from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Canon documentation and approved test equipment. Internal repairs or software recovery should be performed only by qualified personnel.

After repair, verify stable operation through appropriate functional testing before return to clinical use. Knowing that an intermittently recovered system is not necessarily a reliable system is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A system that has unexpectedly restarted during an examination should not be trusted for another patient merely because it powers up normally afterward.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Aplio i900 CV unexpectedly powered off during an echocardiography examination."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external AC power connection was loose and could be interrupted by normal movement of the system."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering secured the power connection, verified stable startup and imaging operation without further interruption, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether the event was a freeze, shutdown, or restart
  • System activity immediately before the event
  • Exact displayed message
  • AC or battery operation
  • Outlet verification result
  • Power cord and connection condition
  • External peripherals connected
  • Probe or accessory in use
  • Ventilation and environmental observations
  • Whether the failure was reproduced
  • Results after restart
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient from intermittent equipment, verify power and external triggers before assuming an internal defect, require stable operation before return to service, escalate recurrent instability appropriately, and document the circumstances surrounding the failure.

That is successful troubleshooting.
