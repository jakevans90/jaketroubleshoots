Reagent Cartridge Is Loaded but Inventory Is Incorrect

Asset Type

Clinical Chemistry Analyzer

Manufacturer

Abbott

Model

Alinity c

What This Guide Helps With

Identify reagent inventory discrepancies involving cartridge recognition, incorrect loading, barcode information, reagent status, or inventory synchronization.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Testing Accuracy

  • Notify laboratory personnel that the affected reagent may not be available for reliable testing.
  • Do not release patient results using a reagent with unresolved identification, expiration, or inventory discrepancies.
  • Arrange alternate validated testing when necessary.
  • Do not manually override reagent identity or expiration controls.

Expected outcome: Testing dependent on the questionable reagent is suspended or redirected until inventory accuracy is verified.

2. Confirm the Inventory Discrepancy

  • Review the reagent inventory display.
  • Identify the affected assay and cartridge.
  • Determine whether the cartridge is missing, assigned to the wrong location, displaying an incorrect quantity, or showing an unexpected lot or expiration.
  • Compare the displayed inventory information with the physical cartridge label and laboratory records.
  • Document any active identification or reagent-management error.

Expected outcome: The exact inventory mismatch is identified.

3. Inspect the Physical Reagent Cartridge

  • Have authorized laboratory personnel access the cartridge using the approved loading workflow.
  • Verify the cartridge is intended for the Alinity c and the selected assay.
  • Inspect the cartridge for physical damage, leakage, improperly attached labeling, or contamination.
  • Verify lot and expiration information.
  • Confirm appropriate storage and handling requirements were followed.

Expected outcome: The cartridge is physically intact, correctly identified, and suitable for use.

4. Verify Cartridge Seating and Loading Position

  • Confirm the cartridge occupies the expected reagent location.
  • Check for incomplete seating or interference from surrounding cartridges.
  • Inspect the accessible loading area for foreign material or obstruction.
  • Ensure the access area is properly closed before attempting recognition.
  • Do not manually rotate internal mechanisms or bypass access interlocks.

Expected outcome: The reagent cartridge is correctly seated and available for normal identification.

5. Check Reagent Identification and Inventory Updating

  • Have laboratory personnel perform the approved reagent loading or inventory update process.
  • Observe whether the system identifies the cartridge correctly.
  • Verify the displayed assay, lot, expiration, and remaining quantity information as applicable.
  • Check for inventory messages involving reagent validity or an unavailable cartridge.
  • Do not edit protected reagent data to force acceptance.

Expected outcome: The cartridge is recognized and its displayed inventory agrees with verified cartridge information. If inventory is correct and QC is acceptable, troubleshooting can stop.

6. Compare With a Known-Good Cartridge

  • If permitted by laboratory procedures, use a verified compatible cartridge to determine whether the problem follows the original cartridge.
  • Record whether the replacement is recognized in the applicable loading location.
  • If multiple cartridges fail at the same location, suspect a location-specific identification or loading problem.
  • If only one cartridge fails, isolate that cartridge for laboratory or Abbott evaluation.

Expected outcome: The problem is narrowed to a cartridge-specific or instrument-side condition.

7. Verify Reagent Availability and Analytical Readiness

  • Confirm that the inventory now reports the correct reagent information.
  • Have laboratory personnel perform applicable calibration and QC requirements.
  • Verify that the affected assay is available without reagent-management alarms.
  • Ensure the laboratory confirms result validity before resuming patient testing.

Expected outcome: Inventory is accurate, reagent status is acceptable, and required QC passes. Troubleshooting can stop.

8. Escalate Persistent Recognition Problems

  • If inventory remains incorrect after proper cartridge loading and approved updating, stop using the affected assay or module.
  • Preserve cartridge identification information and error history.
  • Contact Abbott technical support.

Expected outcome: The unresolved issue is documented for investigation without introducing incorrect reagent information.

If the Problem Persists

After verifying cartridge compatibility, labeling, expiration, seating, and inventory processing, remaining causes may involve reagent identification hardware, location sensing, inventory database management, or service-level software faults.

The affected instrument or module should be:

  • Removed from service and labeled Out of Service when reliable reagent identification cannot be maintained.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation by authorized personnel.
  • Evaluated using Abbott documentation and approved service equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
  • Returned to service after correct inventory recognition, functional verification, calibration when required, and acceptable QC.

Stopping rather than forcing reagent acceptance is appropriate troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Correct cartridge identification is essential for patient-result traceability. Verify the physical reagent lot against the displayed inventory before returning the assay to clinical use.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Laboratory staff reported that a newly loaded reagent cartridge was not appearing in the Alinity c inventory."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found that the cartridge was not fully seated in its designated loading position."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Laboratory personnel correctly reseated the cartridge, the instrument recognized its identification and inventory information, and the required QC passed."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Affected assay
  • Cartridge identification and lot
  • Expiration date
  • Reported and actual inventory information
  • Loading position
  • Cartridge and label condition
  • Inventory update results
  • Known-good cartridge comparison
  • Calibration and QC outcome
  • Final assay availability

Final Thought

Reagent inventory problems should be approached through cartridge verification, proper loading, and inventory comparison before suspecting internal recognition failures. Protect testing accuracy, document the discrepancy, and escalate when reliable reagent tracking cannot be restored.

That is successful troubleshooting.
