Physio-Control LIFEPAK 15

Error: TH_ERROR_CAP_OVERCHARGED Capacitor Over-Charged

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Physio-Control

Model

LIFEPAK 15

What This Guide Helps With

This guide helps troubleshoot TH_ERROR_CAP_OVERCHARGED on the Physio-Control LIFEPAK 15 when the defibrillator reports a therapy capacitor over-charged condition. This is a serious therapy-related fault because the capacitor is part of the defibrillation energy storage and delivery system.

This condition may affect safe shock delivery, synchronized cardioversion readiness, and overall confidence in the therapy subsystem. The device should not remain in patient use while this error is active, repeated, or documented in the service log.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

Ensure Patient Safety First

  • Remove the LIFEPAK 15 from patient use immediately if this error appears.
  • Provide clinical staff with another verified defibrillator before troubleshooting continues.
  • Do not attempt to use the device for defibrillation, synchronized cardioversion, or pacing until it has been evaluated and passed required performance testing.
  • Expected: Patient care is transferred to another verified defibrillator.
  • Why it matters: A capacitor over-charge condition involves the therapy energy storage system and may affect safe energy delivery.

Verify the Reported Error and Behavior

  • Confirm the displayed or logged error is TH_ERROR_CAP_OVERCHARGED.
  • Confirm whether therapy service code a005 is present.
  • Document the reported condition as capacitor over-charged.
  • Check whether the error occurred during startup, charge attempt, shock preparation, after a failed discharge, during therapy testing, or only during service log review.
  • Expected: The error is reproducible or clearly documented in the service log.
  • Why it matters: This confirms a true therapy subsystem fault rather than a user setup issue.

Stop Clinical Use Before External Checks

  • Because this error involves the defibrillator capacitor charge state, do not continue clinical troubleshooting during active patient care.
  • Place the device out of service before performing inspection or controlled bench checks.
  • Do not perform shock testing while the device is connected to a patient.
  • Expected: The device is no longer available for bedside use.
  • Why it matters: Therapy capacitor faults should be evaluated in a controlled service setting, not during patient care.

Check Power Source and Battery Condition

  • Verify the LIFEPAK 15 is connected to a known-good AC power source or has a known-good charged battery installed.
  • Inspect the battery bay and battery contacts for obvious damage, contamination, poor seating, or signs of heat.
  • Confirm the battery is seated properly and the device does not show obvious unstable power behavior.
  • Expected: The device has stable power and no obvious external power issue.
  • Why it matters: Unstable power should be ruled out, but it does not clear a therapy capacitor fault by itself.

Inspect the Therapy Cable and Electrode Connection Externally

  • Inspect the therapy cable, paddles, test load connection, and electrode connector for visible damage, contamination, bent pins, or loose seating.
  • Verify that any controlled testing setup uses appropriate accessories and a proper test load.
  • Do not perform patient-connected shock testing.
  • Expected: Therapy accessories are intact and properly connected for controlled testing only.
  • Why it matters: External therapy cable issues can cause therapy-related complaints, but an over-charged capacitor error is not normally resolved as a basic accessory issue.

Confirm No Obvious Physical Damage or Fluid Intrusion

  • Inspect the device exterior for cracked housing, drop damage, fluid contamination, burn marks, unusual odor, excessive heat, damaged therapy connector, or damaged battery contacts.
  • Document any visible damage before repair evaluation.
  • Do not open the device or perform internal disassembly as part of front-line troubleshooting.
  • Expected: Any visible damage is documented before repair evaluation.
  • Why it matters: Physical or liquid damage may support escalation and help repair teams identify the failure path.

Review the Service Log

  • Process or retrieve the service log per facility procedure.
  • Confirm whether therapy service code a005 is present.
  • Check whether other therapy-related service codes are also logged.
  • Record whether the error is a one-time event or a recurring condition.
  • Expected: The service log supports the reported capacitor over-charge condition.
  • Why it matters: Therapy service codes help distinguish a one-time report from a recurring defibrillator subsystem fault.

Do Not Attempt Routine Clearing as a Fix

  • Do not return the device to service simply because the error disappears after a restart.
  • Do not rely on clearing the log, cycling power, reseating the battery, or reconnecting accessories as a final repair for this condition.
  • Keep the device out of service until repair evaluation and performance testing are completed.
  • Expected: The device remains out of service until corrective action and testing are complete.
  • Why it matters: A therapy capacitor over-charge condition can represent an internal charging, measurement, discharge, or therapy control fault.

Isolate the Issue Clinically and Operationally

  • Classify the issue as accessory-related only if visible therapy accessory damage or incorrect test setup is clearly found.
  • Classify the issue as power-related only if unstable power is clearly confirmed.
  • Classify the issue as device-related if the error remains logged or recurs during controlled operation.
  • Classify the issue as an internal therapy subsystem concern if service code a005 persists.
  • Expected: The problem is treated as a device-level therapy fault unless a clear external cause is proven.

Stop When Resolved

  • For this specific error, resolution should not be considered complete in the clinical area.
  • The device may only be returned to service after corrective action, service log review, and successful defibrillator performance testing according to facility and manufacturer service requirements.
  • Expected: The LIFEPAK 15 passes required therapy testing before clinical use.

If the Problem Persists

If TH_ERROR_CAP_OVERCHARGED or therapy service code a005 remains present, external and common causes have been ruled out. A persistent capacitor over-charge error strongly indicates an internal defibrillator therapy subsystem failure.

Remove the device from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Do not return the device to patient care until corrective action is completed and defibrillation performance testing passes.

Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot this error on an active patient. Move the patient to a backup device first and maintain therapy continuity with another verified defibrillator. A defibrillator with an unresolved capacitor charge error should not be trusted for emergency shock delivery.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"LIFEPAK 15 reported TH_ERROR_CAP_OVERCHARGED with therapy service code a005, indicating a capacitor over-charged condition. Device removed from clinical use due to potential defibrillation output safety concern."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"External power, battery seating, therapy cable connection, and visible physical condition checked. Error relates to therapy capacitor charge control and is not a basic external troubleshooting item. Likely internal therapy subsystem fault pending repair evaluation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed LIFEPAK 15 from service and labeled Out of Service. Service log to be processed. Device requires bench evaluation, corrective action, and successful defibrillator performance testing before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Alarm behavior, including whether TH_ERROR_CAP_OVERCHARGED appeared at startup, during charge attempt, during shock preparation, after a failed discharge, during therapy testing, or only in the service log.
  • Accessories swapped, including battery, therapy cable, paddles, electrodes, or test load used during controlled testing.
  • Power behavior, including AC power source verified, battery tested or swapped, battery seating, and any restart behavior.
  • Environmental factors, including visible fluid intrusion, heat, burn marks, odor, drop damage, or contamination.
  • Indicator lights, service indicator behavior, other therapy service codes, and service log findings.
  • Final device status, including removed from service, labeled Out of Service, sent for repair, repaired, or passed defibrillator performance testing.

Final Thought

Patient safety comes first with any defibrillator therapy error. A capacitor over-charge message should be treated as a serious therapy subsystem condition, not a routine accessory complaint. Logical troubleshooting means confirming the error, ruling out obvious external issues, documenting clearly, and escalating appropriately for repair and performance testing.

That is successful troubleshooting.
