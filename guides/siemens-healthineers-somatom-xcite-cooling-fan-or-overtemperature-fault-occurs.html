What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide for cooling warnings, abnormal fan behavior, overheating indications, excessive heat, or environmental conditions that prevent reliable scanner operation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Stop Operation When Overheating Is Suspected

If an overtemperature warning, burning odor, smoke, unusual heat, or abnormal fan noise occurs, stop clinical scanning and remove the patient when safe to do so. Expected outcome: The patient and staff are protected from continued operation of potentially overheated equipment.

2. Confirm the Exact Cooling Complaint

Determine whether the issue is a displayed temperature warning, fan alarm, unusual noise, excessive room heat, unexpected shutdown, or another cooling-related symptom. Expected outcome: The condition is clearly characterized before further testing.

3. Check Room Environmental Conditions

Verify that scanner-room HVAC is operating normally and that the room is not obviously warmer than usual. Check whether facilities work or an HVAC interruption has occurred. Expected outcome: A facility cooling issue is identified or ruled out.

4. Inspect Accessible Ventilation Areas

Look for blocked external vents, stored items, dust accumulation visible from the exterior, or equipment positioned in a way that restricts airflow. Do not remove covers or access internal cooling components. Expected outcome: External airflow paths are unobstructed.

5. Listen for Abnormal Fan Operation

From outside the enclosure, note whether expected cooling airflow is absent, intermittent, unusually loud, or accompanied by grinding or other abnormal mechanical sounds. Expected outcome: Fan operation appears normal externally or a clear abnormal condition is identified for service.

6. Check for Recent High Utilization or Environmental Events

Determine whether the fault followed sustained scanning, a power interruption, HVAC outage, room construction, or another event that may have affected cooling. Expected outcome: Relevant operating or environmental factors are documented.

7. Allow Only Approved Recovery Procedures

If the manufacturer-supported normal operating workflow permits recovery after an external environmental problem has been corrected, allow the system to return to normal operating condition before attempting a controlled nonpatient test. Do not bypass temperature protection. Expected outcome: The scanner returns to a normal ready state without temperature or fan warnings.

8. Perform Controlled Functional Verification

If the warning clears and no abnormal heat, odor, or noise remains, perform an approved nonpatient functional check while monitoring for recurrence. Expected outcome: The scanner remains stable and no cooling or overtemperature condition returns. Troubleshooting can stop if normal operation is verified.

9. Escalate Persistent or Recurrent Thermal Problems

If the temperature warning remains, cooling appears abnormal, the fault returns during testing, or there is excessive heat, odor, or mechanical noise, stop troubleshooting. Expected outcome: The scanner is removed from service and evaluated by qualified personnel.

If the Problem Persists

External ventilation, room HVAC, obvious airflow obstruction, and operating conditions have been checked. Remaining causes may involve internal fans, cooling assemblies, temperature sensing, power electronics, X-ray subsystem cooling, or other service-level components.

The scanner should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or service evaluation
  • Evaluated using Siemens Healthineers documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Following service, verify cooling operation and scanner function under appropriate test conditions before return to clinical use. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never defeat a thermal warning or continue scanning solely because the scanner still appears capable of acquiring images.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"CT staff reported an overtemperature warning on the SOMATOM X.cite during operation."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found that materials stored near an external ventilation area were restricting airflow."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The obstruction was removed, normal airflow was restored, and the scanner completed an approved nonpatient functional test without recurrence."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact temperature or cooling message
  • Room temperature concern
  • HVAC status
  • External vent condition
  • Fan sound or airflow observation
  • Recent high scanner utilization
  • Recent power or HVAC interruption
  • Presence of heat, odor, or smoke
  • Result of controlled functional test
  • Final scanner status

Final Thought

Treat thermal warnings seriously, rule out room conditions and external airflow restrictions first, never bypass protection, and escalate recurring cooling faults before allowing further patient imaging.

That is successful troubleshooting.
