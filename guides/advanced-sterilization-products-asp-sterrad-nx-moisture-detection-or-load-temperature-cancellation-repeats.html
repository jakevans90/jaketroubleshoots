Moisture Detection or Load-Temperature Cancellation Repeats

Asset Type

Sterilizer

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

STERRAD NX

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot recurring moisture or load-temperature cancellations caused by wet instruments, packaging, load preparation, room conditions, recent equipment use, or sterilizer problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Sterility Assurance

Do not release any load from a canceled cycle. Move processing to another validated sterilizer or approved process while investigating repeated cancellations.

Expected outcome: No potentially unsterile instrument is released for patient care.

2. Record the Exact Cancellation Condition

Document the complete displayed message and determine whether the system reports:

  • Moisture
  • Load temperature
  • Both under different circumstances
  • The condition only with specific trays or instruments

Expected outcome: The reported condition is distinguished from a general cycle cancellation.

3. Inspect the Load for Moisture

Coordinate with Sterile Processing to inspect instruments and packaging for:

  • Residual rinse water
  • Moisture in lumens or channels
  • Wet tray surfaces
  • Damp wraps or pouches
  • Condensation
  • Recently washed equipment that has not adequately dried

Expected outcome: The load is visibly dry before processing. If moisture is found, correct the preparation problem before further testing.

4. Review Load Preparation and Timing

Determine whether the affected devices were:

  • Recently washed
  • Recently removed from another heat-producing process
  • Stored in a cold location
  • Brought directly from a substantially different environment
  • Packaged before drying was complete

Do not modify validated processing procedures; coordinate process corrections with Sterile Processing leadership.

Expected outcome: Instruments are prepared and conditioned appropriately for the intended STERRAD process.

5. Check Load Arrangement

Verify that the chamber is not overloaded and that packages, trays, and devices are positioned according to approved processing practices without crowding or contact that could trap moisture.

Expected outcome: The load is arranged appropriately with no obvious external condition likely to interfere with processing.

6. Inspect the Chamber

With the sterilizer safe and idle, check the accessible chamber for:

  • Residual moisture
  • Debris
  • Spilled material
  • Packaging fragments
  • Unusual condensation

Do not disassemble chamber components.

Expected outcome: The chamber is clean and dry.

7. Compare With a Known-Good Dry Load

When permitted, run an approved test or controlled dry load.

If only specific instruments or load configurations trigger the cancellation, the problem likely follows the load rather than the sterilizer.

Expected outcome: A known-good dry load processes normally. If so, stop equipment troubleshooting and address the load-preparation issue.

8. Check Room and Equipment Conditions

Verify there has not been an unusual environmental event such as:

  • Significant room temperature change
  • HVAC problem
  • Sterilizer relocation
  • Prolonged shutdown
  • Condensation-producing conditions

Expected outcome: The sterilizer and loads are operating under normal facility environmental conditions.

9. Verify Repeatable Successful Processing

After correcting an external cause, complete the facility-required verification cycle and review the cycle record.

Expected outcome: The cycle completes without moisture or temperature cancellation. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Unexplained Repeated Cancellations

If known-good dry and appropriately conditioned loads continue to produce the same cancellation, remove the sterilizer from service.

Expected outcome: Qualified service personnel evaluate the sensing, vacuum, chamber-conditioning, control, or related systems.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as wet instruments, damp packaging, load arrangement, chamber moisture, and environmental conditions have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve pressure or temperature sensing, chamber conditioning, vacuum performance, control functions, or another service-level problem.

The sterilizer should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or service evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

A complete approved post-repair verification must be performed before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Repeated moisture cancellations often follow the load, so verify instrument drying before treating the sterilizer as the failed component.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported repeated STERRAD NX moisture cancellations with a recently washed instrument load."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found residual moisture in the load while a controlled dry load processed normally."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Coordinated proper drying and reprocessing of the affected instruments, verified successful operation with a dry load, and returned the sterilizer to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact cancellation message
  • Whether moisture or temperature was reported
  • Instruments involved
  • Load preparation status
  • Visible moisture or condensation
  • Chamber condition
  • Load arrangement
  • Room environmental concerns
  • Controlled-load result
  • Final cycle result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Repeated moisture or temperature cancellations should be approached by protecting the load, confirming preparation and environment, and proving the sterilizer fault before escalating to internal service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
