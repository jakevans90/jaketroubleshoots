Integrated Gas Analyzer Water Trap Is Full, Blocked, or Not Detected

Asset Type

Anesthesia Machine

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Atlan A350

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot gas analyzer water-trap recognition, blockage, moisture accumulation, seating problems, sampling-line issues, and associated gas-monitoring interruptions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Gas Monitoring

Do not troubleshoot unreliable respiratory-gas or anesthetic-agent monitoring while a patient depends on the affected system.

If the condition occurs during a case:

  • Inform anesthesia staff of the monitoring problem.
  • Ensure adequate alternate monitoring is available.
  • Move the patient to another verified anesthesia machine if safe monitoring cannot be maintained.
  • Do not bypass alarms or defeat protective monitoring functions to continue operation.

Expected outcome: Patient care continues with reliable monitoring independent of the affected gas analyzer.

If safe monitoring is restored using another verified device, remove the affected Atlan A350 from clinical use and continue troubleshooting off-patient.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Review what clinical staff observed and reproduce the condition only in a safe, non-patient test setup.

Check for:

  • Water-trap related messages or indications.
  • Missing respiratory-gas measurements.
  • Missing anesthetic-agent measurements.
  • Slow or intermittent gas readings.
  • Visible moisture in the trap or sample line.
  • Whether the problem occurs continuously or only after prolonged use.

Record the exact displayed message without assuming that the water trap itself has failed.

Expected outcome: The specific gas-analysis symptom and operating conditions are clearly identified.

If the condition cannot be reproduced and the analyzer operates normally through appropriate functional testing, document the findings and stop troubleshooting.

3. Inspect the Water Trap Externally

Inspect the installed water trap without opening or modifying it.

Look for:

  • Excess accumulated liquid.
  • Cracks or physical damage.
  • Contamination.
  • Improper installation.
  • A trap that is loose, tilted, or incompletely seated.
  • Incorrect or incompatible consumables.

Do not attempt to clean, modify, or reuse a disposable component unless specifically permitted by manufacturer documentation.

Expected outcome: The correct water trap is intact, appropriately installed, and not visibly overfilled or contaminated.

If correcting the trap installation restores reliable gas monitoring, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

4. Remove and Reseat the Water Trap

With the equipment in a safe test condition, remove and reinstall the water trap according to normal approved handling practices.

Inspect the mating area for:

  • Debris.
  • Moisture.
  • Damaged external interfaces.
  • Anything preventing full seating.

Do not probe internal ports or use tools that could damage seals or sensing interfaces.

Expected outcome: The water trap seats securely and the system recognizes it normally.

If recognition returns and gas measurements remain stable, the issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

5. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Water Trap

If the installed trap remains suspect, replace it with a known-good compatible water trap.

This separates a consumable problem from a problem within the analyzer or its detection interface.

Expected outcome: The analyzer recognizes the known-good trap and gas sampling resumes normally.

If the known-good trap corrects the problem, replace the defective consumable, document the finding, complete functional verification, and stop troubleshooting.

6. Inspect the Gas Sampling Line

Trace the external sample line from the patient sampling location to the analyzer.

Check for:

  • Kinks.
  • Compression.
  • Liquid contamination.
  • Disconnection.
  • Blockage.
  • Cracked tubing.
  • Loose connections.
  • Incorrect tubing or adapters.

Replace suspect disposable tubing rather than attempting unauthorized clearing methods.

Expected outcome: The sampling path is open, dry, securely connected, and appropriate for the system.

If replacing or reconnecting the sample line restores stable gas measurements, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

7. Check for Excess Moisture Entering the Sampling Path

Determine whether excessive condensation or liquid is repeatedly reaching the trap.

Inspect external contributing conditions such as:

  • Sampling-line routing.
  • Condensation collecting at low points.
  • Excessive moisture in the breathing circuit.
  • Incorrect positioning of sampling components.
  • Recently changed circuit accessories.

Correct external routing or accessory problems without changing clinical settings outside approved practice.

Expected outcome: Moisture is managed before it repeatedly overwhelms the analyzer water trap.

If the trap remains dry and analyzer operation stabilizes after correcting the external moisture source, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

8. Verify Gas Analyzer Operation With Known-Good External Components

With a known-good water trap and sampling line installed, verify that the system:

  • Detects the water trap.
  • Draws a sample normally.
  • Displays expected respiratory-gas information.
  • Provides anesthetic-agent information when applicable to the test setup.
  • Does not repeatedly report obstruction or water-trap problems.

Use only approved test methods and equipment.

Expected outcome: Gas analysis remains stable without water-trap, sampling, or recognition faults.

If successful, return-to-service testing may be completed and troubleshooting can stop.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Before returning the Atlan A350 to service:

  • Confirm normal startup.
  • Verify gas-monitoring operation.
  • Verify appropriate alarm functionality.
  • Confirm sample-line and water-trap installation.
  • Check for leaks or abnormal moisture accumulation.
  • Perform required return-to-service testing according to facility and manufacturer procedures.

Expected outcome: The anesthesia machine and integrated gas-monitoring path operate normally and reliably.

If any gas-monitoring function remains unreliable, do not return the device to service.

If the Problem Persists

If a known-good water trap and sample line are correctly installed and external moisture, blockage, routing, and connection problems have been ruled out, the fault may involve the analyzer's sampling system, trap-detection interface, internal pneumatic path, analyzer electronics, or service-level configuration.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not perform deep disassembly or internal analyzer repair without appropriate authorization, training, and service documentation. Complete all required functional and anesthesia-system return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate an unreliable gas-analysis system is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A working ventilator does not compensate for unreliable respiratory-gas monitoring; ensure alternate verified monitoring is available before troubleshooting during a case.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Anesthesia staff reported that the Atlan A350 repeatedly indicated a water-trap problem and stopped displaying respiratory-gas values."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the installed gas analyzer water trap was fully seated but blocked with accumulated moisture, while the external sampling line was otherwise intact."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the water trap with a compatible known-good unit, verified stable gas sampling and alarm operation, and completed functional return-to-service testing successfully."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact displayed message or indication.
  • Whether gas values disappeared or became intermittent.
  • Water-trap condition.
  • Sample-line condition and routing.
  • Visible moisture or condensation.
  • Results with a known-good water trap.
  • Results with a known-good sampling line.
  • Associated anesthetic-agent monitoring behavior.
  • Alarm behavior.
  • Functional test results.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Protect the patient first, then isolate the water trap, sampling line, moisture source, and external connections before assuming an internal analyzer failure. Verify normal gas monitoring before return to service and document the complaint, confirmed cause, corrective action, and final test results clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
