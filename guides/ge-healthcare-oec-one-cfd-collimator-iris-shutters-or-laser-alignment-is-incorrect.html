What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when field-shaping controls move incorrectly, appear asymmetric, or the positioning laser does not correspond with the expected imaging field.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient From Unverified Beam Alignment

Do not use a C-arm for patient imaging when beam collimation or alignment is suspected to be inaccurate. Incorrect field limitation can affect radiation exposure and clinical positioning.

Expected outcome: The unit is withheld from patient use until field operation and alignment are verified.

2. Confirm the Exact Complaint

Determine whether the issue affects the iris, shutters, laser, field centering, control response, or the relationship between the indicated and actual imaging field.

Expected outcome: The suspected alignment or collimation problem is clearly identified.

3. Inspect the Beam Path and Collimator Area Externally

Check for accessories, drapes, covers, tape, debris, or physical interference around the collimator opening or laser window. Inspect accessible surfaces for impact damage.

Expected outcome: Nothing external obstructs the field-shaping components or laser. If an obstruction caused the problem, remove it and verify operation.

4. Verify Normal Collimator Controls

Operate the standard iris and shutter controls through their normal user-accessible range while observing the displayed image under a controlled test setup.

Expected outcome: Field boundaries respond smoothly and consistently to control commands. If normal operation is restored after correcting an external control issue, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

5. Check C-Arm and Detector Positioning

Verify that the detector and source assembly are positioned normally and that the apparent misalignment is not caused by unusual angulation, positioning, or an object located above or below the intended reference plane.

Expected outcome: The geometry used for evaluation is appropriate and repeatable.

6. Inspect Laser Output

If a positioning laser is provided for the affected function, verify that it illuminates normally and is not obscured by contamination or external damage. Do not attempt internal laser adjustment without authorized procedures.

Expected outcome: The laser is visible and stable. A missing, unstable, or obviously displaced laser requires further evaluation.

7. Compare Visible Field With an Approved Alignment Test

Use an appropriate radiographic field or beam-alignment test device and the applicable facility or manufacturer procedure. Do not estimate alignment solely by visual impression.

Expected outcome: The actual radiation field and indicated or laser field agree within the applicable acceptance criteria.

8. Verify Repeatability at More Than One Field Setting

Check the field-shaping controls at several normal positions to identify sticking, asymmetry, or inconsistent movement.

Expected outcome: The iris and shutters respond consistently and field alignment remains acceptable throughout the tested range.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm normal control operation, field shape, image centering, and laser behavior using the approved test method.

Expected outcome: Collimation and alignment performance are repeatable and acceptable. If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Alignment or Collimator Faults

If field-shaping movement remains incorrect or alignment cannot be verified, do not adjust internal shutters, motors, mechanical stops, calibration values, or laser assemblies without authorized service procedures.

Expected outcome: The system remains out of service pending qualified repair and calibration.

If the Problem Persists

External obstruction, positioning, controls, and test geometry have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve collimator mechanics, motors, position sensing, laser alignment, calibration, or related control electronics.

Remove the device from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved imaging and radiation test equipment. Internal collimator or laser adjustment should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Return-to-service testing should verify field size, centering, shutter and iris movement, and applicable beam-alignment performance.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not accept visual laser appearance alone as proof of beam alignment; use the approved alignment test method before returning the C-arm to clinical use.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the OEC One CFD collimator field appeared asymmetric and did not match the intended imaging area."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found adhesive residue partially obstructing the external collimator opening and affecting the visible field."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering removed the residue, cleaned the accessible surface, and verified normal shutter movement and acceptable field alignment using the approved test method."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Iris, shutter, or laser function affected
  • Visible damage or obstruction
  • C-arm geometry during testing
  • Control response
  • Laser visibility
  • Test device used
  • Field settings tested
  • Alignment-test results
  • Repeatability results
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Treat beam-alignment concerns as radiation-safety issues, eliminate simple obstruction and geometry problems first, verify with proper test equipment, and escalate adjustment work appropriately.

That is successful troubleshooting.
