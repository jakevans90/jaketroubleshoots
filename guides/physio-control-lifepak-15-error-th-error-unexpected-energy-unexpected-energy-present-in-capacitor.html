Physio-Control LIFEPAK 15

Error: TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY Unexpected Energy Present in Capacitor

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Physio-Control

Model

LIFEPAK 15

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY when unexpected capacitor energy is detected and the LIFEPAK 15 does not deliver a charge.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

Ensure Patient Safety First

  • Immediately remove the LIFEPAK 15 from patient use if this error occurs during operation, testing, charging, or therapy preparation.
  • Use another verified defibrillator for patient care.
  • Expected: Patient care continues without relying on a defibrillator with an unresolved therapy fault.
  • Why it matters: Unexpected capacitor energy is a high-risk defibrillator condition and should not be evaluated while the device is needed for patient therapy.

Verify the Reported Error and Behavior

  • Confirm the exact displayed or logged error is TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY.
  • Confirm the associated therapy service code if available: a004.
  • Verify whether unexpected energy is present in the capacitor.
  • Verify whether no charge was delivered.
  • Note whether the device failed to proceed normally through charge or therapy operation.
  • Document whether the error appeared during startup, service review, charging attempt, or controlled testing.
  • Expected: The error is confirmed as a true LIFEPAK 15 therapy system fault.
  • Why it matters: This separates a documented therapy service condition from an accessory issue, operator setup issue, or failed shock attempt due to pads or cable placement.

Remove the Device From Clinical Service

  • Do not continue bedside troubleshooting after this error is confirmed.
  • Label the unit Out of Service and prevent clinical staff from returning it to use.
  • Expected: The LIFEPAK 15 is removed from patient care until evaluated and cleared.
  • Why it matters: Unexpected capacitor energy is a serious therapy system condition. The device should not be trusted for patient therapy until corrective action and performance testing are completed.

Check for Obvious External or Use-Related Conditions

  • Before sending the unit for repair, document basic external findings only.
  • Check the device housing condition.
  • Check the therapy cable condition.
  • Check the paddle or therapy connector condition.
  • Check battery seating.
  • Check the AC power adapter condition, if used.
  • Look for evidence of fluid intrusion, impact damage, burning smell, heat, or contamination.
  • Expected: Any visible external damage or contamination is identified and documented.
  • Why it matters: External findings may support the repair evaluation, but they do not clear the error.

Do Not Attempt Clinical Shock Testing at the Bedside

  • Do not attempt repeated charge, discharge, or shock delivery testing in a clinical area.
  • Any therapy testing should be performed only in a controlled Clinical Engineering or service environment using the proper analyzer or defibrillator test load.
  • Expected: Testing is controlled, safe, and documented.
  • Why it matters: This error involves capacitor energy status. Repeated uncontrolled testing increases safety risk and may not provide useful isolation.

Process the Service Log

  • Review and save the LIFEPAK 15 service log per department procedure.
  • Document TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY.
  • Document therapy service code a004.
  • Document when the error occurred.
  • Document whether a charge was attempted.
  • Document whether shock delivery failed or was inhibited.
  • Document any related therapy system messages.
  • Expected: The fault history is captured before repair or vendor escalation.
  • Why it matters: Service log data helps confirm the fault path and supports corrective action.

Isolate Only External Accessories if Required by Local Procedure

  • If your department requires basic external isolation before repair, inspect or swap only user-accessible accessories during controlled bench evaluation.
  • Check the therapy cable.
  • Check QUIK-COMBO electrodes or test load connection.
  • Check paddles, if applicable.
  • Check the battery pack.
  • Expected: Accessory issues are ruled out without opening the device or performing internal repair.
  • Why it matters: Most TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY cases point toward an internal therapy system issue, but external accessory condition should still be documented.

Complete Required Corrective Action and Performance Inspection

  • After repair or vendor corrective action, the LIFEPAK 15 must pass required performance inspection before return to service.
  • Include applicable defibrillation performance checks according to facility procedure and approved service requirements.
  • Expected: The device passes therapy-related performance testing before clinical release.

Return to Service Only When Fully Cleared

  • Return the LIFEPAK 15 to service only when the service code has been addressed.
  • Confirm the error no longer recurs.
  • Confirm therapy performance testing passes.
  • Confirm required documentation is complete.
  • Confirm the unit is physically safe and ready for use.
  • Expected: The device is safe for clinical deployment.

If the Problem Persists

If TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY remains active or returns after basic external checks, remove the LIFEPAK 15 from service and send it for repair or bench evaluation.

External and common causes have been ruled out. A persistent unexpected capacitor energy condition strongly indicates an internal therapy system failure.

The device should remain removed from service, labeled Out of Service, and held for service log review, repair, and required performance inspection.

Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot this error on an active patient. Move the patient to a backup device first and maintain therapy continuity. Do not troubleshoot this error while the device is needed for patient care. Transfer care to another verified defibrillator immediately. A defibrillator with unresolved capacitor or therapy errors should not be used for monitoring-dependent therapy readiness or emergency shock delivery.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"LIFEPAK 15 reported error TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY with therapy service code a004. Unexpected energy was present in the capacitor, and no charge was delivered."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Confirmed therapy system service error. External inspection performed; no bedside-correctable issue identified. Fault indicates likely internal defibrillator therapy/capacitor control condition requiring repair evaluation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed LIFEPAK 15 from clinical service and labeled Out of Service. Service log processed. Unit sent for repair/bench evaluation. Device not returned to use pending corrective action and successful defibrillation performance inspection."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Alarm behavior, including exact error TH_ERROR_UNEXPECTED_ENERGY and therapy service code a004.
  • Accessories swapped, including therapy cable, electrodes, paddles, test load, or battery pack.
  • Power behavior, including battery condition, AC power adapter condition, and whether the error occurred at startup, during charging, during testing, or after attempted therapy.
  • Environmental factors, including visible impact, fluid intrusion, heat, smell, contamination, or unusual storage conditions.
  • Indicator lights, service indicators, related therapy messages, and service log status.
  • Final device status, such as removed from service, repaired, passed testing, or awaiting evaluation.

Final Thought

This error should be treated as a serious therapy system condition. Clinical Engineering should confirm the reported error, check only safe external factors, capture the service log, and escalate appropriately. Patient safety and documented performance testing matter more than trying to force the device back into service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
