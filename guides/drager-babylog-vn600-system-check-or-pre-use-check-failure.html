Drager Babylog VN600

System Check or Pre-Use Check Failure

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Babylog VN600

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses failed system or pre-use checks caused by setup, circuit, accessory, gas supply, connection, or configuration problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ventilation

Do not troubleshoot a Babylog VN600 that is providing unreliable ventilation or alarms while a patient depends on it. Transfer ventilation to another verified ventilator or appropriate backup method before continuing.

Inspect the ventilator for obvious damage, contamination, fluid intrusion, unusual odor, excessive heat, or other unsafe conditions.

Expected outcome: The patient is supported independently and the ventilator can be evaluated safely. If damage or another unsafe condition is present, remove the ventilator from service and stop troubleshooting.

2. Confirm the Exact Check Failure

Repeat or review the system/pre-use check only when the ventilator is disconnected from a patient. Record which portion of the check fails and any displayed message.

Verify whether the failure is repeatable or occurred only once during setup.

Expected outcome: The failed portion of the check is clearly identified. If the check passes normally on repeat and subsequent operation is verified, troubleshooting can stop.

3. Verify AC Power and Basic Startup

Confirm the ventilator is connected to a known-good AC outlet and that the power cord and external connections are fully seated and undamaged.

Observe startup for abnormal resets, blank displays, or incomplete initialization.

Expected outcome: The ventilator powers and initializes normally without power-related interruptions. If correcting the power source resolves the system check failure, troubleshooting can stop after successful verification.

4. Verify Gas Supplies

Confirm required air and oxygen supplies are available and connected correctly. Inspect hoses and connectors for damage, loose fittings, kinks, or incorrect connections.

If appropriate, compare operation using another verified gas outlet or known-good approved gas hose.

Expected outcome: Required gas sources are present, stable, and correctly connected. If restoring the gas supply allows the check to pass, troubleshooting can stop.

5. Inspect the Patient Circuit and Accessories

Verify the breathing circuit is assembled correctly for the intended configuration. Inspect tubing, connectors, filters, humidification components, test lungs, adapters, and other external accessories for:

  • Loose connections
  • Cracks or splits
  • Occlusions
  • Incorrect assembly
  • Water accumulation
  • Missing components

Reseat all accessible connections.

Expected outcome: The circuit is complete, unobstructed, leak-free, and correctly assembled. If correcting the circuit allows the system check to pass, troubleshooting can stop.

6. Inspect the Expiratory Valve and Flow Measurement Components

Verify the expiratory valve and externally accessible flow-sensing components are the correct compatible items, properly installed, clean, dry, and fully seated.

Do not force components into place or perform internal disassembly.

Expected outcome: External measurement and expiratory components are recognized and correctly positioned. If reseating or replacing an approved external component resolves the check failure, troubleshooting can stop.

7. Substitute Known-Good External Components

When available, substitute known-good approved breathing circuit components, filters, sensors, hoses, or expiratory components one at a time.

Avoid changing several components simultaneously because this can obscure the cause.

Expected outcome: A defective or incorrectly assembled external component is isolated. If the check passes with the known-good component and repeat testing is successful, troubleshooting can stop.

8. Verify Intended Configuration

Confirm the ventilator is configured for the installed circuit, accessories, and intended patient application without entering unauthorized service menus or altering protected settings.

Correct only settings within normal Clinical Engineering authority and manufacturer-supported procedures.

Expected outcome: Installed hardware and selected configuration are compatible. If correcting an external configuration mismatch resolves the failure, troubleshooting can stop.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Repeat the complete required system or pre-use check using the appropriate manufacturer documentation.

Confirm normal startup, ventilation using suitable test equipment, alarm operation, displayed measurements, gas delivery, and accessory recognition as applicable.

Expected outcome: The ventilator completes the required checks successfully and operates normally. Document the results and return the device to service only when all required verification passes.

If the Problem Persists

If the Babylog VN600 continues to fail its system or pre-use check after external power, gas sources, circuit components, sensors, accessories, connections, and configuration have been verified, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve an internal pneumatic, sensing, electronic, calibration, software, or service-level issue.

The ventilator should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Drager documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not bypass a failed system check or return the ventilator to clinical use simply because it appears to ventilate. Return-to-service testing must demonstrate that required checks and functional tests pass.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never place a ventilator into patient service after an unresolved pre-use or system check failure.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported that the Babylog VN600 repeatedly failed the pre-use system check before patient use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a loose patient-circuit connection that caused the system check to fail."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The connection was properly seated, the complete system check was repeated successfully, and ventilator operation and alarms were verified before return to service."
