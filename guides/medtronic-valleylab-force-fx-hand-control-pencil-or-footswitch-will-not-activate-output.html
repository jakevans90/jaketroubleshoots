Medtronic Valleylab Force FX

Hand-Control Pencil or Footswitch Will Not Activate Output

Asset Type

Electrosurgical Unit (ESU)

Manufacturer

Medtronic

Model

Valleylab Force FX

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots failure to activate electrosurgical output from a hand-control pencil or footswitch, including accessory, connection, control, and setup-related causes.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Surgical Continuity

If the ESU cannot reliably activate during a procedure, do not continue troubleshooting while the patient depends on it. Provide a verified alternate ESU or approved surgical method before evaluating the affected unit.

Remove the Valleylab Force FX from active clinical use if output is absent, intermittent, or unpredictable.

Expected outcome: Patient care continues using reliable equipment, and the affected ESU is available for safe Clinical Engineering evaluation.

2. Confirm the Exact Activation Failure

Determine whether the reported problem involves:

  • Hand-control pencil activation only
  • Footswitch activation only
  • Both activation methods
  • Cut, coagulation, or both modes
  • No activation tone or indication
  • Activation indication present but no apparent output
  • Intermittent operation when the accessory or cable is moved

Reproduce the condition only using an appropriate test setup and approved electrosurgical analyzer or test load.

Expected outcome: The failure is narrowed to a specific activation method, mode, accessory, or general output condition.

If the reported condition cannot be reproduced and the unit passes complete functional testing, troubleshooting may stop after documenting the results.

3. Verify Power and Basic ESU Operation

Confirm the power cord is fully seated and undamaged, the unit powers normally, and no obvious abnormal display, alarm, odor, or overheating condition is present.

Test the AC receptacle if facility power is questionable.

Expected outcome: The generator powers normally without unrelated power or startup abnormalities.

If restoring a secure AC connection resolves the problem and output testing passes, troubleshooting can stop.

4. Inspect the Hand-Control Pencil and Connection

For a hand-control complaint:

  • Inspect the pencil, buttons, cable, plug, and active electrode for contamination, damage, cuts, looseness, or bent contacts.
  • Verify the plug is fully seated in the appropriate receptacle.
  • Confirm the accessory type is compatible with the intended connection and clinical use.
  • Do not use visibly damaged or contaminated accessories.

When appropriate, substitute a known-good compatible pencil.

Expected outcome: A properly connected known-good pencil activates the intended output reliably.

If the known-good pencil works and the original accessory does not, remove the defective accessory from use and stop troubleshooting after final ESU verification.

5. Inspect the Footswitch and Connection

For a footswitch complaint:

  • Inspect the footswitch housing, pedals, cable, connector, and strain relief.
  • Check for fluid contamination, crushed cable sections, loose connections, or physical damage.
  • Confirm the connector is fully seated in the correct footswitch receptacle.
  • Verify that the footswitch being tested corresponds with the intended function and connected accessory arrangement.

Use a known-good compatible footswitch when available.

Expected outcome: The known-good footswitch activates the intended output consistently without requiring cable movement or excessive pedal force.

If substitution resolves the problem, remove the failed footswitch from service and stop troubleshooting after confirming generator operation.

6. Verify Accessory and Mode Setup

Confirm that the accessory, selected operating mode, and intended activation method are appropriate for the test configuration.

Avoid changing clinical configuration simply to make an incompatible accessory function.

Expected outcome: The ESU is configured normally for the connected accessory and responds to the proper activation control.

If correcting an external setup condition restores reliable activation, troubleshooting can stop after functional testing.

7. Test Output With Approved Test Equipment

Using an approved electrosurgical analyzer or manufacturer-appropriate test setup, verify that activation produces an appropriate generator response in the applicable mode.

Do not judge output solely by creating open-air sparks or testing against unintended surfaces.

Compare hand-control and footswitch activation when applicable.

Expected outcome: Each intended activation method produces stable, repeatable output and normal activation indication under controlled testing.

If testing is normal with known-good accessories, the ESU may proceed to required return-to-service verification.

8. Evaluate for Intermittent Connections

Without opening the chassis, gently inspect and manipulate external accessory cables and connectors during controlled testing.

Stop immediately if output becomes erratic.

Expected outcome: Output remains stable regardless of normal external cable positioning.

If output cuts in and out at the generator receptacle despite a known-good accessory, remove the ESU from service for further evaluation.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correction:

  • Verify hand-control activation if applicable.
  • Verify footswitch activation if applicable.
  • Verify the required Cut and Coag functions.
  • Verify audible and visual activation indications.
  • Confirm no intermittent response occurs.
  • Complete required electrical safety or performance testing according to organizational and manufacturer requirements.

Expected outcome: The Valleylab Force FX operates reliably with approved accessories and passes required return-to-service testing.

If all tests pass, troubleshooting is complete.

If the Problem Persists

If known-good accessories, secure connections, proper setup, facility power, and controlled output testing do not restore reliable activation, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve an internal activation-input circuit, generator control problem, connector assembly, configuration issue, or another service-level condition.

The ESU should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved electrosurgical test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not continue clinical use when activation is unreliable. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Return the ESU to service only after the fault is corrected and all required functional, output, alarm, and safety checks pass.

Clinical Use Tip

If electrosurgical activation is unreliable during a procedure, exchange the ESU or accessory before continuing rather than repeatedly attempting activation on the patient.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Surgical staff reported that the Valleylab Force FX would not activate from the hand-control pencil."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the pencil cable intermittently open near the connector while the generator operated normally with a known-good pencil."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the defective pencil, verified reliable Cut and Coag activation with approved test equipment, and confirmed the ESU passed return-to-service testing."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Activation method affected
  • Cut, Coag, or both modes affected
  • Hand-control pencil condition
  • Footswitch condition
  • Connector condition
  • Known-good accessory substitutions
  • Activation tone and visual indication
  • Output analyzer results
  • Whether movement of the accessory cable reproduced the fault
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Start by protecting the patient, then verify accessories, connections, controls, and operating setup before assuming an internal generator fault. Escalate unreliable output appropriately and clearly document the complaint, confirmed cause, corrective action, and final testing.

That is successful troubleshooting.
