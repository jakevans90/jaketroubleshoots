What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot humidification or temperature-control problems involving power, circuit assembly, probes, cables, chambers, positioning, settings, and external humidifier components.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Appropriate Respiratory Support

If humidification or temperature control is unreliable during ventilation, follow clinical procedures to maintain appropriate ventilation and humidification using verified equipment. Do not troubleshoot a failing system while a patient depends on it.

Expected outcome: Patient ventilation and required airway conditioning continue safely while the suspect equipment is evaluated.

2. Identify Whether the Fault Is Ventilator-Side or External Humidifier-Side

Determine which device provides the affected humidification or temperature-control function in the installed configuration. Record any alarms or messages on the ventilator and external humidifier.

Avoid assuming a ventilator failure when a separate humidifier or accessory is responsible.

Expected outcome: The affected subsystem is clearly identified. If the problem is isolated to separate equipment, troubleshoot that device under the appropriate procedure.

3. Verify Power to the Humidification Equipment

If an external powered humidifier is used, inspect its AC connection, power cord, outlet, and power indications. Confirm the outlet is functional using facility-approved methods.

Expected outcome: The humidification equipment receives reliable power. If restoring power corrects temperature control, proceed to final system verification.

4. Inspect the Humidifier Chamber and Circuit Assembly

Check the chamber, breathing circuit, heated sections, adapters, and connections for correct assembly, secure seating, leaks, excessive water, incorrect fill condition, or physical damage.

Avoid disturbing a hot chamber until it can be handled safely.

Expected outcome: The chamber and circuit are assembled correctly without obvious leakage or obstruction. If correcting assembly resolves the fault, proceed to verification.

5. Check Temperature Probes and External Cables

Inspect accessible temperature probes and cables for correct placement, secure connections, bent contacts, frayed wiring, contamination, or damage. Confirm probes are positioned in the approved locations for the intended setup.

Expected outcome: Temperature-sensing components are correctly connected and positioned. If reseating or replacing a damaged approved external probe resolves the problem, continue to final verification.

6. Inspect Heated Circuit Connections

Where heated breathing-circuit components are used, verify all external electrical connections are fully seated and free of visible damage or contamination.

Do not bypass protective connections or attempt to energize damaged heating components.

Expected outcome: Heated-circuit connections are intact and secure. If an external cable or circuit section is defective, replace it with an approved component and retest.

7. Verify Settings and Configuration

Confirm externally accessible humidifier or ventilator settings correspond to the actual circuit configuration. Do not alter restricted service settings.

Check that the configured mode does not conflict with the attached accessories.

Expected outcome: Authorized settings match the physical setup. If correcting the setup or authorized configuration restores normal control, continue to verification.

8. Substitute Known-Good External Components

When allowed, use known-good compatible probes, cables, circuits, or humidifier components one at a time to isolate a failed accessory.

Expected outcome: The fault follows a specific external accessory or external components are ruled out. If an accessory is identified, replace it and verify stable operation.

9. Verify Stable Operation and Alarm Function

Operate the complete ventilator/humidification system under controlled non-patient conditions for sufficient time to verify stable temperature and appropriate alarm behavior according to current manufacturer requirements.

Expected outcome: Humidification and temperature indications remain stable, no inappropriate alarms occur, and applicable system testing passes. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Persistent or Unsafe Temperature-Control Problems

Remove the affected equipment from service if temperature regulation remains unreliable, heating is absent when required, overheating is suspected, or any component shows burning, melting, abnormal heat, or electrical damage.

Expected outcome: Unsafe respiratory conditioning equipment is prevented from clinical use and routed for qualified service.

If the Problem Persists

Power, circuit assembly, chamber condition, external probes, cables, heated-circuit connections, positioning, and authorized settings have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve internal sensing, heating control, communications between components, configuration, software, or another service-level condition.

The affected ventilator or humidification equipment should be:

  • Removed from service as appropriate.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated with relevant manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
  • Required to pass applicable electrical, temperature, alarm, circuit, and ventilator testing before return to use.

Stopping when temperature control cannot be demonstrated reliably is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Confirm the entire ventilator, circuit, humidifier, probe, and heated-wire pathway after any humidification-related correction rather than verifying only one component.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported the humidification system used with the bellavista 1000 displayed a temperature-control fault during setup."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external temperature-probe connector was partially disconnected."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering reseated the probe connection, verified stable humidification temperature and alarm operation under controlled testing, and returned the system to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact humidification or temperature complaint
  • Ventilator versus external humidifier indication
  • AC power status
  • Chamber and circuit condition
  • Temperature-probe placement
  • Cable and connector condition
  • Heated-circuit connection status
  • Settings or configuration observed
  • Known-good substitution results
  • Temperature and alarm behavior after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Humidification faults often involve the complete external system rather than the ventilator alone. Protect the patient, identify which device owns the function, inspect power and accessories first, and escalate whenever safe temperature control cannot be verified.

That is successful troubleshooting.
