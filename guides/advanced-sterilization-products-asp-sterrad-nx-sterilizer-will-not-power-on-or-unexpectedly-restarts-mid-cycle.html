Sterilizer Will Not Power On or Unexpectedly Restarts Mid-Cycle

Asset Type

Sterilizer

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

STERRAD NX

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot no-power and unexpected restart complaints caused by facility power, cords, connections, external protection devices, environmental conditions, or internal power faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Load and Processing Workflow

If the STERRAD NX loses power or restarts during a cycle, consider that cycle unsuccessful unless system documentation and facility policy specifically establish otherwise.

Do not release the affected load for patient use. Redirect processing to another validated sterilizer.

Expected outcome: The interrupted load remains controlled and patient care continues using verified equipment.

2. Confirm the Exact Power Complaint

Determine whether:

  • The unit is completely dead
  • The display briefly illuminates
  • It powers on and shuts off
  • It restarts only during a cycle
  • Other equipment on the same circuit was affected
  • The event followed a facility power disturbance

Record the cycle phase if a restart occurred during processing.

Expected outcome: A no-power condition is distinguished from intermittent reset or facility power loss.

3. Inspect the External Power Connection

Check the accessible power cord, plug, and connection for:

  • Loose connection
  • Damage
  • Heat discoloration
  • Cracked insulation
  • Bent or damaged plug components
  • Evidence of liquid contamination

Do not use a damaged power cord.

Expected outcome: The external power connection is secure and undamaged.

4. Verify the Facility Power Source

Using appropriate Clinical Engineering or Facilities procedures, verify that the intended receptacle has power.

If appropriate, compare with another known working outlet or test using approved equipment.

Do not bypass required grounding or electrical protection.

Expected outcome: Correct facility power is available at the equipment connection.

5. Check for Facility Electrical Events

Determine whether there was:

  • Breaker activity
  • Emergency-power transfer
  • Planned electrical work
  • Generator testing
  • Local outage
  • Simultaneous reset of nearby equipment

Coordinate with Facilities when the problem may originate upstream.

Expected outcome: Facility electrical causes are either identified or ruled out.

6. Inspect External Ventilation and Environment

Check that external ventilation openings are unobstructed and that the sterilizer is not exposed to excessive heat, liquid, or environmental conditions that could affect operation.

Expected outcome: The equipment environment is normal and external airflow is unobstructed.

7. Perform a Normal Startup

If safe and no active cycle remains, power the sterilizer on using the normal procedure.

Observe:

  • Startup behavior
  • Display operation
  • Unusual sounds
  • Unexpected shutdown
  • Repeated restart
  • Abnormal odor or heat

Expected outcome: The system completes startup without interruption.

8. Stop Immediately for Electrical Warning Signs

Remove the sterilizer from service if there is:

  • Burning odor
  • Smoke
  • Arcing
  • Excessive heat
  • Repeated breaker trip
  • Repeated spontaneous restart
  • Visible electrical damage

Do not repeatedly energize equipment showing these symptoms.

Expected outcome: A potentially unsafe electrical condition is contained.

9. Perform Controlled Functional Verification

If an external facility-power issue was corrected and the sterilizer now operates normally, complete the required functional and approved cycle verification before return to service.

Expected outcome: The sterilizer remains powered throughout a complete verification cycle without restarting. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Persistent Power or Restart Problems

If correct facility power is present but the sterilizer will not power on or continues restarting, remove it from service.

Expected outcome: Qualified service evaluates the internal power, control, or electrical systems.

If the Problem Persists

External power connection, receptacle power, facility electrical events, ventilation, and environmental causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal power supplies, protective devices, wiring, control electronics, thermal conditions, software, or another service-level failure.

The sterilizer should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or qualified service evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate ASP documentation and approved electrical test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete electrical-safety checks and manufacturer-appropriate functional and cycle verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Any unexpected loss of power during sterilization requires the affected load to be controlled as an incomplete process until appropriately reprocessed.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported that the STERRAD NX unexpectedly restarted during a cycle and the load did not complete processing."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an intermittent facility receptacle supplying the sterilizer while the equipment operated normally from a verified power source."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the affected receptacle from use, coordinated repair with Facilities, verified stable sterilizer operation through an approved cycle, and returned the sterilizer to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • No-power versus restart symptom
  • Cycle phase at power loss
  • Power-cord and plug condition
  • Receptacle test result
  • Other equipment affected
  • Recent breaker or generator events
  • Ventilation condition
  • Odor, heat, smoke, or electrical damage
  • Startup result
  • Verification-cycle result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Power problems require control of the interrupted load first, followed by systematic verification from the facility receptacle inward. Persistent resets with verified external power require proper service escalation and documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
