Vantive PrisMax

Transmembrane Pressure High Alarm

Asset Type

Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT)

Manufacturer

Vantive

Model

PrisMax

What This Guide Helps With

Helps troubleshoot transmembrane pressure alarms caused by circuit conditions, filter loading, setup, or external restrictions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Confirm patient safety before troubleshooting

Ensure the patient is supported and clinical staff are aware of the condition. Do not troubleshoot active therapy without appropriate clinical coordination. Expected outcome: Patient safety is maintained.

2. Verify the alarm condition

Confirm the exact alarm message, timing, and whether the alarm is intermittent or persistent. Expected outcome: The reported condition is confirmed.

3. Inspect tubing and external circuit path

Check visible tubing for kinks, compression, clamps, or restrictions that could affect flow. Expected outcome: External flow restrictions are corrected or ruled out.

4. Verify circuit setup and connections

Inspect visible connections and disposable installation for secure placement and proper external setup. Expected outcome: Circuit setup appears correct.

5. Review filter and treatment-related external factors

Coordinate with clinical staff regarding treatment duration, circuit condition, and any recent changes associated with the alarm. Expected outcome: External contributors are identified or excluded.

6. Perform final verification

After correction, verify stable operation and absence of recurring alarms. Expected outcome: The device operates normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

External causes have been ruled out. The device should be:

  • Removed from service if safe operation cannot be maintained
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Possible remaining causes include internal monitoring or control issues requiring service evaluation.

Clinical Use Tip

Coordinate with clinical staff before making any changes that may interrupt ongoing therapy.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported a transmembrane pressure high alarm during PrisMax CRRT therapy."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found no external tubing or setup issues during inspection."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering verified the device condition, documented findings, and escalated for qualified service evaluation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Alarm message
  • Treatment duration
  • Circuit observations
  • Tubing condition
  • Setup verification
  • Clinical observations
  • Final disposition

Final Thought

Safe troubleshooting requires ruling out external causes, verifying equipment performance, and knowing when escalation is appropriate. Accurate documentation supports safe patient care and equipment reliability. That is successful troubleshooting.
