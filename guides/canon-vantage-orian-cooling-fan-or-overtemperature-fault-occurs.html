What This Guide Helps With

Addresses cooling warnings, abnormal fan conditions, or temperature faults caused by airflow obstruction, room conditions, facility HVAC, or service-level cooling problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Stop Unreliable Operation

If the system reports an overtemperature condition, shuts down, or cooling reliability is uncertain, stop clinical scanning and remove the patient using the normal MRI-safe workflow.

Do not continue scanning to determine whether the fault worsens.

Expected outcome: No patient depends on equipment with a possible thermal-control problem.

2. Record the Exact Condition

Document the displayed warning or fault exactly. Ask whether staff noticed unusual fan noise, reduced airflow, heat, odor, repeated shutdowns, or a room-temperature problem.

Expected outcome: The thermal complaint and associated symptoms are clearly defined.

3. Inspect the Scanner Environment

Check the equipment room and scan room for unusual temperature, blocked ventilation areas, nearby construction, closed vents, portable equipment obstructing airflow, or other obvious environmental changes.

Expected outcome: Required external airflow paths appear unobstructed and the environment is not obviously abnormal.

4. Check Facility HVAC Status

Determine whether the facility HVAC system is operating normally and whether there has been a recent alarm, maintenance event, power interruption, or loss of environmental control.

Do not attempt to compensate for a facility HVAC failure by repeatedly restarting the MRI.

Expected outcome: Facility environmental support is confirmed or the appropriate Facilities team is engaged.

5. Inspect Accessible Airflow Areas

Without removing protected panels, inspect accessible intake and exhaust areas for obvious blockage, debris, or items stored too close to ventilation paths.

Expected outcome: Accessible airflow areas are clear.

6. Listen for Abnormal Cooling Sounds

From a safe location, note whether expected cooling fans appear to operate or whether there is unusual grinding, rattling, cycling, or silence associated with the complaint.

Do not open cabinets to inspect internal fans.

Expected outcome: No obvious external evidence of a mechanical cooling problem is present.

7. Allow Recovery Only According to Approved Procedures

If the issue was caused by a verified room-environment problem and approved procedures permit recovery after conditions normalize, allow the system to return to its normal state without repeated forced restarts.

Expected outcome: The system returns to a normal ready condition without recurring thermal warning.

8. Verify Stability Before Clinical Use

Observe the scanner through an appropriate functional check after the identified external cause is corrected. Verify no cooling or temperature warning returns and the system remains stable.

Expected outcome: Normal system readiness is maintained without abnormal heat or cooling indications.

If achieved, troubleshooting can stop.

9. Stop for Heat, Odor, Smoke, or Recurring Thermal Faults

If abnormal heat, burning odor, smoke, repeated overtemperature warnings, or unusual internal noise is present, stop troubleshooting immediately.

Do not energize the system repeatedly.

Expected outcome: A potentially unsafe thermal condition is isolated from clinical use.

10. Escalate Persistent Cooling Problems

If environmental and airflow checks are normal but the warning remains or returns, remove the MRI from service for qualified evaluation.

Expected outcome: Internal cooling or thermal-control problems are handled at the appropriate service level.

If the Problem Persists

Common external ventilation, room-condition, obstruction, and facility-HVAC causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve internal fans, cooling circuits, temperature sensing, subsystem cooling, power electronics, environmental monitoring, or another service-level condition.

The MRI system should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for qualified repair or system evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Canon documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify stable cooling operation and complete the required functional and safety checks before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

MRI cooling complaints should never be managed by simply clearing the warning and continuing scans; identify whether the environment or the scanner is actually stable.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"MRI staff reported a temperature warning and scanner shutdown during operation."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the equipment-room HVAC was not maintaining normal environmental conditions."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Coordinated HVAC correction with Facilities, verified the room condition normalized, and confirmed the MRI returned to stable operation without recurrence during functional verification."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact warning displayed
  • Room condition at time of fault
  • HVAC status
  • Recent utility or maintenance event
  • Accessible airflow obstruction
  • Fan noise or lack of airflow
  • Heat or odor observed
  • Whether fault returned after recovery
  • Facilities involvement
  • Final scanner status

Final Thought

Thermal faults require prompt attention because both scanner reliability and patient workflow may be affected. Rule out room and airflow causes first, stop for unsafe heat or odor conditions, escalate persistent faults, and verify stable operation before return to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
