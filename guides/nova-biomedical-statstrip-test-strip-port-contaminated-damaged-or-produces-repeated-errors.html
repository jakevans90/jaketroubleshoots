Nova Biomedical StatStrip

Test Strip Port Contaminated, Damaged, or Produces Repeated Errors

Asset Type

Blood Glucose Meter

Manufacturer

Nova Biomedical

Model

StatStrip

What This Guide Helps With

Strip-port problems caused by blood, disinfectant residue, debris, moisture, bent material, physical damage, or service-level strip-interface failure.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Remove an Unreliable Meter From Active Testing

If the strip port is visibly damaged, wet, contaminated internally, or producing repeated test errors, replace the meter for ongoing patient testing.

Expected outcome: Clinical testing continues on a reliable device.

2. Confirm the Reported Behavior

Determine whether strips are difficult to insert, fail to register, disconnect intermittently, trigger repeated messages, or appear physically loose.

Expected outcome: The strip-port symptom is clearly identified.

3. Inspect the Port Externally

Under good lighting, examine the strip-port opening for blood, lint, dried residue, corrosion, foreign material, cracks, deformation, or signs of liquid intrusion.

Do not insert probes, swabs, picks, or tools unless specifically permitted by approved manufacturer instructions.

Expected outcome: The external condition of the strip port is known.

4. Inspect Test Strips

Confirm that strips are flat, clean, dry, undamaged, and being inserted correctly.

A damaged strip can resemble a meter-port failure.

Expected outcome: Strip damage is ruled out.

5. Clean Only by Approved External Procedure

If contamination is limited to externally accessible surfaces, clean and disinfect according to approved instructions.

Allow the meter to dry completely before retesting.

Expected outcome: Accessible contamination is removed without introducing additional moisture.

6. Test With Known-Good Strips

Use a known-good strip supply and observe insertion, recognition, and retention.

Do not force a strip into a resistant or visibly damaged port.

Expected outcome: The meter consistently recognizes properly inserted strips.

7. Compare With Another Meter

Insert a strip from the same known-good supply into another StatStrip.

This helps distinguish strip-lot or handling issues from a port-specific problem.

Expected outcome: The fault follows either the suspect meter or the consumables.

8. Perform Approved Control Testing

If the port appears intact and recognizes strips, complete approved QC or functional testing.

Expected outcome: Testing completes without repeated strip-related errors.

If the meter passes repeated testing, troubleshooting may stop.

9. Reinspect After Testing

Remove the strip and confirm that no unusual resistance, looseness, debris, or visible damage developed during testing.

Expected outcome: The strip port remains mechanically intact and clean.

10. Escalate Physical or Persistent Port Problems

If the port is cracked, deformed, loose, corroded, repeatedly loses strip recognition, or cannot be safely cleaned externally, stop troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: A meter with an unreliable strip interface is removed from clinical service.

If the Problem Persists

Common strip, contamination, and handling causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal strip-contact damage, internal contamination, liquid damage, mechanical wear, or an electronic strip-interface fault.

The meter should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Nova Biomedical documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel.
  • Verified with repeated strip recognition and approved functional testing before return to service.

Attempting to clean or manipulate inaccessible port components beyond approved limits is not appropriate external troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never force a test strip into a damaged or resistant strip port; use another verified meter instead.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported repeated strip errors and intermittent strip recognition on the StatStrip."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found dried blood residue around the accessible strip-port opening while the port showed no visible structural damage."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The meter was cleaned using the approved procedure and passed repeated strip-recognition and control testing before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact strip-related message
  • Strip insertion feel
  • Visible blood or residue
  • Moisture or corrosion
  • Physical damage
  • Known-good strip results
  • Comparison-meter results
  • Cleaning performed
  • QC results
  • Final strip-recognition status

Final Thought

Inspect strips and accessible contamination before assuming internal failure, never force or probe a damaged port, and escalate whenever reliable strip recognition cannot be demonstrated.

That is successful troubleshooting.
