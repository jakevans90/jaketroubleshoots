Oxygen Control or Oxygen Readout Is Unavailable or Inaccurate

Asset Type

Infant Incubator

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Babyleo TN500 IncuWarmer

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots unavailable or questionable oxygen control/readout caused by gas supply, connections, configuration, sensor conditions, accessories, or environmental factors.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ordered Oxygen Therapy

Do not troubleshoot questionable oxygen control or oxygen measurement while an infant depends on the affected function.

Move the patient to a verified alternate device or establish another approved method of maintaining and monitoring the ordered oxygen environment before troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: Required oxygen therapy and monitoring are maintained independently of the affected Babyleo TN500.

2. Confirm the Exact Oxygen Complaint

Determine whether:

  • Oxygen control cannot be selected.
  • Oxygen measurement is unavailable.
  • A reading is present but appears incorrect.
  • The displayed concentration does not respond as expected.
  • The function becomes unavailable intermittently.
  • A message indicates a supply, measurement, or system condition.

Record the exact display information.

Expected outcome: The problem is distinguished as a control, supply, measurement, configuration, or intermittent issue.

3. Verify the External Oxygen Supply

Check the external oxygen source and confirm that the appropriate gas supply is available according to facility requirements.

Inspect the supply connection for:

  • Correct gas source.
  • Secure attachment.
  • Damaged hose or fitting.
  • Kinks or compression.
  • Obvious leaks.
  • Recently disconnected or changed connections.

Do not assume the incubator has an internal oxygen fault until the external supply has been verified.

Expected outcome: The correct oxygen source is available and securely connected. If restoring the external supply restores normal oxygen operation, proceed to verification and troubleshooting can stop.

4. Verify Gas Hose Routing and Connections

Trace the external oxygen connection from the source to the Babyleo TN500. Make sure the hose is connected to the intended inlet and is not pinched behind equipment or under casters.

Use only appropriate compatible gas connections and accessories.

Expected outcome: Oxygen reaches the device through an intact, correctly routed connection. Correcting a loose or obstructed connection should restore expected operation.

5. Inspect the Patient Compartment and External Openings

Inspect the incubator configuration for open access points, poorly positioned panels, improperly seated components, or other external conditions that could prevent the controlled environment from stabilizing.

Do not attempt to defeat normal access or safety mechanisms.

Expected outcome: The patient compartment and accessible components are positioned normally. If correcting an external positioning issue stabilizes the reading, continue to final verification.

6. Check Normal Oxygen Configuration

Verify that oxygen functionality is enabled and configured appropriately using only authorized user-accessible settings.

Compare the configuration with facility standards, manufacturer documentation, or a known-good equivalent unit when appropriate.

Do not enter unauthorized service menus or change protected calibration data.

Expected outcome: Oxygen functionality is appropriately configured. If an authorized configuration correction restores the function, document the change and verify operation.

7. Allow Appropriate Stabilization

If the unit was recently powered on, opened extensively, moved, or disconnected from the gas source, allow the oxygen system and incubator environment to stabilize according to approved operating procedures before declaring the reading inaccurate.

Avoid repeatedly opening the compartment during verification.

Expected outcome: The oxygen indication stabilizes and responds consistently. If it remains unstable or implausible, continue troubleshooting.

8. Compare the Oxygen Reading With Approved Test Equipment

With the device off-patient, use an appropriate calibrated oxygen analyzer or approved test method to independently evaluate the oxygen environment when this comparison is within Clinical Engineering scope.

Position and use the reference instrument according to its instructions and applicable manufacturer procedures.

Do not adjust calibration solely to force agreement with the reference instrument.

Expected outcome: The Babyleo TN500 oxygen indication agrees acceptably with the approved reference according to applicable manufacturer criteria. If it does, the reported issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop.

9. Evaluate for External Influences

Consider external factors that can affect an oxygen verification, including:

  • Frequent opening of the incubator.
  • Incorrectly positioned access components.
  • Strong room airflow near the unit.
  • Incorrect test-instrument placement.
  • Unstable external gas supply.
  • Recently changed equipment configuration.

Correct these factors and repeat the approved verification.

Expected outcome: The oxygen environment becomes stable and repeatable. If the unit remains inaccurate or unavailable, escalation is required.

10. Perform Final Oxygen and Alarm Verification

Before returning the unit to service, verify oxygen control/readout and associated alarm functions using applicable Drager procedures and calibrated test equipment.

Confirm that the unit responds consistently and that no unresolved oxygen-related messages remain.

Expected outcome: Oxygen measurement/control and applicable alarms pass required functional checks. If so, the issue is resolved and troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

If oxygen control remains unavailable, the reading remains inaccurate, or the oxygen environment cannot be verified after checking the external gas source, connections, compartment configuration, authorized settings, stabilization, and reference measurement, the problem may involve an internal oxygen measurement, control, gas-delivery, configuration, or service-level condition.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Drager documentation and approved calibrated gas-analysis equipment.
  • Repaired, calibrated, or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not perform unsupported calibration adjustments or internal gas-system repairs. Following repair or calibration, complete the required oxygen, alarm, and overall return-to-service verification before clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never rely on a questionable incubator oxygen reading for an infant; maintain ordered oxygen using a verified alternate method until accuracy is confirmed.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Babyleo TN500 oxygen function was available but the displayed concentration would not stabilize."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external oxygen supply hose partially kinked behind the unit, restricting the gas supply."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering corrected the hose routing, verified stable oxygen operation with approved test equipment, confirmed applicable alarms, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact oxygen-related message
  • Whether control, measurement, or both were affected
  • External oxygen-source status
  • Hose and fitting condition
  • Gas connection tested
  • Incubator access-panel or compartment condition
  • Authorized oxygen configuration observed
  • Independent analyzer result
  • Conditions under which the reading became unstable
  • Results before and after correction
  • Oxygen alarm verification
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Oxygen-control complaints require verification rather than assumption. Protect the patient, confirm the gas source and complete delivery path, eliminate environmental and configuration causes, compare with approved calibrated equipment, and escalate unresolved measurement or control problems.

That is successful troubleshooting.
