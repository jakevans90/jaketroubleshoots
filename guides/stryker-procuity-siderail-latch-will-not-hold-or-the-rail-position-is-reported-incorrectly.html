Siderail Latch Will Not Hold or the Rail Position Is Reported Incorrectly

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

ProCuity

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot a ProCuity siderail that will not latch securely or whose physical rail position does not agree with the bed’s reported status.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Remove the Bed From Active Dependence

Do not troubleshoot an unreliable siderail while a patient depends on it for fall protection, positioning, or support. Transfer the patient to another verified bed when necessary, or ensure equivalent fall-prevention measures are in place before testing.

Inspect for a rail that drops unexpectedly, fails to remain raised, or could create an entrapment or fall hazard. If the rail cannot be secured reliably, remove the bed from service.

Expected outcome: The patient is protected and the bed can be evaluated without relying on the affected siderail. If the siderail presents an immediate safety risk, label the bed Out of Service and stop clinical use.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Operate the affected siderail through its normal external range without forcing it. Determine whether the complaint is:

  • The rail will not remain latched.
  • The release mechanism does not reset.
  • The rail physically latches but the bed reports the wrong position.
  • The indication changes intermittently when the rail is moved.
  • Only one siderail is affected.

Compare operation with another siderail on the same bed when appropriate.

Expected outcome: The mechanical latch problem, position-reporting problem, or combination of both is clearly identified. If normal operation is restored and remains repeatable, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

3. Inspect the Siderail and Latch Area Externally

Inspect the rail, latch engagement area, release handle or control, mounting points, and surrounding covers for:

  • Bent or damaged components.
  • Loose hardware visible without disassembly.
  • Debris or dried contamination.
  • Linens or equipment interfering with rail travel.
  • Cracks, impact damage, or obvious misalignment.

Do not lubricate or adjust components unless specifically permitted by approved service documentation.

Expected outcome: The rail can move freely into its normal latch position without external obstruction. If removing an obstruction restores reliable latching, proceed to final functional verification.

4. Verify Full Rail Travel and Latch Engagement

Raise and lower the rail several times using normal technique. Make sure it reaches its intended mechanical stop and that the latch fully engages rather than catching only partially.

Lightly verify that the raised rail remains secure without applying excessive force.

Expected outcome: The siderail repeatedly reaches its normal position and remains mechanically secured. If it does not latch consistently, remove the bed from service and continue only with safe external inspection.

5. Compare Physical Position With the Bed Indication

With the bed powered normally, move the affected rail between its available positions and observe any displayed or indicated siderail status.

Compare the physical rail position with the system indication and, when useful, compare response with another functioning rail.

Do not enter restricted service menus or alter calibration values to force the indication to agree.

Expected outcome: The reported rail position changes consistently with physical movement. If physical operation is correct but reporting remains incorrect, service-level evaluation is required.

6. Check External Wiring and Accessible Connections

If the siderail incorporates external controls or position sensing, inspect accessible cables, strain-relief points, connectors, and harness routing near the rail for pinching, stretching, contamination, looseness, or visible damage.

Reseat only user-accessible or service-approved external connectors.

Expected outcome: Accessible wiring is secure and undamaged. If reseating an approved accessible connection restores correct status reporting, repeat rail operation several times and proceed to final verification.

7. Verify Bed Power and Basic Controls

Confirm that the bed is connected to an appropriate AC source and that normal bed functions operate without unrelated power or control problems.

If the complaint occurs only on battery operation, compare operation on AC and battery without assuming the siderail itself is defective.

Expected outcome: The siderail complaint is not being caused by a broader power or control problem. If normal operation returns under proper power conditions and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Cycle the affected siderail repeatedly. Confirm:

  • Smooth travel.
  • Reliable latch engagement.
  • Proper release.
  • No unexpected dropping or movement.
  • Correct position indication when applicable.
  • No visible damage or interference.

Perform any required return-to-service inspection according to approved hospital and manufacturer procedures.

Expected outcome: The rail latches securely, releases normally, and its reported status agrees with its physical position. The bed may return to service only when all safety-related functions are reliable.

If the Problem Persists

If the siderail still will not latch, will not remain secured, or its reported position remains incorrect after external causes are ruled out, the problem may involve the latch mechanism, position-sensing system, wiring, adjustment, or another service-level component.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired, adjusted, or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not continue external troubleshooting when reliable siderail retention or position reporting cannot be verified. Knowing when to stop is proper troubleshooting.

After repair, verify mechanical rail security, position reporting, controls, alarms or indicators affected by siderail position, and all required return-to-service safety checks.

Clinical Use Tip

A siderail that appears raised but does not positively latch is a fall hazard; never rely on visual position alone when returning the bed to clinical use.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the left siderail would raise but would not remain latched and occasionally showed the wrong rail position."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found dried contamination in the accessible latch area preventing full rail travel and complete engagement."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the accessible latch area, cycled the siderail repeatedly, verified secure latching and correct position indication, and returned the bed to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Which siderail is affected.
  • Whether the rail physically latches.
  • Whether the displayed rail position is correct.
  • Whether the issue is intermittent or constant.
  • Visible damage or contamination.
  • Accessible connector condition.
  • AC versus battery operation.
  • Comparison with another siderail.
  • Results before and after correction.
  • Final bed status.

Final Thought

Siderail problems can directly affect patient fall prevention. Start with patient safety, verify mechanical engagement before assuming an electronic failure, check accessible external causes logically, and escalate whenever secure operation cannot be proven. Document the complaint, actual cause, corrective action, and final verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
