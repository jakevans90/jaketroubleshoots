Getinge ED-Flow

Water Filter Is Not Recognized or Final-Rinse Quality Test Fails

Asset Type

Endoscope Reprocessor (AER)

Manufacturer

Getinge

Model

ED-Flow

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots filter-recognition and final-rinse quality failures caused by filter installation, connections, utilities, consumables, or external water-quality conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Reprocessing Workflow

Do not release scopes processed during a cycle in which required final-rinse quality cannot be confirmed.

Provide another verified reprocessor or follow the facility's approved contingency process while the ED-Flow is evaluated.

Expected outcome: Potentially affected scopes remain out of clinical use until reprocessing requirements are satisfied.

2. Confirm the Exact Failure

Determine whether the complaint is:

  • Filter not detected or recognized.
  • Filter status unexpectedly rejected.
  • Final-rinse quality test failure.
  • Intermittent recognition.
  • Failure affecting every cycle or only occurring after a recent filter replacement.

Record the exact displayed message if available without interpreting it beyond what the device reports.

Expected outcome: The failure is clearly identified as recognition-related, quality-test-related, or both.

3. Verify the Correct Filter Is Installed

Confirm that the installed water filter is the approved type for the ED-Flow configuration and is being used according to facility and manufacturer requirements.

Check for packaging, labeling, or installation discrepancies that could indicate the wrong consumable was fitted.

Do not bypass filter-recognition functions.

Expected outcome: The correct approved filter is present.

4. Inspect Filter Installation

With the system safe to access, inspect the filter housing and accessible connections.

Look for:

  • Filter not fully seated.
  • Loose fittings.
  • Incorrect orientation.
  • Damaged connector surfaces.
  • Pinched tubing.
  • Visible leaks.
  • Missing or displaced external seals.

Correct only externally accessible installation issues.

Expected outcome: The filter and its external connections are fully seated, secure, and leak-free.

5. Inspect Filter Recognition Interfaces

If the filter uses an external identification or recognition feature, inspect the accessible interface for contamination, damage, misalignment, or incomplete engagement.

Clean only in accordance with approved maintenance practices.

Do not defeat, simulate, or bypass the recognition feature.

Expected outcome: The filter identification interface is clean, intact, and correctly engaged.

6. Verify External Water Supply

Confirm that the reprocessor has an available water supply and that there has been no recent:

  • Facility water shutdown.
  • Plumbing work.
  • Pressure interruption.
  • Water-treatment service.
  • Utility change affecting the department.

Inspect accessible hoses and connections for restrictions, leakage, or accidental closure of an external supply valve.

Expected outcome: The incoming water source is available and the external supply path is intact.

7. Check Whether the Failure Began After Filter Replacement

Review the timing of the complaint.

If the problem began immediately after a filter change, recheck:

  • Filter identity.
  • Installation.
  • Seating.
  • External connections.
  • Any required approved replacement process.

Do not perform undocumented resets or unauthorized service-menu changes to force acceptance.

Expected outcome: A replacement-related installation problem is either corrected or ruled out.

8. Differentiate Filter Recognition From Water-Quality Failure

If the filter is recognized but the final-rinse quality test still fails, focus on the water path and facility water conditions rather than repeatedly reinstalling a correctly detected filter.

If the filter is not recognized at all, recognition interfaces and approved consumable compatibility remain the primary external checks.

Expected outcome: Troubleshooting follows the actual failure mode instead of treating two different conditions as identical.

9. Compare With a Known-Good Approved Filter When Appropriate

If facility procedures permit and an approved known-good filter is available, substitute it to determine whether the condition follows the filter.

Do not reuse a filter in a manner prohibited by infection-control or manufacturer requirements.

Expected outcome: A known-good comparison helps distinguish a filter-specific issue from a reprocessor or utility problem.

10. Perform Controlled Verification

After correcting an external issue, perform the appropriate approved verification cycle or quality check.

Confirm that:

  • The filter is recognized.
  • Required rinse-quality verification passes.
  • No related warning remains.
  • The system completes its required checks.

Expected outcome: Filter recognition and final-rinse quality verification succeed. Troubleshooting can stop once proper operation is confirmed.

11. Remove From Service if Quality Cannot Be Confirmed

If the filter remains unrecognized or final-rinse quality continues to fail after external causes have been ruled out, do not continue processing clinical scopes.

Expected outcome: The ED-Flow remains unavailable until its water-quality function is reliable.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes including wrong consumable, incorrect installation, poor seating, accessible recognition-interface problems, external water restrictions, and obvious facility utility issues have been ruled out.

Remaining possibilities include an internal recognition circuit, sensor, water-treatment component, internal flow-path problem, configuration issue, or facility water-quality condition requiring further investigation.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Getinge documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Coordinate with facilities, infection prevention, water-treatment personnel, or the manufacturer when the failure appears related to infrastructure or water quality.

Complete required verification before returning the unit to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A completed cycle does not override a failed required final-rinse quality check; affected scopes must remain out of use.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported that the ED-Flow would not recognize the newly installed water filter and would not proceed with normal verification."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the approved filter incompletely seated in its external connection."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reseated the filter correctly, verified secure connections, completed the approved system verification successfully, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact filter-related message.
  • Filter identification or type.
  • Whether the issue followed a filter change.
  • Filter seating and connector condition.
  • Evidence of leaks.
  • Incoming water availability.
  • Recent plumbing or water-treatment work.
  • Known-good filter substitution result.
  • Final-rinse quality-test result.
  • Results before and after correction.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Protect the reprocessing chain by treating failed water-quality verification seriously, confirm the approved filter and external water path before suspecting internal failure, escalate unresolved conditions, and document the verified cause and final status.

That is successful troubleshooting.
