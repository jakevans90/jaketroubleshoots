GE Healthcare AMX Navigate

Powered Drive Will Not Engage or Steering Assist Pulls to One Side

Asset Type

Mobile X-Ray System

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

AMX Navigate

What This Guide Helps With

Powered transport will not engage, steering feels uneven, or the unit pulls to one side during movement.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Control the Equipment

Do not troubleshoot unreliable powered movement while transporting the system near a patient, bed, staff, or other equipment. Move the AMX Navigate to a controlled area if this can be done safely. If movement cannot be controlled predictably, remove the unit from clinical use.

Expected outcome: The system is positioned where unexpected motion cannot injure a patient or staff member.

2. Confirm the Exact Movement Complaint

Determine whether the problem is:

  • No powered drive at all
  • Intermittent drive engagement
  • Excessive effort required to move the unit
  • Steering assist pulling consistently left or right
  • A problem occurring only forward, reverse, or during turning

Operate the controls only enough to reproduce the condition safely.

Expected outcome: The failure pattern is clearly identified. If operation is normal and repeatable, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

3. Verify Normal Startup and System Status

Confirm the system completes normal startup and does not display an active condition that intentionally inhibits transport. Verify the main battery is sufficiently charged for testing and that an emergency stop is not activated.

Expected outcome: The system is powered normally and no obvious safety inhibit is preventing drive operation.

4. Inspect Wheels, Casters, and the Travel Path

Inspect all accessible wheels and casters for:

  • Debris wrapped around the wheel or axle area
  • Foreign objects
  • Flat spots or obvious damage
  • Uneven rolling resistance
  • A caster that does not rotate or swivel freely
  • Floor conditions that could create apparent pulling

Do not disassemble wheel-drive components during this check.

Expected outcome: Wheels and casters move freely without obvious mechanical obstruction. If removing external debris restores normal movement, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

5. Check Drive and Steering Controls

Inspect accessible drive handles, switches, grips, or sensors for physical damage, sticking, contamination, or incomplete return to the neutral position. Compare left and right control response where applicable.

Expected outcome: Controls operate smoothly and consistently without sticking or visible damage.

6. Compare Powered and Manual Movement

When permitted by the equipment condition and local procedure, determine whether the unit rolls evenly without powered assist. A unit that pulls even without powered drive suggests a wheel, caster, brake, or mechanical resistance issue rather than solely an electronic steering-assist problem.

Expected outcome: Manual rolling behavior helps separate mechanical resistance from powered-drive behavior.

7. Inspect Accessible External Connections

Inspect visible external harness areas, connectors, covers, and control assemblies associated with transport for loose, damaged, pinched, or contaminated components. Do not open drive electronics or perform internal board-level troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: Accessible connections are secure and free from obvious damage.

8. Perform a Controlled Movement Test

In an open area, test:

  • Straight forward movement
  • Straight reverse movement
  • Gentle left and right turns
  • Stop response
  • Steering assist

Do not return the unit to service if movement is unpredictable.

Expected outcome: Powered movement is smooth, controllable, and does not consistently pull to one side.

9. Complete Final Functional Verification

After any external correction, repeat several controlled transport maneuvers and confirm normal stopping and steering behavior.

Expected outcome: Normal movement is repeatable. If so, document the correction and return the system to service according to facility procedure.

10. Escalate an Unresolved Drive Problem

If powered drive remains unavailable, intermittent, or asymmetric after external causes are ruled out, stop troubleshooting at the external level.

Expected outcome: The unit is removed from service rather than used with unreliable transport control.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities may involve the drive system, steering-assist components, braking mechanism, control inputs, wiring, battery-power delivery, or service-level calibration/configuration.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare service documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired, adjusted, or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify controlled forward/reverse travel, turning, stopping, steering response, and any applicable safety functions before return to clinical use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A mobile X-ray unit with unpredictable powered movement should never be maneuvered around a patient until transport control has been verified.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Radiology reported the AMX Navigate powered drive would engage but the unit consistently pulled to the right during transport."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found debris restricting free movement of the right-side caster, creating unequal rolling resistance."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the external obstruction, inspected the caster and wheel condition, and verified smooth forward, reverse, turning, and stopping operation before returning the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether drive failed completely or intermittently
  • Direction of pulling
  • Battery charge state
  • Emergency-stop status
  • Wheel and caster condition
  • Debris or obstruction found
  • Manual rolling behavior
  • Drive-control condition
  • Forward and reverse test results
  • Final steering and stopping verification
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Begin with patient and staff safety, then separate mechanical resistance, control problems, power conditions, and external obstructions before assuming an internal drive-system failure. Verify predictable movement before return to service and document exactly what was found and corrected.

That is successful troubleshooting.
