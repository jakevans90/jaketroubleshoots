Manual CPR Release Will Not Lower the Deck or Rehome Correctly

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Hillrom

Model

Compella

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot manual CPR release or reset problems caused by obstructions, control interference, mechanical binding, or incomplete return to normal operation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Establish Emergency Readiness

A malfunctioning CPR release can delay emergency patient positioning.

  • Remove the bed from clinical use if the emergency release cannot be trusted.
  • Transfer the patient to another appropriate bed when clinically safe.
  • Ensure an alternate method of providing emergency care is available.
  • Do not attempt repeated CPR release operations on an occupied bed.
  • Keep personnel clear of moving deck sections and pinch points.

Expected outcome: Emergency patient care does not depend on the malfunctioning CPR mechanism.

2. Confirm the Reported CPR Release Failure

Determine whether the release fails to activate, lowers incompletely, or does not reset.

  • Ask staff what occurred during the attempted release.
  • Identify whether the deck moved at all.
  • Determine whether the release handle felt loose, stuck, or unusually resistant.
  • Record any abnormal noise.
  • Determine whether normal articulation was affected afterward.

Expected outcome: The specific release or reset failure is identified.

3. Inspect the External CPR Release Control

Examine the accessible release mechanism.

  • Check the handle or lever for visible damage.
  • Confirm that it is not obstructed by equipment.
  • Inspect accessible external linkage areas without removing covers.
  • Look for bent or loose components.
  • Do not force a jammed release.

Expected outcome: The external release control is accessible and visibly intact.

4. Check Deck Clearance and Accessories

Inspect the surrounding bed components.

  • Remove loose bedding from moving areas.
  • Check for trapped cables, hoses, or straps.
  • Confirm that nearby equipment does not restrict deck movement.
  • Verify that the mattress is positioned correctly.
  • Check that siderails and accessories do not interfere with the approved release operation.

Expected outcome: The deck has sufficient clearance for the intended emergency movement.

5. Inspect the Bed's Current Position

Determine whether the bed configuration contributes to the malfunction.

  • Record the initial head and deck positions.
  • Check for visible misalignment.
  • Confirm that no section is mechanically supported by an external object.
  • Inspect for signs of previous incomplete movement.
  • Do not attempt to manually force the deck into alignment.

Expected outcome: The deck is correctly supported and no external positioning issue is identified.

6. Test the CPR Release Under Approved Conditions

With the bed unoccupied, follow the manufacturer-approved CPR release test.

  • Position personnel clear of moving components.
  • Activate the release using the designated control.
  • Observe whether the intended deck section lowers.
  • Check for binding, hesitation, or abnormal movement.
  • Stop if the mechanism does not operate as intended.

Expected outcome: The CPR release lowers the designated deck section in the manner specified by the manufacturer.

7. Verify Reset and Normal Articulation

Follow the approved procedure for returning the bed to normal operation.

  • Confirm the release mechanism returns to its normal state.
  • Verify that the deck is correctly positioned.
  • Test the affected powered articulation functions.
  • Check for unusual movement or noise.
  • Confirm that the CPR release remains ready for subsequent operation.

Expected outcome: The release resets correctly and normal articulation is restored.

8. Complete Emergency Function Verification

After correcting an external issue:

  • Repeat the approved CPR release test.
  • Verify the intended emergency lowering function.
  • Confirm the required reset or rehome operation.
  • Test normal powered articulation.
  • Complete applicable mechanical and electrical safety checks.

Expected outcome: CPR release, reset, and normal articulation pass all required verification.

Troubleshooting can stop only when the emergency function and subsequent reset operate reliably.

If the Problem Persists

If the release control, deck clearance, accessories, positioning, and external mechanical conditions have been checked, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible service-level causes include release linkage, mechanical latching, articulation components, position sensing, or the reset mechanism.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service and labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for qualified repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using Hillrom service documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel.
  • Returned to service only after successful CPR release, reset, articulation, and safety verification.

Do not return a bed with an unreliable emergency release to clinical service.

Clinical Use Tip

CPR release functionality must be verified independently of normal powered head articulation because the two functions serve different emergency needs.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the Compella manual CPR release would not lower the head deck fully."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an accessory cable trapped beneath the moving head section, restricting its travel."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the obstruction and verified normal CPR release movement, reset, and powered head articulation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Release or reset failure
  • Initial deck position
  • Release handle condition
  • Visible obstructions
  • Accessory and cable routing
  • Mechanical noise or binding
  • CPR lowering test result
  • Reset or rehome result
  • Powered articulation verification
  • Final equipment status

Final Thought

Emergency release mechanisms require dependable operation without delay or uncertainty. Protect the patient, check external interference before assuming mechanical failure, verify both release and reset, and escalate any unresolved defect with clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
