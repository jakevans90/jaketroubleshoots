Docking Station Has No Power or Does Not Detect the Meter

Asset Type

Blood Glucose Meter

Manufacturer

Nova Biomedical

Model

StatStrip

What This Guide Helps With

Docking failures caused by AC power, loose connections, dirty contacts, poor seating, damaged docks, meter contacts, or network and communication problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Maintain Meter Availability

Move the StatStrip to another verified charging or docking location if needed so battery depletion or missed synchronization does not interrupt patient testing.

Expected outcome: Clinical glucose testing remains available while the dock is evaluated.

2. Confirm the Docking Symptom

Determine whether the station is completely unpowered, powers on but does not recognize meters, charges without communicating, or communicates intermittently.

Expected outcome: The problem is narrowed to power, detection, charging, or communication.

3. Inspect the Dock and Power Supply

Check the docking station, power supply, cord, connectors, and housing for cracks, loose connections, liquid contamination, damaged insulation, bent contacts, heat, or odor.

Expected outcome: No obvious unsafe physical defect is present.

If there is significant damage, overheating, or liquid intrusion, remove the dock from service immediately.

4. Verify AC Power

Confirm that the dock's power connection is secure.

Test the outlet using an approved method or connect the dock to a known-good appropriate power source if permitted.

Expected outcome: Reliable AC power is available to the dock.

5. Inspect Dock Contacts

Check accessible meter-contact areas for dirt, dried disinfectant, corrosion, debris, or physical damage.

Clean only using approved external procedures.

Expected outcome: Contact areas are clean, dry, and intact.

6. Reseat the Meter

Place the meter firmly into the dock according to its normal orientation and observe whether charging or communication begins.

Expected outcome: Correct seating produces normal meter detection.

7. Test Another Meter

Place a known-good StatStrip in the suspect docking station.

Expected outcome: The test identifies whether the failure follows the dock or the original meter.

8. Test the Meter in Another Dock

Place the original meter into a known-good compatible docking station.

Expected outcome: The problem is further isolated between the meter and dock.

9. Check Data Connectivity

If the dock powers and charges normally but does not communicate, inspect accessible network connections and link status.

Avoid unauthorized network or configuration changes.

Expected outcome: Required physical data connections are intact.

10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm with a known-good meter that the dock powers, detects the meter, charges when applicable, and communicates through the expected workflow.

Expected outcome: Docking operation is stable and repeatable.

If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

External AC power, seating, contacts, and accessible network causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal dock electronics, damaged data contacts, power-supply failure, network configuration, or infrastructure faults.

The docking station should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation when the problem is hardware-related.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Nova Biomedical documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.
  • Verified for power, meter detection, charging, and communication before return to service.

Proper isolation between the meter, dock, AC source, and network prevents unnecessary replacement of good equipment.

Clinical Use Tip

A failed dock may affect both charging and result transmission, so confirm both functions after repair.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the StatStrip docking station did not detect meters and provided no charging indication."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the docking-station power connection loose while the outlet and meter tested normally."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The power connection was secured and the dock successfully detected, charged, and communicated with a known-good meter before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Dock power indication
  • Outlet tested
  • Power-supply condition
  • Contact condition
  • Known-good meter results
  • Alternate dock results
  • Charging status
  • Network link status
  • Physical damage
  • Final communication result

Final Thought

Check AC power, seating, and contacts before assuming a dock electronics failure, isolate the meter from the docking station, and verify both charging and communication before returning the station to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
