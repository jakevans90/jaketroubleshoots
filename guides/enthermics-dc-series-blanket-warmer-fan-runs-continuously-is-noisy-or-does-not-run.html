Enthermics DC Series (Blanket Warmer)

Fan Runs Continuously, Is Noisy, or Does Not Run

Asset Type

Blanket Warmer

Manufacturer

Enthermics

Model

DC Series (Blanket Warmer)

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses abnormal fan behavior caused by airflow obstruction, loading, door position, contamination, mechanical interference, controls, or fan-system faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patients and Maintain Warming Availability

Remove the warmer from clinical use if the fan is not operating when required, makes grinding or severe mechanical noise, produces a burning odor, or is associated with overheating.

Move warming needs to another verified cabinet.

Expected outcome: Clinical operations no longer depend on equipment with uncertain air circulation or overheating risk.

If continuity of care is established, continue troubleshooting.

2. Confirm the Fan Symptom

Determine whether the fan:

  • Never starts
  • Starts only intermittently
  • Runs continuously
  • Runs after the cabinet appears idle
  • Makes rubbing, rattling, scraping, or grinding sounds
  • Changes behavior when the door is opened or closed

Observe the unit through a normal operating cycle without bypassing switches or controls.

Expected outcome: The exact fan behavior is confirmed.

If fan behavior appears normal and the reported condition cannot be reproduced, complete a functional check before closing the work order.

3. Check Cabinet Loading and Airflow Paths

Inspect the cabinet for blankets or accessories blocking accessible:

  • Air outlets
  • Return-air openings
  • Interior circulation spaces

Correct overpacking or material pressed against airflow openings.

Expected outcome: Air can circulate freely through the cabinet.

If correcting the load restores normal fan behavior and warming performance, troubleshooting can stop.

4. Inspect Accessible Air Openings

With power disconnected where appropriate, inspect accessible ventilation openings for:

  • Dust accumulation
  • Lint
  • Debris
  • Labels
  • Nearby objects restricting airflow

Clean external vents using an approved method without pushing debris into the cabinet.

Expected outcome: External airflow openings are clear.

If airflow obstruction was causing abnormal noise or operation and the fan returns to normal afterward, verify function and stop troubleshooting.

5. Verify Door Position and Door Operation

Confirm the door closes and latches correctly. Some abnormal fan complaints can actually result from a door that remains slightly open, a door-switch problem, or heat loss causing prolonged heating and circulation.

Expected outcome: The door is fully closed and normal operating conditions are established.

If proper door closure resolves the reported continuous-fan condition, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

6. Listen for Mechanical Interference

Operate the cabinet while listening from outside the enclosure.

Note whether noise changes with:

  • Door position
  • Cabinet vibration
  • Light pressure on an exterior panel
  • Fan startup or shutdown

Do not open powered internal compartments or reach into moving fan components.

Expected outcome: No external panel, loose accessory, or cabinet component is producing the noise.

If an accessible loose external panel or accessory is safely secured and the abnormal noise disappears, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

7. Check the Controls and Displayed Operating State

Verify the warmer is not simply in an active warming state that would reasonably require continued circulation.

Confirm:

  • Power state
  • Temperature setting
  • Any displayed status
  • Whether the unit responds to normal control changes

Do not alter hidden service settings or calibration values.

Expected outcome: Fan behavior is consistent with the commanded operating state.

If a normal control setting explains the behavior and the warmer functions correctly, no repair is required.

8. Perform Final Warming Verification

After external causes are corrected:

  • Operate the unit from a normal starting condition
  • Confirm fan operation is smooth
  • Confirm no abnormal vibration or odor
  • Confirm the cabinet warms normally
  • Confirm the fan is responsive to normal operating changes

Expected outcome: Air circulation is stable, quiet, and consistent with normal operation.

If final functional verification passes, troubleshooting can stop and the warmer can be returned to service.

If the Problem Persists

If airflow obstructions, loading, door condition, accessible contamination, and normal operating controls have been ruled out, the fan system may have an internal motor, bearing, wiring, control, temperature-control, or switching problem.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not operate a warmer with a seized, intermittently operating, or mechanically noisy circulation fan. Perform complete functional and applicable electrical safety testing after repair.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Reliable air circulation is necessary for predictable cabinet warming; do not leave a fan-deficient warmer available for clinical use simply because the display still illuminates.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported that the blanket warmer fan was unusually loud during operation."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found lint restricting an accessible exterior ventilation opening and causing abnormal airflow noise."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The obstruction was removed, the cabinet was operated through a heating check, and quiet, stable fan operation was verified."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Fan does not run, runs continuously, or runs intermittently
  • Type of noise reported
  • Whether noise occurs only during heating
  • Cabinet loading condition
  • Ventilation openings inspected
  • Door closure and latch condition
  • Control settings observed
  • Vibration or panel interference
  • Odor or overheating
  • Results after cleaning or load correction
  • Final fan and heating operation
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect patients from unreliable warming equipment, then rule out loading, airflow, door, and accessible mechanical causes before assuming a fan-system failure. Verify the complete warming function and escalate safely when abnormal fan behavior remains.

That is successful troubleshooting.
