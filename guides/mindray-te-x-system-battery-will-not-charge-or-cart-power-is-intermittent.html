Mindray TE X

System Battery Will Not Charge or Cart Power Is Intermittent

Asset Type

Ultrasound System

Manufacturer

Mindray

Model

TE X

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots charging failure or intermittent cart power caused by outlets, cords, connectors, docking, batteries, or external power-path problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient

If the TE X loses power unexpectedly during patient care, move the examination or procedure to another verified ultrasound system. Do not depend on equipment with unreliable power.

Expected outcome: Patient care continues independently of the affected power system.

2. Confirm the Power Complaint

Determine whether the system fails to charge, runs only on AC, shuts down when moved, loses AC intermittently, or has unexpectedly short battery operation.

Expected outcome: The complaint is categorized as AC-input, charging, battery-runtime, or intermittent-power failure.

3. Verify the Wall Outlet

Confirm the outlet is energized using appropriate approved test equipment when needed. Check whether the outlet is switched, damaged, or otherwise unsuitable.

Expected outcome: The outlet provides reliable facility power. If a different verified outlet resolves the condition, stop after confirming normal charging and operation.

4. Inspect the Power Cord and External Power Path

Inspect the complete accessible AC cord, plug, strain relief, power supply, cart connections, and docking interfaces for looseness, damage, heat discoloration, contamination, or intermittent seating.

Expected outcome: All external power components are intact and securely connected. Damaged power components are removed from use.

5. Observe Charging Indications

Connect the TE X to known-good AC power and observe the system's normal battery or charging indications. Allow sufficient time to determine whether the indicated state changes normally.

Expected outcome: The system recognizes AC input and indicates charging appropriately.

6. Check for Movement-Related Power Loss

With no patient dependent on the system, observe whether normal cart movement or gentle repositioning of accessible external cables causes AC or charging status to change.

Expected outcome: Power remains stable during normal movement. Repeatable interruption requires removal from service until corrected.

7. Compare Battery Behavior

If the system can operate on battery, document whether the battery is recognized and whether the problem occurs immediately after disconnecting AC or only after some runtime. Do not perform unauthorized battery disassembly.

Expected outcome: The system transitions between AC and battery operation without unexpected shutdown.

8. Check Environmental Conditions

Confirm ventilation openings are not blocked and the system is not being charged in an environment with excessive heat, liquid exposure, or another condition that could interfere with normal operation.

Expected outcome: The TE X operates and charges in an appropriate environment.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify stable AC operation, visible charging response, normal transition to battery operation, normal return to AC power, and absence of interruption during routine cart positioning.

Expected outcome: Power and charging remain stable. If the system still shuts down or loses power unexpectedly, remove it from service.

If the Problem Persists

Outlet, external cord, accessible power connections, charging status, cart movement, and basic battery behavior have been checked. Remaining causes may involve the battery, charging electronics, internal power distribution, cart power interface, or another service-level condition.

Remove the TE X from service and label it Out of Service if power or battery operation is unreliable. Send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Mindray documentation and approved electrical and functional test equipment.

Repairs and battery replacement should be performed only by qualified personnel using approved parts and procedures. Verify charging, AC/battery transitions, and required electrical and functional safety checks before return to service.

Clinical Use Tip

Unexpected power loss during bedside imaging can interrupt a procedure and documentation; exchange the system before troubleshooting intermittent power.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the TE X intermittently stopped charging when the cart was moved."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external AC cord was not fully seated at the cart power connection."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering secured the AC connection and verified continuous AC operation, normal battery charging, and stable power during routine cart movement."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • AC versus battery behavior
  • Outlet test result
  • Cord and plug condition
  • Charging indication
  • Battery recognition
  • Whether movement affected power
  • Any heat, odor, or damage
  • AC-to-battery transition result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Treat intermittent power as a reliability problem, eliminate facility and external connection causes first, verify both AC and battery behavior, and escalate before clinical reuse if power cannot be trusted.

That is successful troubleshooting.
