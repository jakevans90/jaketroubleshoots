Lid/Door Will Not Lock or Reports Open During the Cycle

Asset Type

Endoscope Reprocessor (AER)

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

AEROFLEX

What This Guide Helps With

Lid or door interlock problems caused by obstruction, loading, damaged seals, alignment, latch contamination, or failed position detection.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Personnel and Processing Safety

Do not bypass, defeat, tape, hold, or otherwise override a lid or door interlock. Stop processing and use another validated reprocessor if needed.

Expected outcome: The AER remains unable to run unless the enclosure is safely secured and recognized.

2. Confirm the Exact Condition

Determine whether the lid or door physically will not close, closes but will not lock, unlocks unexpectedly, or remains displayed as open during a cycle.

Expected outcome: Mechanical closure and electronic recognition are distinguished.

3. Inspect for Obstructions

Check the basin edge, door or lid path, latch area, and sealing surface for tubing, endoscope components, labels, packaging, debris, or other material preventing full closure.

Expected outcome: The lid or door closes freely without interference. If removing an obstruction restores normal locking, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Check Endoscope and Tubing Loading

Verify no connector, hose, scope segment, or accessory extends into the closure path.

Expected outcome: The load remains entirely within the intended processing area and does not interfere with the enclosure.

5. Inspect Seals and Accessible Latch Surfaces

Look for displaced, folded, contaminated, damaged, or swollen seals and inspect accessible latch surfaces for residue or damage.

Expected outcome: The sealing and latching areas are clean, intact, and correctly positioned. Do not operate the unit with visibly damaged safety-related closure components.

6. Operate the Lid or Door Without a Load

With the basin empty and the device otherwise ready, check whether the enclosure closes and is recognized normally.

Expected outcome: If the problem disappears without a load, loading or tubing placement was likely responsible. If it remains, continue.

7. Check for Intermittent Recognition

Without forcing the mechanism, observe whether normal gentle closure consistently changes the displayed status from open to closed.

Expected outcome: Recognition is stable. Intermittent or position-sensitive recognition suggests a service-level interlock or alignment problem.

8. Verify Normal Cycle Initiation After Correction

After correcting an external issue, perform an approved test and verify the lid or door remains properly locked and recognized throughout the required portion of the cycle.

Expected outcome: The enclosure remains secure and no false open indication occurs. Troubleshooting can stop.

9. Escalate Persistent Interlock Failure

If the enclosure is unobstructed and properly aligned externally but still will not lock or is reported open, stop troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The unit is removed from service rather than operated with an unreliable safety interlock.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving loading, obstructions, seals, and accessible latch surfaces have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve the lock mechanism, alignment, position sensing, interlock circuitry, or control software.

Remove the AEROFLEX from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment. Do not bypass safety interlocks.

Return the device to service only after reliable closure, locking, status recognition, and cycle operation have been verified.

Clinical Use Tip

A lid or door interlock is a safety function; never bypass it to complete a reprocessing cycle.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported the AEROFLEX lid would close but continued to display open and would not start the cycle."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a channel hose trapped along the basin edge and preventing full lid closure."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Repositioned the hose, verified normal lid recognition and locking, and completed an approved operational cycle test successfully."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Physically open versus electronically reported open
  • Loaded or empty basin
  • Tubing position
  • Obstruction found
  • Seal condition
  • Latch condition
  • Intermittent recognition behavior
  • Test-cycle result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Interlock troubleshooting should begin with the physical closure path and loading before internal failure is suspected. Never defeat the safety function, and escalate unreliable locking promptly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
