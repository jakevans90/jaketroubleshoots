Enthermics DC Series (Blanket Warmer)

Unit Will Not Power On or Trips the Branch Circuit

Asset Type

Blanket Warmer

Manufacturer

Enthermics

Model

DC Series (Blanket Warmer)

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses no-power conditions and branch-circuit trips caused by external power, cord, outlet, loading, moisture, or equipment electrical faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patients and Maintain Warming Availability

Remove the blanket warmer from clinical use if it will not power reliably, repeatedly trips a circuit, has visible electrical damage, or produces unusual heat, odor, or noise.

Provide staff with another verified warming cabinet if warmed blankets are required for patient care. Do not repeatedly reset a breaker or continue operating equipment suspected of having an electrical fault.

Expected outcome: Patient-care needs are transferred to reliable equipment and the affected warmer can be evaluated safely.

If continuity of care is established, continue troubleshooting.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Determine whether the unit:

  • Is completely dead
  • Powers briefly and shuts off
  • Trips the branch circuit immediately
  • Trips after heating begins
  • Operates intermittently
  • Failed after being moved, cleaned, or serviced

Ask whether other devices on the same circuit were affected.

Expected outcome: The failure pattern and operating conditions are clearly identified.

If the reported problem cannot be reproduced and the unit operates normally through an appropriate functional check, document the findings and stop troubleshooting.

3. Inspect the Power Cord and Plug

Disconnect power and inspect the accessible power cord and plug for:

  • Cuts or crushed insulation
  • Bent or damaged blades
  • Loose plug components
  • Heat discoloration
  • Evidence of liquid exposure
  • Strain near the cabinet or plug

Do not use the warmer if the cord or plug presents an electrical safety concern.

Expected outcome: The external power connection is intact with no visible damage.

If correcting an approved external cord-related issue restores reliable operation and subsequent safety and functional testing passes, troubleshooting can stop.

4. Verify the Branch-Circuit Power Source

Verify the receptacle is energized using appropriate approved test equipment or a known-good method permitted by facility policy.

Determine whether the receptacle is controlled by:

  • A wall switch
  • A power strip
  • A local disconnect
  • A tripped breaker or protective device

Do not repeatedly reset a protective device that trips again.

Expected outcome: A stable, appropriate power source is confirmed.

If moving the warmer to another approved circuit resolves the problem and the original receptacle or circuit is found defective, remove the infrastructure source from use or escalate to Facilities as appropriate. Troubleshooting of the warmer can stop after verifying normal operation.

5. Check for Circuit Loading

Determine whether other high-load equipment shares the affected branch circuit.

If the warmer operates normally on another appropriate dedicated or lightly loaded circuit, the original circuit may be overloaded or have an infrastructure problem rather than a warmer failure.

Expected outcome: Branch loading is ruled out as the cause of nuisance tripping.

If circuit loading is identified and corrected and the warmer operates normally afterward, troubleshooting can stop.

6. Inspect for Moisture or Contamination

Check the exterior, power-entry area, cabinet floor, and accessible interior for evidence of:

  • Recent cleaning fluid
  • Spilled liquids
  • Condensation
  • Water entry
  • Residue near electrical openings

Do not energize a warmer with suspected internal liquid intrusion.

Expected outcome: No moisture-related electrical hazard is present.

If liquid intrusion is suspected, remove the warmer from service and escalate for bench evaluation rather than continuing powered troubleshooting.

7. Test Basic Power-Up Operation

With the unit connected to a verified electrical source, operate the normal power control and observe:

  • Display or indicator response
  • Fan operation
  • Heating initiation
  • Unexpected shutdown
  • Abnormal sound or odor
  • Branch-circuit behavior

Do not repeatedly cycle power if the breaker trips.

Expected outcome: The warmer powers normally and remains energized without tripping the circuit.

If stable operation is restored and the failure does not recur during functional verification, troubleshooting can stop.

8. Perform Final Functional and Electrical Safety Verification

Before returning the unit to service:

  • Confirm normal startup
  • Confirm the display and controls respond
  • Confirm warming begins normally
  • Verify no abnormal odor, sound, or overheating is present
  • Perform electrical safety testing when required by facility policy or after electrical repair

Expected outcome: The warmer operates reliably and meets applicable return-to-service requirements.

If all verification passes, return the unit to service and document the results.

If the Problem Persists

If the Enthermics DC Series blanket warmer continues to remain dead, shut down unexpectedly, or trip a verified branch circuit after external power and circuit causes have been ruled out, an internal electrical, heating, control, wiring, or protection-system fault may be present.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not repeatedly reset breakers or perform unauthorized internal electrical repairs. Complete appropriate electrical safety and functional testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

If the warmer loses power during clinical operations, provide another verified source of warmed blankets rather than relying on intermittent operation.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the blanket warmer would not power on and had tripped the wall circuit."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the unit connected to an overloaded branch circuit and verified normal operation on an appropriate alternate circuit."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The warmer was connected to an appropriate power source, operated through a complete heating check without interruption, and was returned to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether the unit was completely dead or intermittently powered
  • Whether the breaker tripped immediately or during heating
  • Outlet or circuit tested
  • Other equipment sharing the circuit
  • Power cord and plug condition
  • Evidence of moisture or cleaning fluid
  • Indicator or display behavior
  • Unusual heat, odor, or sound
  • Results on an alternate verified receptacle
  • Electrical safety test results when performed
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Start with patient safety, verify the external power path before assuming internal failure, and stop when repeated circuit trips indicate a potentially unsafe electrical condition. Escalate appropriately and document the complaint, verified cause, corrective action, and return-to-service testing clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
