Analyzer Remains in Warm-Up After Sensor-Cassette Replacement

Asset Type

Blood Gas Analyzer

Manufacturer

Radiometer

Model

ABL90 FLEX PLUS

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots prolonged warm-up after sensor-cassette replacement involving installation, recognition, temperature, consumables, power, configuration, or environmental conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patient Testing and Provide an Alternate Analyzer

An analyzer that has not completed warm-up is not ready for patient testing. Redirect required specimens to another verified analyzer or approved laboratory method.

Do not attempt to bypass warm-up or force the analyzer into clinical operation.

Expected outcome: Patient testing continues safely while the affected analyzer remains unavailable for clinical use.

2. Confirm the Warm-Up Condition

Review the analyzer display and determine whether it is actually remaining in warm-up or whether another preparation, calibration, consumable, or readiness condition is preventing transition to normal operation.

Record the exact displayed status or message.

Expected outcome: The analyzer is confirmed to be specifically delayed in warm-up following sensor-cassette replacement. If another external readiness condition is identified and corrected, troubleshooting can stop after successful startup verification.

3. Confirm the Sensor-Cassette Replacement Was Completed Correctly

Visually verify that the replacement sensor cassette is the appropriate approved item, that packaging and protective materials have been removed as required, and that the cassette is fully seated in its intended position.

Do not force the cassette or manipulate internal contacts.

Expected outcome: The cassette is correctly installed and physically seated. If correcting installation allows the analyzer to complete warm-up, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Check Analyzer Recognition of the Cassette

Review the normal user-visible status to confirm the analyzer recognizes the installed cassette and does not indicate an installation, identification, or consumable-related problem.

If the analyzer does not recognize the cassette, inspect accessible cassette interfaces for contamination, damage, or incomplete seating.

Expected outcome: The analyzer recognizes the replacement cassette normally. If recognition is restored by correcting seating or an external interface issue, allow the normal startup process to complete and stop if the analyzer becomes ready.

5. Verify Other Required Consumables

Confirm associated consumables required for normal analyzer startup are installed, available, and recognized.

A warm-up complaint may actually be prolonged because another consumable or fluidic requirement has not been satisfied.

Inspect for obvious leaks, empty consumables, improper installation, or disconnected external waste components when applicable.

Expected outcome: All required consumables are available and recognized. If correcting another consumable condition allows startup to proceed, troubleshooting can stop after full readiness verification.

6. Verify Stable Power

Confirm the analyzer is connected to its intended power source and has remained powered without repeated interruptions or resets.

Inspect the power cord and connection for looseness or visible damage. If power stability is questionable, evaluate the approved outlet according to facility procedures.

Do not repeatedly power-cycle the analyzer as a substitute for troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The analyzer has stable AC power and remains continuously powered through the warm-up process. If restoring stable power allows startup to complete normally, troubleshooting can stop.

7. Evaluate Environmental Conditions

Confirm the analyzer is located in its normal operating environment and has not recently been moved from an unusually cold, hot, or otherwise unsuitable area.

Verify that ventilation openings are unobstructed and that the analyzer is not exposed to direct heating, cooling airflow, or other obvious environmental extremes.

Do not invent or apply temperature limits unless using approved manufacturer documentation.

Expected outcome: The analyzer is in an appropriate stable environment with unobstructed ventilation. If environmental correction allows warm-up to complete normally, troubleshooting can stop.

8. Review the Timing of the Problem

Determine whether the analyzer entered prolonged warm-up immediately after the cassette replacement and whether the previous cassette had operated normally.

Verify whether any other maintenance, cleaning, relocation, software activity, or power event occurred at the same time.

Expected outcome: The problem is isolated to the cassette replacement event or another specific recent change. If an associated external change is corrected and normal startup resumes, troubleshooting can stop.

9. Use an Approved Known-Good Replacement When Appropriate

If facility procedure and manufacturer guidance allow, replace the suspect cassette with another approved cassette when there is reasonable evidence the installed cassette may be defective or improperly recognized.

Avoid repeatedly installing multiple consumables without documenting the reason.

Expected outcome: The analyzer completes normal warm-up with the known-good approved cassette. If it does, document the suspected consumable failure and troubleshooting can stop after required verification.

10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Once the analyzer completes warm-up, confirm it reaches its normal ready state. Perform all applicable quality-control or verification procedures required after sensor-cassette replacement.

Do not return the analyzer to clinical use merely because the warm-up message disappears.

Expected outcome: The analyzer becomes ready and successfully completes required post-replacement verification. When this result is achieved, troubleshooting can stop and the analyzer may be returned to service.

If the Problem Persists

If cassette installation, recognition, required consumables, stable power, ventilation, environmental conditions, and an approved known-good replacement have been ruled out, the prolonged warm-up may involve internal temperature regulation, sensor-cassette interface circuitry, internal fluidics, control electronics, or another service-level condition.

The analyzer should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Radiometer service documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not bypass the warm-up state, alter restricted calibration parameters, open sealed analytical assemblies, or perform board-level repair.

After service, confirm normal startup and complete all required quality-control, analytical, and facility return-to-service verification before patient testing.

Recognizing that a device unable to complete its normal preparation state is not ready for use is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A blood-gas analyzer that remains in warm-up should be treated as unavailable until it reaches ready status and completes required analytical verification.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Laboratory staff reported that the ABL90 FLEX PLUS remained in warm-up after a sensor-cassette replacement and would not become ready for testing."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the replacement sensor cassette was not fully seated and was intermittently recognized by the analyzer."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reinstalled the cassette correctly, confirmed normal recognition and completion of warm-up, completed required quality-control verification, and returned the analyzer to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact displayed warm-up or readiness message
  • Time problem began relative to cassette replacement
  • Cassette recognition status
  • Cassette seating and condition
  • Other consumable status
  • AC power condition
  • Recent power interruption or relocation
  • Ventilation and environmental observations
  • Results with another approved cassette
  • Quality-control results after startup
  • Final analyzer status

Final Thought

A prolonged warm-up should be handled by confirming installation, consumables, power, environment, and analyzer recognition before assuming an internal problem. Never bypass analyzer readiness controls; verify analytical performance after correction and escalate when normal startup cannot be achieved.

That is successful troubleshooting.
