Head-of-Bed Angle Alarm or 30-Degree Indicator Is Incorrect

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Hillrom

Progressa

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot incorrect head-of-bed angle indication or alarm behavior caused by bed position, controls, configuration, sensing, or external operating conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Do Not Rely on an Unverified Angle Indication

If the head-of-bed angle display, 30-degree indicator, or related alarm is suspected to be inaccurate, do not rely on it for a clinical positioning requirement until accuracy is verified.

Use another verified method or another bed when patient care depends on maintaining a specific head elevation.

Expected outcome: Clinical care does not depend on an unverified angle indication. If accurate positioning cannot be assured, remove the bed from service.

2. Confirm the Exact Indication or Alarm Problem

Determine whether the issue involves:

  • The displayed head angle.
  • The 30-degree indicator.
  • An audible or visual head-angle alarm.
  • Alarm activation at an apparently incorrect position.
  • Failure to indicate when the head section crosses the expected threshold.
  • Intermittent indication during movement.

Record the displayed behavior while operating the head section normally.

Expected outcome: The specific indication or alarm failure is reproducible. If repeated testing shows normal behavior, document the result and stop after functional verification.

3. Place the Bed on a Stable, Level Surface

Verify the bed is on a firm, level floor and is not positioned across a floor transition, threshold, ramp, or uneven surface that could alter the relationship between the bed frame and gravity.

Apply the brakes.

Expected outcome: The bed is stable on a level surface. If relocating the bed to a level surface corrects the indication, repeat the verification and stop troubleshooting.

4. Return the Bed to a Neutral Starting Position

Using normal controls, place the bed frame and head section into a neutral or flat position appropriate for functional checking.

Verify that other bed sections are not creating an unusual frame attitude that could affect interpretation of the displayed head angle.

Expected outcome: The bed begins testing from a stable and repeatable position. If the indication behaves normally after repositioning, complete repeated checks before return to service.

5. Compare Displayed Head Angle With an Independent Reference

Using an approved external angle-measurement method, compare the physical head-section angle with the value or threshold indicated by the bed.

Take measurements only in a safe unloaded or appropriately controlled test condition.

Do not alter calibration values or enter restricted service menus.

Expected outcome: The bed's angle indication changes logically with the physical head-section position. A persistent discrepancy confirms an indication or sensing problem rather than a staff-perception issue.

6. Verify the 30-Degree Indicator or Alarm Through Controlled Movement

Move the head section slowly through the area where the indicator or alarm should change state.

Observe whether:

  • The indication changes consistently.
  • The alarm activates and clears logically.
  • The displayed angle changes smoothly.
  • The behavior is repeatable in both directions.

Expected outcome: Indicator and alarm behavior are consistent with actual head-section movement. If the function behaves correctly on repeated tests, troubleshooting can stop.

7. Review Normal User-Accessible Alarm and Configuration Settings

Verify that any user-accessible head-angle alarm setting or related control has not been disabled, changed, or left in an unexpected state.

Do not access unauthorized service configuration or change calibration parameters.

Expected outcome: Normal settings match the intended clinical operation. If correcting an authorized setting restores proper alarm behavior, document the change and complete functional verification.

8. Inspect Accessible Controls, Cables, and Frame Areas

With the bed removed from clinical use, inspect accessible wiring and connections around the head section and control interfaces for:

  • Loose connectors.
  • Pinched or stretched cables.
  • Fluid contamination.
  • Impact damage.
  • Wiring that changes tension as the head section moves.

Do not disassemble internal sensing or control assemblies.

Expected outcome: Accessible connections and wiring are secure and undamaged. If correcting an approved external connection restores accurate indication, repeat angle checks before return to service.

9. Check for Intermittency During Repeated Head Movement

Cycle the head section several times while observing the angle display and alarm status.

Look for jumping values, delayed changes, loss of indication, or different alarm points between cycles.

Expected outcome: Angle indication and alarm behavior remain stable and repeatable. Intermittent or inconsistent behavior requires escalation even if the feature occasionally appears normal.

10. Perform Final Functional Verification

After any correction, verify:

  • Head section raises and lowers smoothly.
  • Displayed angle changes logically.
  • The 30-degree indication behaves consistently.
  • Related alarm behavior activates and clears as expected.
  • No abnormal alerts occur.
  • Other major bed functions remain normal.

Expected outcome: The head-of-bed indication and related alarm operate consistently and correspond with independently verified bed position. If so, troubleshooting is complete after required return-to-service testing.

If the Problem Persists

If the displayed angle, 30-degree indicator, or head-angle alarm remains incorrect after level positioning, setting checks, independent comparison, and external inspection, remaining causes may involve angle sensing, calibration, wiring, configuration, or internal control electronics.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Hillrom service documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Calibrated, configured, or repaired only by qualified personnel.

Before returning the bed to service, independently verify head-angle indication and alarm behavior throughout the clinically relevant movement range, along with related bed safety functions.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not use an unverified head-angle display or 30-degree indicator to satisfy a clinical positioning requirement until its accuracy has been confirmed.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the Progressa 30-degree head-of-bed indicator activated even though the head section appeared below the indicated angle."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the bed positioned partially across an uneven floor transition, affecting the relationship between the frame and the physical head-section angle."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Moved the bed to a level surface and verified the head-angle display and 30-degree indication against an independent angle reference through repeated movements."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Displayed angle reported by staff.
  • Independently measured angle.
  • Alarm or indicator behavior.
  • Whether the problem is repeatable.
  • Floor condition and bed location.
  • Bed frame and head-section starting position.
  • User-accessible settings observed.
  • Accessible wiring condition.
  • Controls tested.
  • Results through repeated movement.
  • Final verification method.
  • Final bed status.

Final Thought

Treat incorrect angle information as a clinical positioning risk, verify the bed's physical setup before assuming a sensor failure, independently confirm the indication, and escalate any persistent discrepancy with clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
