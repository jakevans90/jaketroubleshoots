CO2 Absorber Canister Will Not Lock, Bypass, or Seal Correctly

Asset Type

Anesthesia Machine

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Atlan A350

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot CO2 absorber canister seating, locking, sealing, bypass operation, physical obstruction, incorrect assembly, and external breathing-system leak concerns.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ventilation

Do not troubleshoot an absorber mounting, bypass, or sealing problem while a patient depends on the affected breathing system.

If the problem occurs during a procedure:

  • Notify anesthesia staff immediately.
  • Maintain ventilation and CO2 control using an appropriate alternate method.
  • Exchange the anesthesia machine if absorber integrity cannot be assured.
  • Do not force the canister into position or bypass a safety mechanism to continue use.

Expected outcome: The patient remains safely ventilated using a verified breathing system.

Once continuity of care is secured, troubleshoot the Atlan A350 off-patient.

2. Confirm the Exact Mechanical or Sealing Problem

Determine precisely what staff observed.

Identify whether the canister:

  • Will not insert fully.
  • Will not lock.
  • Will not release normally.
  • Appears seated but leaks.
  • Will not transition correctly through its normal bypass arrangement.
  • Produces a breathing-system leak after installation.
  • Becomes difficult to operate only with a particular canister.

Do not assume the machine-side mechanism has failed until the absorber assembly itself has been evaluated.

Expected outcome: The fault is narrowed to insertion, locking, bypass operation, sealing, or leakage.

If normal operation is demonstrated repeatedly and no leak is present, document the findings and stop troubleshooting.

3. Inspect the Absorber Canister

Remove the canister in a safe condition and inspect it externally.

Look for:

  • Cracks.
  • Warping.
  • Damaged locking features.
  • Damaged sealing surfaces.
  • Foreign material.
  • Improperly installed components.
  • Excess absorbent interfering with closure or seating.
  • An incompatible canister or accessory.

Do not modify locking features or sealing surfaces.

Expected outcome: The canister is physically intact, correctly assembled, and compatible with the Atlan A350.

If a damaged or improperly assembled canister is identified, correct or replace it and proceed to verification.

4. Inspect the Machine-Side Mounting Area

With the canister removed, inspect the accessible mounting interface.

Check for:

  • Loose absorbent granules.
  • Debris.
  • Damaged visible seals.
  • Residue on sealing surfaces.
  • Objects preventing full insertion.
  • Obvious external mechanical damage.

Clean only accessible surfaces using approved practices. Do not lubricate or alter seals unless specifically directed by manufacturer documentation.

Expected outcome: The mounting interface is clean and unobstructed with no obvious external damage.

If removing debris restores normal canister installation and sealing, proceed to final verification and stop troubleshooting.

5. Reinstall the Canister Without Excessive Force

Reinstall the absorber carefully using its normal alignment and engagement.

Observe whether:

  • It aligns naturally.
  • The locking mechanism engages.
  • The assembly sits flush.
  • The normal bypass or engagement action occurs.
  • Unusual resistance is present.

Excessive force can damage the absorber assembly or breathing-system interface.

Expected outcome: The absorber installs, locks, and operates smoothly using normal force.

If correct alignment resolves the problem, complete a leak and functional test before returning the machine to service.

6. Compare With a Known-Good Absorber Canister

If available, install another compatible known-good absorber assembly.

This helps determine whether the problem follows the canister or remains with the machine.

Expected outcome: A known-good absorber locks and seals normally.

If only the original absorber produces the fault, replace or remove that absorber from use and stop troubleshooting after verification.

If multiple known-good canisters produce the same fault, continue evaluating the machine-side interface.

7. Check Seals and Accessible Interfaces

Inspect visible external sealing areas for:

  • Cuts.
  • Distortion.
  • Contamination.
  • Improper seating.
  • Missing externally serviceable components where readily apparent.

Do not disassemble internal valves or breathing-system mechanisms beyond authorized Clinical Engineering procedures.

Expected outcome: Accessible sealing interfaces are clean, intact, and correctly positioned.

If an approved external seal replacement or correction restores normal operation, proceed to leak testing and verification.

8. Perform the Appropriate Breathing-System Leak Test

With the absorber correctly installed, perform the approved system leak or pre-use test appropriate for the Atlan A350.

If the test fails:

  • Recheck absorber seating.
  • Verify breathing-circuit connections.
  • Inspect accessible circuit components.
  • Separate absorber-related leakage from unrelated breathing-system leakage.

Do not repeatedly adjust internal components to force a passing result.

Expected outcome: The breathing system completes its appropriate leak check without evidence of absorber-related leakage.

If the leak test passes consistently and mechanical operation is normal, the issue is resolved.

9. Verify Normal Absorber and Bypass Operation

Exercise the absorber through its normal user-accessible operating positions with the machine off-patient.

Confirm:

  • Secure installation.
  • Normal locking and release.
  • Correct physical engagement.
  • No abnormal looseness.
  • No repeated leak indication.
  • No binding or unexpected mechanical resistance.

Expected outcome: Absorber operation is smooth, repeatable, and does not compromise breathing-system integrity.

If these conditions are met, troubleshooting can stop.

10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Before return to service:

  • Complete the required anesthesia-machine checkout.
  • Verify breathing-system integrity.
  • Verify ventilation.
  • Verify relevant alarms.
  • Confirm absorber mounting and operation.
  • Confirm no abnormal leak is present.
  • Document final test results.

Expected outcome: The Atlan A350 passes required functional checks with the absorber correctly installed.

Any unresolved sealing, locking, bypass, or leak problem requires removal from service.

If the Problem Persists

If known-good absorber canisters have been tested and debris, assembly, alignment, visible seal condition, and external breathing-system connections have been ruled out, the problem may involve the absorber mounting mechanism, bypass mechanism, internal sealing components, breathing-system assembly, or other service-level mechanical components.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Do not attempt unauthorized internal breathing-system disassembly or mechanical modification. After repair, complete appropriate leak, ventilation, alarm, and anesthesia-machine return-to-service testing.

Stopping when a critical breathing-system seal cannot be verified is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never force an absorber canister into place; a canister that appears installed but does not seal correctly can compromise the breathing system.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Anesthesia staff reported that the Atlan A350 CO2 absorber canister would not fully lock and the machine failed its breathing-system leak check."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found absorbent granules lodged on the accessible canister mounting surface, preventing the absorber from seating completely."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the debris, reinstalled the absorber, verified normal locking and release, and completed the breathing-system leak and functional tests successfully."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether the canister would insert, lock, or release.
  • Whether a leak test failed.
  • Visible damage to the absorber.
  • Condition of accessible seals.
  • Debris or absorbent found at the interface.
  • Results with a known-good absorber.
  • Whether the fault followed the original canister.
  • Abnormal mechanical resistance.
  • Breathing-system test results.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Treat any absorber sealing problem as a breathing-system safety concern. Rule out incorrect seating, damaged consumables, contamination, and external leaks before assuming an internal mechanical failure, then verify the complete breathing system before clinical use.

That is successful troubleshooting.
