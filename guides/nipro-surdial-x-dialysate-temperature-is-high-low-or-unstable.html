Dialysate Temperature Is High, Low, or Unstable

Asset Type

Hemodialysis (HD) Machine

Manufacturer

Nipro

Model

SURDIAL X

What This Guide Helps With

Use this guide when dialysate temperature is abnormal or unstable due to supply, environment, setup, sensing, heating, or flow-related conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient

Do not continue treatment using a machine that cannot maintain appropriate dialysate temperature.

Transfer the patient to another verified dialysis machine or follow the facility's clinical contingency procedure before troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The patient is no longer dependent on equipment with questionable dialysate temperature control.

2. Confirm the Temperature Problem

Determine whether the reported condition is:

  • Temperature too high
  • Temperature too low
  • Slow warm-up
  • Repeated fluctuation
  • A displayed temperature that disagrees with an independent measurement
  • A temperature-related alarm during preparation or treatment

Record the exact displayed information.

Expected outcome: The actual symptom and point of occurrence are clearly identified.

3. Verify Water Supply and Environmental Conditions

Confirm normal facility water supply and check for environmental conditions that could affect machine operation.

Look for:

  • Unusually hot or cold incoming water
  • Interrupted or restricted water supply
  • Recent water-system maintenance
  • Extreme room temperature
  • Blocked ventilation around the machine

Compare with nearby dialysis machines when appropriate.

Expected outcome: The machine has a normal external water source and suitable environment.

4. Inspect External Fluid Connections

Check accessible water and fluid connections for kinks, restrictions, leaks, or incomplete seating.

Restricted fluid flow can affect temperature regulation and should be corrected before suspecting internal heating components.

Expected outcome: External fluid supply and drainage connections are unobstructed and properly connected.

5. Verify Treatment Temperature Configuration

Confirm the configured dialysate temperature corresponds to the intended treatment prescription.

Do not change a prescribed treatment setting simply to compensate for a suspected machine fault.

Have clinical staff verify the prescription if there is any uncertainty.

Expected outcome: The programmed treatment setting is correct and not the source of the reported condition.

6. Allow Normal Preparation to Complete

After external conditions are verified, run the machine through the approved preparation process and allow sufficient time for normal stabilization.

Observe whether the temperature reaches and maintains the configured condition without repeated alarms.

Expected outcome: Temperature reaches a stable ready condition. If it remains stable, continue to final verification.

7. Compare With Approved Independent Test Equipment

When required by facility or manufacturer procedure, verify dialysate temperature using approved calibrated test equipment.

Use the comparison to determine whether the problem is true dialysate temperature instability or an apparent measurement discrepancy.

Expected outcome: Independent verification agrees with acceptable machine performance. If so, troubleshooting can stop after completion of all required checks.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm:

  • Temperature remains stable
  • No related alarms recur
  • Conductivity and other required dialysate checks are satisfactory
  • Fluid flow and preparation remain normal
  • Applicable return-to-service testing passes

Expected outcome: The machine maintains appropriate dialysate conditions through the verification period and may be returned to service.

If the Problem Persists

If incoming water, environment, external fluid connections, configuration, and independent verification have been checked and temperature remains abnormal, common external causes have been ruled out.

Possible remaining categories include internal heating, temperature sensing, flow-control, calibration, or control-system faults. Do not perform unauthorized heater adjustments or internal calibration.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Nipro documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Required thermal and dialysate performance checks must pass before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

An alarm that clears after warm-up does not by itself prove the machine is safe; verify that dialysate temperature remains stable before clinical reuse.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Dialysis staff reported the SURDIAL X dialysate temperature remained low and would not stabilize during preparation."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external water inlet hose partially kinked behind the machine."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering corrected the hose routing, repeated preparation, verified stable dialysate temperature and required checks, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact temperature alarm or message
  • High, low, or unstable condition
  • Incoming water condition
  • Room temperature or ventilation concern
  • External hose condition
  • Programmed treatment temperature
  • Independent test result
  • Conductivity status
  • Results before and after correction
  • Final machine status

Final Thought

Temperature faults can affect treatment safety, so protect the patient first and verify water supply, external flow, configuration, and actual temperature before assuming internal failure. Escalate unresolved faults and document verified results before return to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
