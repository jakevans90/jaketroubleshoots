Midmark M9 UltraClave Series

Clean-Water Reservoir Level or Fill Sensor Is Incorrect

Asset Type

Sterilizer

Manufacturer

Midmark

Model

M9 UltraClave Series

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot incorrect reservoir-level indication, unexpected fill behavior, or apparent water-detection problems caused by water level, contamination, positioning, or external conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patients and Sterile Processing Workflow

Do not rely on the sterilizer for patient-care instruments if water-level behavior is abnormal or cycle performance cannot be verified. Remove any questionable load from clinical use and follow facility procedures for reprocessing.

If another verified sterilizer is available, transfer required processing to that unit while troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: Clinical workflow continues without relying on equipment whose sterilization performance may be compromised.

2. Confirm the Exact Reservoir-Level Complaint

Ask staff what was observed:

  • Reservoir indicates low water when visibly filled.
  • Unit requests water unexpectedly.
  • Reservoir appears empty but no indication is provided.
  • Fill behavior changes between cycles.
  • Problem occurs continuously or only after several cycles.

Inspect the display and reservoir condition before making changes.

Expected outcome: The reported symptom is reproduced or clearly documented. If normal operation is confirmed and the complaint cannot be reproduced after verification, troubleshooting can stop.

3. Verify Correct Water Quantity and Condition

Inspect the clean-water reservoir and verify that it contains an appropriate amount of water for normal operation according to facility and manufacturer requirements.

Check for:

  • Reservoir being nearly empty or overfilled.
  • Water contaminated with debris or residue.
  • Incorrect water type.
  • Visible buildup that could interfere with sensing or water movement.

Do not add chemicals or cleaners unless specifically approved for the equipment.

Expected outcome: The reservoir contains clean, appropriate water at a normal operating level. If correcting the water level restores normal indication and operation, troubleshooting can stop.

4. Inspect the Reservoir Area for Contamination

With the sterilizer cool and safely removed from operation, visually inspect accessible reservoir surfaces and the area associated with level detection.

Look for:

  • Mineral deposits.
  • Film or residue.
  • Floating debris.
  • Corrosion.
  • Physical damage.
  • Moisture or contamination around accessible connections.

Clean only areas permitted by approved maintenance instructions.

Expected outcome: Accessible sensing and reservoir areas are clean and unobstructed. If cleaning restores stable level indication, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

5. Verify Sterilizer Position and Installation

Confirm that the sterilizer is sitting securely on a stable, level surface and has not recently been moved or tilted.

Improper leveling can affect water distribution and may contribute to misleading reservoir or fill behavior.

Also verify that the unit has adequate clearance and is not exposed to unusual vibration.

Expected outcome: The sterilizer is properly positioned and stable. If correcting its position restores normal reservoir behavior, troubleshooting can stop.

6. Inspect Accessible Water Path Components

Inspect externally accessible tubing, drains, caps, fittings, and reservoir-related components for:

  • Kinks.
  • Loose connections.
  • Leakage.
  • Blockage.
  • Improper seating.
  • Signs that water is being lost from the reservoir unexpectedly.

Do not disassemble internal valves, plumbing assemblies, or sensing circuits as part of basic external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: No accessible leak, blockage, or connection problem is present. If an external issue is corrected and level sensing becomes normal, troubleshooting can stop.

7. Power-Cycle and Recheck the Indication

When the sterilizer is not processing a load, perform a normal shutdown and restart using approved operating controls.

Observe the reservoir indication before beginning another test cycle.

Do not use unauthorized service modes or configuration changes.

Expected outcome: The reservoir indication initializes normally and agrees with the actual water condition. If it does, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

8. Perform a Controlled Functional Check

Using approved test procedures and without relying on the device for patient-care instruments, observe a normal cycle initiation and water-handling behavior.

Confirm that:

  • The sterilizer recognizes an adequate water supply.
  • Water is drawn or managed normally.
  • No leakage is observed.
  • No abnormal reservoir indication develops.

Terminate testing and remove the device from service if water handling becomes abnormal.

Expected outcome: Water-level indication and fill behavior remain consistent throughout the controlled check. If verified, the sterilizer may proceed to appropriate return-to-service testing.

9. Escalate Persistent Sensor or Fill Problems

If the reservoir contains the correct water, accessible areas are clean, installation is correct, and no external leak or obstruction is found, stop external troubleshooting.

Possible remaining categories include:

  • Level-sensing component failure.
  • Wiring or connection problem.
  • Internal plumbing or valve problem.
  • Control-system interpretation fault.
  • Internal contamination requiring service-level access.

Expected outcome: The sterilizer is removed from service rather than being used with unreliable water-level control.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remove the sterilizer from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation.

Further evaluation should use appropriate Midmark documentation and approved test equipment. Internal sensing, wiring, plumbing, control, or calibration work should be performed only by qualified personnel.

After repair, verify reservoir detection, water handling, complete cycle operation, alarms, and required sterilization performance before returning the sterilizer to clinical service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate an unreliable sterilizer is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not process patient-care instruments in a sterilizer whose water supply or reservoir-level control cannot be reliably verified.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported that the Midmark M9 showed an incorrect clean-water level even though the reservoir had been filled."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found residue in the accessible reservoir area that was interfering with consistent water-level detection."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the accessible reservoir area per approved maintenance practices, refilled with appropriate water, and verified stable level indication and normal cycle operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact reservoir or fill indication observed.
  • Actual water level at time of complaint.
  • Water type used.
  • Visible contamination or residue.
  • Evidence of leakage.
  • Sterilizer position or recent relocation.
  • Accessible tubing or connection condition.
  • Behavior before and after cleaning or refill.
  • Results of controlled cycle verification.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Protect patient safety first, verify water level and external conditions before assuming sensor failure, escalate unresolved sensing problems appropriately, and document the complaint, cause, correction, and final verification clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
