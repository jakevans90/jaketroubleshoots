GE Healthcare Giraffe OmniBed Carestation

Fan Failure, Weak Airflow, or Excessive Fan Noise

Asset Type

Infant Incubator

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

Giraffe OmniBed Carestation

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots poor circulation or abnormal fan noise caused by airflow obstruction, contamination, enclosure setup, foreign material, or fan-system failure.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Infant From Unreliable Air Circulation

The incubator fan is part of maintaining the controlled thermal environment. Do not troubleshoot weak or absent circulation while an infant depends on the OmniBed.

Transfer the infant to another verified thermal-support device before extended testing.

Expected outcome: The infant remains safely supported while airflow performance is evaluated.

2. Confirm the Exact Fan Complaint

Determine whether staff observed:

  • No apparent airflow
  • Weak airflow
  • Intermittent fan operation
  • Grinding, rubbing, rattling, or high-pitched noise
  • Noise only at certain positions
  • Temperature instability associated with fan behavior

Operate the device without a patient and listen for the reported condition.

Expected outcome: The airflow or noise condition is reproduced or clearly described.

3. Check Accessible Airflow Paths

Inspect air intake and circulation openings for:

  • Linens
  • Disposable materials
  • Labels
  • Dust
  • Packaging
  • Other equipment blocking vents

Ensure nothing is placed where it can restrict circulation.

Expected outcome: All accessible airflow openings are unobstructed. If removing an obstruction restores normal airflow, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Inspect Accessible Filters and Screens

Check accessible filter or vent-screen areas for visible loading, contamination, incorrect installation, or damage according to facility maintenance practices.

Clean or replace serviceable external items only according to approved instructions.

Expected outcome: Air can pass freely through the intended intake path.

5. Verify Enclosure Setup

Confirm the hood, doors, mattress platform, and related components are positioned correctly for incubator operation.

Unexpected airflow can sometimes be perceived when doors or panels are not correctly closed.

Expected outcome: The enclosure is correctly configured and airflow behavior can be evaluated under stable conditions.

6. Locate the Source of Abnormal Noise Externally

Listen from different sides of the device without removing covers. Determine whether the noise follows:

  • Fan operation
  • Mattress movement
  • Canopy movement
  • A loose external panel
  • An accessory resting against the device

Remove or secure nonessential external objects that may vibrate.

Expected outcome: External vibration sources are ruled out or corrected.

7. Observe Thermal Performance

Allow the unit to operate under the applicable controlled test conditions and observe whether air temperature stabilizes normally.

Persistent poor thermal performance combined with abnormal fan sound or low airflow supports removal from service.

Expected outcome: Normal circulation supports stable thermal operation.

8. Perform Approved Airflow or Fan Verification

If the problem remains, use the appropriate GE Healthcare service procedure and approved test equipment to evaluate fan or circulation performance.

Do not reach into the fan path, bypass safety protections, or operate exposed moving parts.

Expected outcome: Fan and airflow performance meet applicable requirements.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correcting an external airflow restriction or other approved issue, verify fan sound, circulation, temperature control, and associated alarms before return to service.

Expected outcome: Airflow is stable, fan noise is normal, and thermal control passes required testing. Troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

If vents, filters, enclosure position, external vibration, and operating conditions have been ruled out, the problem may involve the fan motor, bearings, fan mounting, internal airflow path, control electronics, wiring, or another service-level component.

The OmniBed should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using current GE Healthcare service documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel

Complete airflow, temperature-control, alarm, and functional testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Weak circulation can affect the entire incubator thermal environment even when the temperature display initially appears normal.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"NICU staff reported weak incubator airflow and unusually loud fan noise."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the accessible air-intake screen heavily obstructed with debris."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Corrected the airflow obstruction according to approved maintenance practice, verified normal fan sound and stable thermal operation, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Fan running or stopped
  • Type of abnormal noise
  • Airflow strength observed
  • Vent obstruction
  • Filter or screen condition
  • Enclosure configuration
  • Temperature stability
  • External vibration source
  • Approved functional-test result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Begin fan complaints with airflow restrictions and external vibration sources before assuming internal motor failure. Because circulation is fundamental to incubator performance, remove the unit from service whenever stable airflow and temperature control cannot be verified.

That is successful troubleshooting.
