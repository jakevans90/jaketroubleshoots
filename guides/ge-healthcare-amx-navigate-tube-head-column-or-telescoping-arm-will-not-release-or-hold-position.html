GE Healthcare AMX Navigate

Tube Head, Column, or Telescoping Arm Will Not Release or Hold Position

Asset Type

Mobile X-Ray System

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

AMX Navigate

What This Guide Helps With

The tube assembly, column, or telescoping arm is difficult to position, will not release, drifts, or will not remain where placed.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient From Uncontrolled Movement

Do not position a tube head or arm over a patient if it cannot reliably hold position. Support the assembly manually only as required to move it safely away from the patient.

Expected outcome: No patient is exposed to an uncontrolled or drifting tube assembly.

2. Confirm Which Motion Is Affected

Determine whether the problem involves:

  • Vertical movement
  • Horizontal extension
  • Tube rotation
  • Column rotation
  • Release mechanism
  • Locking/holding
  • Drift after positioning
  • Mechanical binding

Expected outcome: The affected motion is clearly identified.

3. Inspect for External Obstruction

Check for:

  • Cables caught around moving structures
  • Accessories interfering with travel
  • Objects stored against the column or arm
  • Covers displaced into the motion path
  • Equipment positioned too close to walls, beds, or other devices

Expected outcome: The assembly has an unobstructed range of movement.

4. Inspect Accessible Mechanical Surfaces

Look for visible:

  • Bent components
  • Loose covers
  • Impact damage
  • Cracked handles
  • Damaged release controls
  • Foreign material in exposed tracks or sliding surfaces

Do not lubricate or adjust counterbalance systems unless specifically authorized.

Expected outcome: No obvious external mechanical damage is present.

5. Check Release Controls

Operate the normal positioning release controls and determine whether the affected axis releases consistently.

Expected outcome: The commanded motion releases and relocks predictably.

6. Compare All Available Motions

Test each tube-head, arm, and column motion separately in a controlled area.

Expected outcome: The problem is isolated to one motion or confirmed to affect multiple positioning functions.

7. Check Cable Routing

Observe accessible cables while moving the assembly slowly through a safe range. Look for stretching, snagging, pinching, or unusual resistance.

Expected outcome: Cables move freely without restricting positioning.

8. Check Whether the Assembly Holds Position

Position the tube assembly away from a patient and observe whether it remains stationary without drifting.

Expected outcome: The tube head and arm remain in the selected position. If they do, troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

9. Repeat Positioning Through the Normal Working Range

Perform several release, move, and hold cycles using normal controls.

Expected outcome: Movement is smooth, release is consistent, and the assembly holds its position every time.

10. Escalate Unsafe Positioning

If the tube head, arm, or column binds, drifts, drops, fails to release, or cannot reliably hold position, remove the system from service.

Expected outcome: An unstable mechanical assembly is not positioned over a patient.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities may involve the locking mechanism, counterbalance system, brake or release mechanism, internal cables, sensors, structural components, or service-level adjustments.

The system should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or controlled mechanical evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate GE Healthcare documentation and approved tools
  • Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel

Return-to-service testing should verify smooth motion, full normal positioning capability, reliable release, and stable holding without drift.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never position a tube assembly over a patient when there is any doubt that the arm or column will hold securely.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Radiology reported the AMX Navigate tube head was difficult to extend and would not move smoothly into position."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an external cable routed into the telescoping-arm travel path and creating mechanical resistance."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Corrected the cable routing and verified smooth extension, retraction, release, and stable positioning throughout the normal working range."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Motion or axis affected
  • Drift versus binding
  • Release-control behavior
  • Visible impact damage
  • Cable routing
  • External obstruction
  • Range of motion tested
  • Ability to hold position
  • Repeatability after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Mechanical positioning faults can become direct patient-safety hazards. Eliminate external obstructions and cable interference first, then verify release and holding performance before assuming an internal mechanical problem.

That is successful troubleshooting.
