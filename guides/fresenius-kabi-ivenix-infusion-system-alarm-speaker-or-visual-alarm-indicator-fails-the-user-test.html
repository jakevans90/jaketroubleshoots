Fresenius Kabi Ivenix Infusion System

Alarm Speaker or Visual Alarm Indicator Fails the User Test

Asset Type

Infusion Pump

Manufacturer

Fresenius Kabi

Model

Ivenix Infusion System

What This Guide Helps With

Missing, weak, distorted, intermittent, or abnormal audible or visual alarm indication during user testing or functional verification.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Remove the Pump From Patient Use

A pump with an unreliable alarm speaker or visual alarm indicator must not remain in clinical use while being evaluated. Transfer any active infusion to another verified device.

Expected outcome: No patient depends on a pump with questionable alarm annunciation.

2. Confirm the Exact Alarm Failure

Determine whether the issue involves no sound, weak sound, distorted sound, intermittent sound, missing visual indication, partial visual indication, or failure of both.

Expected outcome: The failed alarm modality is clearly characterized.

3. Repeat the Normal User Alarm Test

Outside patient use, repeat the standard user-accessible alarm test exactly as intended for normal operation. Do not use hidden service functions.

Expected outcome: The audible and visual alarm indicators operate normally. If the original report cannot be reproduced, continue additional verification before release.

4. Inspect for External Obstruction

Check whether tape, labels, protective covers, mounting hardware, dirt, residue, or other objects obstruct the speaker openings or visible alarm indicator.

Expected outcome: Alarm outputs are unobstructed. If removing an obstruction restores normal operation, verify repeatedly and stop.

5. Inspect for Fluid or Physical Damage

Look for evidence of impact, cracked housing, fluid intrusion, contamination, or damage near the speaker and alarm-light areas.

Expected outcome: The enclosure and alarm-output areas are intact. Evidence of damage or liquid intrusion requires removal from service.

6. Check Normal Audio Settings

Verify that user-accessible alarm-volume or audio settings are not set unexpectedly and that the configuration is appropriate for the facility. Do not alter protected alarm configuration without authorization.

Expected outcome: User-accessible settings are appropriate and the alarm remains clearly audible.

7. Power Cycle the Pump Outside Patient Use

Perform a normal shutdown and restart.

Expected outcome: Startup completes normally and the alarm test passes. If the failure returns intermittently, the pump remains unsafe for use.

8. Generate a Controlled Alarm Condition

Using an approved bench or non-patient test setup, create a safe condition that produces a normal alarm without defeating protective features.

Expected outcome: The audible alarm, visual indicator, on-screen message, and alarm clearing behavior all function appropriately.

9. Compare With a Known-Good Pump

If alarm output seems weak or abnormal but not completely absent, compare it with another verified Ivenix pump in the same environment.

Expected outcome: A device-specific difference is identified or ruled out.

10. Check for Intermittency

Repeat the alarm test several times and observe the pump while repositioning it normally on its stand or mounting system without striking or stressing it.

Expected outcome: Alarm output remains reliable and does not cut in or out.

11. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm the alarm speaker, visual indicator, display annunciation, and normal alarm behavior all operate reliably.

Expected outcome: All alarm outputs pass verification. Any intermittent or failed alarm modality requires removal from service.

If the Problem Persists

External obstruction, contamination, settings, temporary software behavior, and obvious mechanical damage have been evaluated. Persistent failure may involve the alarm speaker, visual indicator, associated wiring, control electronics, or other service-level hardware.

The device should remain removed from service and labeled Out of Service. Send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Fresenius Kabi documentation and approved test equipment. Alarm-system repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Return to service only after full alarm verification and any required post-repair performance and electrical safety testing are successfully completed.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A pump that delivers fluid correctly but cannot reliably alert staff is still unsafe for patient use.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the Ivenix pump displayed alarms on screen but no audible alarm was heard during the user test."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an adhesive equipment label covering the external speaker opening."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the obstructing label, repeated the user alarm test and controlled alarm verification successfully, and returned the pump to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Audible or visual alarm affected
  • User-test result
  • Alarm volume setting
  • Speaker or indicator obstruction
  • Physical or liquid damage
  • Restart result
  • Controlled alarm result
  • Known-good pump comparison
  • Intermittent behavior
  • Final alarm verification
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Alarm reliability is a patient-safety function. Eliminate simple external causes first, but never return a pump with uncertain audible or visual alarm performance to clinical use.

That is successful troubleshooting.
