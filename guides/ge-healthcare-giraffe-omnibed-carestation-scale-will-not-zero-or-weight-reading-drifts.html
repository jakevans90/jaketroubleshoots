GE Healthcare Giraffe OmniBed Carestation

Scale Will Not Zero or Weight Reading Drifts

Asset Type

Infant Incubator

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

Giraffe OmniBed Carestation

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots scale zeroing failure or weight drift caused by bed loading, positioning, cable problems, accessories, movement, or scale-system faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Infant and Verify Weight by Another Method if Needed

Do not rely on a questionable OmniBed scale for clinical decisions involving medication, feeding, or fluid management.

If an accurate weight is immediately required, use another verified weighing method according to clinical practice.

Expected outcome: Clinical care does not depend on an unverified scale reading.

2. Confirm the Exact Scale Complaint

Determine whether:

  • The scale will not zero
  • Weight changes while the load is stationary
  • The displayed value is obviously incorrect
  • The problem occurs only when the bed is tilted or rotated
  • The scale intermittently disappears
  • The weight differs from another verified scale

Reproduce the condition without a patient if possible.

Expected outcome: The type of weighing problem is clearly identified.

3. Remove Unintended Loads From the Mattress Platform

Check for items contacting or loading the weighing surface, including:

  • Tubing pulling against the bed
  • Cables draped tightly across the mattress
  • Equipment touching the frame
  • Linens trapped between moving surfaces
  • Objects underneath the mattress platform
  • Accessories resting partly on and partly off the scale

Expected outcome: The scale platform is mechanically free from unintended external forces. If zeroing becomes normal, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Verify Bed and Mattress Position

Place the bed and mattress in the position required by the applicable scale procedure. Make sure the mattress and scale assembly are correctly seated and nothing is binding against surrounding structures.

Expected outcome: The weighing platform is correctly positioned and mechanically free.

5. Inspect the Scale Cable and Accessible Connector

Inspect the accessible scale cable and connector for:

  • Pinching
  • Cuts
  • Loose connection
  • Damaged strain relief
  • Contamination
  • Damage associated with bed rotation or movement

Reseat an accessible connector only if permitted and with the device safely out of patient use.

Expected outcome: The scale connection is intact and stable.

6. Repeat the Zero Procedure Under Stable Conditions

With the bed unloaded as required, allow movement to stop and perform the normal zero function.

Avoid touching the bed, leaning on the frame, or moving connected accessories during zeroing.

Expected outcome: The scale establishes and maintains a stable zero. If successful, proceed to weight verification.

7. Test With a Known Reference Weight

Using an approved calibrated test weight and the manufacturer's applicable procedure, verify scale performance at the appropriate test condition.

Do not use an arbitrary object of assumed weight.

Expected outcome: The displayed weight is stable and meets the applicable manufacturer requirements.

8. Check for Position-Dependent Intermittency

If the complaint occurs during bed movement, repeat the approved test in the relevant allowed bed positions while observing the scale cable and display.

Do not continue using a scale that becomes intermittent when the mattress platform is moved.

Expected outcome: Weight remains stable through normal permitted positioning.

9. Calibrate Only if Indicated and Authorized

If the scale fails approved verification after external causes have been ruled out, perform calibration only according to current GE Healthcare service documentation and using approved test weights.

Do not use calibration to conceal mechanical binding or intermittent connections.

Expected outcome: The scale passes the required post-calibration verification.

If the Problem Persists

If loading, bed position, cable condition, zeroing technique, and reference-weight testing have been addressed, the problem may involve the in-bed scale assembly, load-sensing components, communication path, wiring, calibration, or processor electronics.

The OmniBed should be:

  • Removed from scale-dependent clinical use
  • Labeled Out of Service if the scale function cannot be safely isolated
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using current GE Healthcare documentation and calibrated test weights
  • Repaired or calibrated only by qualified personnel

Complete required scale and overall functional testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Tubing or monitoring cables pulling on the mattress can change a neonatal weight enough to matter clinically; eliminate external forces before evaluating accuracy.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"NICU staff reported that the OmniBed scale would not hold a stable zero."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found monitor cables routed tightly across the mattress platform and applying force to the weighing surface."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Corrected cable routing, successfully zeroed the scale, verified accuracy with an approved test weight, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Zeroing behavior
  • Amount or pattern of drift
  • Bed position
  • Mattress and scale seating
  • Tubing or cables contacting the platform
  • Scale cable condition
  • Connector condition
  • Known test-weight result
  • Position-dependent behavior
  • Calibration performed

Final Thought

A neonatal scale is sensitive to small external forces, so eliminate loading, binding, cable tension, and positioning problems before assuming an internal failure. Verify performance with approved test weights and document the result clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
