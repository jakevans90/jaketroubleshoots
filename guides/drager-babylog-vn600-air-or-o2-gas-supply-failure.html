Drager Babylog VN600

Air or O2 Gas Supply Failure

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Babylog VN600

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses loss or instability of air or oxygen supply caused by wall outlets, hoses, fittings, connections, or upstream gas infrastructure.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ventilation

A gas supply failure can immediately affect ventilation and oxygen delivery. Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator or appropriate backup source if the ventilator cannot reliably provide required support.

Do not troubleshoot a questionable gas supply while a patient depends on the ventilator.

Expected outcome: Patient ventilation and oxygenation are maintained safely. Once alternate support is established, troubleshooting may continue.

2. Identify Which Gas Supply Is Affected

Confirm whether the reported problem involves:

  • Air
  • Oxygen
  • Both gas supplies
  • Intermittent gas loss
  • A supply alarm with apparently normal wall gas

Observe the ventilator's external connections and available indications without assuming the problem is internal.

Expected outcome: The affected gas source and failure pattern are clearly identified.

3. Verify the Wall Outlet or External Gas Source

Confirm the gas hose is connected to the intended wall outlet or approved external source.

Check whether other equipment supplied from the same infrastructure is experiencing similar problems when appropriate.

If permitted, test using another verified gas outlet.

Expected outcome: A functional gas source is confirmed. If changing to a verified outlet resolves the problem, the ventilator may not be the cause and troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

4. Inspect the Gas Hoses

Inspect air and oxygen hoses from end to end for:

  • Kinks
  • Crushing
  • Cuts
  • Loose fittings
  • Damaged connectors
  • Incorrect connections
  • Excessive strain

Reseat each connection securely.

Expected outcome: Gas hoses are intact, unrestricted, and fully connected. If correcting a hose condition restores normal operation, troubleshooting can stop.

5. Substitute a Known-Good Hose

If available, substitute a known-good approved gas hose for the affected supply.

Change only one component at a time.

Expected outcome: If normal gas supply is restored with the known-good hose, remove the defective hose from use and stop troubleshooting after ventilator verification.

6. Compare With Another Gas Outlet

Where facility policy allows, connect the affected gas hose to another verified outlet of the correct gas type.

This helps distinguish the ventilator and hose from an upstream infrastructure issue.

Expected outcome: If the ventilator operates normally from another outlet, escalate the original outlet or pipeline issue to the appropriate facility department rather than repairing the ventilator.

7. Inspect the Ventilator Gas Inlet Externally

Inspect the accessible inlet and fitting for visible contamination, damage, looseness, or obstruction.

Do not insert tools into gas pathways or disassemble internal pneumatic components.

Expected outcome: The inlet appears intact and accepts the connector normally. Any visible inlet damage requires removal from service.

8. Verify Operation With Controlled Test Equipment

After restoring the gas source, perform appropriate system checks and controlled ventilation testing.

Verify oxygen delivery, pressure generation, alarms, and other required functions using approved equipment.

Expected outcome: The ventilator operates normally with stable required gas supplies. If all verification passes, troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

If the gas source, wall outlet, external hoses, fittings, and connections are verified but the Babylog VN600 continues to report or demonstrate inadequate air or oxygen supply, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve an internal pneumatic pathway, inlet assembly, pressure sensing system, gas-mixing system, valve, or electronic control issue.

The ventilator should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Drager documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

If testing indicates a facility gas infrastructure problem, notify the appropriate facilities or medical-gas support team and prevent use of the affected outlet until cleared.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

When a gas supply alarm occurs, protect ventilation first and verify both the ventilator and the upstream medical-gas source.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported that the Babylog VN600 repeatedly indicated loss of oxygen supply."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the oxygen hose connector was loose at the wall outlet."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The oxygen hose was securely reconnected, gas supply indications normalized, and system and ventilation checks passed before return to service."
