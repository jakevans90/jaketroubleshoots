Canon Aplio i900 CV

Accessory or Module Is Not Recognized

Asset Type

Echocardiography System

Manufacturer

Canon

Model

Aplio i900 CV

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting an attached probe, accessory, peripheral, or module that is missing, unavailable, intermittently detected, or not accepted by the system.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Imaging Capability

If the unrecognized accessory is required for the current examination, stop using that accessory and move to another compatible verified accessory or another echocardiography system. Do not repeatedly connect and disconnect patient-applied equipment during active patient dependence. Expected outcome: Patient care continues using verified equipment.

2. Confirm the Exact Recognition Problem

Identify which probe, accessory, module, or peripheral is not recognized and whether the issue occurs consistently. Record any on-screen message exactly. Determine whether other accessories of the same general type are recognized normally. Expected outcome: The failure is narrowed to one accessory, one connection position, or the system generally.

3. Verify Compatibility and Intended Connection

Confirm that the accessory is intended for use with the Aplio i900 CV and is connected to the correct accessible interface according to approved documentation. Do not force a connector that does not seat normally. Expected outcome: The correct accessory and connection point are being used.

4. Inspect the Accessory and Connector Externally

Disconnect the accessory when safe and inspect the accessible connector, cable, strain relief, latch, contacts, and housing for contamination, bent or damaged areas, fluid intrusion, or physical damage. Inspect the system-side connection without inserting tools or altering contacts. Expected outcome: The accessory and connection are clean, dry, intact, and capable of seating securely.

5. Reconnect the Accessory Correctly

Reconnect the accessory fully using its normal locking or seating mechanism. Do not apply excessive force. Observe whether the system recognizes it after proper connection. Expected outcome: The accessory is recognized and becomes available. If recognition is stable, proceed to functional verification.

6. Try Another Approved Connection Position When Applicable

If the system provides another compatible external connection position and normal workflow permits it, test the accessory there. This helps distinguish an accessory problem from a single connection-path problem. Expected outcome: Recognition at another compatible connection point helps isolate the issue to the accessory or original connection location.

7. Use a Known-Good Compatible Accessory

When available, connect a known-good compatible accessory of the same appropriate type. Do not substitute equipment that has not been approved for the system. Expected outcome: If the known-good accessory is recognized, the original accessory is suspect. If neither is recognized, a system-side problem becomes more likely.

8. Restart the System if Appropriate

If external inspection and substitution do not resolve the problem and there is no sign of connector damage, perform a normal system restart. Reconnect or recheck the accessory according to normal workflow. Expected outcome: The system completes startup and recognizes the accessory normally.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify that the recognized accessory operates correctly during an appropriate functional test. For imaging probes or clinically significant modules, verify expected image acquisition or functional behavior before clinical use. Expected outcome: Recognition remains stable and the accessory functions normally. Troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Persistent Recognition Failures

If known-good compatible accessories are not recognized, a connection point is physically damaged, or recognition remains intermittent, remove the affected accessory or system from service as appropriate. Do not perform internal connector or board-level repair. Expected outcome: The unresolved problem is contained and referred for qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common compatibility, connection, contamination, seating, accessory, and restart-related causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve the accessory itself, an internal connection interface, system configuration, software, communication circuitry, or another service-level condition.

Remove the affected equipment from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use Canon documentation and approved test equipment. Repair or configuration changes should be completed only by qualified personnel.

After repair, verify accessory recognition and functional operation before return to service. Knowing when substitution has isolated the problem and when to escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not use an intermittently recognized echocardiography probe or accessory simply because reconnecting it temporarily restores operation.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that a probe connected to the Aplio i900 CV was not consistently recognized by the system."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the probe connector was not fully seated and locked into the system connection."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering reconnected the probe correctly, verified stable recognition and image acquisition, and returned the system to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Specific accessory or probe affected
  • Exact recognition message
  • Connection position used
  • Connector and cable condition
  • Whether another compatible connection was tested
  • Known-good accessory substitution result
  • Recognition before and after restart
  • Functional verification result
  • Final accessory and system status

Final Thought

Protect the examination workflow, confirm compatibility and connection before assuming internal failure, isolate the accessory from the system with safe substitutions, verify normal function after correction, escalate persistent problems, and document the findings clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
