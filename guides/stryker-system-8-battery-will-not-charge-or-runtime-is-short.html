Stryker System 8

Battery Will Not Charge or Runtime Is Short

Asset Type

Surgical Power System

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

System 8

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses System 8 batteries that do not charge normally, provide inadequate operating time, or appear to discharge unexpectedly.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Surgical Continuity

Do not continue relying on a battery with questionable capacity during a procedure. Provide a verified charged battery or alternate surgical power system before evaluating the suspect battery.

Expected outcome: Clinical use continues without dependence on an unreliable power source.

2. Confirm the Battery Complaint

Determine whether the reported problem is:

  • Failure to begin charging
  • Failure to reach the charger's normal ready state
  • Short operating time
  • Rapid discharge
  • Intermittent power
  • Excessive battery heating
  • A problem affecting one battery or several

Record which charger position and handpiece were involved.

Expected outcome: The battery problem is clearly defined and can be compared across known-good equipment.

3. Inspect the Battery Externally

Inspect the battery for:

  • Cracks
  • Swelling or deformation
  • Damaged housing
  • Contaminated contacts
  • Corrosion
  • Evidence of fluid exposure
  • Burn marks or unusual odor
  • Excessive heat

Do not charge or use a visibly damaged, swollen, leaking, or overheated battery.

Expected outcome: The battery is physically suitable for testing. Unsafe batteries are immediately removed from service.

4. Verify the Charger Has Power

Confirm that the applicable Stryker charging equipment is properly connected to AC power and displays its normal power indication.

Verify the receptacle with an appropriate tester or another known-good device when power availability is uncertain.

Inspect the charger power cord and accessible connectors for damage.

Expected outcome: The charging system has a verified power source and no obvious external power defect.

5. Inspect and Clean Accessible Charging Contacts

Examine battery and charger contacts for debris, oxidation, contamination, or physical damage.

Use only approved external cleaning methods. Do not scrape contacts aggressively or open the battery or charger.

Reinstall the battery fully into the charging position.

Expected outcome: Clean, intact contacts establish reliable engagement between battery and charger.

6. Compare Charger Positions or Known-Good Batteries

If the charging system has multiple compatible positions, determine whether the condition remains with the battery or follows a specific charging location.

Test a known-good compatible battery in the same charger position, and test the suspect battery in another verified position when appropriate.

Expected outcome: The problem is isolated to the battery, charger position, or broader charging system.

7. Compare Runtime With a Known-Good Battery

Install the suspect battery in a verified System 8 handpiece and evaluate operation under appropriate controlled conditions. Compare its behavior with a known-good battery rather than estimating battery condition from a single brief activation.

Do not perform unnecessary prolonged operation that could damage equipment or accessories.

Expected outcome: A battery that consistently performs substantially worse than a known-good battery is isolated from the handpiece as the likely problem source.

8. Check for Environmental or Workflow Factors

Determine whether the battery was:

  • Stored for an extended period
  • Used repeatedly without adequate charging opportunity
  • Exposed to inappropriate temperatures
  • Placed on a charger that was not powered
  • Rotated improperly with other batteries

Follow facility and manufacturer requirements for battery storage and charging.

Expected outcome: A workflow or environmental cause is corrected, or the battery remains unreliable despite proper handling.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After replacing or correcting the affected component, confirm:

  • Normal charger indication
  • Reliable battery seating
  • Successful operation in the handpiece
  • Stable power during repeated controlled activations
  • No abnormal heat or intermittent connection

Expected outcome: Battery and handpiece operation are reliable. Troubleshooting can stop once required return-to-service checks are complete.

10. Escalate Persistent Charging or Capacity Problems

If multiple known-good batteries fail in the same charger position, or a suspect battery repeatedly fails after external conditions are verified, remove the affected component from service.

Do not open battery packs or perform unauthorized internal battery repairs.

Expected outcome: The battery or charging equipment is routed for appropriate service-level evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Power availability, charger connections, external contacts, battery seating, known-good substitutions, and environmental causes have been evaluated. Persistent problems may involve battery degradation, charger circuitry, internal battery protection functions, or another service-level condition.

The affected battery or charger should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair, replacement, or bench evaluation as appropriate
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel

Any serviced charging equipment should complete applicable functional and electrical safety testing before return to use. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not place a marginal battery back into the surgical rotation simply because it powers the handpiece briefly; verify dependable operation before reuse.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"OR staff reported that one System 8 battery showed very short runtime after being removed from the charger."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found that the battery repeatedly provided short operation while known-good batteries charged and operated normally in the same equipment."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the degraded battery from service, replaced it with a verified battery, and confirmed normal charging and handpiece operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Battery identification
  • Charger identification and position tested
  • Charger power status
  • Contact condition
  • Charging indication
  • Known-good battery comparison
  • Known-good charger-position comparison
  • Reported runtime behavior
  • Battery temperature or physical abnormalities
  • Storage or charging history
  • Final device status

Final Thought

A battery complaint should be separated logically from charger, contact, workflow, and handpiece problems before replacement decisions are made. Keep questionable batteries out of clinical use, verify the correction, and document the comparison testing clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
