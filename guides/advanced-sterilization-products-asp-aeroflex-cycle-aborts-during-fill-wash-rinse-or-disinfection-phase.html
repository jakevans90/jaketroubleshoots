Cycle Aborts During Fill, Wash, Rinse, or Disinfection Phase

Asset Type

Endoscope Reprocessor (AER)

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

AEROFLEX

What This Guide Helps With

Mid-cycle aborts caused by water supply, drainage, chemistry, tubing, loading, lid status, external utilities, or persistent internal process-control problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patient Safety and Quarantine the Scope

Any endoscope from an aborted cycle must be treated as incompletely processed. Do not return it to clinical use. Follow the facility's validated restart or reprocessing procedure.

Expected outcome: An incompletely disinfected scope is not released.

2. Identify the Phase and Exact Message

Determine whether the abort occurs during fill, wash, rinse, or disinfection. Record the displayed alarm or message and whether the abort occurs at the same point every time.

Expected outcome: The failure is associated with a specific process phase instead of being treated as a generic cycle problem.

3. Verify Basic Utilities

Confirm AC power remained available, facility water is on, accessible water valves are in their normal position, and any required external air or drain services are available.

Expected outcome: Required external utilities remain stable. Correct an external interruption before repeating the test.

4. Inspect Water Supply and Drain Connections

Check accessible hoses for kinks, loose fittings, leaks, compression, or blocked routing. Verify the drain path is not visibly obstructed or backed up.

Expected outcome: Water can enter and drain without external restriction. If correcting a hose or drain problem restores operation, troubleshooting can stop after a successful cycle.

5. Verify Chemistry Recognition and Availability

Confirm AERO-OPA chemistry is properly installed and recognized and that no chemistry-related warning is present.

Expected outcome: Chemistry is available to the system without bypassing required identification. Correct an external chemistry issue and repeat the approved test.

6. Inspect Basin Loading and Channel Tubing

Check for pinched hoses, blocked connectors, improperly loaded scopes, or tubing interfering with the lid, drains, or normal fluid movement.

Expected outcome: The load and connections allow normal circulation and drainage.

7. Confirm Lid/Door Status Is Stable

Verify the lid or door closes, locks, and remains recognized as closed. An intermittent interlock can cause a cycle interruption.

Expected outcome: The enclosure remains securely recognized throughout operation.

8. Repeat an Approved Test Under Observation

After correcting external issues, run a controlled cycle or service-approved operational check and observe the point where the previous abort occurred.

Expected outcome: The cycle progresses normally through the previously affected phase and completes successfully.

9. Assess Reproducibility

If the cycle continues to abort at the same phase despite normal external utilities, chemistry, loading, and drainage, do not repeatedly run clinical scopes through the failing unit.

Expected outcome: A repeatable internal or service-level fault is recognized promptly.

10. Escalate the Repeated Abort

Stop external troubleshooting and remove the AEROFLEX from service.

Expected outcome: The device is sent for qualified evaluation before further clinical processing.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving AC power, water, drain routing, chemistry recognition, loading, tubing, and lid status have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal pumps, valves, sensors, heaters, plumbing, control software, or other process-monitoring systems.

Remove the AEROFLEX from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment. Repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Return the system to service only after a complete required processing cycle and associated verification checks pass.

Clinical Use Tip

Every aborted cycle should be treated as incomplete processing unless the approved reprocessing procedure explicitly establishes otherwise.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported the AEROFLEX repeatedly aborted during the rinse phase."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external drain hose compressed behind the unit and restricting drainage."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Corrected the drain-hose routing, completed an approved test cycle without abort, and verified normal fill, rinse, and drain operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact abort message
  • Phase where abort occurred
  • Whether failure repeats at same point
  • Water availability
  • Drain condition
  • Chemistry recognition
  • Lid status
  • Scope and tubing loading
  • Test-cycle result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

A cycle abort should be traced by process phase. Verify the utilities and external flow path first, quarantine incompletely processed scopes, and escalate repeatable failures rather than forcing repeated cycles.

That is successful troubleshooting.
