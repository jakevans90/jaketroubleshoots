CO2 Module Will Not Zero or Reports a Sampling-Line Occlusion

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot CO2 zeroing failures or sampling occlusions caused by blocked lines, water traps, accessories, or environmental conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety and Monitoring Continuity

  • Determine whether the patient requires continuous capnography monitoring.
  • Arrange another verified CO2 monitoring method before removing the affected system.
  • Do not interrupt airway monitoring or disconnect a sampling line without clinical coordination.
  • Perform technical testing with the monitor disconnected from patient use.

Expected outcome: Required ventilation and CO2 monitoring remain available during troubleshooting.

2. Confirm the Reported CO2 Failure

  • Identify whether the module fails zeroing, reports an occlusion, or displays an unreliable waveform.
  • Record the exact displayed message.
  • Determine whether the failure occurs during startup or after prolonged sampling.
  • Identify the installed CO2 module and sampling configuration.

Expected outcome: The failure type and associated module are identified.

3. Inspect the Sampling Line

  • Examine the sampling line for kinks, compression, contamination, or fluid accumulation.
  • Check the connection at the module.
  • Verify that the sampling line is approved for the installed CO2 system.
  • Replace a visibly obstructed or damaged line.

Expected outcome: The sampling line is open and properly connected. If the occlusion clears and functional verification passes, troubleshooting can stop.

4. Inspect the Water Trap

  • If the installed module uses a water trap, verify that the correct type is installed.
  • Inspect for excessive moisture, contamination, or improper seating.
  • Replace the water trap according to approved procedures when indicated.
  • Do not bypass the water trap or attempt to defeat moisture protection.

Expected outcome: The water trap is correctly installed and does not obstruct sampling.

5. Inspect Sampling Accessories

  • Check compatible airway adapters, sampling connectors, and external tubing.
  • Verify that connections are secure.
  • Inspect for damaged fittings or contamination.
  • Substitute approved known-good accessories.

Expected outcome: The external sampling path is unobstructed and properly assembled.

6. Verify Zeroing Conditions

  • Review the manufacturer-approved zeroing requirements for the installed CO2 module.
  • Confirm that the required environmental and sampling conditions are present.
  • Ensure that any specified warm-up period has completed.
  • Perform only the authorized zeroing procedure.
  • Do not introduce calibration gas or alter calibration settings unless required by the approved procedure.

Expected outcome: The module completes zeroing under the specified conditions.

7. Check Environmental Conditions

  • Inspect for excessive moisture, condensation, or contamination around the sampling system.
  • Verify that module ventilation openings are unobstructed.
  • Determine whether the problem follows a particular clinical environment or sampling setup.
  • Avoid exposing the module to liquids or unapproved cleaning agents.

Expected outcome: No external environmental condition interferes with sampling or zeroing.

8. Compare With Known-Good Accessories or Module

  • Repeat the approved test using a new compatible sampling line and water trap.
  • If supported and authorized, compare with a known-good compatible CO2 module.
  • Record whether the fault follows the accessories, module, or monitor.
  • Do not interchange unsupported module types.

Expected outcome: The comparison helps isolate the failure to the external sampling path or a service-level component.

9. Perform Final CO2 Verification

  • Use an approved CO2 test method and appropriate test equipment.
  • Verify sample acquisition, waveform response, and displayed CO2 values as applicable.
  • Confirm that zeroing completes.
  • Test applicable CO2 alarms and sampling occlusion detection according to approved procedures.

Expected outcome: The CO2 system passes required functional and alarm checks. If successful, document the correction and stop troubleshooting.

If the Problem Persists

Sampling lines, water traps, external connectors, zeroing conditions, and environmental causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include internal sampling pump performance, pneumatic sensing, CO2 measurement electronics, contamination, or module communication faults.

Remove the B40 and affected CO2 module from service, label the equipment Out of Service, and arrange repair or bench evaluation. Qualified personnel must use manufacturer documentation and approved CO2 testing equipment. Only qualified personnel should perform internal cleaning, repair, or calibration.

Complete applicable zeroing, CO2 performance, sampling, and alarm verification before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting prevents unnecessary damage and protects patient safety.

Clinical Use Tip

A normal-looking CO2 waveform does not eliminate the need to verify sampling integrity and applicable alarms after an occlusion or zeroing failure.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported repeated CO2 sampling-line occlusion messages on the B40 monitor."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found moisture accumulation obstructing the installed sampling line."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the sampling line with an approved accessory and verified normal CO2 sampling, waveform response, and applicable alarm operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact CO2 message
  • Installed CO2 module
  • Sampling-line type and condition
  • Water-trap condition
  • Evidence of moisture or contamination
  • Zeroing conditions and result
  • Known-good accessory substitutions
  • CO2 test equipment used
  • Waveform and measurement results
  • Alarm verification

Final Thought

CO2 troubleshooting begins with uninterrupted patient monitoring and careful evaluation of the external sampling path. Check tubing, water traps, accessories, and zeroing conditions before suspecting internal faults. Verify performance, escalate persistent problems, and document the findings clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
