What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots unresponsive controls, missing alarm audio, display interaction problems, or startup failure caused by power, contamination, damage, or device malfunction.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Remove Unreliable Equipment

Do not leave a patient dependent on an infusion pump with unreliable controls, missing alarm audio, or incomplete startup.

Transfer therapy to another verified pump before troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: Patient care is maintained using reliable equipment. A pump with absent alarms or unusable controls is removed from clinical use.

2. Confirm the Exact Failure

Determine whether the problem involves:

  • No startup
  • Frozen startup sequence
  • Touchscreen unresponsive
  • Keypad unresponsive
  • Only one control affected
  • No alarm sound
  • Weak or intermittent speaker
  • Display present but controls do not respond

Expected outcome: The affected function is clearly identified.

3. Check Power and Battery State

Connect the pump to a known-good approved power source and verify that external power is recognized.

Inspect the power supply and connector for damage.

Expected outcome: The pump has reliable input power. If restoring external power resolves startup, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

4. Perform a Normal Power Cycle

If the pump is not supporting a patient and no hazardous condition is present, shut it down and restart it using normal user-accessible controls.

Do not repeatedly power-cycle a device showing signs of overheating, fluid intrusion, or electrical damage.

Expected outcome: The pump completes a normal startup sequence. If the issue does not recur and all tests pass, troubleshooting can stop.

5. Inspect the External Controls and Display

Check the touchscreen, keypad, and surrounding surfaces for:

  • Dried medication
  • Sticky residue
  • Cracks
  • Impact damage
  • Moisture
  • Foreign material
  • Protective coverings interfering with operation

Clean only according to approved procedures.

Expected outcome: External control surfaces are clean, dry, and unobstructed. If cleaning restores normal response, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

6. Check for Isolated Versus General Control Failure

Determine whether all controls are unresponsive or whether only one key or touchscreen region fails.

Do not repeatedly press or strike the display.

Expected outcome: Control response is consistent across the accessible interface. A localized persistent dead area or failed key indicates the need for service.

7. Verify Speaker and Alarm Audio

Using an approved non-patient test condition, verify that audible alarms can be heard clearly.

Confirm the issue is not simply related to an expected volume setting or environmental noise, without altering protected configuration improperly.

Expected outcome: Required audible alarm output is present and clearly perceptible. If alarm audio is absent or intermittent, remove the pump from service.

8. Inspect for Environmental or Physical Damage

Check for evidence of:

  • Fluid exposure
  • Recent drop or impact
  • Excessive heat
  • Cracked housing
  • Loose external components
  • Unusual odor

Do not continue energizing a device with suspected fluid intrusion or electrical damage.

Expected outcome: No unsafe physical condition is present. If damage or fluid intrusion is found, stop troubleshooting and remove the pump from service.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

If the pump resumes normal operation, verify:

  • Complete startup
  • Display function
  • Touchscreen or keypad response
  • Alarm speaker
  • Programming controls
  • Infusion start and stop
  • Power operation

Use approved test methods and equipment.

Expected outcome: All user-interface and alarm functions operate reliably. If they do, troubleshooting can stop and the pump may be returned to service according to policy.

10. Escalate Persistent Interface or Startup Failure

If controls remain unresponsive, startup is incomplete, or alarm audio is unreliable despite verified power and clean external surfaces, remove the pump from service.

Do not disassemble the display, keypad, speaker, or internal electronics or use unauthorized service functions.

Expected outcome: The pump is routed for qualified service evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving power, contamination, coverings, environmental conditions, and obvious physical damage have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve the display assembly, control interface, speaker system, startup software, power-management system, or internal electronics.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, complete startup, control, alarm-audio, and infusion-performance verification before returning the pump to patient care.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A pump with unreliable alarm audio or control input should never be returned to use simply because it still delivers fluid.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported the Sapphire touchscreen intermittently failed to respond during programming."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found dried medication residue along the touchscreen edge interfering with consistent touch input."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the external control surface using the approved method and verified repeated touchscreen response, startup, alarm audio, and pump operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact startup or control symptom
  • AC or battery operation
  • Display behavior
  • Specific key or screen region affected
  • Alarm speaker behavior
  • External contamination
  • Evidence of drop or fluid exposure
  • Power-cycle result
  • Functional verification result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

User-interface and alarm failures directly affect safe operation. Verify power, cleanliness, and external condition first, but remove the pump from service whenever controls, startup, or audible alarms remain unreliable.

That is successful troubleshooting.
