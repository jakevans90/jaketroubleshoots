Carestream DRX-Rise

Emergency Stop, Collision Sensor, or Parking Brake Prevents Movement

Asset Type

Mobile X-Ray System

Manufacturer

Carestream

Model

DRX-Rise

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot movement inhibition caused by emergency-stop status, collision sensing, parking brake conditions, obstruction, control state, or external mechanical interference.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Prevent Unexpected Motion

Do not troubleshoot powered motion while a patient, staff member, bed, wall, or accessory could be struck. Move the system to a safe evaluation area when possible. Expected outcome: Motion controls can be tested without collision risk.

2. Confirm the Exact Movement Inhibit

Determine whether the unit will not drive at all, releases intermittently, stops immediately after movement begins, or remains in a braking condition. Record any displayed movement or safety message. Expected outcome: The exact inhibit behavior is documented.

3. Check the Emergency-Stop Control

Inspect the emergency-stop control and verify it is in the normal released position using the approved operating method. Do not bypass the emergency-stop circuit. Expected outcome: Emergency stop is fully released. If this restores normal movement, complete final verification and stop.

4. Inspect for Collision-Sensor Contact

Check bumpers, collision sensors, and accessible sensing surfaces for contact with walls, carts, cables, linens, accessories, or debris. Expected outcome: No external object is continuously activating a collision sensor.

5. Inspect the Parking Brake Condition

Confirm the parking brake or normal drive/park control is fully in the intended position. Look for mechanical obstruction or incomplete control travel. Expected outcome: Parking brake releases and applies normally.

6. Check the Floor and Wheel Area

Inspect for wheel blocks, cords, debris, uneven flooring, or objects underneath the chassis that could prevent motion or hold a sensor active. Expected outcome: The path and wheel area are clear.

7. Verify System Power and Drive Readiness

Confirm the DRX-Rise has adequate power and is in the normal state for powered transport. A low-power or inhibited system condition may prevent drive engagement. Expected outcome: Drive controls are available and the system shows normal readiness.

8. Test Movement in an Open Area

With no patient and adequate clearance, command slow forward, reverse, and steering movement as permitted by normal controls. Observe whether any particular direction or chassis area triggers the inhibit. Expected outcome: Drive movement is smooth and does not stop unexpectedly.

9. Check Repeated Sensor and Brake Operation

Apply and release the parking brake and verify accessible collision-sensor operation through approved functional checks. Do not defeat or hold sensors bypassed. Expected outcome: Safety controls inhibit movement when intended and release movement normally afterward.

10. Escalate Persistent Safety-System Inhibition

If movement remains inhibited or any safety device operates intermittently, remove the DRX-Rise from service. Expected outcome: A mobile system with unreliable braking or collision protection is not used for clinical transport.

If the Problem Persists

External obstruction, emergency-stop state, parking brake position, floor conditions, power, and accessible collision sensors have been checked. Remaining causes may involve safety switches, sensors, brake hardware, drive controls, wiring, software, or other internal motion-control components.

Remove the DRX-Rise from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Evaluate the system using appropriate Carestream documentation and approved test equipment. Safety circuits should be repaired or configured only by qualified personnel and must never be bypassed for clinical use.

Before return to service, verify emergency-stop function, parking brake operation, collision protection, powered movement, steering, and safe stopping behavior.

Knowing when to stop rather than bypass a safety device is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never defeat a collision sensor, emergency stop, or parking-brake interlock to keep the mobile system in service.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Radiology reported that the DRX-Rise powered drive would not engage and remained in a movement-inhibited condition."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a supply strap caught against a lower collision sensor and holding it activated."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed and secured the strap, inspected the sensor area, and verified normal forward, reverse, steering, braking, and collision-sensor operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact movement or safety message
  • Emergency-stop position
  • Collision sensor involved
  • Parking brake condition
  • Obstruction or debris found
  • Battery/power state
  • Direction of movement affected
  • Whether inhibit is intermittent
  • Functional verification performed
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Safety interlocks exist to prevent injury and equipment damage, so verify external activation and obstruction first, never bypass them, and escalate any intermittent safety-system behavior.

That is successful troubleshooting.
