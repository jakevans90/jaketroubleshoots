Waste Container, Drain, or Pneumatic Supply Fault Stops Analysis

Asset Type

Hematology Analyzer

Manufacturer

Sysmex

Model

XN-2000

What This Guide Helps With

Analysis stops because of waste handling, drainage, external tubing, or pneumatic supply conditions affecting normal analyzer operation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patient Testing and the Work Area

Stop patient testing if the analyzer reports a waste, drain, or pneumatic fault that prevents reliable operation.

If there is leakage, overflow, unusual odor, or visible fluid outside the intended containment system, keep staff away from the affected area and follow facility exposure procedures.

Expected outcome: Patient testing is redirected and any immediate spill or exposure hazard is controlled.

2. Confirm the Exact Fault Category

Determine whether the problem involves a full waste container, blocked drain, leaking waste connection, external air supply, or another visible pneumatic condition.

Expected outcome: The fault is narrowed to waste, drainage, or pneumatic supply rather than treated as a generic analyzer failure.

3. Inspect the Waste Container

If the system uses an external waste container in the installed configuration, verify it is not full, improperly positioned, disconnected, collapsed, damaged, or otherwise unable to receive waste.

Handle biological waste according to facility policy.

Expected outcome: The waste container is correctly installed and has adequate capacity.

If correcting a full or improperly installed waste container clears the fault, verify operation and stop.

4. Inspect Drain Tubing

Check accessible drain and waste tubing for kinks, compression, improper routing, loose fittings, or visible blockage.

Do not disconnect contaminated tubing unless permitted by approved procedures and appropriate precautions are in place.

Expected outcome: Drain tubing is secure, unobstructed, and routed normally.

If correcting external tubing routing restores normal drainage, verify for leaks and stop.

5. Check the Facility Drain Connection

Where the analyzer connects to a facility drain, inspect the accessible connection for backup, dislodgement, or obvious restriction.

Do not perform plumbing repair unless within Clinical Engineering scope.

Expected outcome: The external drain interface is open and functioning without backup.

If a facility drain issue is identified, keep the analyzer out of service until Facilities or the appropriate support group corrects it.

6. Verify External Pneumatic Supply if Applicable

If the installed configuration depends on an external pneumatic or air source, confirm the source is available, connected, and not intentionally shut off.

Inspect accessible tubing for kinks, loose connections, or damage.

Expected outcome: Required external pneumatic supply is present and connected normally.

If restoring an external supply resolves the analyzer condition, verify stable operation and stop.

7. Inspect for Visible Leaks

Look around accessible waste, drain, and pneumatic connections for fluid, moisture, damaged tubing, loose fittings, or signs of repeated leakage.

Expected outcome: No external leakage is present.

If a replaceable external tube or connection within approved scope is corrected, verify the system remains dry and stable before proceeding.

8. Perform an Approved Restart or Recovery

After an external waste, drain, or pneumatic condition has been corrected, follow the approved recovery or restart procedure.

Do not repeatedly clear the fault without correcting the underlying cause.

Expected outcome: The analyzer returns to a ready state without the fault recurring.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Use approved control or nonpatient testing as appropriate. Confirm analysis completes normally, waste drains correctly, no leakage occurs, and the fault does not return.

Expected outcome: Analysis completes with stable waste, drain, and pneumatic operation.

If verification passes, return the analyzer to service.

10. Escalate Persistent Supply or Drainage Faults

If the analyzer continues to report the condition despite verified external waste capacity, clear external tubing, functioning drain, and available pneumatic supply, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: An analyzer with an unresolved fluid or pneumatic fault is not used for patient testing.

If the Problem Persists

Remaining causes may include internal pumps, valves, pressure sensing, vacuum or pneumatic components, internal drainage, or service-level fluidics.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Sysmex documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel

Return-to-service testing should confirm normal analysis, drainage, pneumatic operation, and absence of leaks.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Waste and drain faults can become both analytical and exposure hazards; treat visible leakage as a reason to stop testing immediately.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Laboratory staff reported that the Sysmex XN-2000 stopped analysis with a waste-related fault."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external waste tubing kinked behind the analyzer, restricting drainage."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The tubing was rerouted without restriction, and the analyzer completed control testing with normal drainage and no recurrence of the fault."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Fault category displayed
  • Waste container status
  • Drain tubing condition
  • Facility drain status
  • Pneumatic supply status
  • Visible leaks
  • Spill or exposure concern
  • Corrective action
  • Recovery or restart result
  • Final control result
  • Final leak check

Final Thought

Start with the external waste, drain, and supply path before considering an internal fluidic failure. Protect staff and patient testing, correct only accessible causes, verify reliable operation, and escalate persistent faults.

That is successful troubleshooting.
