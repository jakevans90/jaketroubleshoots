Getinge Servo-air

Expiratory Cassette Not Recognized or Not Seated

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Getinge

Model

Servo-air

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses cassette recognition or seating problems caused by improper insertion, contamination, moisture, damage, incompatible components, or external obstruction.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient

Do not remove, reseat, or troubleshoot the expiratory cassette while a patient depends on the ventilator. Move the patient to another verified ventilator or alternate ventilation method first.

Expected outcome: The patient is safely supported and the affected ventilator is removed from active clinical use.

2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Power the Servo-air in a controlled test setting and verify whether the cassette is actually not recognized, will not seat, or produces a related startup or pre-use check failure.

Note any displayed message without assuming an internal failure.

Expected outcome: The exact cassette-related symptom is reproduced. If the problem cannot be reproduced, inspect carefully and complete full verification before considering return to service.

3. Remove and Inspect the Cassette

Remove the expiratory cassette using normal approved handling. Inspect for:

  • Cracks or broken housing
  • Warping
  • Contamination
  • Excess moisture where it should not be present
  • Damaged interfaces
  • Foreign material

Do not disassemble the cassette beyond approved handling.

Expected outcome: The cassette is visibly intact and suitable for reinsertion. A damaged cassette should be replaced rather than forced into the ventilator.

4. Inspect the Cassette Receptacle

With the unit safely powered as appropriate for inspection, examine the external cassette mounting area for debris, fluid, bent external features, or an obstruction preventing full insertion.

Do not probe internal contacts or disassemble the ventilator.

Expected outcome: The receptacle is clean, unobstructed, and capable of accepting the cassette normally.

5. Reseat the Cassette

Install the cassette evenly and completely without excessive force. Verify that it reaches its normal seated position.

Do not force a cassette that binds or does not align normally.

Expected outcome: The cassette seats securely and the ventilator recognizes it. If recognition is restored and subsequent pre-use testing passes, troubleshooting can stop.

6. Verify Cassette Compatibility and Processing Status

Confirm the cassette is intended for the Servo-air and has been processed, cleaned, dried, and prepared according to facility and manufacturer requirements.

A cassette that is still wet, improperly assembled after processing, or incompatible should not be used.

Expected outcome: A compatible, correctly prepared cassette is installed.

7. Substitute a Known-Good Cassette

Install a known-good compatible expiratory cassette when available.

Expected outcome: If the known-good cassette is recognized normally, the original cassette is the likely external cause and should be removed from use. If neither cassette is recognized, stop repeated substitutions and escalate.

8. Repeat the Pre-Use Check

Perform the complete required pre-use check after the cassette is properly recognized.

Expected outcome: The cassette remains recognized and the ventilator completes the required checks. Troubleshooting can stop after final verification.

9. Escalate Persistent Recognition Failure

If multiple known-good compatible cassettes fail to be recognized or cannot seat despite a clean and unobstructed receptacle, do not attempt deeper disassembly or adjustment.

Expected outcome: The ventilator remains removed from service for qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Persistent failure may involve the cassette interface, sensing mechanism, internal electronics, mechanical alignment, or another service-level condition.

Remove the ventilator from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Getinge documentation and approved test equipment. Repairs or internal adjustments should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Complete pre-use, ventilation, alarm, and applicable performance testing before returning the device to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A cassette that requires unusual force to install should not be used; forcing it may damage both the cassette and ventilator interface.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Servo-air would not recognize the expiratory cassette during setup."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the installed cassette was not fully seated in the cassette receptacle."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering removed and correctly reinstalled the cassette, confirmed recognition, completed the pre-use check successfully, and returned the ventilator to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Recognition message displayed
  • Cassette condition
  • Moisture or contamination observed
  • Receptacle condition
  • Whether cassette fully seated
  • Known-good cassette result
  • Pre-use check result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient, inspect and reseat external components before assuming internal failure, verify cassette recognition with appropriate testing, and escalate persistent failures with clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
