Rack/Cart Identification or Transfer Sensor Is Missing

WD 290 Series

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots missing rack, cart, or transfer detection caused by positioning, identification components, obstruction, alignment, connectivity, or service-level sensing problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Patients and Processing Workflow

Do not force a rack or cart through an automated transfer sequence or bypass identification or position detection.

If the washer-disinfector cannot reliably identify or detect the load-handling equipment, use an alternate verified processing path.

Expected outcome: Unexpected rack movement, incorrect cycle selection, and processing errors are prevented.

2. Confirm What Is Not Being Detected

Determine whether the system is failing to recognize:

  • The rack itself
  • A cart or transfer interface
  • A rack identification feature
  • Rack position
  • Entry or exit transfer status
  • One particular rack or every rack

Record the exact displayed message or observed behavior.

Expected outcome: The detection problem is narrowed to a specific rack, cart, position, or transfer condition.

3. Inspect Rack and Cart Positioning

Verify the rack or cart is:

  • Fully inserted or aligned
  • Square to the transfer path
  • Not obstructed by instruments or accessories
  • Correctly seated at stops or guides
  • Not visibly bent or damaged

Do not force equipment past resistance.

Expected outcome: The rack or cart reaches its normal detection position without binding. If repositioning restores detection, troubleshooting can stop after functional verification.

4. Inspect Identification Features

Inspect accessible identification components on the affected rack or cart for:

  • Missing parts
  • Dirt or residue
  • Physical damage
  • Incorrect orientation
  • Loose components
  • Use of an incompatible rack or cart

Clean only according to approved practices and do not modify identification hardware.

Expected outcome: Identification features are present, clean, properly positioned, and undamaged.

5. Inspect Accessible Sensor Areas

With the system safely stopped, visually inspect accessible detection areas for:

  • Debris
  • Misaligned external targets
  • Obstruction
  • Physical damage
  • Excessive moisture or residue
  • Loose externally accessible components

Do not bypass, defeat, manually actuate, or internally adjust safety-related sensors.

Expected outcome: The accessible sensing area is unobstructed and physically intact.

6. Compare With a Known-Good Rack or Cart

When compatible equipment is available, test with a known-good rack or cart.

Compare:

  • Detection
  • Positioning
  • Alignment
  • Transfer behavior
  • Identification response

Expected outcome: If the known-good rack is detected normally, the original rack or cart is isolated as the likely problem and should be removed from use for evaluation.

7. Check the Transfer Path

Inspect the accessible path for anything that could prevent the rack or cart from reaching the required position, including:

  • Debris
  • Misaligned guides
  • Damaged wheels or rollers on the transport equipment
  • Objects in the pathway
  • Mechanical interference

Use caution around powered transfer equipment and follow applicable lockout or safe-access procedures.

Expected outcome: The rack can travel to its intended position without obstruction or abnormal resistance.

8. Verify Normal Controls and Operating State

Confirm the washer-disinfector is in the expected normal operating state and that no active door, transfer, or process condition is preventing recognition.

Do not enter restricted service menus or change sensor configuration.

Expected outcome: No normal operating-state condition is intentionally preventing rack or cart detection.

9. Perform Functional Verification

After correcting an external problem, perform a controlled rack/cart transfer or cycle setup according to facility procedure.

Confirm:

  • Rack or cart is detected consistently
  • Correct identification is shown when applicable
  • Transfer occurs normally
  • No unexpected movement occurs
  • Associated doors or process controls operate normally

Expected outcome: Detection and transfer are repeatable and normal. Troubleshooting can stop after required return-to-service verification.

10. Escalate Persistent Detection Problems

If detection fails with proper positioning, clean identification features, an unobstructed path, and a known-good rack or cart, stop external troubleshooting.

Potential service-level categories include sensor assemblies, alignment, wiring, controls, transfer mechanisms, or configuration.

Expected outcome: Unreliable automated transfer or identification equipment is removed from clinical processing until qualified service is completed.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as positioning, rack identification features, transfer obstruction, compatibility, and visible sensor-area contamination have been ruled out.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or qualified service evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Belimed documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not bypass sensors or alter transfer interlocks to maintain operation. After repair, verify correct rack identification, positioning, transfer behavior, and normal cycle operation before return to service.

Knowing when to stop rather than bypassing an unreliable detection system is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never manually defeat a rack-position or transfer interlock; unexpected movement can injure staff and may result in the wrong load or process being selected.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported that one processing rack was not recognized when positioned at the washer transfer interface."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found residue covering the rack's accessible identification feature, while a known-good rack was detected normally."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the identification area according to approved practice, retested the rack, and verified consistent recognition and normal transfer operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact displayed message
  • Rack or cart identification
  • Whether one or all racks are affected
  • Position where detection fails
  • Identification component condition
  • Sensor-area condition
  • Transfer-path condition
  • Known-good rack/cart result
  • Recent rack or transfer damage
  • Functional verification result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Treat rack identification and transfer detection as part of the washer's safety and process-control system. Verify positioning, identification hardware, obstructions, and known-good equipment before escalating, and never bypass an unreliable sensor.

That is successful troubleshooting.
