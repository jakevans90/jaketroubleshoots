Exhalation Valve Will Not Seat, Cycle, or Pass the Preoperational Check

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Hamilton

Model

C2

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots exhalation-valve failures caused by incorrect assembly, contamination, moisture, damaged components, poor seating, or breathing-circuit problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient

Do not troubleshoot an exhalation-valve problem while the patient depends on the affected ventilator.

Move the patient to another verified ventilator or approved alternate ventilation method.

Expected outcome: The patient is safely supported and the C2 is available for bench testing.

2. Confirm the Failure

Run the applicable preoperational check with a test setup and determine whether the valve fails to seat, fails to cycle, leaks, or causes another test failure.

Record the exact message displayed.

Expected outcome: The valve-related symptom is confirmed under controlled conditions.

3. Inspect the Valve Assembly

Remove only components intended for routine external handling. Inspect the exhalation valve for improper assembly, contamination, moisture, deformation, cracks, or damaged sealing surfaces.

Expected outcome: The assembly is clean, dry, undamaged, and correctly assembled.

If correcting assembly restores normal operation, proceed to final verification.

4. Verify Proper Seating

Reinstall the exhalation valve carefully and verify that it is fully seated and latched as designed.

Do not force the assembly or modify seals.

Expected outcome: The valve seats securely without excessive force or looseness.

If the valve now passes testing, complete verification and stop.

5. Inspect the Breathing Circuit

Check the expiratory limb, connectors, filters, water traps, and related accessories for obstruction, leaks, incorrect orientation, or excessive condensate.

Expected outcome: The expiratory path is correctly assembled and unobstructed.

If an external circuit issue caused the valve-related failure, correct it and retest.

6. Substitute a Known-Good Valve Assembly When Permitted

Install a known-good compatible exhalation-valve assembly if available and permitted by facility procedure.

Repeat the preoperational check.

Expected outcome: The problem either follows the original valve assembly or remains with the ventilator.

If a known-good valve corrects the issue, replace the defective external assembly and continue to final verification.

7. Check for Repeated Abnormal Cycling

During test-lung ventilation, observe the exhalation phase for abnormal noise, sticking, delayed movement, or unstable pressure behavior.

Do not access or adjust internal pneumatic controls.

Expected outcome: Exhalation appears stable and repeatable without abnormal valve behavior.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

Run all required preoperational checks and ventilate a test lung. Confirm normal pressure release, exhalation, alarms, and absence of leakage.

Expected outcome: The exhalation valve passes the required checks and supports stable ventilation.

If successful, troubleshooting can stop and the ventilator may be returned to service.

If the Problem Persists

If the C2 continues to report exhalation-valve problems after correct valve assembly, seating, circuit condition, and known-good external components have been verified, the problem may involve internal pneumatic actuation, pressure sensing, valve drive components, or calibration.

The ventilator should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using Hamilton service documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or calibrated only by qualified personnel
  • Fully tested before return to clinical use

Clinical Use Tip

A leaking or sticking exhalation valve can affect pressure control and patient ventilation, so a questionable valve requires immediate device exchange.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported that the Hamilton C2 repeatedly failed the exhalation-valve portion of the preoperational check."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the removable exhalation-valve assembly improperly seated."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reinstalled the valve correctly, completed the preoperational checks successfully, and verified stable ventilation and alarm operation with a test lung."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact displayed message
  • Valve seating condition
  • Valve contamination or moisture
  • Seal or housing damage
  • Circuit condition
  • Known-good valve substitution
  • Preoperational-check result
  • Pressure behavior during test
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Verify correct assembly, seating, cleanliness, and circuit condition before assuming an internal pneumatic fault. Reliable exhalation is essential to safe ventilation, and unresolved valve problems require removal from service and qualified evaluation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
