Touchscreen or Physical Navigation Controls Are Unresponsive

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot frozen touch input or unresponsive physical controls caused by contamination, accessories, startup problems, or control-system faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety and Confirm the Failure

If the monitor cannot be controlled reliably, remove it from patient use and provide another verified monitor.

Determine whether the touchscreen is completely unresponsive, responds intermittently, selects incorrect locations, or whether an installed physical control fails.

Identify the specific control configuration present on the unit.

Expected outcome: The affected controls and failure behavior are clearly identified.

2. Inspect the Display Surface

Check for:

  • Moisture or cleaning residue.
  • Contamination on the touchscreen.
  • Cracks or impact damage.
  • An improperly fitted screen protector, if installed.
  • Physical pressure from surrounding accessories.

Clean the display only using manufacturer-approved methods.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen surface is clean, dry, and free from obvious physical interference.

3. Check Physical Controls

Inspect the installed power button and any applicable physical navigation controls.

Look for sticking, binding, damage, or contamination.

Do not force a stuck button or attempt to remove the front-panel assembly.

Expected outcome: Accessible controls move or respond normally without mechanical obstruction.

4. Verify Power Stability

Confirm that the monitor has a stable external power source or adequately charged battery.

Observe whether the display dims, flickers, restarts, or becomes unresponsive during operation.

Expected outcome: The monitor remains powered without an unexplained interruption.

5. Perform an Approved Restart

After removing the device from clinical use, shut it down using the normal approved procedure.

Restart and observe the startup sequence.

Do not repeatedly interrupt power or attempt undocumented reset combinations.

Expected outcome: The monitor completes startup and accepts normal input.

If control operation returns and remains stable, continue to verification.

6. Evaluate Touchscreen Response

Using an approved nonclinical test workflow, check touch response across the accessible display.

Confirm that selections correspond to the intended on-screen controls.

Do not enter protected service menus or perform undocumented touchscreen calibration.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen responds accurately and consistently throughout the required operating areas.

7. Verify Normal Monitor Functions

Confirm that the monitor can navigate its normal screens, initiate supported measurements, and display results using approved test equipment.

Check that control responsiveness remains stable during normal operation.

Expected outcome: The display and controls support the required clinical workflow without freezing or unintended selections.

If all functional checks pass, troubleshooting can stop.

8. Escalate Persistent Control Failure

If the touchscreen or physical controls remain unreliable, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The monitor is held from clinical use pending qualified service evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External contamination, interference, power, and restart-related causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include touchscreen digitizer faults, display interface problems, physical control hardware failure, or software instability.

Remove the monitor from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for qualified repair or bench evaluation using manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.

Only authorized personnel should perform internal repairs, software restoration, or calibration.

Complete control and overall functional verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

An intermittently responsive touchscreen can create incorrect patient selections or measurement actions; do not release the monitor until input accuracy is verified.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Connex Spot Monitor touchscreen intermittently failed to respond to selections."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found dried cleaning residue on the display surface that interfered with touch response."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the display using the approved method and verified accurate touchscreen response and normal monitor operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Specific unresponsive control.
  • Touchscreen failure location.
  • Display contamination or damage.
  • Physical button condition.
  • Power stability.
  • Restart behavior.
  • Functional test results.
  • Final equipment status.

Final Thought

Control failures require prompt removal from unreliable clinical operation. Inspect the display and accessible controls, verify power and startup behavior, and test functionality before assuming internal failure. Escalate unresolved faults and document the verified result.

That is successful troubleshooting.
