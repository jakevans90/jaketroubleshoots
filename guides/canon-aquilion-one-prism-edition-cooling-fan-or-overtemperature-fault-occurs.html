What This Guide Helps With

Addresses cooling, fan, or temperature faults caused by blocked ventilation, room HVAC, environmental heat, airflow restrictions, or service-level cooling problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Stop Use if Overheating Is Suspected

If the scanner reports overtemperature, emits unusual heat or odor, or loses cooling during a patient examination, stop use safely and transfer the patient if necessary.

Do not continue scanning to see whether the temperature fault clears.

Expected outcome: The patient is removed from dependence on potentially overheating equipment.

2. Confirm the Exact Cooling Complaint

Determine whether the system reports a temperature warning, fan fault, cooling fault, shutdown, intermittent restart, unusual noise, or visible reduction in airflow.

Record the displayed message exactly.

Expected outcome: The thermal problem is clearly characterized.

3. Check Room Temperature and HVAC Operation

Inspect the scan room and equipment-room environment for excessive heat or loss of air conditioning.

Ask whether building HVAC maintenance, power loss, or room-temperature alarms recently occurred.

Expected outcome: Facility cooling is confirmed operational or an environmental cause is identified.

If facility HVAC is the cause, keep the scanner out of service until environmental conditions are restored and the system is verified.

4. Inspect External Airflow Paths

Check accessible vents, grilles, filters, surrounding clearance, and equipment-room areas for dust buildup, obstruction, stored materials, or blocked airflow.

Do not remove internal cooling assemblies unless authorized.

Expected outcome: External airflow paths are unobstructed.

If an external obstruction is corrected and the fault clears, continue to verification.

5. Listen for Abnormal Fan or Cooling Noise

From a safe external position, note whether expected fans appear to be operating and whether there is grinding, rattling, cycling, or other unusual sound.

Do not place hands or tools near moving components.

Expected outcome: No obvious abnormal cooling-system behavior is present externally.

6. Inspect for Heat, Odor, Moisture, or Leakage

Check around the scanner and associated equipment for abnormal heat, burning odor, condensation, water, or coolant-like leakage.

If smoke, burning odor, or active leakage is present, remove power only according to site emergency procedure and escalate.

Expected outcome: No unsafe environmental or leakage condition is present.

7. Allow Appropriate Recovery if Permitted

If the scanner shut down because of a known room-temperature or airflow event and approved procedures permit it, allow conditions to normalize before restarting.

Do not defeat thermal protection or repeatedly reset a recurring temperature fault.

Expected outcome: The system restarts only after appropriate environmental recovery.

8. Perform an Approved Controlled Restart

Once safe environmental conditions are confirmed, perform an approved restart and observe whether cooling or temperature faults recur.

Expected outcome: The scanner completes startup and remains thermally stable.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Confirm normal ready status, expected fan operation, stable room conditions, and successful non-patient operation as appropriate.

Expected outcome: No thermal warnings recur and the system remains stable.

If achieved, troubleshooting is complete.

10. Escalate Persistent Thermal Faults

If cooling alarms, fan faults, abnormal temperatures, odor, leakage, or shutdowns recur, remove the system from service. Do not continue operation or perform internal cooling repairs without qualified service support.

Expected outcome: Potential thermal damage or unsafe operation is prevented.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities may include internal fans, cooling circuits, temperature sensors, heat exchangers, power components, X-ray system cooling, or service-level monitoring faults.

The CT scanner should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or qualified on-site evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Canon documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Following repair, verify cooling operation, temperature stability, scanner readiness, and required functional performance before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

A temperature fault is not an alarm to repeatedly reset; continued CT operation can worsen equipment damage and interrupt patient care unexpectedly.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"CT staff reported an overtemperature warning and scanner shutdown during room cooling problems."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the equipment-room HVAC was not maintaining normal environmental conditions and scanner ventilation was otherwise unobstructed."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Kept the scanner out of service until HVAC was restored, restarted the system after environmental recovery, and verified stable operation with no recurring temperature warning."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact cooling or temperature message.
  • Room and equipment-room conditions.
  • HVAC status.
  • Vent and grille condition.
  • Fan sound or airflow observations.
  • Unusual heat, odor, moisture, or leakage.
  • Recent power or HVAC event.
  • Restart results.
  • Whether fault returned.
  • Final scanner status.

Final Thought

Treat overheating as a safety and reliability issue, rule out room and airflow problems first, and escalate persistent thermal faults before equipment damage or clinical interruption occurs.

That is successful troubleshooting.
