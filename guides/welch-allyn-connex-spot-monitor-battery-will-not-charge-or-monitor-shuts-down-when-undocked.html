Battery Will Not Charge or Monitor Shuts Down When Undocked

Asset Type

Vital Signs Monitor

Manufacturer

Welch Allyn

Model

Connex Spot Monitor

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot charging failures or unexpected battery shutdowns caused by AC power, charging contacts, battery condition, or external power connections.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety and Confirm the Failure

Remove the monitor from patient use if it unexpectedly shuts down or cannot maintain the power required for its intended workflow.

Provide another verified vital signs monitor.

Ask whether the monitor fails to charge, reports a low battery, shuts down immediately when disconnected from external power, or operates for an unexpectedly short period.

Expected outcome: The power-related failure is identified and patient assessment continues using reliable equipment.

2. Inspect the AC Power Source

Confirm that the charging adapter or stand is connected to an appropriate approved outlet.

Check the power cord and plug for visible damage.

Verify outlet operation using an approved facility method.

Expected outcome: The external AC source is available and the power connection is secure.

3. Inspect the Power Adapter and Charging Connections

Check the approved power adapter, cable, and accessible connector surfaces for:

  • Frayed insulation.
  • Bent or damaged contacts.
  • Loose connections.
  • Discoloration or overheating.
  • Contamination or corrosion.

Do not use a visibly damaged adapter or connector.

Expected outcome: The external power components are intact and properly connected.

4. Check Charging Stand or Docking Contact

If a charging stand is installed, verify that the monitor seats correctly.

Inspect accessible charging contacts for debris, wear, or damage.

Clean only by approved methods and do not bend or modify the contacts.

Expected outcome: The monitor makes consistent charging contact with the stand.

5. Observe Charging Indicators

Connect the monitor to approved external power.

Observe the charging status, battery indication, and any displayed messages.

Allow an appropriate charging period based on manufacturer guidance.

Expected outcome: The monitor indicates charging and the battery state progresses appropriately.

If charging resumes after correcting an external connection, continue to battery verification.

6. Compare With Known-Good Power Accessories

Where compatible and authorized, substitute a known-good approved AC adapter or charging stand.

Observe whether the charging indication becomes stable.

Do not use generic power adapters solely because their connectors fit.

Expected outcome: The monitor charges normally with a verified external power source.

If the replacement accessory corrects the issue, remove the defective accessory from service.

7. Verify Battery Operation

After charging according to the manufacturer's instructions, disconnect external power in a controlled, nonclinical setting.

Observe whether the monitor remains operational and performs the required functions.

Conduct any specified battery performance or runtime test using the applicable manufacturer procedure.

Expected outcome: The monitor operates on battery power and meets the applicable manufacturer requirements.

If all required checks pass, troubleshooting can stop.

8. Escalate Persistent Charging or Battery Failure

If the monitor continues to shut down or fails to charge with verified power accessories, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The device is withheld from clinical use until the power system is evaluated.

If the Problem Persists

External power, adapter, docking, and accessible connection problems have been ruled out. Remaining possibilities include battery deterioration, internal charging circuitry faults, power-management problems, or internal connection issues.

Remove the monitor from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for qualified repair or bench evaluation.

Use manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment. Battery replacement, if required, must follow authorized service procedures and use approved parts.

Complete charging, battery operation, functional, and applicable electrical safety verification before returning the monitor to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Before using the monitor away from its charging location, confirm that battery operation is reliable rather than relying only on the displayed charge percentage.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Connex Spot Monitor would not charge consistently while positioned on its charging stand."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found contamination on the accessible charging contacts that prevented consistent electrical contact."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the contacts using the approved method and verified stable charging and successful battery operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Charging or battery error messages.
  • AC outlet verification.
  • Power adapter condition.
  • Charging stand contact condition.
  • Battery status before and after charging.
  • Known-good power accessory substitutions.
  • Battery operation test results.
  • Final equipment status.

Final Thought

Battery troubleshooting should begin with safe external power verification and charging connections before assuming internal failure. Confirm reliable operation away from AC power, escalate persistent problems, and document the corrective action and final tests.

That is successful troubleshooting.
