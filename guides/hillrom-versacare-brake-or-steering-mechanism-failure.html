Hillrom VersaCare

Brake Or Steering Mechanism Failure

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Hillrom

Model

VersaCare

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting brake or steering problems caused by pedal position, caster obstruction, debris, floor conditions, linkage issues, damage, or incorrect operation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Prevent Unintended Movement

Do not troubleshoot an unreliable brake or steering system while the bed is being used for patient entry, exit, transfer, transport, or repositioning.

Notify the responsible clinical staff.

Transfer the patient to another verified bed when practical.

If immediate transfer is unsafe, keep staff with the patient and prevent bed movement.

Use approved wheel chocks or another facility-approved method only when necessary for temporary stabilization.

Do not rely on a questionable brake to secure an occupied bed.

Expected outcome: The patient is protected from falls, collisions, and unexpected bed movement.

If the bed cannot be stabilized safely, remove it from service immediately and troubleshooting can stop.

2. Confirm the Exact Reported Condition

Determine whether:

  • The brake pedal will not move into the brake position.
  • The pedal moves, but one or more casters still roll.
  • The bed rolls despite the brake appearing engaged.
  • The steering function does not guide the bed.
  • The bed is difficult to push or pulls to one side.
  • The pedal is loose, bent, binding, or does not remain in position.
  • The problem occurs on all floor surfaces or only in one location.
  • A brake warning remains active after engagement, if equipped.

Expected outcome: The failure is clearly identified as a pedal, braking, steering, caster, warning, or environmental problem.

3. Remove External Loads and Obstructions

With the bed unoccupied and safely stabilized:

Remove equipment, cables, linens, or accessories contacting the floor.

Confirm no power cord or nurse-call cable is wrapped around a caster.

Check for bedding trapped between the frame and wheels.

Move portable devices, furniture, and wall-mounted equipment away from the bed.

Confirm the bed is not resting against a threshold, floor transition, or fixed object.

Expected outcome: The bed and all casters are free from external obstructions.

If removing the obstruction restores normal braking or steering, proceed to final verification.

4. Inspect the Floor and Test Location

Evaluate the floor where the problem was reported.

Look for:

  • Wet, oily, dusty, polished, or contaminated surfaces.
  • Uneven flooring, thresholds, damaged tiles, or slopes.
  • Debris that may prevent caster contact.
  • A surface that is unusually slippery or soft.
  • Construction coverings or floor mats interfering with the casters.
  • Move the unoccupied bed to a clean, level, dry area and repeat the test.

Expected outcome: Brake and steering performance is evaluated on a suitable floor surface.

If the bed functions normally in the alternate area, address the environmental condition and troubleshooting can stop after verification.

5. Verify Correct Pedal Position and Operation

Operate the accessible brake and steering pedals through their normal positions.

Confirm the pedal is moved fully into the intended brake, neutral, or steering position.

Do not force a pedal that is jammed or unusually resistant.

Observe whether the pedal travels evenly and remains in position.

Compare pedal position and feel with another verified VersaCare bed when available.

Confirm staff were using the correct pedal and position.

Expected outcome: The pedal moves smoothly through its normal positions, remains engaged, and is being operated correctly.

If correct positioning restores normal operation, complete final verification and troubleshooting can stop.

6. Inspect Casters and Accessible Brake Components

Inspect each caster and all accessible external brake or steering components.

Look for:

  • Hair, tape, suture material, plastic, or debris wrapped around the wheel or swivel.
  • Flat spots, cracks, missing tread, or visibly damaged wheels.
  • Bent forks, loose hardware, misaligned casters, or impact damage.
  • A caster that does not swivel or rotate freely in neutral.
  • A visibly damaged or disconnected external linkage.
  • Fluid contamination or corrosion.

Do not place hands beneath an unstable bed or near moving linkage while operating pedals.

Expected outcome: Casters and external brake components are clean, aligned, secure, and free of visible damage.

If debris removal restores operation, proceed to final verification. If damage is present, remove the bed from service.

7. Test Brake Holding Performance

On a clean, level floor with the bed unoccupied:

Place the pedal fully in the brake position.

Apply controlled push and pull force from both ends of the bed.

Observe each caster for rolling or swiveling.

Repeat the test from each side.

Release and reapply the brake to confirm repeatability.

Do not use excessive force that could overturn equipment or injure personnel.

Expected outcome: The bed remains stable, the brake engagement is repeatable, and no caster rolls unexpectedly.

If the bed holds securely during repeated testing, troubleshooting can stop after completing steering verification.

8. Test Neutral and Steering Operation

Release the brake and place the pedal in the appropriate neutral or steering position.

Confirm the bed rolls without abnormal drag.

Verify that all casters rotate and swivel as intended.

Test steering in a clear hallway or approved test area.

Observe whether the bed tracks predictably without pulling sharply.

Compare operation with a known-good VersaCare bed when available.

Confirm accessories or attached equipment are not altering the handling.

Expected outcome: The bed rolls freely in neutral and tracks predictably when steering is engaged.

If steering operates normally after clearing an external condition, proceed to final verification.

9. Evaluate Brake Warning Behavior When Applicable

If the complaint includes a brake warning:

Engage and release the brake several times.

Observe whether the warning follows the actual pedal position.

Confirm the bed is connected to normal power.

Check for visible damage or obstruction around accessible pedal components.

Do not adjust sensors, linkages, or internal switches without the approved manufacturer procedure.

Expected outcome: Any brake-status indication accurately matches the physical brake condition.

If the warning remains incorrect despite normal pedal operation, remove the bed from service for service-level evaluation.

10. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correction:

  • Test brake engagement several times.
  • Push and pull the bed from multiple directions.
  • Confirm neutral rolling.
  • Confirm steering operation and predictable tracking.
  • Verify all casters rotate and swivel normally.
  • Check pedal stability and return.
  • Confirm any brake warning corresponds to actual brake position.
  • Inspect the power cord and attached cables after moving the bed.
  • Verify safe operation with the bed unloaded before clinical release.

Expected outcome: The bed holds securely when braked, rolls freely in neutral, steers predictably, and provides accurate brake status when equipped.

If all checks pass, return the bed to service and troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as incorrect pedal position, caster debris, floor contamination, attached cables, environmental obstructions, and visible external damage have been ruled out.

The remaining problem may involve internal brake linkage, steering linkage, caster assemblies, pedal mechanism, brake-status sensing, adjustment, or another service-level mechanical fault.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using current Hillrom documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or adjusted only by qualified personnel.

Do not return a bed to use if it can roll with the brake engaged, cannot steer predictably, binds during transport, or gives an inaccurate brake indication. After repair, perform complete brake, steering, caster, warning, and transport verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate an unreliable mobility system is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Always push and pull the VersaCare bed after setting the brake to confirm that it is physically stable before patient transfer, entry, or exit.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the VersaCare bed would not steer properly and pulled sharply to one side during transport."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a nurse-call cable wrapped around a front caster, preventing normal swivel movement."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering removed and rerouted the cable, inspected the caster, and verified normal neutral rolling, steering, and brake holding performance."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether brake, neutral, steering, or multiple positions were affected.
  • Patient occupancy and clinical activity at the time.
  • Pedal position and feel.
  • Caster or wheel affected.
  • Debris, cable entanglement, or floor condition.
  • Visible impact or linkage damage.
  • Brake warning behavior.
  • Push-pull brake test result.
  • Neutral rolling and steering results.
  • Comparison with another bed.
  • Results before and after correction.
  • Final bed status.

Final Thought

Prevent unintended movement first, then inspect pedal position, cables, floor conditions, casters, and accessible mechanisms before assuming an internal failure. Verify braking and steering under controlled conditions, escalate unsafe mechanical faults, and document the complete result.

That is successful troubleshooting.
