Drager Isolette C2000

Scale Will Not Zero or Weight Reading Is Unstable

Asset Type

Infant Incubator

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Isolette C2000

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting scale zeroing failure or unstable weight caused by positioning, contact, movement, accessories, cables, setup, or scale-system problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Infant and Avoid Using Unreliable Weight Data

If accurate weight is clinically important and the integrated scale is unstable or will not zero, do not rely on the displayed value.

Use another verified weighing method if necessary and troubleshoot the incubator scale without a patient.

Expected outcome: Clinical decisions are not based on unreliable weight information and the scale can be safely evaluated.

2. Confirm the Exact Scale Problem

Determine whether the scale refuses to zero, drifts after zeroing, displays changing values with no load change, or produces inconsistent repeated weights.

Record the conditions under which the problem occurs.

Expected outcome: The scale symptom is clearly defined and reproducible.

3. Verify the Incubator Is Stationary and Level

Ensure the incubator is parked on a stable surface and is not being moved, pushed, or subjected to vibration during weighing.

Check that casters are secure and that the unit is not rocking because of floor conditions.

Expected outcome: The incubator remains mechanically stable during the weighing process.

4. Remove Unintended Contact With the Weighing Platform

Inspect the mattress tray, scale platform, bed assembly, tubing, blankets, cables, and accessories for contact with the incubator enclosure or another fixed surface.

Items bridging between the weighing surface and a non-weighing structure can cause unstable or inaccurate readings.

Expected outcome: The weighing assembly is free from unintended mechanical contact.

5. Check Patient Cables and Tubing Routing

Without a patient, arrange any cables, hoses, or tubing that would normally be present so they do not pull on or support the weighing platform.

For testing, remove unnecessary items and compare scale stability.

Expected outcome: External tubing or cable tension is not influencing the weight reading.

6. Verify Correct Zeroing Conditions

With the intended mattress and standard setup in place but no patient load, perform the normal accessible zero function.

Avoid touching or leaning on the incubator while it zeros.

Expected outcome: The scale reaches and maintains a stable zero. If successful, repeat the test before proceeding.

7. Test With a Known Stable Load

Use an appropriate facility-approved test load or weight-check method suitable for functional verification.

Apply and remove the test load consistently while observing whether repeated readings remain stable.

Do not claim calibration based solely on an informal weight check.

Expected outcome: Repeated readings are stable and return appropriately after the load is removed. If so, proceed to final verification.

8. Inspect Accessible Scale Components

Inspect externally accessible scale surfaces, connectors, and mechanical interfaces for visible damage, contamination, binding, or looseness.

Do not disassemble load cells or internal weighing electronics.

Expected outcome: No visible mechanical interference or accessory damage remains.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Repeat zeroing and stable-load testing several times under controlled conditions. If calibration or accuracy verification is required by facility procedure, perform it using approved test equipment and appropriate manufacturer documentation.

Expected outcome: The scale zeros consistently and provides stable, repeatable readings. The device may proceed through required return-to-service testing.

10. Escalate Persistent Scale Problems

If the scale still drifts, cannot zero, or provides inconsistent readings after external causes are ruled out, stop troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The scale function is considered unreliable and the device remains out of service as required by facility policy.

If the Problem Persists

Mechanical interference, environmental movement, cable tension, setup, and zeroing conditions have been eliminated.

The remaining issue may involve the weighing mechanism, load sensing, signal processing, calibration, internal wiring, or another service-level condition.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service if the integrated scale is required for intended clinical use or cannot be reliably disabled according to policy.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Calibrated or repaired only by qualified personnel.
  • Verified for scale performance and all other affected incubator functions before return to service.

Knowing the difference between a functional weight check and an approved calibration is part of proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never document or act on an infant weight from an integrated scale that is drifting, unstable, or unable to zero reliably.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the Isolette C2000 scale would not remain at zero and the displayed weight changed continuously."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found monitor cables resting against the mattress platform and applying variable force to the weighing surface."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Rerouted the cables, successfully zeroed the scale, and verified stable repeatable readings with an approved test load before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Whether failure involved zeroing, drift, or unstable readings.
  • Incubator movement or floor condition.
  • Mattress and platform setup.
  • Cable and tubing routing.
  • Items contacting the weighing surface.
  • Results of zeroing attempts.
  • Test-load method used.
  • Repeatability before and after correction.
  • Whether calibration was required.
  • Final device status.

Final Thought

Protect against incorrect clinical weight data, eliminate movement and mechanical interference first, distinguish functional verification from calibration, and escalate unresolved scale instability with complete CCR documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
