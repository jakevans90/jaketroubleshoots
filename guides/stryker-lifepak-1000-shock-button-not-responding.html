Stryker LIFEPAK 1000

Shock Button Not Responding

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

LIFEPAK 1000

What This Guide Helps With

Helps troubleshoot shock button issues caused by control conditions, physical damage, or external operational factors.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Confirm Patient Safety and Equipment Availability

If the device is needed for emergency use, provide another verified defibrillator before troubleshooting. Expected outcome: Emergency care remains supported.

2. Confirm the Shock Button Complaint

Verify that the shock button does not respond when a shock is advised. Document whether the issue is intermittent or consistent. Expected outcome: Exact control problem is identified.

3. Inspect the Shock Button Area

Check for visible damage, contamination, sticking, or obstruction around the button. Expected outcome: External mechanical issues are identified or ruled out.

4. Verify Device State During Test

Confirm the device reaches the appropriate condition where shock delivery would normally be available during approved testing. Expected outcome: The control issue is evaluated under the correct operating condition.

5. Perform Approved Functional Verification

Use appropriate test equipment to verify shock control operation. Expected outcome: Shock control functions correctly or failure is confirmed.

6. Remove From Service if Failure Continues

If the shock button remains unreliable, remove the device from clinical use. Expected outcome: Unsafe equipment is prevented from use.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as physical obstruction, contamination, and operating conditions have been ruled out. The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete functional verification is required before return to service.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not rely on a defibrillator with uncertain shock control operation during emergency response.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the LIFEPAK 1000 shock button did not respond during operational use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found contamination around the shock button affecting mechanical operation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering cleaned the external control area, verified shock button operation with approved testing, and returned the device to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Button response observed
  • Physical condition
  • Cleaning performed
  • Functional test results
  • Final readiness status

Final Thought

Controls affecting emergency therapy require careful verification before return to service. Logical external troubleshooting and appropriate escalation protect patients and equipment reliability. That is successful troubleshooting.
