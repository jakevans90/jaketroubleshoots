Touchscreen, Rotary Knob, or Hard Key Is Unresponsive

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Evita XL

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot unresponsive operator controls caused by contamination, obstruction, power instability, external connections, localized control failure, or service-level interface faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient Before Testing Controls

If a touchscreen, rotary knob, or hard key cannot reliably control the ventilator, do not continue patient dependence on the affected interface.

Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator before troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The patient remains safely ventilated while the affected unit is evaluated.

2. Identify Exactly Which Controls Are Affected

Determine whether the problem involves the entire touchscreen, one region, the rotary control, one hard key, multiple keys, or the complete control panel.

Document whether the display itself continues updating normally.

Expected outcome: The failure is localized or confirmed as a broader interface problem.

3. Inspect for External Obstruction or Contamination

Check the touchscreen and controls for dried fluid, cleaning residue, adhesive film, protective coverings, foreign material, or physical damage that could interfere with operation.

Clean only using approved methods.

Expected outcome: Controls are clean, dry, unobstructed, and physically intact. If normal function returns and remains stable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Check for Mechanical Binding

Operate the rotary knob and accessible hard keys gently through their normal movement. Look for sticking, binding, missing tactile response, or damage.

Do not force a control that is mechanically jammed.

Expected outcome: Controls move and respond normally. A visibly damaged or mechanically failed control requires removal from service.

5. Verify Power and Control-Unit Connections

Confirm stable AC power and inspect accessible display/control connections for looseness or damage.

An unstable interface may result from a broader communication or power issue rather than the individual control itself.

Expected outcome: Power and external control-unit connections are normal. If reseating an accessible connection restores stable operation, complete full verification.

6. Perform a Controlled Restart

With the ventilator removed from patient use, shut it down normally and restart it. Observe whether the control interface initializes and responds normally.

Expected outcome: All controls initialize and respond consistently after startup. If the fault does not recur during extended testing, continue to final functional verification.

7. Test Every Relevant Control Function

Do not stop after confirming only the originally reported button. Test touchscreen selections, rotary input, confirmation actions, and accessible hard keys during controlled operation.

Expected outcome: Every required operator control responds correctly and consistently. If successful, troubleshooting can stop following final device testing.

8. Check for Intermittent Failure

Operate the device through normal setup and test-lung ventilation long enough to determine whether the interface freezes or loses input intermittently.

Do not return a ventilator with an unresolved intermittent control problem to clinical use.

Expected outcome: Control response remains stable throughout testing.

9. Escalate an Unresolved Interface Failure

If external contamination, mechanical obstruction, power, and accessible connections are normal but a control remains unresponsive, stop troubleshooting.

The issue may involve internal touchscreen, encoder, keypad, communication, or control electronics.

Expected outcome: The ventilator is removed from service for qualified repair.

If the Problem Persists

External contamination, physical obstruction, power, startup state, and accessible control-unit connections have been checked.

Remove the Evita XL from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation using appropriate Drager documentation. Internal interface electronics or control-panel repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Following repair, verify every operator control, display response, alarms, ventilation functions, and required return-to-service tests.

Clinical Use Tip

An intermittent control is as significant as a completely failed control because critical ventilator settings may not be reliably changed when needed.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported that the Evita XL rotary control intermittently failed to register selections."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found dried residue around the external rotary control causing mechanical binding."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The control area was cleaned using the approved method, all operator controls were verified through repeated testing, and the ventilator was returned to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Specific control affected
  • Display behavior during failure
  • Physical damage or contamination
  • Control-unit connection condition
  • Restart result
  • Whether failure was intermittent
  • Controls tested after correction
  • Test-lung verification result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient first, determine whether the problem is local or system-wide, eliminate contamination and connection problems before assuming electronics have failed, and never return an intermittently unreliable control to service.

That is successful troubleshooting.
