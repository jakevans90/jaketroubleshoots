Philips V60

Touchscreen, Navigation Ring, or Control Input Failure

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Philips

Model

V60

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting an unresponsive, inaccurate, intermittent, or locked touchscreen, navigation ring, or control input caused by screen lock, contamination, accessories, software state, or hardware failure.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot a control-input failure while the Philips V60 is actively supporting a patient.

  • Notify respiratory therapy and the clinical team that ventilator controls may be unavailable or unreliable.
  • Transfer the patient to another verified ventilator using the facility’s approved clinical procedure.
  • Provide manual ventilation support when clinically required and directed by qualified staff.
  • Confirm that the replacement ventilator is operating correctly before removing the affected V60.

Expected outcome: The patient is safely supported without relying on a ventilator that cannot be reliably controlled.

Continue Clinical Engineering troubleshooting only after the V60 has been removed from patient use.

2. Confirm the Exact Control Failure

Determine whether the problem affects:

  • The entire touchscreen
  • Only a specific area of the touchscreen
  • The navigation ring
  • The Accept button or another physical control
  • Intermittent input response
  • Incorrect selections or phantom touches
  • Controls that appear locked
  • Controls that stop responding after startup
  • Controls that work only after repeated pressing

Observe whether the display continues updating and whether alarms remain visible and audible.

Expected outcome: The failed control and the conditions under which it fails are clearly identified.

If the controls begin responding normally, continue with operational verification before returning the ventilator to service.

3. Check for Screen Lock

Verify that the touchscreen has not been intentionally locked. The V60 screen-lock function disables most touchscreen buttons and tabs until the screen is unlocked.

  • Look for the screen-lock indication.
  • Follow the normal on-screen procedure to unlock the controls.
  • Confirm that touchscreen selections now register normally.

Expected outcome: The touchscreen unlocks and responds consistently.

If screen lock caused the complaint and all controls pass functional testing, document the finding and stop.

4. Inspect the Touchscreen and Control Panel

Examine the touchscreen, navigation ring, buttons, and surrounding bezel for:

  • Liquid residue
  • Cleaning-solution buildup
  • Tape, labels, or protective film
  • Dirt or debris around the navigation ring
  • Cracks or impact damage
  • Depressed or stuck buttons
  • Loose control-panel trim
  • Signs of fluid intrusion
  • Unusual heat, odor, or discoloration

Do not introduce liquid into the touchscreen bezel, buttons, or navigation ring.

Expected outcome: The control surfaces are clean, dry, unobstructed, and free of obvious physical damage.

If impact damage, fluid intrusion, unusual heat, or burning odor is found, remove the ventilator from service and send it for bench evaluation.

5. Clean and Dry the External Controls

Using only facility-approved cleaning methods compatible with the V60:

  • Power the ventilator off.
  • Disconnect AC power.
  • Wipe the touchscreen and control surfaces with a properly dampened—not dripping—cloth.
  • Remove residue from the edges of the touchscreen and around the navigation ring.
  • Allow all surfaces to dry completely.

Do not spray cleaning solution directly onto the ventilator.

Reconnect AC power and restart the unit.

Expected outcome: Touch and physical-control response returns after contamination or moisture is removed.

If the problem is resolved, complete functional and performance verification before returning the device to service.

6. Remove External Accessories and Interference

Disconnect nonessential external accessories that could interfere with operation or apply pressure to the control panel, including:

  • USB devices
  • Communication cables
  • Nurse-call or remote-alarm connections
  • External mounting accessories contacting the bezel
  • Covers or screen protectors not approved for the device

Confirm that no equipment, cable, or mounting component is resting against the touchscreen or navigation ring.

Restart and retest the controls with the ventilator in a basic standalone configuration.

Expected outcome: The controls respond normally after the interfering accessory or connection is removed.

Reconnect accessories individually to identify whether the problem returns.

7. Perform a Controlled Power Cycle

With the ventilator removed from patient use:

  • Note any displayed error messages or diagnostic codes.
  • Shut the V60 down using the normal power-off procedure when possible.
  • Disconnect AC power.
  • Allow the unit to complete shutdown.
  • Reconnect it to a verified AC outlet.
  • Power the ventilator on and observe the entire startup sequence.

Do not repeatedly restart a ventilator that displays a serious fault or fails its startup checks.

Expected outcome: The V60 completes startup without errors and all control inputs respond normally.

If the control failure returns after restart, continue troubleshooting.

8. Compare Touchscreen and Navigation-Ring Operation

Test each control method separately.

For the touchscreen:

  • Open several menus.
  • Select controls in the center and near each edge of the screen.
  • Confirm that the selected item matches the area touched.
  • Check for delayed, repeated, or phantom inputs.

For the navigation ring and physical controls:

  • Rotate the navigation ring in both directions.
  • Confirm that the highlighted selection moves smoothly.
  • Press the Accept control and verify that selections are entered once.
  • Test other accessible control-panel buttons.

Expected outcome: Each touchscreen area and physical input produces the correct response without delay or repeated activation.

A localized dead area, inaccurate touch location, skipping navigation ring, or repeated input suggests a control-panel hardware problem.

9. Check Whether the Failure Is Menu-Specific

Determine whether the input failure occurs:

  • On the main monitoring screen
  • During mode selection
  • While changing settings
  • In alarm menus
  • During standby
  • In diagnostic or configuration screens
  • Only after the ventilator has operated for a period of time

A control that works in one screen but not another may indicate a software-state or configuration issue rather than a completely failed input device.

Expected outcome: The failure is confirmed as global, intermittent, or limited to a specific software screen.

Document any repeatable sequence that causes the controls to stop responding.

10. Review Displayed Errors and Event Information

Record any:

  • Technical alarms
  • Startup errors
  • Diagnostic codes
  • Communication errors
  • Unexpected reboots
  • Frozen screen behavior
  • Control-panel or display-related messages

Do not clear valuable event information before it has been documented.

Expected outcome: Available fault information is preserved for repair personnel and manufacturer support.

11. Perform Approved Operational Verification

After the controls appear functional:

  • Connect the ventilator to an appropriate test lung.
  • Select a suitable test mode.
  • Adjust settings using both the touchscreen and navigation ring.
  • Verify that each selected value is accepted correctly.
  • Confirm that ventilation begins and stops as commanded.
  • Test alarm acknowledgment and alarm-menu access.
  • Confirm that the screen does not freeze or generate phantom inputs.
  • Allow the ventilator to operate long enough to determine whether the problem returns.

Use the applicable service manual, facility procedure, and calibrated test equipment for formal performance testing. Philips identifies the V60 as a microprocessor-controlled positive-pressure ventilator and provides specific service and verification procedures for qualified personnel.

Expected outcome: All controls operate consistently during simulated ventilation and required verification tests pass.

If any input remains unreliable, do not return the ventilator to service.

If the Problem Persists

If screen lock, contamination, external accessories, mounting pressure, power state, and basic software conditions have been ruled out, the failure is likely associated with the touchscreen assembly, navigation ring, control-panel electronics, internal connection, or system software.

The ventilator should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for qualified bench evaluation or manufacturer-authorized repair
  • Accompanied by documented fault conditions, error messages, and repeatable test steps

Do not continue using the V60 because some controls still function. An intermittent or inaccurate control input can prevent timely setting changes, alarm management, or emergency intervention.

Knowing when to stop and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot a touchscreen or control-input problem while the V60 is supporting an active patient. Move the patient to another verified ventilator before restarting, disconnecting, cleaning, or testing the affected device.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory therapy reported that the Philips V60 touchscreen would not respond and the navigation ring intermittently skipped menu selections."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Screen lock and external contamination were ruled out; testing confirmed a repeatable touchscreen dead area and intermittent navigation-ring input."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the ventilator from service, labeled it Out of Service, documented the failed control areas, and sent the unit for control-panel bench evaluation and repair."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Patient safely transferred before troubleshooting
  • Screen-lock status
  • Touchscreen areas affected
  • Navigation-ring response
  • Accept-button response
  • Phantom or repeated inputs
  • Display freeze or reboot behavior
  • Startup or diagnostic messages
  • Evidence of liquid intrusion or impact damage
  • External accessories disconnected
  • AC outlet and power condition verified
  • Test-lung verification performed
  • Whether the problem changed as the unit warmed up
  • Final device status

Final Thought

A V60 control-input failure must be treated as a patient-safety concern. Logical troubleshooting begins with screen lock, contamination, obstructions, accessories, and software state before suspecting internal hardware. Reliable CCR documentation helps repair personnel reproduce the failure and prevents an unsafe ventilator from being returned to clinical use.

That is successful troubleshooting.
