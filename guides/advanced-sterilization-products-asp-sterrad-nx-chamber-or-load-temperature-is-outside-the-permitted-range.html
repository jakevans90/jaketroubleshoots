Chamber or Load Temperature Is Outside the Permitted Range

Asset Type

Sterilizer

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

STERRAD NX

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot temperature-related processing problems caused by load conditioning, room environment, recent instrument processing, placement, chamber condition, or temperature-control faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Sterility Assurance

Do not release a load from a canceled or invalidated cycle associated with an unacceptable temperature condition.

Move time-sensitive instrument processing to another validated system.

Expected outcome: Patient instruments are not released from a cycle with questionable processing conditions.

2. Record the Exact Temperature-Related Condition

Document:

  • Complete displayed message
  • Whether the issue concerns the chamber or load
  • When in the cycle it occurs
  • Instruments involved
  • Whether the condition repeats with every load

Do not assume a failed temperature sensor without first evaluating load and environment.

Expected outcome: The problem is clearly characterized.

3. Evaluate Recent Load History

Determine whether instruments were recently:

  • Washed
  • Heated
  • Removed from another processing system
  • Stored in a cold area
  • Transported between substantially different environments

Coordinate corrective handling with Sterile Processing.

Expected outcome: The load has been appropriately conditioned before processing.

4. Inspect for Moisture and Condensation

Check the load for:

  • Visible water
  • Condensation
  • Damp packaging
  • Moisture retained in channels or complex devices

Temperature and moisture problems may occur together.

Expected outcome: The load is visibly dry.

5. Verify Load Arrangement

Confirm trays and packages are positioned correctly and are not crowded, stacked improperly, or contacting the chamber in an unusual manner.

Expected outcome: The load is appropriately arranged.

6. Check the Chamber Condition

With the sterilizer idle and safe, inspect the accessible chamber for:

  • Moisture
  • Debris
  • Unusual residue
  • Foreign material

Expected outcome: The chamber is clean and dry.

7. Check the Room Environment

Determine whether HVAC failure, room-temperature changes, sterilizer relocation, or prolonged shutdown could have affected the equipment or loads.

Do not adjust manufacturer calibration or temperature settings as an external troubleshooting step.

Expected outcome: No facility environmental condition explains the problem.

8. Compare With a Properly Conditioned Known-Good Load

When permitted, process an approved test or controlled load that is dry and at normal room conditions.

Expected outcome: The controlled load processes successfully. If so, the original load condition was responsible and equipment troubleshooting can stop.

9. Verify Successful Processing

After correcting the external condition, complete the required cycle and quality verification.

Expected outcome: The cycle completes without temperature-related cancellation and all required records and indicators are acceptable.

10. Escalate Repeated Temperature Faults

If correctly prepared and conditioned loads continue to trigger temperature faults, remove the sterilizer from service.

Expected outcome: Qualified service evaluates temperature sensing, chamber conditioning, controls, or related systems.

If the Problem Persists

Load conditioning, moisture, arrangement, chamber condition, and room environment have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve temperature sensors, chamber-control functions, electrical components, software, or another service-level condition.

The sterilizer should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or service evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment
  • Calibrated, repaired, or configured only by qualified personnel

Complete manufacturer-appropriate post-service verification before return to use.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Allow unusual hot or cold loads to reach appropriate processing conditions rather than repeatedly restarting canceled sterilization cycles.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Sterile Processing reported repeated STERRAD NX load-temperature cancellations with instruments brought directly from another processing area."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the problem followed recently heated loads while a properly conditioned control load processed normally."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Coordinated appropriate load conditioning with Sterile Processing, verified a successful cycle with a properly prepared load, and returned the sterilizer to routine service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact temperature-related message
  • Chamber or load indication
  • Instrument source before processing
  • Load temperature history
  • Visible moisture
  • Load arrangement
  • Chamber condition
  • Room environmental changes
  • Controlled-load result
  • Final cycle result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Temperature-related cancellations should be investigated from the load and environment inward. Verify preparation and conditioning before assuming a sterilizer sensing or control failure.

That is successful troubleshooting.
