Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP) AEROFLEX

Water Filter, Water-Quality Test, or Final-Rinse Condition Fails

Asset Type

Endoscope Reprocessor (AER)

Manufacturer

Advanced Sterilization Products (ASP)

Model

AEROFLEX

What This Guide Helps With

Water-related processing failures involving external supply, filter condition, installation, water-quality verification, inadequate flow, or unacceptable final-rinse conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect Reprocessing Safety

Do not release endoscopes processed when required water-quality or final-rinse conditions are not verified. Use an alternate validated reprocessing workflow.

Expected outcome: No scope is returned to patient use after an uncertain rinse or water-quality condition.

2. Identify the Exact Water-Related Failure

Determine whether the issue involves a filter warning, failed water-quality test, poor fill performance, final-rinse failure, or repeated cycle abort associated with water supply.

Expected outcome: The water problem is narrowed to supply, filtration, testing, or processing.

3. Check Facility Water Availability

Verify the water supply to the AEROFLEX is available and that accessible shutoff valves are in their normal operating position. Ask whether plumbing or facility work recently occurred.

Expected outcome: Normal facility water is available to the unit. A closed valve or facility outage is corrected before further testing.

4. Inspect External Supply Hoses and Connections

Look for kinks, pinching, leaks, loose fittings, or obvious damage in accessible water-supply hoses.

Expected outcome: Water can reach the AER without external restriction or leakage. If correcting a hose issue restores operation, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

5. Inspect the Water Filter Installation

Check whether the approved filter is correctly installed and whether accessible filter housings or connections show leakage, improper seating, or obvious contamination.

Expected outcome: The filter system is correctly assembled and leak-free. Correct installation problems and repeat the required check.

6. Review Filter Status and Replacement History

Determine whether staff recently replaced a filter or whether the failure began immediately after filter service. Do not invent or override replacement intervals.

Expected outcome: A newly introduced installation issue is identified if present. If proper approved filter replacement resolves the condition, troubleshooting can stop.

7. Repeat the Approved Water-Quality Verification

Using the facility's approved test method and applicable manufacturer instructions, repeat the required water-quality check. Do not substitute unapproved test methods.

Expected outcome: Water quality meets the required pass condition. If it does not, investigate facility water or filtration rather than repeatedly processing scopes.

8. Compare With Facility Water Conditions

If appropriate, determine whether other equipment supplied by the same water source is showing similar problems or whether facilities has identified a supply-quality issue.

Expected outcome: A building-side water problem is separated from an AEROFLEX-specific problem.

9. Verify Final-Rinse Operation After Correction

Perform an approved operational test and confirm the AER fills, rinses, drains, and completes the required final-rinse stage normally.

Expected outcome: Final-rinse operation and required water checks pass. The device may return to service after all required verification is complete.

10. Escalate Persistent Water Failures

If supply, hoses, filter installation, and facility water have been ruled out, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The AEROFLEX is removed from service for qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes involving building water supply, hoses, filter installation, and external water-quality conditions have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal water valves, flow sensing, pressure sensing, plumbing, filtration monitoring, or control functions.

Remove the unit from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate ASP documentation and approved test equipment. Internal repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Return the AEROFLEX to service only after water-quality and final-rinse requirements are verified.

Clinical Use Tip

A completed cycle is not sufficient if the required rinse-water condition is uncertain; maintain reprocessing traceability and hold affected scopes.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported the AEROFLEX repeatedly failed the water-quality check after a filter change."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the replacement water filter was not fully seated in its housing."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Corrected the approved filter installation, repeated the required water check successfully, and verified normal final-rinse operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact water or filter message
  • Facility water status
  • Recent plumbing work
  • Hose condition
  • Filter replacement history
  • Filter housing condition
  • Water-quality test result
  • Other equipment affected
  • Final-rinse verification
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Water and filtration problems should be separated into building supply, external plumbing, filter installation, and internal AER functions. Verify the simple external causes first and do not bypass required water-quality checks.

That is successful troubleshooting.
