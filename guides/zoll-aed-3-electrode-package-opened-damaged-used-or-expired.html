ZOLL AED 3

Electrode Package Opened, Damaged, Used, or Expired

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

ZOLL

Model

AED 3

What This Guide Helps With

Identifying and correcting AED readiness problems caused by opened, damaged, previously used, disconnected, missing, or expired CPR Uni-padz electrode packages.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot electrode readiness while the ZOLL AED 3 is the only available defibrillator for an active emergency response.

If the electrode problem is discovered during a cardiac-arrest response:

  • Begin or continue CPR according to the facility’s emergency-response procedure.
  • Immediately obtain another verified AED or an unopened, compatible replacement electrode package.
  • Do not delay defibrillation while inspecting damaged or expired supplies.
  • Keep the questionable electrode package available for later evaluation and documentation when practical.

Expected outcome: Emergency care continues without depending on electrodes of uncertain condition.

Continue Clinical Engineering troubleshooting only after emergency coverage has been restored.

2. Confirm the Reported Electrode Condition

Determine whether the CPR Uni-padz package is:

  • Opened or partially unsealed
  • Punctured, torn, crushed, or wet
  • Previously applied to a patient
  • Contaminated with blood, fluids, dirt, or cleaning chemicals
  • Beyond the printed expiration date
  • Missing from the AED
  • Connected improperly
  • Displaying damaged wires, connectors, or insulation

Expected outcome: The specific reason the electrode set is unsuitable or questionable is identified.

ZOLL readiness guidance requires AED electrodes to be present, sealed in their package, and within their expiration date.

3. Do Not Attempt to Reuse or Reseal the Package

Do not tape, glue, heat-seal, or otherwise attempt to restore an opened electrode package.

Do not return previously applied electrodes to service, even when:

  • No shock was delivered
  • The pads appear clean
  • Adhesive remains present
  • The electrodes were applied only briefly
  • The package was opened accidentally

Expected outcome: A compromised single-use electrode set is permanently removed from service.

4. Verify Electrode Compatibility

Confirm that the replacement is approved for the ZOLL AED 3.

The standard ZOLL AED 3 uses CPR Uni-padz universal electrodes for adult and pediatric rescue. Pediatric treatment is selected using the AED’s Child Mode rather than installing a separate pediatric electrode set.

Check the replacement package for:

  • Correct ZOLL AED 3 compatibility
  • Correct connector configuration
  • Intact factory seal
  • Legible labeling
  • Acceptable expiration date
  • No punctures, moisture, crushing, or contamination

Expected outcome: A correct, unopened, in-date electrode package is available.

5. Inspect the Electrode Connector and Cable

Without opening the replacement package, inspect the visible cable and connector.

Check for:

  • Bent or damaged connector surfaces
  • Broken locking features
  • Cuts, pinching, or exposed conductors
  • Contamination inside the connector
  • Excessive pulling or strain near the package
  • Cable trapped under the AED cover or carrying case

Expected outcome: The electrode connection components are intact and undamaged.

If damage is found, do not install that electrode set. Obtain another replacement.

6. Inspect the AED Electrode Receptacle

Examine the AED electrode receptacle externally using adequate lighting.

Look for:

  • Foreign material
  • Bent or recessed contacts
  • Fluid contamination
  • Cracked surrounding plastic
  • Evidence of forced insertion
  • A connector fragment remaining inside the receptacle

Do not insert metal tools, probes, or cleaning fluids into the receptacle.

Expected outcome: The receptacle is visibly clean, dry, and mechanically intact.

If physical damage or contamination is present, remove the AED from service and send it for bench evaluation.

7. Install the Replacement Electrodes

Connect the unopened replacement CPR Uni-padz package according to the AED’s normal storage configuration.

Ensure that:

  • The connector is aligned correctly.
  • It is fully seated without excessive force.
  • The cable is routed without sharp bends or pinching.
  • The sealed package remains accessible for emergency removal.
  • The expiration date remains visible for routine inspection.

Expected outcome: The replacement electrodes are securely connected and stored without damaging the package or cable.

8. Verify the AED Readiness Indicator

After installing the replacement electrodes, confirm the AED’s status indicator.

The ZOLL AED 3 status indicator provides a quick indication of whether the device passed its most recent self-test and is ready for use.

Verify that:

  • The ready indication is displayed.
  • No warning tone is present.
  • No electrode-related warning remains.
  • The AED enclosure and installed supplies appear complete.

Allow sufficient time for the AED to recognize the replacement electrodes and complete any applicable readiness check.

Expected outcome: The AED returns to its normal ready condition.

If the ready indication returns, the electrode problem is resolved. Stop troubleshooting and return the AED according to facility policy.

9. Check the Replacement Electrode Set if the Warning Remains

If the AED does not return to ready status:

  • Reseat the connector once.
  • Confirm that the replacement package is compatible and in date.
  • Inspect the connector for shipping damage.
  • Try another known-good, unopened, compatible electrode package when available.
  • Verify that the battery and other AED supplies are also installed correctly.

Expected outcome: A defective replacement package or incomplete connection is ruled out.

If another verified electrode package corrects the condition, remove the first replacement set from stock and document it as defective.

10. Complete a Final Readiness Inspection

Before returning the AED to service, verify:

  • Green or normal ready status
  • No audible warning
  • Unopened and undamaged electrode package
  • Expiration date acceptable under facility policy
  • Electrode cable and connector properly stored
  • Battery installed and within its replacement interval
  • AED clean, complete, accessible, and free of visible damage

ZOLL describes the AED 3 as using long-life CPR Uni-padz electrodes and a smart battery designed to report readiness-related status.

Expected outcome: The complete AED system is ready for emergency deployment.

If the Problem Persists

If the AED continues to report an electrode or readiness problem after installation of a verified, unopened, compatible, in-date electrode package, common external causes have been ruled out.

The issue may involve the electrode receptacle, connector-detection circuit, self-test system, or another internal condition.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Replaced with another verified AED
  • Sent for repair or qualified bench evaluation

Do not repeatedly connect electrode packages or force the connector into the receptacle. Knowing when to stop and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never open an electrode package for routine inspection or testing. Maintain an unopened, compatible, in-date electrode set on every deployed AED and replace used or compromised supplies immediately after an event.

Do not troubleshoot the AED while it is being relied upon for an active patient. Move the patient or emergency response to a verified backup device first and maintain therapy continuity throughout the equipment exchange.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported that the ZOLL AED 3 electrode package was torn and the AED was not considered ready for emergency use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Inspection found the connected CPR Uni-padz package punctured near the cable entry, making the electrode seal and adhesive condition unreliable."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed and discarded the damaged electrode set, installed an unopened in-date CPR Uni-padz package, verified normal ready status, and returned the AED to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Electrode part number and lot number
  • Printed expiration date
  • Whether the package was opened, torn, wet, contaminated, or used
  • Whether the electrodes had been applied to a patient
  • Original AED readiness-indicator condition
  • Audible warning or displayed message
  • Connector and receptacle inspection results
  • Replacement electrode expiration date
  • Whether a second electrode package was tested
  • Final readiness-indicator condition
  • Final device status
  • Disposition of the damaged or expired electrodes

Final Thought

AED electrodes are essential components of the defibrillation system, not optional supplies. Patient safety depends on maintaining sealed, undamaged, compatible, and in-date electrodes, following a logical replacement process, and escalating persistent readiness failures appropriately. Complete CCR documentation also supports inventory control, post-event replenishment, and future troubleshooting.

That is successful troubleshooting.
