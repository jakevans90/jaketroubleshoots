Philips V680

Inspiratory Flow/Pressure Sensor Calibration Fails

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Philips

Model

V680

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots flow or pressure calibration failure caused by circuit setup, leaks, obstruction, accessories, environment, or service-level sensing problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Remove the Ventilator From Therapy

Do not perform sensor calibration or troubleshoot unreliable flow or pressure sensing while a patient depends on the ventilator.

Expected outcome: The patient is safely supported by another verified ventilator and calibration can be performed under controlled conditions.

2. Confirm the Exact Calibration Failure

Identify whether inspiratory flow, pressure, or another related verification step fails. Record any displayed message and note whether the failure began after circuit replacement, cleaning, maintenance, or accessory changes.

Expected outcome: The failed sensing function and circumstances are documented.

3. Inspect the Breathing Circuit

Verify tubing is correctly assembled and free from leaks, kinks, occlusions, standing water, loose fittings, or damaged connectors.

Expected outcome: The circuit is intact and properly assembled. Correct any external defect and repeat the calibration.

4. Inspect Filters and Other Flow-Path Accessories

Check filters, humidification components, adapters, valves, and other external components for restriction, contamination, incorrect orientation, or damage.

Expected outcome: The external flow path is unobstructed and configured correctly. If replacing a restricted component allows calibration to pass, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

5. Use a Known-Good Circuit Configuration

Install a known-good compatible circuit with approved accessories and repeat the calibration or preoperational check.

Expected outcome: Calibration passes with the known-good circuit. If so, the original circuit or accessory caused the problem.

6. Inspect Accessible Ventilator Ports

Examine inspiratory and expiratory external ports for debris, contamination, moisture, damaged sealing surfaces, or improperly attached adapters.

Expected outcome: External gas-path ports are clean, dry, and undamaged.

7. Verify Appropriate Environmental Conditions

Confirm the ventilator has stabilized in a suitable operating environment and is not exposed to unusual temperature, moisture, or airflow conditions that could affect testing.

Expected outcome: Calibration is attempted under stable, appropriate conditions.

8. Repeat the Approved Calibration or Preoperational Procedure

Follow the approved manufacturer procedure without entering unauthorized service menus or altering service calibration values.

Expected outcome: The calibration completes successfully and sensing remains stable during functional testing. If it passes consistently, complete final verification and stop troubleshooting.

9. Escalate Repeated Calibration Failure

If calibration continues to fail with a known-good circuit and verified external conditions, remove the ventilator from service.

Expected outcome: The ventilator is referred for qualified evaluation of its sensing, pneumatic, or calibration systems.

If the Problem Persists

External circuit leaks, restrictions, accessories, port contamination, and environmental conditions have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve internal flow or pressure sensing, pneumatic pathways, reference signals, calibration data, internal connections, or other service-level conditions.

The ventilator should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Philips documentation and approved test equipment
  • Calibrated or repaired only by qualified personnel

After correction, complete required calibration, ventilation-performance, alarm, leak, and functional checks before return to service.

Clinical Use Tip

A ventilator with unresolved flow or pressure calibration failure should not be used even if it appears to generate ventilation.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported the Philips V680 repeatedly failed inspiratory flow calibration during setup."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a restricted external breathing-circuit filter, and calibration passed after a known-good filter was installed."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The restricted filter was replaced and the ventilator passed calibration and final ventilation-performance testing."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact calibration step that failed
  • Displayed message
  • Circuit configuration
  • Filter condition
  • Humidifier configuration
  • Presence of water or contamination
  • Known-good circuit result
  • External port condition
  • Environmental condition
  • Final calibration result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Sensor calibration problems should be approached by eliminating circuit, accessory, leak, restriction, and environmental causes before internal sensing failure is suspected. A failed calibration requires resolution and verification before patient use.

That is successful troubleshooting.
