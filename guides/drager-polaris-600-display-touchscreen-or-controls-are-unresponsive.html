Drager Polaris 600

Display, Touchscreen, or Controls Are Unresponsive

Asset Type

Surgical Light

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Polaris 600

What This Guide Helps With

Controls do not respond, respond intermittently, or cannot reliably adjust the Polaris 600 surgical light.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Required Lighting

If control failure prevents reliable adjustment of lighting during a procedure, provide alternate illumination before troubleshooting. Do not manipulate unreliable controls while clinicians depend on the affected light.

Expected outcome: Surgical illumination remains adequate while the affected controls are evaluated safely.

2. Confirm Which Control Is Affected

Identify whether the problem involves a light-head control, wall-mounted control, touchscreen, control panel, or multiple control locations. Determine whether the light itself remains illuminated.

Expected outcome: The failure is narrowed to one control point or the broader control system.

3. Inspect the Control Surface

Check the affected control for contamination, fluid residue, damaged overlays, cracked surfaces, stuck buttons, or evidence of impact. Follow approved cleaning requirements if contamination may be interfering with operation.

Expected outcome: The control surface is clean, dry, intact, and mechanically free. If cleaning or releasing a stuck external control restores normal operation, verify function and stop troubleshooting.

4. Compare Other Available Controls

If the installation has more than one approved control location, determine whether another control can operate the same light head.

Expected outcome: A functioning alternate control helps determine whether the problem is localized to one interface or affects the light-control system more broadly.

5. Verify Power and Startup State

Confirm that the surgical light is fully powered and has completed its normal startup. A control problem associated with incomplete startup may indicate a broader system issue rather than a failed user interface.

Expected outcome: The system reaches its normal ready state and the control interface responds. If controls become normal after restoring power or normal startup, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

6. Inspect Accessible Connections

Inspect accessible external connections related to the affected control for looseness, damage, pinching, or contamination. Do not disassemble suspension assemblies or internal control electronics.

Expected outcome: Accessible connections are intact and secure.

7. Test the Full Control Range

Without a patient depending on the device, operate the affected controls through normal clinical adjustments and verify consistent response. Check that inputs do not stick, repeat unexpectedly, or stop responding.

Expected outcome: All intended control functions respond consistently. If so, troubleshooting is complete.

8. Escalate Unresolved Control Failure

If the interface remains unresponsive after basic power, cleanliness, and accessible connection checks, discontinue reliance on the affected control.

Expected outcome: The system is referred for qualified service rather than being returned with intermittent controls.

If the Problem Persists

External causes have been ruled out. The remaining problem may involve the control interface, internal communication, control electronics, software, or installation wiring.

The affected equipment should be:

  • Removed from service if reliable lighting adjustment cannot be assured
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or qualified on-site evaluation
  • Evaluated using applicable manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Verify all affected controls and lighting functions after repair before return to service.

Clinical Use Tip

An illuminated light is not fully functional if clinicians cannot reliably adjust it when surgical conditions change.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"OR staff reported that the Polaris 600 light-head controls intermittently failed to respond."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found dried contamination around the external control surface that interfered with consistent button operation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleaned the control surface using the approved method and verified consistent response through repeated lighting adjustments before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Specific control location affected
  • Light remained illuminated or not
  • Buttons or touchscreen functions affected
  • Contamination or physical damage
  • Alternate control tested
  • Power/startup condition
  • Accessible connections inspected
  • Intermittent versus constant failure
  • Final functional test result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Maintain safe illumination first, then isolate whether the problem is a single control or a larger system issue. Check simple external conditions before escalating internal communication or control faults, and document the verified result.

That is successful troubleshooting.
