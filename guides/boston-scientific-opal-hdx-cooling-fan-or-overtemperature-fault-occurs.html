Cooling, Fan, or Overtemperature Fault Occurs

Asset Type

Electrophysiology Mapping System

Manufacturer

Boston Scientific

Model

OPAL HDx

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots overheating indications, abnormal fan behavior, blocked ventilation, room conditions, equipment placement, and recurring thermal shutdowns before service escalation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Respond to Thermal Warnings

Do not continue relying on a mapping system that reports overheating, repeatedly shuts down, emits unusual odor, or shows signs of excessive heat.

Coordinate with the clinical team and transfer the procedure to verified equipment if continued mapping is required.

Expected outcome: The patient no longer depends on equipment with a potentially unsafe thermal condition.

2. Confirm the Thermal Complaint

Determine whether the system:

  • Displays an overtemperature message
  • Has unusually loud fan operation
  • Has no audible fan operation when normally expected
  • Becomes excessively warm externally
  • Shuts down after operating for a period
  • Produces unusual odor or noise

If smoke, burning odor, or visible damage is present, disconnect the device from service and do not continue troubleshooting while energized.

Expected outcome: The thermal symptom and affected component are clearly identified.

3. Inspect Ventilation Openings

Check accessible vents for:

  • Dust accumulation
  • Obstruction
  • Equipment pushed against walls or cabinetry
  • Drapes or supplies blocking airflow
  • Cables or objects covering ventilation areas

Do not open internal covers simply to clean internal fans.

Expected outcome: External ventilation openings are unobstructed. If correcting an airflow obstruction resolves the condition and testing is stable, troubleshooting can stop.

4. Check Equipment Placement

Verify adequate external clearance around system components according to the installed environment and applicable manufacturer guidance.

Look for recently relocated equipment, added carts, or items placed against ventilated surfaces.

Expected outcome: Equipment placement permits normal external airflow.

5. Check the Room Environment

Determine whether the room is unusually warm or whether HVAC performance has changed.

Compare conditions with nearby equipment and ask whether other devices have reported temperature problems.

Expected outcome: A room-level environmental problem is identified or ruled out.

6. Inspect for Excessive Dust Externally

Examine accessible vents and filters only where they are intended for routine external service.

Clean or replace serviceable external items only according to approved procedures.

Expected outcome: Accessible airflow surfaces are reasonably clean and unobstructed.

7. Observe Fan and Thermal Behavior

With the device out of patient use, operate it under normal conditions and observe whether the reported thermal condition returns.

Listen for:

  • Grinding
  • Rattling
  • Fan surging
  • Fan stopping unexpectedly

Do not insert tools into fan openings.

Expected outcome: Cooling behavior remains stable without recurring thermal messages or abnormal noise.

8. Verify Normal Operation After Cooling

If the system was allowed to return to a normal temperature, restart it using the approved sequence and perform an operational check.

Do not repeatedly restart a system that quickly overheats.

Expected outcome: The system starts and remains stable without recurring thermal symptoms. Troubleshooting can stop if the corrected external cause is clear.

9. Escalate Recurring Thermal Faults

If overheating, abnormal fan behavior, thermal shutdown, or excessive heat recurs after external airflow and room conditions are verified, remove the system from service.

Do not perform internal fan, power-supply, or thermal-control repairs without authorized service procedures.

Expected outcome: The system is prevented from further clinical use until qualified evaluation is completed.

If the Problem Persists

Common external ventilation, placement, dust, and room-environment causes have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve internal cooling hardware, power components, thermal sensing, internal contamination, or another service-level fault.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After repair, verify stable operation without thermal warnings or abnormal fan behavior before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Treat repeated thermal shutdowns, burning odor, or excessive external heat as reasons to remove the system from use rather than repeatedly restarting it.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the OPAL HDx displayed an overtemperature condition after operating in the procedure room."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found supplies positioned against an external ventilation opening and restricting airflow."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering cleared the obstruction, allowed the system to normalize, and verified stable operation without recurrence of the thermal condition."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact thermal message
  • Component affected
  • Fan sound or behavior
  • External temperature observations
  • Vent condition
  • Equipment placement
  • Room temperature concern
  • Dust accumulation
  • Runtime before fault occurs
  • Final operational status

Final Thought

Thermal faults should be approached from the outside inward: protect the patient, inspect ventilation and room conditions, verify repeatability, and stop before internal cooling repair. Return the system only after stable operation has been demonstrated and documented.

That is successful troubleshooting.
