Hillrom Progressa

Brake Not Set Warning or Brake Sensor Failure

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Manufacturer

Hillrom

Model

Progressa

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting persistent brake warnings, incorrect brake-status detection, pedal engagement problems, caster movement, and externally verifiable brake-system faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not troubleshoot a questionable brake system while the bed is occupied or being used for patient transfer.

  • Notify the responsible clinical staff.
  • Transfer the patient to another verified bed when practical.
  • If immediate transfer is unsafe, keep staff with the patient and prevent unintended bed movement.
  • Do not use the bed for patient entry, exit, repositioning, or lateral transfer until brake stability is confirmed.

Hillrom instructs that the brakes should be set whenever the bed is occupied, except during transport, and that the bed should be pushed and pulled after brake engagement to verify stability.

Expected outcome: The patient is protected from falls or injury caused by unexpected bed movement.

2. Confirm the Reported Condition

Determine whether:

  • The brake warning appears only when the brake is intentionally released.
  • The warning remains after the brake pedal is fully engaged.
  • The warning is intermittent.
  • The brake indicator changes incorrectly.
  • The bed continues to roll with the brake set.
  • The brake pedal feels loose, obstructed, unusually stiff, or fails to remain in position.

When the Progressa is connected to AC power, releasing the brake normally produces an audible and visual Brake Not Set Alert. The alert should stop when the brakes are set or the bed is unplugged for transport.

Expected outcome: Normal warning operation is separated from an actual brake or sensing failure.

3. Verify the Bed Is Stationary and Properly Positioned

Place the bed on a clean, level floor with adequate clearance around all casters.

Check for:

  • Power cords or cables beneath the bed.
  • Linens, straps, tubing, or debris caught around the casters.
  • Uneven flooring or thresholds.
  • Nearby equipment contacting the bed frame.
  • A caster resting against an obstruction.

Remove any external obstruction before retesting.

Expected outcome: Nothing outside the bed interferes with brake pedal movement or caster locking.

4. Fully Cycle the Brake Pedal

With the bed unoccupied:

  • Release the brake completely.
  • Move the bed slightly to allow the casters to align.
  • Press the brake pedal firmly into the brake position.
  • Confirm the pedal reaches and remains at its normal mechanical stop.
  • Observe whether the audible and visual warning clears.

Do not force a pedal that is binding or visibly damaged.

Expected outcome: The pedal engages normally and the Brake Not Set warning clears.

If the warning clears and repeated operation is consistent, perform the functional checks below before returning the bed to service.

5. Test Mechanical Brake Holding

With the brake fully set, push and pull the bed from both the head and foot ends.

Check whether:

  • The bed remains stationary.
  • Any individual caster rotates or rolls.
  • The bed pivots excessively.
  • The brake pedal releases unexpectedly.
  • Grinding, clicking, or abnormal movement is present.

Expected outcome: The bed remains stable without unintended rolling.

If the bed moves with the brake set, stop testing and remove it from service. A warning that clears does not make the bed safe if the mechanical brakes fail to hold.

6. Inspect the Brake and Steer Pedals Externally

Inspect accessible pedal assemblies and surrounding areas for:

  • Cracks or broken pedal surfaces.
  • Bent or loose linkage components.
  • Missing hardware.
  • Impact damage.
  • Fluid residue or contamination.
  • Debris restricting pedal travel.
  • A pedal that does not remain in the selected position.

Do not remove covers or attempt internal linkage adjustment unless trained, authorized, and following the current Hillrom service documentation.

Expected outcome: No visible damage or obstruction is found.

If damage is present, remove the bed from service and arrange repair.

7. Check AC Power and Warning Behavior

Connect the bed directly to a known-good grounded AC outlet.

Confirm:

  • The bed indicates that AC power is present.
  • The warning activates when the brake is intentionally released.
  • The warning clears when the brake is reapplied.
  • The display and audible alert respond consistently during several brake cycles.

Do not use the disappearance of the warning after unplugging the bed as proof that the brake sensor is operating correctly. Unplugging the bed normally silences the transport-related Brake Not Set Alert.

Expected outcome: The warning accurately follows the physical brake position while the bed is connected to AC power.

8. Inspect Accessible Brake-Sensing Areas

Without disassembly, inspect around the pedal and lower frame for:

  • Loose or visibly damaged external wiring.
  • Pinched cables.
  • Disconnected accessible connectors.
  • Fluid intrusion.
  • Corrosion.
  • Evidence of impact near the brake pedal or caster area.
  • Excessive movement in the pedal assembly.

If fluid has entered the bed electronics, unplug the bed, remove it from service, and arrange a complete maintenance inspection before reuse.

Expected outcome: No external evidence of sensor, wiring, or contamination damage is found.

9. Perform a Controlled Functional Retest

Cycle the brake between released and set positions several times.

For each cycle, verify:

  • The pedal reaches the correct position.
  • The brake indicator changes correctly.
  • The audible and visual warning activates when released.
  • The warning clears when set.
  • The casters hold securely when the brake is engaged.
  • The bed rolls normally when the brake is released.

Expected outcome: Mechanical braking and electronic brake-status detection operate reliably during every cycle.

If the problem was corrected by removing an obstruction or fully reseating the brake pedal, document the finding and stop troubleshooting.

10. Determine Whether the Failure Is Mechanical or Sensor-Related

The issue is likely mechanical when:

  • The bed rolls with the brake set.
  • A pedal or linkage is damaged.
  • The pedal will not remain engaged.
  • One or more casters do not lock.
  • Abnormal mechanical sounds or movement are present.

The issue is likely related to the brake-position sensing circuit when:

  • The brakes hold securely.
  • The pedal operates normally.
  • The Brake Not Set warning remains active or changes intermittently.
  • The display reports the wrong brake position.

Do not bypass, defeat, or electrically simulate the brake sensor to keep the bed in service.

Expected outcome: The failure category is identified sufficiently for safe escalation and work order documentation.

If the Problem Persists

If the warning remains after the brake is fully engaged, the indication is intermittent, or the bed does not remain stationary, common external causes have been ruled out.

The problem may involve the brake-position sensor, pedal linkage, caster brake mechanism, associated wiring, control electronics, or adjustment requiring authorized service procedures.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Unplugged when appropriate.
  • Sent for bench evaluation or authorized repair.
  • Tested for brake holding and correct warning operation after repair.

Hillrom provides dedicated technical training and service documentation for Progressa troubleshooting and repair. Internal adjustments and component replacement should be performed only by appropriately trained personnel using the correct service information.

Knowing when to stop and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot a brake complaint during an active patient transfer or while a patient is entering or exiting the bed. Move the patient to a verified bed first whenever clinically safe.

A brake warning that appears incorrect must not be dismissed without physically confirming that the bed remains stationary. Always push and pull the bed after setting the brakes.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the Hillrom Progressa continued to display and sound a Brake Not Set warning after the brake pedal was engaged."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Brake pedal and casters engaged mechanically, but the brake-status indication remained active during repeated testing, indicating a probable brake-position sensor or associated wiring failure."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the bed from service, applied an Out of Service label, and routed it for authorized brake sensor and linkage evaluation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Patient removed or transferred safely
  • Exact warning message recorded
  • Audible warning confirmed
  • Visual indicator behavior documented
  • Known-good outlet used
  • Brake pedal cycled
  • Pedal and linkage inspected externally
  • Caster area checked for debris
  • Push-pull brake holding test completed
  • Individual caster movement noted
  • Visible cable or connector damage noted
  • Fluid exposure, unusual heat, or odor noted
  • Final device status documented
  • Out of Service label applied when required

Final Thought

Brake troubleshooting must verify both the electronic indication and the bed’s physical ability to remain stationary. Protect the patient first, rule out external interference, test the brake logically, and escalate unreliable mechanical or sensor operation. Clear CCR documentation ensures the next technician understands the complaint, findings, and final disposition.

That is successful troubleshooting.
