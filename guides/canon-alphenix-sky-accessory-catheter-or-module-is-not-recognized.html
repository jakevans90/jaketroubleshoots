Accessory, Catheter, or Module Is Not Recognized

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot compatible accessories, catheter-related interfaces, or modules that are connected but unavailable, intermittently recognized, or reported as disconnected.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Procedure

Do not troubleshoot an accessory or module while a patient depends on its function. If recognition is lost during a procedure, establish a safe clinical alternative before manipulating connections.

Expected outcome: The patient is not dependent on the affected accessory or module.

2. Identify the Exact Unrecognized Component

Determine the accessory, catheter-related interface, module, or connected device involved and record the exact system indication.

Expected outcome: The affected component and recognition failure are clearly identified.

3. Verify Compatibility and Intended Use

Confirm that the connected component is approved and appropriate for the intended system function. Do not force connectors or use unapproved adapters to obtain recognition.

Expected outcome: The component is suitable for use with the system and current function.

4. Inspect the Component and Connector

Inspect accessible connector surfaces, housings, cables, strain reliefs, latches, and contacts for contamination, physical damage, bent components, fluid exposure, or wear.

Expected outcome: No visible defect prevents reliable connection. Damaged components are removed from service.

5. Reseat the Connection

Reconnect the accessory or module using its normal approved connection and confirm that locking or retaining features fully engage.

Expected outcome: The component becomes recognized and remains connected. If recognition is stable, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

6. Verify Required External Power

If the accessory or module has an independent approved power source, confirm that it is powered and indicates normal operation.

Expected outcome: The connected component is not unavailable because of an external power problem.

7. Substitute a Known-Good Compatible Component

When available, connect a known-good compatible accessory, cable, interface, or module.

Expected outcome: Recognition of the known-good component isolates the original accessory or module as the likely cause.

8. Test the Component Through Normal Positioning

Verify recognition while the system, cable, accessory, or table is placed through normal expected positions without stressing the connection.

Expected outcome: Recognition remains stable throughout normal operation.

9. Escalate Persistent Recognition Failure

If known-good compatible components are not recognized or recognition repeatedly drops out, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The affected interface remains out of clinical service pending qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Accessory compatibility, physical condition, seating, external power, and known-good substitution have been checked. Remaining possibilities include an internal interface, communication channel, configuration issue, software compatibility problem, or another service-level fault.

Remove the affected system or function from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for qualified repair or service evaluation. Use manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment. Do not attempt internal connector-board repair or unauthorized configuration changes.

Return to service only after compatible components are repeatedly and reliably recognized. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not reconnect or exchange catheter-related interfaces during a procedure until the clinical team confirms that doing so will not interrupt required monitoring or care.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that an accessory module connected to the Canon Alphenix Sky+ was not recognized."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found contamination on the accessible external connector and confirmed normal recognition after approved cleaning and reconnection."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering cleaned and reseated the connection, verified stable module recognition through normal positioning, and returned the function to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Accessory or module involved
  • Exact recognition message
  • Compatibility confirmed
  • Connector and cable condition
  • External power status
  • Result after reseating
  • Known-good substitution result
  • Recognition during movement
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the procedure, verify compatibility and the external connection first, use known-good substitution when practical, and escalate persistent recognition faults instead of assuming an internal component failure.

That is successful troubleshooting.
