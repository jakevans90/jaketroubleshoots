Width Expansion Will Not Extend, Retract, or Indicate Position

Asset Type

Hospital Bed

Hillrom

Model

Compella

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot width expansion that will not move, moves unevenly, or reports an incorrect position due to obstructions, controls, power, or positioning.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety and Bed Stability

Confirm that the patient is safely supported before troubleshooting the width expansion system.

  • Transfer the patient to another appropriate bed if the malfunction compromises support, clearance, or safe operation.
  • Engage the brakes and confirm the bed is stable.
  • Do not operate expansion components against the patient, bedding, or surrounding equipment.
  • Never force the width expansion mechanism.

Expected outcome: The bed is stable, the patient is protected, and expansion can be evaluated without creating a pinch or fall hazard.

2. Confirm the Reported Expansion Problem

Determine whether the problem involves extension, retraction, uneven movement, or incorrect position indication.

  • Ask clinical staff when the malfunction began.
  • Identify whether one side or both sides are affected.
  • Observe any displayed message or position indicator.
  • Determine whether the mechanism stopped during movement or failed to respond initially.
  • Record whether the problem is continuous or intermittent.

Expected outcome: The exact malfunction is identified and can be reproduced safely without a patient on the bed.

3. Verify Electrical Power

Inspect the bed's external power connections.

  • Confirm that the power cord is fully connected.
  • Inspect the cord and plug for damage.
  • Verify the electrical outlet using approved test equipment.
  • Observe available power and battery indicators.
  • Confirm whether other powered bed functions operate normally.

If the entire bed is unresponsive, investigate the external power supply before focusing on the expansion mechanism.

Expected outcome: Reliable power is available and the bed responds to normal controls.

If restoring power resolves the expansion problem, continue to final functional verification.

4. Inspect for Mechanical Obstructions

Examine the accessible width expansion areas.

  • Look for trapped linens, straps, tubing, or accessories.
  • Check for contact with walls, furniture, or nearby equipment.
  • Inspect accessible expansion sections for visible bending, damage, or misalignment.
  • Confirm that the mattress and accessories are positioned correctly.
  • Remove external obstructions without forcing any component.

Expected outcome: Expansion components have adequate clearance and no visible obstruction prevents movement.

If removing an obstruction restores normal movement, verify the full permitted operating range before stopping.

5. Check Expansion Controls and Lockouts

Inspect the available expansion controls.

  • Confirm that the correct control is being operated.
  • Check applicable lockout indicators.
  • Inspect buttons for physical damage, sticking, or contamination.
  • Compare response from other authorized control locations, if equipped.
  • Do not bypass a lockout or protective interlock.

Expected outcome: The controls respond normally and no unintended lockout prevents operation.

6. Compare Both Expansion Sides

With the bed unoccupied, operate the expansion system according to the approved procedure.

  • Observe whether both sides move.
  • Watch for hesitation, binding, or uneven travel.
  • Listen for unusual mechanical noise.
  • Stop immediately if a section twists, binds, or contacts another component.
  • Do not repeatedly cycle a stalled mechanism.

Expected outcome: Expansion sections move smoothly and maintain their intended alignment.

Uneven movement or mechanical binding requires removal from service.

7. Verify Position Indication

Compare the displayed expansion status with the actual bed position.

  • Observe available width or position indicators.
  • Confirm that the physical position corresponds to the indicated state.
  • Check for intermittent indication during movement.
  • Record any discrepancy between physical movement and displayed status.
  • Do not adjust position sensors or internal calibration without approved service instructions.

Expected outcome: The position indication accurately represents the actual expansion state.

8. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correcting an external issue:

  • Verify extension and retraction through the manufacturer-permitted range.
  • Confirm both sides move consistently.
  • Verify position indication.
  • Check for unusual noise, interference, or binding.
  • Confirm that the mattress and siderails remain correctly positioned.
  • Complete applicable manufacturer return-to-service checks.

Expected outcome: Width expansion operates reliably, indicates position correctly, and passes required safety checks.

Troubleshooting can stop when all functions pass and no unresolved safety concerns remain.

If the Problem Persists

If power, controls, clearance, positioning, and accessible mechanical conditions have been verified, common external causes have been ruled out.

The problem may involve an expansion drive, position-sensing system, control circuitry, wiring, or mechanical alignment requiring service-level evaluation.

The bed should be:

  • Removed from service and labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation by qualified personnel.
  • Evaluated using appropriate Hillrom service documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired, adjusted, or configured only by authorized qualified personnel.
  • Returned to service only after successful expansion, position indication, and applicable safety verification.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is an important part of safe Clinical Engineering practice.

Clinical Use Tip

Confirm that the mattress and patient-support configuration remain compatible with the selected bed width before returning the Compella to clinical use.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Nursing reported that the Compella bed width expansion would not fully retract."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found a bed linen trapped in the accessible expansion path, preventing complete retraction."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the obstruction and verified smooth extension, retraction, accurate position indication, and normal bed operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Expansion direction affected
  • One side or both sides
  • Position indicator status
  • AC power condition
  • Lockout status
  • Visible obstructions
  • Mechanical binding or unusual noise
  • Actual versus indicated width position
  • Functional test results
  • Final equipment status

Final Thought

Patient safety comes first when troubleshooting bariatric width expansion. Verify power, controls, positioning, and external obstructions before suspecting an internal failure. Stop when operation is verified, escalate unresolved mechanical or indication faults, and document the findings clearly.

That is successful troubleshooting.
