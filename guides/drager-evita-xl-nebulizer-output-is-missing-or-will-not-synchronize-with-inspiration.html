Nebulizer Output Is Missing or Will Not Synchronize With Inspiration

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Evita XL

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot absent or mistimed nebulizer output caused by setup, tubing, nebulizer condition, control settings, circuit placement, or service-level pneumatic faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ordered Therapy

If required aerosol medication cannot be delivered reliably, notify the clinical team and provide an approved alternative method of medication delivery before troubleshooting.

Do not compromise ventilation while evaluating nebulizer operation.

Expected outcome: The patient's ventilation and ordered medication therapy remain safely supported by an alternate verified method when necessary.

2. Confirm the Exact Nebulizer Failure

Determine whether there is no output, weak output, continuous output, or output that does not occur at the expected point in the respiratory cycle.

Reproduce the condition on a test lung rather than a patient.

Expected outcome: The nebulizer problem is clearly characterized and repeatable.

3. Inspect the Nebulizer Assembly

Check the external nebulizer cup or compatible aerosol device, tubing, fittings, and connections for damage, blockage, incorrect assembly, leakage, or contamination.

Confirm that the accessory is appropriate for the setup being tested.

Expected outcome: The nebulizer assembly is intact, patent, correctly assembled, and securely connected. If correcting assembly restores proper output, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

4. Verify Tubing and Circuit Placement

Inspect nebulizer drive tubing and the nebulizer's position in the breathing circuit. Check for kinks, disconnected tubing, trapped condensate, or an unintended connection point.

Expected outcome: The nebulizer pathway and circuit placement are correct and unobstructed. If output returns, complete synchronization verification and stop.

5. Verify Accessible Nebulizer Controls

Confirm that nebulizer operation has been enabled as intended using normal operator controls and that the ventilator is in a state where the requested function should operate.

Do not alter protected service configuration.

Expected outcome: The appropriate nebulizer function is selected and the device indicates activation as expected.

6. Test With a Known-Good Nebulizer Assembly

Substitute a known-good compatible nebulizer and tubing when available.

This distinguishes an accessory problem from a ventilator output problem.

Expected outcome: Proper operation with the known-good assembly identifies the original external nebulizer or tubing as defective. Replace it and stop troubleshooting after verification.

7. Observe Synchronization on a Test Lung

Operate the ventilator with a test lung and observe whether nebulizer output occurs consistently relative to inspiration as intended for the configured mode.

Use appropriate approved testing methods rather than relying only on sound or subjective mist appearance.

Expected outcome: Nebulizer output is present and appropriately coordinated during controlled ventilation. If successful, troubleshooting can stop.

8. Escalate Persistent Output or Synchronization Failure

If the external nebulizer, tubing, circuit placement, and controls are correct but output remains absent or incorrectly timed, stop external troubleshooting.

Possible causes include internal pneumatic control, output valves, timing signals, or associated control electronics.

Expected outcome: The ventilator is removed from service if the nebulizer function is required or if the unresolved fault suggests broader pneumatic malfunction.

If the Problem Persists

External nebulizer components, tubing, circuit positioning, user controls, and known-good substitutions have been ruled out.

Remove the ventilator from service when the nebulizer function is required for intended clinical use or when a broader pneumatic fault cannot be excluded. Label it Out of Service and send it for qualified bench evaluation using appropriate Drager documentation and approved test equipment.

Following repair, verify nebulizer operation, synchronization, ventilation performance, alarms, and all required return-to-service functions.

Clinical Use Tip

Maintain medication delivery by an approved alternate method whenever nebulizer operation cannot be verified without interrupting ventilation.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported that the Evita XL indicated nebulizer activation but no aerosol output was observed."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the external nebulizer drive tubing was kinked and restricting flow."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"The tubing was repositioned and replaced, nebulizer output and inspiratory synchronization were verified on a test lung, and the ventilator was returned to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • No output, weak output, or timing complaint
  • Nebulizer type used
  • Tubing and fitting condition
  • Circuit position
  • Control indication during activation
  • Known-good nebulizer substitution
  • Synchronization result
  • Ventilator mode during testing
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect ventilation and medication continuity first, inspect the complete external nebulizer pathway before assuming an internal pneumatic fault, verify synchronization objectively, and escalate unresolved output problems.

That is successful troubleshooting.
