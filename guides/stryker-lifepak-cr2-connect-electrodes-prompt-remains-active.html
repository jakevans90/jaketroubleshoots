Stryker LIFEPAK CR2

Connect Electrodes Prompt Remains Active

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

LIFEPAK CR2

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshooting a persistent Connect Electrodes prompt caused by pad application, electrode condition, patient contact, packaging, or likely internal detection faults.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Ensure Patient Safety First

Do not perform extended troubleshooting during an active resuscitation.

If the LIFEPAK CR2 continues prompting to connect electrodes despite correct pad application, immediately provide another verified defibrillator/AED and continue care according to clinical protocol.

Expected: Patient care is not delayed by an AED that cannot reliably detect applied electrodes.

Why it matters: The LIFEPAK CR2 uses audible and visual guidance during rescue operation, and unresolved electrode detection can prevent normal rhythm-analysis and therapy progression.

If another device resolves the immediate clinical concern, stop troubleshooting on the patient and evaluate the affected CR2 in a controlled Clinical Engineering setting.

2. Confirm the Exact Failure Behavior

Reproduce or document the complaint without unnecessarily consuming a serviceable electrode set.

Determine whether:

  • The prompt begins normally before pad placement but never clears
  • The prompt is intermittent
  • One or both pads were visibly loose
  • The electrodes were previously opened or used
  • The electrode package or tray was damaged
  • The issue occurred only during one rescue event
  • The unit shows additional abnormal prompts, indicators, or tones

Expected: The complaint is narrowed to a persistent electrode-contact problem versus a broader device malfunction.

If the reported prompt cleared after correcting an obvious application issue and the device subsequently performs normally, document the finding and stop.

3. Verify the Correct Electrode Type

Confirm that the installed electrodes are the correct LIFEPAK CR2 QUIK-STEP electrodes and are approved for the device.

Stryker identifies the CR2 electrode set as QUIK-STEP pacing/ECG/defibrillation electrodes; Stryker’s current CR2 product information lists one set with the device.

Check for:

  • Incorrect LIFEPAK-family electrodes
  • Trainer electrodes
  • Noncompatible substitutes
  • Improvised adapters or connections

Expected: Genuine, correct CR2-compatible electrodes are present.

If incorrect electrodes caused the issue, replace them with the proper approved set, restore the device according to facility policy, document the correction, and stop.

4. Inspect Electrode Condition and Expiration

Inspect the electrode assembly externally.

Check for:

  • Expired electrodes
  • Previously opened or deployed pads
  • Torn packaging
  • Damaged lead conductors
  • Contamination
  • Dried or compromised adhesive/gel
  • Visible physical damage

Stryker cautions against using damaged, expired, or dried-out pads and notes that CR2 QUIK-STEP electrodes should not be opened until immediately before use.

Stryker currently lists the CR2 QUIK-STEP replacement set with a four-year shelf life.

Expected: Electrodes are within expiration, unopened before use, physically intact, and suitable for deployment.

If the electrode set is expired, opened, damaged, or otherwise questionable, replace it with an approved in-date set. If that resolves the complaint, document and stop.

5. Review Pad Application and Full Skin Contact

For the actual reported event, verify whether both pads were applied in the illustrated locations and were fully adhered to the patient.

Look for possible causes such as:

  • Pad only partially contacting skin
  • Folded adhesive surface
  • Clothing trapped beneath a pad
  • Excessive chest hair preventing adhesion
  • Moisture interfering with contact
  • Pad placed over another material
  • Pads touching or overlapping

Stryker specifically instructs that pads should adhere completely to skin and should not touch each other or other material on the patient’s chest.

Expected: Both pads had broad, direct, secure patient contact.

If correcting pad contact clears the prompt, the issue was external. Stop and document the cause.

6. Check for Patient-Specific Contact Factors

Review whether the failure may have been unique to the patient rather than the CR2.

Consider:

  • Very wet skin
  • Heavy chest hair
  • Significant movement
  • Poor adhesion due to body contour
  • Extremely small patient anatomy
  • Pads becoming displaced during CPR or transport

For small children where standard placement risks pad contact or overlap, Stryker describes anterior-posterior placement guidance.

Expected: The event history either identifies a plausible patient-contact problem or supports a true equipment complaint.

If evidence strongly indicates poor contact during the event and no device abnormality remains, document the finding and return the device only after the required readiness checks are satisfactory.

7. Inspect the Electrode Tray and Accessible Interface Area

With the unit removed from clinical use, inspect the CR2 electrode storage area and all externally accessible portions of the electrode assembly.

Check for:

  • Electrode tray not properly seated
  • Cover or packaging displaced
  • Pinched lead conductors
  • Visible contamination
  • Foreign material
  • Cracked or damaged accessible components
  • Evidence of impact or fluid intrusion

Do not open the defibrillator housing or perform unauthorized internal disassembly.

Expected: The electrode assembly is correctly installed and no external mechanical damage is visible.

If reseating an approved replaceable electrode assembly according to facility-authorized practice corrects an obvious installation issue, complete the required post-service verification, document, and stop.

8. Evaluate With a Known-Good, Approved Electrode Set When Justified

If the original electrodes are suspect, replace them with a known-good, in-date CR2 QUIK-STEP electrode set using approved replacement practice.

Do not repeatedly deploy consumable pads solely for exploratory testing. Follow facility policy and manufacturer-authorized test methods.

Stryker identifies CR2 QUIK-STEP electrodes as the dedicated replacement consumable for the device.

Expected: A defective or compromised electrode assembly is either confirmed or ruled out.

If replacing a suspect electrode set restores normal operation, document the failed consumable and stop.

9. Check for Broader Device Readiness Abnormalities

Inspect the CR2 readiness indication and note any additional alert behavior.

Determine whether the device also shows:

  • Abnormal readiness status
  • Repeating alert tones
  • Unexpected startup behavior
  • Additional voice prompts
  • Evidence of battery or self-test concern

Stryker’s CR2 operating information describes the readiness indicator as part of routine readiness assessment and instructs checking prompt behavior during inspection.

Expected: No unrelated readiness or startup abnormalities are present.

If multiple abnormalities exist, treat the complaint as a broader device fault rather than an isolated electrode issue.

10. Perform Only Facility-Authorized Functional Verification

After external causes are ruled out, perform the CR2 functional or performance verification available to Clinical Engineering under your facility’s approved procedures and authorized Stryker service resources.

Do not improvise patient simulations or defeat safety features.

Expected: The unit either recognizes the approved test condition normally or the electrode-detection complaint remains reproducible.

If the Connect Electrodes prompt remains active despite correct approved electrodes and verified external conditions, stop troubleshooting and escalate.

If the Problem Persists

If correct CR2-compatible electrodes, expiration, pad condition, application, patient contact, external assembly condition, and basic readiness checks have been ruled out, the problem may involve an internal electrode-detection, signal-acquisition, connection, or other device fault.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or qualified bench evaluation

Do not proceed into unauthorized internal disassembly or board-level troubleshooting. Stryker’s safety information directs repair concerns to authorized service personnel.

Knowing when external causes have been exhausted and when to stop is proper Clinical Engineering troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot a persistent Connect Electrodes prompt while the affected CR2 remains the only available defibrillator for an active patient.

Move care immediately to another verified defibrillator/AED, then investigate the CR2 off patient. During a real rescue, dependable electrode recognition takes priority over determining whether the cause is the pads, application, or device.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported the LIFEPAK CR2 repeatedly announced “Connect Electrodes” after the pads were applied during use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Inspection found the installed QUIK-STEP electrode set was expired and showed compromised pad adhesion, preventing reliable patient contact."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the expired electrode set, installed an approved in-date LIFEPAK CR2 QUIK-STEP set, verified satisfactory device readiness, and returned the AED to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact voice prompt confirmed
  • Prompt continuous or intermittent
  • Correct CR2 QUIK-STEP electrodes verified
  • Electrode expiration checked
  • Packaging condition inspected
  • Pads previously opened or deployed
  • Pad adhesion/contact issue reported
  • Patient skin condition reviewed
  • Electrode tray inspected
  • Visible lead damage noted
  • Abnormal readiness indicator present
  • Alert tones documented
  • Known-good electrode set evaluated
  • Impact or fluid exposure noted
  • Final device status documented

Final Thought

A persistent Connect Electrodes prompt should be approached logically: protect the patient first, verify the correct electrodes, inspect consumable condition, review pad contact, and rule out external causes before suspecting internal failure. When reliable electrode detection cannot be confirmed, remove the CR2 from service and escalate appropriately. Clear CCR documentation preserves both the technical findings and the patient-safety decision.

That is successful troubleshooting.
