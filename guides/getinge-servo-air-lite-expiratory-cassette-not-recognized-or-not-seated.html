Getinge Servo-air Lite

Expiratory Cassette Not Recognized or Not Seated

Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Getinge

Model

Servo-air Lite

What This Guide Helps With

Addresses cassette recognition or seating problems caused by incomplete insertion, contamination, moisture, damage, incompatibility, or obstruction at the cassette interface.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient

Do not remove or reseat the expiratory cassette while a patient depends on the ventilator. Transfer ventilation to another verified device or appropriate alternate method first. Expected outcome: Patient ventilation is safely maintained away from the affected unit.

2. Confirm the Cassette Symptom

Verify whether the cassette is physically difficult to seat, appears seated but is not recognized, or causes a pre-use check failure. Note the displayed condition. Expected outcome: The specific recognition or seating problem is confirmed.

3. Remove and Inspect the Cassette

Inspect the cassette for cracks, warping, contamination, fluid, damaged interfaces, or other visible defects. Do not disassemble it beyond approved handling. Expected outcome: The cassette is physically suitable for use. Any visibly damaged cassette is removed from service.

4. Inspect the Receptacle

Inspect the external cassette receiving area for contamination, debris, fluid, or an obstruction. Do not probe or adjust internal contacts. Expected outcome: The receptacle is clean and unobstructed.

5. Reinstall the Cassette

Align and install the cassette normally without unusual force. Expected outcome: The cassette seats completely and is recognized. If recognition returns, proceed to the complete pre-use check.

6. Confirm Compatibility and Proper Processing

Verify the cassette is approved for the Servo-air Lite and has been cleaned, dried, and prepared according to applicable requirements. Expected outcome: A correct, properly prepared cassette is installed.

7. Substitute a Known-Good Cassette

Use a known-good compatible cassette. Expected outcome: Recognition with the substitute identifies the original cassette as the cause. If neither cassette is recognized, escalate after confirming the receptacle is unobstructed.

8. Complete the Pre-Use Check

Run the full required pre-use check. Expected outcome: The cassette remains recognized and all checks pass. Troubleshooting can stop.

9. Verify Normal Ventilation

Operate the ventilator with an appropriate test load and verify normal expiratory function and alarm behavior. Expected outcome: Ventilation is stable and no cassette-related fault recurs.

10. Escalate Persistent Recognition Failure

If known-good cassettes are not recognized or do not seat normally, stop external troubleshooting. Expected outcome: The unit remains out of service for qualified evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External cassette condition, seating, compatibility, and receptacle obstruction have been ruled out. The remaining issue may involve the cassette detection interface, internal sensing, alignment, electronics, or other service-level hardware. Remove the ventilator from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for bench evaluation using appropriate Getinge documentation and approved test equipment. Complete pre-use, alarm, and ventilation testing following repair. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never force an expiratory cassette into the Servo-air Lite; abnormal resistance is a reason to stop and inspect the cassette and receptacle.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Respiratory Therapy reported that the Servo-air Lite would not recognize the installed expiratory cassette."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the cassette was not completely seated after reprocessing."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering removed and correctly reinstalled the cassette, confirmed recognition, completed the pre-use check successfully, and returned the ventilator to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Displayed cassette message
  • Cassette physical condition
  • Moisture or contamination
  • Receptacle condition
  • Compatibility confirmed
  • Known-good cassette result
  • Pre-use check result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Inspect and verify the cassette and its external interface before assuming internal failure, and remove the ventilator from service when recognition cannot be restored reliably. That is successful troubleshooting.
