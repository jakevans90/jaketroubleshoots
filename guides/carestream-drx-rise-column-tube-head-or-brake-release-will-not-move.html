Carestream DRX-Rise

Column, Tube Head, or Brake Release Will Not Move

Asset Type

Mobile X-Ray System

Manufacturer

Carestream

Model

DRX-Rise

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot restricted column or tube movement, failed brake release, mechanical obstruction, positioning interference, and external control problems.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient From Unintended Equipment Movement

Do not troubleshoot positioning controls while the tube head, column, or detector could strike a patient, staff member, bed, or accessory. Move the patient away from the equipment when appropriate. Expected outcome: Equipment can be evaluated without risk of collision or unintended movement.

2. Confirm Which Motion Is Affected

Determine whether the problem involves vertical column movement, tube-head positioning, rotational movement, brake release, or multiple axes. Note whether the mechanism is completely locked or only intermittently releases. Expected outcome: The affected motion and operating condition are clearly identified.

3. Inspect for Physical Obstruction

Check around the column, tube head, handles, cables, storage areas, bed, wall, ceiling, detector holders, and accessories for anything physically blocking movement. Expected outcome: No external object or cable prevents travel. If an obstruction is removed and motion returns normally, verify operation and stop.

4. Verify Normal System Power and Operating State

Confirm the DRX-Rise is powered normally and is not displaying a condition that intentionally prevents movement. Check that the system is not in an emergency-stop or other inhibited state. Expected outcome: System is powered and available for normal positioning.

5. Check Brake-Release Controls

Operate the appropriate accessible release controls while observing whether the brake releases consistently. Check for stuck, damaged, contaminated, or mechanically binding buttons or handles. Expected outcome: The commanded brake releases and re-engages normally. If an external control was obstructed or stuck and correction restores operation, stop after verification.

6. Inspect Accessible Cables and Control Areas

Look for pinched, stretched, loose, or visibly damaged external wiring near handles, articulating areas, and accessible control interfaces. Do not disassemble the column or tube-head mechanism. Expected outcome: No visible external cable damage or loose accessible connection is present.

7. Check Equipment Position and Mechanical Loading

Return the system to a neutral, unloaded position where practical and retest movement. Determine whether the failure occurs only near a particular travel position or when external accessories contact the system. Expected outcome: Motion remains smooth and controllable throughout normal accessible travel.

8. Compare Multiple Release Controls When Available

If the same motion can be released from more than one normal operator control, compare their operation. This can help distinguish a single control problem from a broader brake or motion problem. Expected outcome: All normal controls provide consistent response, or the failed control path is isolated.

9. Perform Final Positioning Verification

Cycle the affected motion several times without a patient. Confirm smooth movement, predictable brake release, secure holding when released, and no unexpected drift or binding. Expected outcome: Motion and braking are reliable. If so, troubleshooting can stop.

10. Escalate Unsafe or Persistent Motion Problems

If a brake does not release reliably, movement binds, or the tube/column does not hold position securely, remove the unit from clinical service. Expected outcome: Equipment with unreliable positioning or braking is not used clinically.

If the Problem Persists

External obstruction, accessible controls, system state, and basic positioning conditions have been ruled out. Remaining causes may involve the brake mechanism, motion-control hardware, wiring, sensors, mechanical assemblies, or service-level configuration.

Remove the DRX-Rise from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use appropriate Carestream documentation and approved service equipment. Internal mechanical adjustment or repair should be performed only by qualified personnel.

Before return to service, verify full required range of motion, brake release, holding force, stability, and safe positioning behavior.

Knowing when to stop before deep mechanical disassembly is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Never position a patient beneath a tube head or column assembly that does not reliably hold its commanded position.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Radiology reported that the tube head would not release for positioning."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found an accessory cable caught against the brake-release handle and preventing full control travel."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Repositioned and secured the cable, then verified repeated brake release, normal tube positioning, and secure holding throughout the accessible range."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact motion affected
  • Whether brake releases intermittently
  • System operating state
  • Obstructions found
  • Control or handle condition
  • Accessible cable condition
  • Position where failure occurs
  • Presence of drift or binding
  • Results after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Protect the patient from uncontrolled motion, eliminate simple mechanical and control causes first, verify safe positioning before assuming internal failure, and escalate any unreliable brake or holding condition.

That is successful troubleshooting.
