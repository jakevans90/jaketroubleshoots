Drager Polaris 600

Accessory or Module Is Not Recognized

Asset Type

Surgical Light

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Polaris 600

What This Guide Helps With

An installed compatible accessory or system module is unavailable, not detected, or intermittently recognized by the Polaris 600 installation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Determine Clinical Impact

If the unrecognized accessory or module is required for the planned procedure, provide an approved alternate solution before troubleshooting. Do not repeatedly disconnect equipment during active clinical use.

Expected outcome: Patient care can continue independently of the failed accessory.

2. Identify the Exact Accessory or Module

Record the specific accessory, module, mounting location, and function involved. Confirm whether it was previously working with this same installation.

Expected outcome: Troubleshooting is directed at the correct component rather than the surgical light generally.

3. Verify Compatibility

Confirm through approved documentation or local equipment records that the accessory is intended for the specific Polaris 600 configuration. Do not force incompatible connectors or modify configuration to make an unsupported component work.

Expected outcome: The accessory is confirmed as appropriate for the installation.

4. Inspect Mechanical Installation

Check that the accessory or module is fully seated, correctly positioned, latched where applicable, and not obstructed by damaged hardware, debris, or contamination.

Expected outcome: The accessory is physically installed as intended. If proper seating restores recognition, verify operation and stop troubleshooting.

5. Inspect Accessible Connections

Examine accessible cables, plugs, contacts, and connection points for looseness, bent or damaged parts, contamination, or strain.

Expected outcome: Connections are secure and visibly intact. If correcting an external connection restores recognition, perform functional verification and stop.

6. Power-Cycle Only When Clinically Safe

If permitted and no patient depends on the system, perform a normal power restart so the installation can reinitialize connected components.

Expected outcome: The accessory is detected during normal startup. If recognition remains stable, troubleshooting is complete.

7. Compare With a Known-Good Compatible Component

When an approved identical or known-compatible accessory is available, substitute it to determine whether the problem follows the accessory or remains with the same system connection.

Expected outcome: The comparison isolates the problem to the accessory or the host installation without internal disassembly.

8. Escalate Persistent Recognition Problems

If compatibility, installation, connections, and known-good comparison have been checked and recognition remains unreliable, stop external troubleshooting.

Expected outcome: The affected component is removed from use and qualified service evaluation is initiated.

If the Problem Persists

Likely remaining categories include accessory electronics, host communication, configuration, connector assemblies, installation wiring, or internal control hardware.

The affected equipment should be:

  • Removed from service if the unavailable module is required for safe clinical use
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or qualified evaluation
  • Evaluated using manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

After service, verify recognition and the complete clinical function of the accessory before return to service.

Clinical Use Tip

Confirm the accessory's complete function, not merely that the system detects its presence.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"OR staff reported that an installed Polaris 600 accessory was intermittently unavailable at the associated control interface."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the accessory's accessible external connection was not fully seated."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Reseated and secured the connection and verified repeated recognition and normal accessory operation before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Accessory or module identification
  • Installation location
  • Whether previously functional
  • Compatibility confirmed
  • Mechanical seating condition
  • Cable and connector condition
  • Known-good substitution result
  • Recognition before and after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Verify compatibility, installation, connections, and known-good comparisons before assuming a host-system failure. Protect the clinical workflow, stop before internal repair, and document the verified cause and resolution.

That is successful troubleshooting.
