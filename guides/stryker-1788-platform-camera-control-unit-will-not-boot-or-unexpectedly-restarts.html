Stryker 1788 Platform

Camera Control Unit Will Not Boot or Unexpectedly Restarts

Asset Type

Endoscopic / OR Camera System

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

1788 Platform

What This Guide Helps With

Startup or restart problems caused by AC power, cords, connections, accessories, ventilation, environment, or a service-level control-unit problem.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Replace the Visualization System

If the camera control unit fails to boot or restarts during a procedure, notify the clinical team immediately and transition to a verified backup visualization system.

Do not troubleshoot an unstable camera control unit while the surgical team depends on it.

Expected outcome: The procedure has a reliable visualization method before troubleshooting begins.

2. Confirm the Exact Power Symptom

Determine whether the camera control unit:

  • Shows no power
  • Begins startup but does not complete
  • Freezes during startup
  • Repeatedly reboots
  • Restarts only when specific accessories are connected
  • Restarts after operating for a period of time

Document any displayed message without interpreting undocumented codes.

Expected outcome: The startup or restart pattern is clearly established.

3. Inspect the Unit for Immediate Safety Concerns

Check for:

  • Liquid intrusion
  • Burn odor
  • Smoke
  • Unusual heat
  • Damaged enclosure
  • Damaged power inlet
  • Abnormal noise

If any of these are present, disconnect the unit from AC power and remove it from service.

Expected outcome: No immediate electrical or physical hazard is present.

4. Verify the AC Power Source

Confirm the outlet is functioning using an appropriate approved method. If permitted by facility policy, test the unit from another verified power source.

Do not assume an illuminated power strip or wall outlet is providing reliable power without verification.

Expected outcome: The control unit receives stable AC power.

5. Inspect the Power Cord and Connections

Inspect the power cord for:

  • Cuts or crushing
  • Bent or damaged plug blades
  • Loose equipment connection
  • Damaged strain relief
  • Heat discoloration

Verify the cord is fully seated at both ends.

Use a known-good approved replacement cord when appropriate.

Expected outcome: The control unit powers normally with a verified cord. If the original cord caused the problem, replace it and stop troubleshooting after final verification.

6. Disconnect Nonessential External Accessories

With the system removed from clinical use, disconnect nonessential external accessories and communication connections, leaving only those needed for a basic startup test.

Then power the unit normally.

Expected outcome: The camera control unit completes startup. If startup becomes reliable only after a particular external component is removed, isolate that component before reconnecting the system clinically.

7. Reconnect Accessories One at a Time

Reconnect approved accessories individually while observing system stability.

Include the camera head, light-source communication, external recording equipment, video routing, or other connected devices as applicable.

Expected outcome: The unit remains stable, or the restart becomes associated with one external connection or accessory.

8. Check Ventilation and Environment

Verify that ventilation openings are unobstructed and the unit is not tightly enclosed, covered, positioned against another heat-producing device, or exposed to obvious excessive contamination.

Do not open the enclosure to inspect internal fans unless authorized manufacturer service procedures specifically permit it.

Expected outcome: The control unit operates in a suitable environment with unobstructed external ventilation.

9. Observe a Controlled Startup and Operating Test

Power the system on from a verified supply with the necessary components attached.

Allow startup to complete and operate the camera system through normal bench functions while observing for:

  • Restart
  • Freeze
  • Loss of video
  • Loss of accessory recognition
  • Abnormal heat or noise

Expected outcome: The system remains continuously stable. If it does, proceed to final verification.

10. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify:

  • Normal startup
  • Camera-head recognition
  • Stable video
  • Connected light-source communication when applicable
  • Normal controls
  • No unexpected restart during operation
  • Stable operation through the intended clinical configuration

If required by facility policy following a power-related repair or cord replacement, complete the applicable electrical safety checks before return to service.

Expected outcome: The system starts and remains stable through functional verification. Troubleshooting can stop and the equipment may be returned to service.

If the Problem Persists

If AC power, power cord, external accessories, connections, ventilation, and operating environment have been verified and the camera control unit still fails to boot or unexpectedly restarts, common external causes have been ruled out.

The remaining cause may involve an internal power, thermal, software, storage, processing, or other service-level condition.

The camera control unit should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Stryker documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired, updated, or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not perform board-level power-supply troubleshooting or deep disassembly without authorized service procedures. Following repair, perform complete functional and applicable electrical safety verification before return to clinical use.

Knowing when an intermittently restarting device is not reliable enough for clinical use is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

An OR camera control unit that has unexpectedly restarted once during active use should be treated as unreliable until the cause is identified or stability is adequately verified.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"OR staff reported that the Stryker 1788 camera control unit unexpectedly restarted several minutes after startup."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the unit restarted when powered through a damaged external power cord but remained stable with a verified replacement cord and power source."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the damaged power cord and verified normal startup, camera operation, video output, and extended stable operation before returning the system to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • No-power, failed-startup, freeze, or restart symptom
  • Exact displayed message
  • Outlet verification result
  • Power-cord condition
  • Known-good cord result
  • Accessories connected when restart occurred
  • Behavior with nonessential accessories removed
  • Ventilation condition
  • Unusual heat, odor, or sound
  • Final stability and safety-test results

Final Thought

Start with the power source and external connections before assuming an internal control-unit failure. A system that cannot remain reliably powered should be removed from clinical use, escalated appropriately, fully verified after correction, and clearly documented.

That is successful troubleshooting.
