GE Healthcare Giraffe OmniBed Carestation

Battery Not Charging or Short Transport Runtime

Asset Type

Infant Incubator

Manufacturer

GE Healthcare

Model

Giraffe OmniBed Carestation

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots poor battery charging or transport runtime caused by AC supply, connection, charging conditions, battery condition, or excessive load.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient During Battery Evaluation

If dependable battery operation is needed for transport or continuity of thermal support, do not test a questionable battery while an infant depends on the OmniBed.

Use another verified device or an approved transport plan before removing AC power.

Expected outcome: Battery troubleshooting can proceed without risking interruption of patient support.

2. Confirm the Exact Battery Complaint

Determine whether the reported problem is:

  • No charging indication
  • Battery remains low despite AC connection
  • Very short runtime
  • Shutdown immediately after unplugging
  • Battery problem only during transport
  • Charging behavior that is intermittent

Record the indicated battery condition before testing.

Expected outcome: The battery symptom and operating conditions are clearly established.

3. Verify AC Power and Charging Conditions

Confirm the OmniBed is connected to a verified AC receptacle and that the power cord and plug are intact and fully seated.

Observe the unit's normal power or charging indications. A battery cannot be evaluated fairly until reliable AC input has been confirmed.

Expected outcome: Stable AC input is present and the unit recognizes AC operation.

4. Allow an Appropriate Charging Opportunity

If the OmniBed has recently been stored, transported, or repeatedly operated away from AC, leave it connected to approved AC power according to facility and manufacturer guidance before judging battery condition.

Do not assume battery failure solely from a low indication immediately after extended battery use.

Expected outcome: The battery receives a reasonable charging opportunity and its indicated condition improves appropriately.

5. Inspect for External Signs of Battery-System Problems

Inspect accessible areas for damage, overheating, unusual odor, fluid contamination, or signs that the unit was stored in conditions that could affect battery performance.

Do not open the battery compartment or replace internal battery components unless authorized and following applicable service documentation.

Expected outcome: No external safety condition requires immediate removal from service.

6. Compare AC and Battery Operation

With no patient depending on the device, establish stable operation on AC and then perform the facility-approved battery operational check.

Observe whether the OmniBed:

  • Transfers to battery without restarting
  • Maintains display and control operation
  • Produces expected power-source indications
  • Remains operational for the required test period

Expected outcome: The unit transitions normally to battery operation and remains stable. If normal runtime is demonstrated, troubleshooting can stop after documentation.

7. Consider Load and Recent Use

Determine whether the short-runtime report occurred while multiple powered functions were active. A heavily loaded unit may expose a weak battery sooner than a stationary unit connected to AC.

Do not disable required safety functions merely to extend battery time.

Expected outcome: The reported runtime is evaluated under a realistic and controlled condition rather than assumed to represent a charger fault.

8. Perform Approved Battery Testing or Replacement

If authorized for the facility, evaluate the battery using the applicable GE Healthcare service procedure and approved test equipment. Replace the battery only when supported by testing or applicable maintenance criteria.

After replacement, complete required charging and functional verification.

Expected outcome: Battery capacity and charging performance meet the applicable service requirements. Troubleshooting can stop.

If the Problem Persists

If AC power, charging opportunity, external connections, operating load, and battery condition have been evaluated and the problem remains, a charging circuit, power-management system, wiring, or other internal service-level problem may be present.

The OmniBed should be:

  • Removed from service when reliable battery operation is required
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using current GE Healthcare documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired only by qualified personnel

Complete required battery, power-transition, electrical-safety, and functional testing before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Verify battery readiness before transport; do not discover a weak battery after the infant has already been disconnected from AC power.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"NICU staff reported that the OmniBed battery discharged quickly during transport."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering verified normal AC charging but found the battery failed the approved operational capacity check."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Replaced the battery using the approved service procedure, verified charging and battery operation, and completed functional testing before return to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Battery indication when received
  • AC power verification
  • Charging indication
  • Length of recent battery use
  • Whether shutdown occurred immediately after unplugging
  • Device functions active during reported transport
  • Battery operational test result
  • Battery replacement performed
  • AC-to-battery transition result
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Confirm reliable AC input and realistic charging conditions before condemning the battery. Evaluate battery performance with the patient protected, use approved testing when needed, and escalate charging-system problems that cannot be safely isolated externally.

That is successful troubleshooting.
