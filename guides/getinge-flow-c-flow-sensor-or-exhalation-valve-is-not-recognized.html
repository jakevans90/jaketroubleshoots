Getinge Flow-c

Flow Sensor or Exhalation Valve Is Not Recognized

Asset Type

Anesthesia Machine

Manufacturer

Getinge

Model

Flow-c

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoots recognition problems caused by incorrect installation, poor connection, contamination, incompatible components, physical damage, or breathing-system assembly.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Maintain Ventilation

Do not troubleshoot an unrecognized flow sensor, exhalation component, or related breathing-system part while a patient depends on the affected machine. Use another verified anesthesia and ventilation system first.

Expected outcome: Patient care is safely separated from equipment troubleshooting.

2. Confirm the Exact Recognition Problem

Record the displayed message and determine whether the problem appeared at startup, after cleaning, after circuit replacement, after component reinstallation, or during use. Identify the specific removable component involved without assuming it has failed.

Expected outcome: The recognition failure is reproducible and associated with a defined component or setup condition.

3. Inspect Component Installation

Check that the flow-sensing or exhalation-related component is installed in the correct location and orientation and is fully seated or locked as intended. Do not force connectors or components.

Expected outcome: The component is properly installed. If reseating corrects recognition and the machine passes its checks, stop troubleshooting after final verification.

4. Inspect Accessible Connections

Examine external electrical, pneumatic, or mechanical interfaces associated with the component for looseness, contamination, bent contacts, damaged seals, or obvious physical damage. Do not probe or modify internal contacts.

Expected outcome: Accessible interfaces are clean, intact, and correctly connected.

5. Check for Moisture or Contamination

Inspect removable sensor and breathing-system areas for moisture, residue, debris, or cleaning-related contamination. Follow approved cleaning and drying requirements; do not apply unapproved cleaners or compressed air.

Expected outcome: The component and its interface are clean and appropriately dry before retesting.

6. Verify the Correct Approved Component Is Installed

Confirm that the installed sensor, valve-related part, breathing-system assembly, or disposable component is appropriate for the machine and its current configuration.

Expected outcome: Component mismatch is excluded as the cause of the recognition fault.

7. Substitute a Known-Good Approved Component When Appropriate

If an approved equivalent is available, substitute a known-good removable component to determine whether recognition follows the accessory or remains with the machine.

Expected outcome: A successful known-good substitution isolates the external component; continued failure points toward the machine or its interface.

8. Complete Startup and Functional Verification

After correction, complete required pre-use checks and verify ventilation, flow measurement, pressure behavior, alarms, and breathing-system operation.

Expected outcome: The component is consistently recognized and the Flow-c passes required functional checks.

If the Problem Persists

If installation, orientation, connections, contamination, component compatibility, and known-good substitution have been ruled out, the issue may involve the machine's internal sensing interface, valve control, electronics, pneumatic system, or another service-level condition.

Remove the Flow-c from service, label it Out of Service, and send it for repair or bench evaluation. Use current manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment. Do not perform board-level repair, internal valve disassembly, or undocumented configuration changes.

Return the machine to service only after the affected component is reliably recognized and ventilation and alarm functions pass applicable testing. Proper troubleshooting includes stopping before unsafe internal repair begins.

Clinical Use Tip

A component that is intermittently recognized is not acceptable for patient use even if reseating temporarily clears the message without repeat verification.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"OR staff reported the Getinge Flow-c did not recognize the breathing-system flow sensor after room turnover."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the removable sensor assembly not fully seated after reinstallation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering correctly reseated the assembly, completed the required system checks, and verified stable flow measurement and ventilation operation."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact recognition message
  • When the problem began
  • Component orientation and seating
  • Connector or contact condition
  • Moisture or contamination observed
  • Cleaning or reassembly history
  • Component identification
  • Known-good substitution result
  • Functional-test result after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Recognition faults should first be treated as installation, connection, contamination, or compatibility problems rather than internal failures. Verify with controlled substitution when appropriate, complete functional testing, and escalate persistent faults with clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
