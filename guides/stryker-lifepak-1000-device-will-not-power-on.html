Stryker LIFEPAK 1000

Device Will Not Power On

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Stryker

Model

LIFEPAK 1000

What This Guide Helps With

Helps troubleshoot a LIFEPAK 1000 that will not start due to external power, battery, connection, or environmental causes.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Confirm Patient Safety and Equipment Availability

If the device is needed for an emergency or patient care, provide a verified backup defibrillator before removing the LIFEPAK 1000 from service for troubleshooting. Do not troubleshoot while relying on this device for active patient support. Expected outcome: Patient care is maintained with a ready device available.

2. Confirm the Reported No-Power Condition

Verify the exact symptom reported by staff. Attempt to power on the device and note whether there is no response, intermittent startup, display activity, audible indicators, or any readiness indication. Expected outcome: The reported power failure condition is confirmed and documented.

3. Inspect Battery Installation and Condition

Remove and reinstall the battery according to approved handling procedures. Inspect the battery compartment for obvious damage, contamination, or improper seating. Expected outcome: Battery is properly installed and the device powers on if the issue was caused by battery seating.

4. Verify Battery Condition Using a Known-Good Battery

Install a verified compatible battery if available and attempt startup. This helps separate a depleted or failed battery from a device issue. Expected outcome: Device powers on with a known-good battery, indicating the original battery requires replacement or evaluation.

5. Inspect External Condition and Environment

Check for signs of physical damage, liquid exposure, extreme temperatures, or storage conditions that could affect operation. Expected outcome: External environmental causes are ruled out.

6. Perform Basic Functional Verification

If the device powers on, verify normal startup behavior and readiness status before returning it to service. Expected outcome: Device completes startup and indicates it is ready for use. Troubleshooting can stop when normal operation is restored.

7. Escalate When Power Failure Remains Unresolved

If the LIFEPAK 1000 remains unresponsive after external checks, remove it from clinical use and proceed with service evaluation. Expected outcome: Device is safely removed from service for further evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

Common external causes such as battery installation, battery condition, and environmental factors have been ruled out. The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate manufacturer documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel

Do not assume an internal failure until external causes have been eliminated. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting. After repair, perform appropriate defibrillator functional verification and return-to-service testing before clinical use.

Clinical Use Tip

Maintain immediate access to another verified defibrillator before troubleshooting a device that may be needed for emergency response.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Clinical staff reported that the LIFEPAK 1000 would not power on when attempting to perform readiness checks."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the battery was not properly seated and was not making reliable contact."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering reseated the battery, verified normal startup and readiness indication, and returned the device to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Battery installed during evaluation
  • Battery condition or indicator status
  • Startup response observed
  • Any display or audible activity
  • Signs of physical damage or contamination
  • Final readiness status

Final Thought

Patient safety depends on having a ready defibrillator available and verifying the actual cause before assuming internal failure. Logical external checks, proper escalation, and clear documentation support safe equipment management. That is successful troubleshooting.
