DePuy Synthes TRS

Battery Will Not Charge or Runtime Is Short

Asset Type

Surgical Power System

Manufacturer

DePuy Synthes

Model

TRS

What This Guide Helps With

Power sources that do not charge normally, provide unexpectedly short runtime, or appear unreliable due to charging, contact, storage, or load-related conditions.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient and Remove Questionable Power Sources

Do not continue using a TRS power source that cannot provide reliable surgical runtime. If the problem occurs during a procedure, transition to a verified backup power source or backup surgical power system before troubleshooting.

Remove any power source showing swelling, cracking, leakage, excessive heat, damaged contacts, or other physical abnormalities from use.

Expected outcome: Clinical use continues with reliable equipment and visibly unsafe power sources are isolated from service.

2. Confirm the Reported Battery Problem

Determine whether staff are reporting failure to charge, incomplete charging, unexpectedly short operating time, intermittent operation, or a power source that appears charged but cannot operate the equipment reliably.

Identify whether the condition affects one power source, multiple power sources, one charger position, or multiple TRS devices.

Expected outcome: The problem is narrowed to a specific power source, charger position, charger, handpiece, or broader workflow condition.

3. Inspect the Power Source

Examine accessible power-source surfaces and contacts for contamination, corrosion, moisture, recessed or damaged contacts, cracks, deformation, or labeling indicating that the item should no longer be used.

Verify that the power source is compatible with the TRS configuration being evaluated.

Expected outcome: The power source is physically acceptable and compatible, or a visible condition is found that requires removal from service.

4. Verify Charger Power and Basic Operation

Confirm that the charging equipment used for the TRS power source is receiving its required external power. Check the power cord, detachable connections, accessible charger contacts, and the facility receptacle when applicable.

Test the receptacle or charger power source with approved methods rather than assuming the wall outlet is functional.

Expected outcome: The charger is powered and available for normal operation. If restoring charger power corrects the issue, verify charging behavior and stop.

5. Inspect Charger Interfaces

Inspect the charging position and accessible contacts for debris, contamination, physical damage, bent components, or incomplete seating. Confirm the power source sits in the charger as intended without needing to be held, twisted, or manipulated.

Expected outcome: The power source seats normally and charger contacts are clean and undamaged.

6. Compare Charger Positions or Known-Good Power Sources

If the charger supports multiple positions, determine whether the problem stays with one charging position or follows the power source. Alternatively, test a known-good compatible power source in the same charger position.

Change one variable at a time.

Expected outcome: The failure follows either the power source or the charging equipment, allowing the affected item to be isolated.

7. Compare the Suspect Power Source in a Known-Good TRS Device

After an appropriate charge cycle using approved procedures, install the power source in a known-good compatible TRS device where feasible. Compare operation with another verified power source under similar no-patient test conditions.

Do not judge runtime solely from a brief unloaded test if the reported failure occurs only during normal operating load.

Expected outcome: A healthy power source operates the known-good device normally, while a degraded or unreliable power source reproduces the reported short-runtime or shutdown condition.

8. Consider Usage and Storage Conditions

Determine whether repeated rapid exchanges, extended storage, environmental extremes, poor charging practices, or unusually demanding use could explain the complaint. Do not assign a battery failure solely on reported runtime without reproducing or otherwise verifying the condition.

Expected outcome: Environmental or workflow factors are identified or reasonably excluded.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

After correcting the external cause or replacing an approved power source, verify normal charging indication and stable operation in the intended TRS configuration. Confirm that the device does not unexpectedly shut down during a controlled operational check.

Expected outcome: The system operates reliably with the verified power source. If charging and operation are normal, document the results and stop troubleshooting.

10. Escalate Persistent Charging or Runtime Problems

If multiple known-good power sources fail on the same charger or TRS device, or if the problem cannot be reliably isolated externally, remove the affected equipment from service.

Expected outcome: Equipment with an unresolved power-source, charging, or runtime problem is sent for appropriate bench or manufacturer-level evaluation.

If the Problem Persists

External charging power, seating, contacts, power-source condition, charger position, compatibility, and known-good substitutions have been evaluated. The remaining problem may involve internal charging circuitry, power-management functions, the power source itself, or another service-level electrical condition.

The affected equipment should be:

  • Removed from service.
  • Labeled Out of Service.
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation.
  • Evaluated using appropriate DePuy Synthes documentation and approved test equipment.
  • Repaired or configured only by qualified personnel.

Replace rechargeable components only with approved compatible items and follow applicable facility procedures for damaged or retired batteries. Complete functional verification before return to service.

Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting and escalate is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Do not begin a procedure with a TRS power source whose charge state or runtime reliability is questionable; have a verified replacement immediately available.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"OR staff reported that a DePuy Synthes TRS power source showed inadequate runtime and the handpiece stopped during use."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering reproduced reduced operation with the suspect power source while the handpiece operated normally with a known-good compatible power source."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed the unreliable power source from service, verified normal TRS operation with a known-good power source, and returned the handpiece to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Power source identification
  • Reported charge behavior
  • Reported runtime condition
  • Charger and charging position tested
  • Charger power status
  • Contact condition
  • Known-good power-source results
  • Known-good charger or charger-position results
  • Whether shutdown occurs under load
  • Physical battery condition
  • Storage or environmental concerns
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Battery complaints should be isolated systematically between the power source, charger, equipment, and operating conditions. Verify the failure before replacing parts, never use a visibly unsafe battery, and document the comparison testing that supports the final disposition.

That is successful troubleshooting.
