Dialysate Temperature Out of Range

Asset Type

Hemodialysis (HD) Machine

Manufacturer

Outset Medical

Model

Tablo

What This Guide Helps With

Troubleshoot dialysate temperature problems caused by water conditions, setup, airflow, environment, or external operating conditions before qualified service evaluation.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

1. Protect the Patient

Do not initiate or continue dialysis with an unresolved dialysate temperature alarm or unreliable temperature control. If the condition occurs during treatment, follow the facility's clinical procedure for safely maintaining or transferring therapy.

Do not defeat a temperature-related safety alarm.

Expected outcome: The patient is protected from potentially unsafe dialysate temperature and troubleshooting occurs off patient.

2. Confirm the Temperature Problem

Determine whether the reported condition is:

  • Temperature too high
  • Temperature too low
  • Temperature fluctuating
  • Excessive warm-up time
  • Failure to stabilize during preparation
  • A temperature message that appears only intermittently

Record the exact message and when it occurs.

Expected outcome: The symptom and timing are clearly defined.

3. Verify Normal Water Preparation

Confirm the machine has completed its water-preparation process normally and has no unresolved water inlet or purification problem.

An abnormal or interrupted water supply may affect downstream dialysate preparation.

Expected outcome: Water preparation is functioning normally before temperature control is evaluated further.

4. Verify Treatment Settings

Review user-accessible treatment settings to ensure the intended temperature-related selections have not been accidentally changed.

Do not enter service menus or adjust calibration values.

Expected outcome: The accessible treatment configuration matches the intended clinical setup.

5. Inspect Ventilation and Ambient Conditions

Check that ventilation openings are unobstructed and the Tablo is not positioned against objects that restrict airflow. Look for excessive room heat, direct heat sources, unusually cold conditions, or recent relocation.

Expected outcome: The machine has adequate airflow and is operating in a normal clinical environment. If correcting the environment resolves the condition, troubleshooting can stop after verification.

6. Inspect External Fluid Connections

Check accessible water, drain, concentrate, and disposable fluid connections for kinks, restrictions, poor seating, or other conditions that could interfere with normal fluid preparation.

Expected outcome: External fluid paths are correctly connected and unobstructed.

7. Allow a Normal Preparation Cycle

With the machine off patient, perform its normal preparation sequence and observe whether temperature reaches the system's accepted condition without recurring alarms.

Do not repeatedly reset an alarm that immediately returns.

Expected outcome: Dialysate temperature stabilizes normally and the system proceeds through setup. If successful, continue to final verification.

8. Compare Behavior After Correcting External Conditions

If an obvious environmental or connection problem was corrected, repeat preparation from a normal starting condition.

Avoid making multiple simultaneous changes that obscure the cause.

Expected outcome: The temperature-related issue does not recur after correction.

9. Perform Final Functional Verification

Verify the machine completes preparation and remains free of temperature alarms through the normal pre-treatment verification process.

Expected outcome: Temperature control is stable and accepted by the machine. Troubleshooting can stop and return-to-service procedures may be completed.

10. Escalate Persistent Temperature Failure

If normal water preparation, configuration, connections, ventilation, and environmental conditions are confirmed but temperature remains abnormal, remove the machine from service.

Expected outcome: Heating, sensing, fluid-control, or other internal temperature-control problems are evaluated by qualified service personnel.

If the Problem Persists

Common external water, setup, fluid-path, ventilation, and environmental causes have been ruled out. A persistent problem may involve internal temperature sensing, heating, circulation, control, calibration, or related service-level systems.

The device should be:

  • Removed from service
  • Labeled Out of Service
  • Sent for repair or bench evaluation
  • Evaluated using appropriate Outset Medical documentation and approved test equipment
  • Repaired or calibrated only by qualified personnel

Verify proper dialysate preparation and applicable safety functions before return to service. Knowing when to stop external troubleshooting is proper troubleshooting.

Clinical Use Tip

Treat any recurring dialysate temperature alarm as a therapy-safety issue even if the machine appears otherwise functional.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Staff reported the Tablo repeatedly stopped during preparation because dialysate temperature would not stabilize."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Clinical Engineering found the unit positioned with its ventilation openings obstructed by stored supplies."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Cleared the ventilation area, repeated preparation successfully, verified stable operation without a recurring temperature alarm, and returned the unit to service."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact temperature alarm or message
  • High, low, or unstable condition
  • Point in setup where it occurred
  • Water-preparation status
  • Treatment settings observed
  • Room/environmental conditions
  • Ventilation condition
  • External fluid-path condition
  • Results after correction
  • Final device status

Final Thought

Dialysate temperature faults require safe isolation of the machine and logical verification of water preparation, configuration, external connections, and environmental conditions before assuming an internal fault. Verify stable operation before clinical use and escalate persistent failures with clear documentation.

That is successful troubleshooting.
