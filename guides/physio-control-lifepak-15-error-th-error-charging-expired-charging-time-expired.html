Physio-Control LIFEPAK 15

Error: TH_ERROR_CHARGING_EXPIRED Charging Time Expired

Asset Type

Defibrillator

Manufacturer

Physio-Control

Model

LIFEPAK 15

What This Guide Helps With

This guide helps troubleshoot TH_ERROR_CHARGING_EXPIRED on the Physio-Control LIFEPAK 15 when the defibrillator cannot complete charging within the expected time.

This condition may affect defibrillator charge readiness, shock delivery timing, synchronized cardioversion readiness, AED operation, or emergency response availability. Because this is a therapy-related error, it should be treated as a patient safety concern until the device passes required operational and defibrillator output testing.

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting

Ensure Patient Safety First

  • If the LIFEPAK 15 is connected to a patient or needed for active emergency care, remove it from patient use immediately if another defibrillator is available.
  • Provide clinical staff with a verified replacement defibrillator before troubleshooting continues.
  • Do not attempt to clear or evaluate a charging timeout while the device is relied on for defibrillation, cardioversion, pacing, or monitoring.
  • Expected outcome: Patient care continues with a known-good device.

Verify the Reported Error and Behavior

  • Confirm the exact error reported is TH_ERROR_CHARGING_EXPIRED.
  • Check the service log for therapy service code a00d.
  • Note whether the error occurred during startup, manual defibrillator charge, synchronized cardioversion setup, AED operation, or intermittent testing.
  • Expected outcome: The error is reproducible or clearly documented in the service log.

Translate the Error Into the Real-World Failure

  • TH_ERROR_CHARGING_EXPIRED means the defibrillator charging process exceeded the allowed time.
  • In practical terms, the device may be unable to charge to the selected energy level fast enough, or it may fail to complete charge readiness.
  • Treat the reported condition as a therapy-delivery reliability issue.
  • Expected outcome: The device is not considered safe for routine clinical use until objective testing confirms proper charging and delivered energy performance.

Remove the Device From Service

  • Remove the LIFEPAK 15 from clinical use.
  • Attach an Out of Service tag and follow facility policy for sequestering critical-care equipment.
  • Prevent the unit from being returned to crash cart, emergency department, transport, or procedural use until evaluation is complete.
  • Expected outcome: The device is no longer available for emergency response use while the therapy charging concern is unresolved.

Check Battery and External Power Condition

  • Verify the installed batteries are properly seated, charged, and not damaged.
  • If available, test with known-good LIFEPAK 15 batteries.
  • Connect the device to a known-good power adapter or station power source if used by your facility.
  • Look for low battery indications, abnormal shutdown behavior, poor battery retention, or power interruptions during charging attempts.
  • Expected outcome: The unit has stable power and adequate battery condition before deeper evaluation continues.

Inspect External Physical Condition

  • Check the LIFEPAK 15 for obvious damage, fluid intrusion, cracked housing, missing covers, or signs of impact.
  • Inspect therapy cable ports, paddles, pads cable, and therapy connectors for bent pins, contamination, looseness, corrosion, or damage.
  • Confirm no accessory or connector damage appears related to the reported charging failure.
  • Expected outcome: No external condition is found that would explain unreliable therapy operation.

Check Therapy Accessories

  • Inspect the therapy cable, paddles, hands-free therapy cable, and defib pads connection path.
  • Replace questionable therapy accessories with known-good compatible accessories before bench testing.
  • Confirm the correct test load, analyzer connection, paddles, or therapy cable setup is being used during controlled testing.
  • Expected outcome: The charging error is not caused by a visibly damaged or incompatible external therapy accessory.

Attempt Controlled Bench Verification Only

  • Perform testing only in a controlled Clinical Engineering environment using appropriate defibrillator analyzer equipment and facility-approved procedures.
  • Do not test defibrillation energy output on a patient or in an active clinical area.
  • Confirm whether the device can charge and discharge properly at selected energy levels.
  • Observe whether charge time is normal, delayed, intermittent, or unable to complete.
  • Expected outcome: The device either completes charge/discharge testing normally or the charging timeout is confirmed.

Review Service Code History

  • Check whether therapy service code a00d occurred once or repeatedly.
  • Look for related therapy, charge, energy, battery, capacitor, or discharge errors in the service log.
  • Document whether the error occurred after battery changes, accessory changes, physical damage, cleaning, transport, or a prior repair.
  • Expected outcome: The service history supports either an isolated event requiring verification or a recurring therapy system fault.

Do Not Return to Service Based on Power Cycling Alone

  • If the error clears after restart, continue with full therapy and defibrillator charge testing.
  • Do not return the unit to service simply because the message disappears.
  • Intermittent defibrillator charge failures may not appear during every startup.
  • Expected outcome: Return to service is based on successful functional verification, not temporary error clearance.

Stop When Resolved

  • If external power, battery, accessory, and controlled defibrillator analyzer testing confirm normal charge time and delivered energy, document the findings and return the device to service per facility policy.
  • If the error remains active, recurs, or cannot be confidently ruled out, continue escalation instead of returning the device to clinical use.
  • Expected outcome: The LIFEPAK 15 passes required operational and therapy output checks before it is made available for patient care.

If the Problem Persists

If TH_ERROR_CHARGING_EXPIRED or therapy service code a00d persists after batteries, external power, accessories, therapy cable connections, and basic physical condition have been checked, remove the device from service.

At that point, external causes have been reasonably ruled out and the condition should be treated as a likely internal therapy charging or defibrillator subsystem fault.

The device should be removed from service, labeled Out of Service, sent for repair or bench evaluation, and subjected to defibrillator energy, charge-time, and therapy function testing after repair.

Clinical Use Tip

Never troubleshoot a LIFEPAK 15 charging error on an active patient. Move the patient to a backup device first and maintain therapy continuity before evaluating the failed defibrillator. A defibrillator with unresolved charging errors should not be staged for emergency response.

Work Order Documentation (CCR Method)

CCR = Complaint, Cause, Resolution

Complaint

What was reported by the clinical staff.

Example:
"Physio-Control LIFEPAK 15 reported TH_ERROR_CHARGING_EXPIRED with therapy service code a00d. Device experienced charging time expired condition and may not reliably complete defibrillator charge cycle."

Cause

What was observed during troubleshooting.

Example:
"Battery condition, external power, therapy accessories, and external connections checked. Persistent or logged charging timeout indicates likely internal therapy charging or defibrillator subsystem fault requiring repair evaluation."

Resolution

What action was taken.

Example:
"Removed LIFEPAK 15 from service and labeled Out of Service. Processed therapy service code a00d. Sent for repair or bench evaluation. Defibrillator charge-time and delivered-energy testing required before return to clinical use."

Helpful Details to Include (If Known)

  • Exact alarm behavior, including whether TH_ERROR_CHARGING_EXPIRED occurred during startup, manual charge, AED mode, cardioversion setup, or controlled testing
  • Accessories swapped, including therapy cable, paddles, hands-free cable, pads, analyzer lead path, or test load
  • Power behavior, including battery condition, battery swap results, AC adapter status, station power status, low battery indicators, or shutdowns during charge attempts
  • Environmental factors, including fluid exposure, impact damage, storage condition, transport damage, or abnormal use environment
  • Indicator lights, including Service LED status, battery indicators, charge prompts, charging behavior, and service log entries
  • Final device status, including removed from service, sent for repair, passed defibrillator analyzer testing, returned to service, or awaiting parts/vendor evaluation

Final Thought

A LIFEPAK 15 charging timeout is not a minor nuisance alarm. It is a therapy-delivery reliability concern that must be handled conservatively. Clinical Engineering should confirm the error, rule out simple external causes, remove the unit from service when needed, and require successful defibrillator charge and energy testing before return to use. Clear CCR documentation protects the patient, the user, and the repair process.

That is successful troubleshooting.
