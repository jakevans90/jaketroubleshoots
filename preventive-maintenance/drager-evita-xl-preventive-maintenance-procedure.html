Asset Type

Ventilator

Manufacturer

Drager

Model

Evita XL

Models Covered

Drager Evita XL running software 6.1n. Confirm the model, software, installed options, gas configuration, power system, and regional documentation before service. Do not apply this procedure to Evita 2, Evita 4, Evita V300, Evita V500, or another ventilator.

Manual Reference

Dräger Evita XL Instructions for Use, Software 6.1n, document 9038732, plus current controlled Drager inspection, service, performance-test, and electrical-safety documentation for the serial number and configuration.

Manufacturer Recommendation / AEM Status

  • Every 6 months: Inspection and service by trained service personnel; check internal DC battery capacity and any optional external battery capacity.
  • Every 4 weeks: Clean the ambient-air, cooling-air, and patient-part heating filters and the heating patient-part covering grid; replace damaged filters.
  • Annually: Replace those air filters; trained service replaces the reusable expiratory-valve diaphragm and sealing washer.
  • Every 2 years: Trained service replaces compressed-gas inlet filters and the lithium data-protection battery. Replace the internal DC battery as required and no later than two years.
  • Every 6 years: Replace the clock module and pressure reducer. Replace pneumatic/electronic cooling fans at six years or 33,000 operating hours, whichever comes first.
  • Before patient use and after relevant configuration changes: Perform the Device Check and Airtight Check. Repeat the airtight check after changing the breathing circuit or humidifier.

Service boundary: The public Instructions for Use describes operator checks and scheduled intervals, but it is not the complete six-month technical inspection. Internal service, calibration, performance verification, electrical safety, and component replacement require trained personnel and the current controlled Drager procedure. Do not extend intervals through an AEM program without documented facility approval and risk assessment.

What This PM Procedure Covers

  • Identity, software, configuration, operating hours, history, and scheduled components
  • Cleaning/reprocessing, enclosure, trolley, power, gas, filters, expiratory valve, circuit, and accessories
  • Internal/external batteries, charging, capacity, and AC-to-DC transfer
  • Device Check, Airtight Check, sensor calibration, gas supply, safety valve, and alarms
  • Controlled ventilation-performance and electrical-safety testing
  • Final restoration, readiness verification, documentation, and disposition

Required Tools and Equipment

  • Current Evita XL SW 6.1n Instructions for Use and controlled Drager service/safety-test procedure
  • Calibrated ventilator analyzer, test lung, and configuration-appropriate breathing circuit
  • Approved air and oxygen supplies, hoses, and regulators
  • Calibrated pressure, flow, oxygen, and optional CO₂ test equipment required by the controlled procedure
  • Electrical-safety analyzer suitable for the complete configured system and applicable standard
  • Correct filters, expiratory-valve parts, batteries, and other due authentic service parts
  • Manufacturer-compatible reprocessing supplies, PPE, inspection light, and CMMS worksheet

Step-by-Step PM Procedure

1. Remove From Patient Use

Action: Transfer the patient to suitable backup ventilation under clinical direction. Label the Evita XL unavailable, preserve relevant logs/settings, and move it to a controlled service area.

Expected outcome: No patient depends on the ventilator during inspection or testing.

2. Verify Identity and Configuration

Action: Record asset tag, serial number, software version, operating hours, installed ventilation and monitoring options, humidifier, circuit, trolley, gas supply, internal/external batteries, and accessories. Confirm the controlled documents apply.

Expected outcome: The exact configuration and all applicable test paths are known.

3. Review History and Due Items

Action: Review prior PM/repairs, failed checks, alarm or ventilation complaints, battery runtime, sensor issues, contamination/ingress, recalls, field actions, and component dates/hours. Apply the earliest calendar or operating-hour trigger.

Expected outcome: No overdue task or unresolved safety issue is missed.

4. Clean, Disinfect, and Reprocess

Action: Follow the current IFU and facility infection-control policy. Reprocess reusable expiratory and patient-system components as specified; do not reprocess a single-use expiratory valve. Ensure all components are completely dry before assembly.

Expected outcome: The device and reusable components are clean, dry, compatible, and undamaged.

5. Inspect the Complete System

Action: Inspect the housing, display/control unit, keys/knob, alarm indicators/speaker, labels, covers, cooling openings, trolley/mounts, casters/brakes, cables/plugs, equipotential connection, gas inlets/hoses, communication ports, and accessories for damage, looseness, blockage, corrosion, heat, fluid entry, or modification.

Expected outcome: The complete system is secure, legible, unobstructed, and free of unsafe damage.

6. Service Filters and Covering Grid

Action: Inspect and clean the ambient-air, cooling-air, and patient-part heating filters and the heating patient-part covering grid. Wash filters in warm soapy water when permitted and dry fully. Replace the filters annually or sooner if damaged.

Expected outcome: Air paths are unobstructed and filter service is current and documented.

7. Inspect the Expiratory Valve

Action: Inspect the correct reusable expiratory valve, diaphragm, sealing washer, seating surfaces, water trap, and connectors. Trained service replaces the diaphragm and sealing washer annually. Reassemble exactly as directed.

Expected outcome: The valve is clean, dry, intact, correctly assembled, and current on scheduled parts.

8. Complete Scheduled Internal Service

Action: Under the controlled service procedure, verify and replace due compressed-gas inlet filters, lithium data battery, internal DC battery, clock module, pressure reducer, cooling fans, and any condition-based parts. Record part and date/hour traceability.

Expected outcome: Every applicable two- and six-year/hour-based component is current.

9. Check Power and Batteries

Action: Inspect AC and DC power paths, charging, connectors, and indications. At each six-month service, measure internal DC battery capacity and optional external battery capacity using the controlled load/method. Verify transfer from AC to DC supply and the associated indications/alarms.

Expected outcome: Power transfer is uninterrupted and each battery meets its controlled capacity criterion.

10. Verify Startup and User Interface

Action: Connect approved gas and power, start the unit, and verify boot completion, display, controls, indicators, date/time, alarm audio, cooling, communication between display and ventilator, and absence of unresolved technical alarms.

Expected outcome: Startup completes normally and all controls and indicators operate correctly.

11. Assemble the Test System

Action: Install the intended patient category, humidifier, breathing circuit, filters, temperature sensor, optional CO₂/NeoFlow components, and test lung exactly as required for the Device Check.

Expected outcome: The complete test configuration is compatible, secure, unobstructed, and leak-ready.

12. Perform the Device Check

Action: Run the complete on-screen Device Check immediately before use, following every prompt. Verify assembly, auxiliary and power-failure alarms, expiratory valve, air/O₂ changeover valve, safety valve, sensor calibrations, gas supply, humidifier, hoses, and installed temperature/CO₂/NeoFlow components.

Expected outcome: Every applicable Device Check item shows the successful result; any faulty item is resolved before use.

13. Perform the Airtight Check

Action: Run the Airtight Check after the Device Check and whenever the hose system or humidifier changes. Record the displayed leakage result.

Expected outcome: Leakage is ≤300 mL/min at 60 mbar and no circuit or component leak remains.

14. Verify Sensors and Calibration

Action: Verify flow and O₂ calibration and installed CO₂ zero/calibration, neonatal flow sensor, temperature sensor, and pressure measurements. Replace the O₂ sensor capsule if calibration is no longer possible and the specified O₂-measurement failure occurs.

Expected outcome: Every installed sensor calibrates, reads plausibly, and remains free of sensor alarms.

15. Verify Gas Supply and Safety Functions

Action: Confirm air/O₂ supply recognition and changeover, gas connections, expiratory/non-return functions, and safety-valve operation through the Device Check and controlled technical procedure. Verify the safety valve opens at the IFU criterion of 100 +5 mbar when that test applies.

Expected outcome: Gas supply, changeover, valve, and pressure-relief functions meet their controlled criteria.

16. Verify Alarms and Remote Outputs

Action: Test required disconnection, pressure, volume, apnea, oxygen, sensor, gas-supply, battery, and power-failure conditions. Verify visible/audible priority behavior, auxiliary alarm, two-minute silence behavior, and nurse-call/remote alarm output when installed.

Expected outcome: Every required alarm and installed output activates, indicates, sounds, transmits, and resets correctly.

17. Complete Controlled Performance and Electrical-Safety Tests

Action: Trained service uses calibrated equipment and the current controlled Drager procedure to verify representative volume/pressure modes, delivered and monitored volume/flow, pressure/PEEP, rate/timing, oxygen concentration, installed options, and the full six-month technical inspection. Perform electrical safety as described below.

Expected outcome: All measured values meet the current configuration-specific Drager tolerances.

18. Restore and Document

Action: Remove test equipment, restore approved configuration/settings, reprocess as required, install charged power sources, and repeat final startup, Device Check, Airtight Check, and readiness checks as applicable. Document readings, parts, calibrations, corrective work, and disposition.

Expected outcome: The Evita XL is complete, clean, current on service, and formally returned to use or quarantined.

Electrical Safety Testing

Inspect the mains cord, plug, inlet, fuses, strain reliefs, enclosure, protective-earth/equipotential path, connectors, accessible conductive parts, trolley power distribution, humidifier, and connected electrical accessories. Test the complete clinical configuration under the current controlled Drager procedure and applicable standard. Record analyzer identity/calibration, standard and method, equipment classification, test conditions, governing limits, and actual protective-earth, equipment-leakage, and applicable applied-part readings.

Do not substitute generic limits. The public Evita XL Instructions for Use does not provide the complete service electrical-safety acceptance table. Use the current Drager service documentation for the exact serial number, regional build, trolley, humidifier, and accessories. Remove the system from service for physical damage, unreliable protective earth, excessive leakage, or an incomplete required test.

Pass / Fail Criteria

Pass

  • Identity, configuration, hours, history, field actions, documentation, and service intervals are verified
  • Cleaning, inspection, filters, expiratory valve, batteries, and all scheduled components are complete
  • Startup, controls, display, alarms, remote outputs, gas supply, changeover, and power transfer pass
  • Device Check passes and Airtight Check leakage is ≤300 mL/min at 60 mbar
  • Sensors calibrate and controlled ventilation/performance results meet current Drager tolerances
  • Electrical-safety results meet the exact controlled limits for the configured system
  • Approved configuration is restored and all work/results are traceable

Remove From Service

  • Required trained personnel, controlled service documents, calibrated equipment, or backup ventilation is unavailable
  • Physical, power, gas, battery, valve, circuit, sensor, trolley, humidifier, or accessory damage could affect safe ventilation
  • Device Check, Airtight Check, sensor calibration, alarm, gas-changeover, safety-valve, battery, ventilation-performance, or electrical-safety testing fails
  • A required component, six-month inspection, recall/field action, corrective repair, or final readiness check is incomplete

Evita XL is a life-support ventilator. Never service it while supporting a patient, bypass a failed check, or return it to use with an unresolved alarm, leak, calibration fault, overdue component, performance failure, or electrical-safety failure.
