Branson Bransonic Series

Preventive Maintenance Procedure

Asset Type

Ultrasonic Cleaner

Manufacturer

Branson

Model

Bransonic Series

Manual Reference

Bransonic Ultrasonic Bath Operating Manual 100-214-294 Rev. 10 for M, MH, CPX, and CPXH models 1800, 2800, 3800, 5800, and 8800.

Manufacturer Recommendation / AEM Status

Check the tank whenever solution is changed and clean it as needed with water and a nonabrasive cloth. Branson suggests the glass-slide cavitation test monthly, while noting frequency depends on use. The manual does not list one universal scheduled-PM interval, so use a facility-approved interval for other checks under the approved AEM program, risk assessment, maintenance history, and applicable requirements.

What This PM Procedure Covers

  • Identification, series, rating, heater, drain, and accessories
  • Housing, tank, controls, cord, grounding, cover, basket/tray, and drain
  • Degas, timer, sonics, heater/temperature, and digital functions
  • Monthly manufacturer-suggested glass-slide test
  • Applicable PAT/insulation testing

Required Tools and Equipment

  • Current manual; frosted 1 x 3 inch microscope slide (Fisherbrand 12-550-343 or equivalent); No. 2 pencil
  • 1% household cleaning solution; warm tap water 49-60°C (120-140°F); calibrated timer
  • Calibrated thermometer for heated models; nonabrasive cloth; approved basket/tray
  • Electrical-safety/PAT equipment appropriate to rating and facility policy

Step-by-Step PM Procedure

1. Verify Identification

Action: Record asset, model/series, serial, rating, heater, drain, and accessories; confirm manual applicability.

Expected outcome: Identity and configuration match.

2. Review History

Action: Review performance, heat, controls, drain, noise, GFI, overheat, ingress, and repair history.

Expected outcome: Unresolved problems are identified.

3. Establish Safe Condition

Action: Remove from use, switch off, unplug, cool, drain per waste policy, and dry spills. Never disassemble.

Expected outcome: Shock, burn, chemical, and ultrasonic hazards are controlled.

4. Inspect Housing and Installation

Action: Check enclosure, feet, vents, cover, labels, clearance, stability, corrosion, cracks, and intrusion.

Expected outcome: Unit is intact, ventilated, and dry.

5. Inspect Cord and Grounding

Action: Check cord, inlet, plug, ground pin, strain relief, rating, and receptacle.

Expected outcome: Mains components are intact and correct.

6. Inspect Tank

Action: Inspect tank, bottom, seams, level mark, pitting, erosion, dents, residue, and contamination.

Expected outcome: Tank is intact and clean.

7. Inspect Basket, Tray, and Cover

Action: Verify fit/condition, no bottom contact, and at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) clearance beneath receptacles.

Expected outcome: Load support protects transducers.

8. Inspect Drain

Action: Check fitted valve, adapter, hose, threads, seal, leakage, blockage, and operation.

Expected outcome: Drain works without leak or obstruction.

9. Clean Tank

Action: On solution change, clean as needed with water/nonabrasive cloth. Never use flammable/explosive solutions, mineral acids, or chlorine bleach.

Expected outcome: Tank is clean without chemical damage.

10. Fill Correctly

Action: While unplugged and with accessories installed, fill with warm tap water/compatible solution to within 3/8 inch (1 cm) of operating line.

Expected outcome: Correct level protects heater/transducers.

11. Test Controls

Action: Test M/MH switches/timer or CPX/CPXH keypad, display, timer, power levels, modes, indicators, and standby.

Expected outcome: Controls operate consistently.

12. Degas Solution

Action: Run prescribed degas operation for 5-10 minutes.

Expected outcome: Solution is degassed for repeatable testing.

13. Test Timed Ultrasonics

Action: Run a documented cycle; verify start, stable activity, countdown, and stop.

Expected outcome: Timed sonics completes normally.

14. Test Continuous Mode

Action: Where fitted, verify continuous/constant sonics and stop control; do not use Degas for performance testing.

Expected outcome: Selected mode works correctly.

15. Test Heater

Action: On MH/CPXH, verify selection, indication, warm-up, and reference temperature. Never exceed 70°C (158°F).

Expected outcome: Heater and sensor respond normally.

16. Verify CPXH Temperature Functions

Action: Compare displayed/actual temperature and calibrate only per manual; do not force an overtemperature event.

Expected outcome: Temperature criteria pass without Err/alarm.

17. Prepare Glass-Slide Test

Action: Prepare fresh 1% solution at 49-60°C, correct level, degas 5-10 minutes, wet slide, and draw a pencil X.

Expected outcome: Repeatable test setup is achieved.

18. Perform Glass-Slide Test

Action: Hold frosted end vertically centered and activate Timed/Constant Sonics. All lead must be removed within 10 seconds.

Expected outcome: Cavitation is acceptable.

19. Perform Electrical Safety

Action: Test per rating/manual/policy. For applicable 220/230 V C/E units with MOVs, use Branson's 250 V DC insulation-test recommendation, not 500 V DC.

Expected outcome: Approved criteria pass.

20. Restore and Document

Action: Drain/unplug as required, clean/dry, restore accessories, repeat operation, and document.

Expected outcome: Unit is complete, safe, and ready.

Electrical Safety Testing

The manual calls for user-performed PAT/insulation testing and warns that MOVs in applicable 220/230 V C/E units can fail a 500 V DC test; Branson recommends 250 V DC. It gives no universal leakage or ground-resistance limit. Record analyzer, test voltage, standard/limits, ground, leakage, insulation, and pass/fail.

Pass / Fail Criteria

Pass

  • Structure, tank, drain, accessories, cord/ground, controls, modes, heater where fitted, and electrical safety pass
  • All pencil lead is removed within 10 seconds
  • No leak, fault, overheat, or Err condition remains

Remove From Service

  • Tank integrity, cord/ground, drain, control, heater, display, or electrical safety fails
  • Cavitation fails, prohibited chemical damage or fluid intrusion exists, or dry/low-level damage is suspected
  • Authorized repair or final verification remains incomplete; do not open the unit

Copy/Paste CMMS Work Order Procedure

Use the current manufacturer manual and facility-approved AEM program to determine the final PM interval. Never operate dry, immerse/disassemble the unit, use prohibited chemistry, obstruct vents, or place loads on the tank bottom.
