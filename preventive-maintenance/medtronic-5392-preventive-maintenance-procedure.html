Asset Type

External Pacemaker

Manufacturer

Medtronic

Model

5392

Models Covered

Medtronic Model 5392 dual-chamber temporary external pacemaker only. Confirm the model, serial number, date of sale/service-life status, labeling, patient/surgical cables, pole attachment, and regional documentation before service. Do not apply these limits to the Model 53401, 5388, 5348, or another external pacemaker.

Manual Reference

Medtronic Model 5392 Dual Chamber Temporary External Pacemaker Technical Manual; Model 5392 Checkout Manual; and the Medtronic-supported testing procedure for a transvenous temporary pacemaker analyzer. Use the current controlled manuals for the serial number and region. Where an analyzer-specific workflow differs, the Medtronic Checkout Manual limits govern acceptance.

Manufacturer Recommendation / AEM Status

  • At least every 12 months: Perform safety and technical checks and verify calibration using a transvenous temporary pacemaker analyzer. Repeat after any malfunction or accident.
  • Before each new patient: Clean and disinfect the external surfaces. Replace both AA alkaline batteries for each new patient.
  • Each use: Inspect for physical damage, battery-compartment corrosion/contamination, cable/connection damage, and proper controls/displays; verify the power-on self-test.
  • During continuous use: Replace batteries at least weekly and whenever the low-battery indicator appears, following the current clinical instructions.
  • After a drop, visible damage, spill, ingress, malfunction, or out-of-tolerance result: Remove from use and return to Medtronic for safety evaluation, cleaning, recalibration, or repair as applicable.

Service boundary: Do not open the sealed external case. Medtronic does not recommend field repair, and an out-of-tolerance parameter cannot be adjusted off site. Return a failed unit to Medtronic for evaluation and recalibration. Use only battery-powered test instrumentation at the patient-output connections; pacing cables provide a direct path to the myocardium.

Service-life note: Published revisions differ: the older technical manual states a five-year expected/serviceable life, while later Medtronic-supported test documentation and current product information state seven years. Record the date of sale and confirm current eligibility directly with Medtronic; do not assume a unit remains factory-serviceable from age alone.

If the facility changes the OEM interval or task set, manage the change through its approved AEM program, documented risk assessment, service history, and applicable regulatory requirements.

What This PM Procedure Covers

  • Identity, service-life status, history, field actions, accessories, cleaning, and sealed-case inspection
  • Battery compartment, fresh test batteries, power-on self-test, displays, indicators, keys, dials, lock, pause, and cables
  • Atrial and ventricular rate, output amplitude, fixed pulse width, paced AV interval, demand/sensing response, and channel independence
  • Rapid atrial pacing, emergency DOO operation, inactive/active current drain, and optional battery-removal pacing continuation
  • Patient leakage and auxiliary-current testing under the applicable standards, documentation, and disposition

Required Tools and Equipment

  • Current Medtronic 5392 Technical Manual, Checkout Manual, analyzer procedure, field actions, service-life guidance, and facility policy
  • Calibrated transvenous temporary pacemaker analyzer supporting dual atrial/ventricular channels, 500-ohm loads, output/rate/pulse-width/AV-interval measurement, and atrial/ventricular sensitivity waveforms
  • Analyzer leads and known-good compatible atrial and ventricular patient cables; inspect exact clinical cables separately
  • Two fresh IEC LR6/AA 1.5 V alkaline batteries of the specified equivalent type; calibrated battery-voltage meter when required
  • Battery-current measurement fixture and calibrated microamp/milliamp meter suitable for inactive and active current-drain testing under the controlled checkout procedure
  • Electrical-safety analyzer capable of patient leakage and patient auxiliary-current measurements for a Type CF applied part under the applicable standard
  • 70% isopropyl alcohol prep pads, sterile gauze/pad/sponge, PPE, inspection light, stopwatch, and CMMS worksheet

Safety: Never test while connected to a patient. Use only battery-powered instrumentation at the patient-output connections. Do not connect line-powered test equipment to the pacing leads unless the controlled Medtronic procedure explicitly provides safe isolation. Keep defibrillation capability available during clinical use, but do not perform PM on a clinically connected unit.

Step-by-Step PM Procedure

1. Remove From Clinical Use

Action: Confirm the 5392 is disconnected from the patient and implanted leads. Remove it from clinical availability, remove patient-use batteries and disposable cover, preserve incident/error information, and complete infection-control preparation.

Expected outcome: The pacemaker is safely isolated for bench testing.

2. Verify Identity, Age, and Accessories

Action: Record asset tag, Model 5392, serial number, date of sale, current Medtronic service-life eligibility, patient/surgical cable models, pole attachment, carrying case, disposable cover, and regional labeling. Confirm the controlled manual and checkout revision.

Expected outcome: The exact device, service-life status, accessories, and governing documentation are known.

3. Review History and Field Actions

Action: Review last annual calibration, prior repairs/returns, drops, spills, contamination, battery faults, pacing/sensing complaints, error codes, cable failures, recalls, service advisories, and field actions.

Expected outcome: Any condition requiring immediate factory evaluation is identified before testing.

4. Clean and Disinfect External Surfaces

Action: Remove batteries. Clean visible soil/blood from external surfaces using 70% IPA and allow approximately 5 minutes or until dry. Disinfect all external surfaces with 70% IPA, maintaining 15 minutes wet/damp exposure, then air-dry approximately 5 minutes or until fully dry. Do not immerse, machine-wash, autoclave, gas/gamma sterilize, or use ether, acetone, or chlorinated solvents.

Expected outcome: External surfaces are clean, disinfected, dry, and undamaged.

5. Inspect the Sealed Enclosure and Battery Compartment

Action: Inspect the case, seams, labels, upper/lower screens, status bars, keypad, keys, dials, connector ports, battery drawer/latch, pole attachment, and fasteners for cracks, dents, looseness, wear, corrosion, contamination, ingress, or tampering. Do not clean contamination beneath knobs or inside ports/compartment; return such units to Medtronic.

Expected outcome: The sealed enclosure and battery system are intact, clean, legible, secure, and free of ingress.

6. Inspect Patient/Surgical Cables and Connections

Action: Inspect atrial and ventricular cables end to end, including pins, receptacles, strain relief, insulation, clips, connectors, and channel markings. Verify correct seating and secure connection without bent pins or intermittent movement.

Expected outcome: Each exact cable is correctly identified, intact, secure, and suitable for clinical use.

7. Install Fresh Batteries and Verify Startup

Action: Install two fresh specified AA alkaline batteries with correct polarity and fully latch the drawer. Power on and verify self-test, upper/lower displays, all segments, status indicators, keys, dials, lock/unlock, pause, menu navigation, backlight, battery indication, and no nonrecoverable error. Confirm nominal startup values: DDD, 80 ppm, atrial/ventricular output 10 mA, atrial sensitivity 0.5 mV, ventricular sensitivity 2.0 mV, paced AV interval 170 ms, upper rate 110 ppm, and PVARP 300 ms.

Expected outcome: Self-test passes and all controls, displays, indicators, and nominal settings are correct.

8. Set Up the Pacemaker Analyzer

Action: Use a calibrated battery-powered transvenous temporary pacemaker analyzer, the correct atrial/ventricular cables, and 500-ohm loads. Verify analyzer calibration and configure the waveform widths/polarity specified by the checkout procedure. Keep the channels correctly identified.

Expected outcome: The test system is safe, traceable, and ready to measure both channels independently.

9. Verify Rate, Output, and Pulse Width

Action: Exercise both channels through the controlled checkout points. At minimum document: 30 ppm with 1 mA; 80 ppm with 10 mA; 120 ppm with atrial 20 mA/ventricular 25 mA; and 200 ppm with atrial 20 mA/ventricular 25 mA. Measure atrial and ventricular rate, amplitude, and pulse width.

Expected outcome: Rate is within ±2%; output is within the greater of ±0.1 mA or ±10% in the specified load/range; atrial pulse width is 1.0 ms ±10%; ventricular pulse width is 1.5 ms ±10%.

10. Verify Paced AV Interval

Action: Measure paced AV interval at the controlled rate/interval pairs: 30 ppm/250 ms, 80 ppm/170 ms, 120 ppm/100 ms, and 200 ppm/50 ms.

Expected outcome: Each measured paced AV interval is within ±5% of its test point.

11. Verify Demand Operation and Atrial Sensitivity

Action: Place the atrial channel in demand operation with ventricular output off. Apply the specified 20 ms sensing waveform and verify pacing inhibition/sense indication at 1.0, 5.0, and 10.0 mV settings; repeat reverse polarity if required by the controlled procedure.

Expected outcome: Threshold is 0.40–1.60 mV at 1.0 mV, 3.0–7.0 mV at 5.0 mV, and 6.0–14.0 mV at 10.0 mV.

12. Verify Ventricular Sensitivity

Action: Place the ventricular channel in demand operation with atrial output off. Apply the specified 40 ms sensing waveform and verify inhibition/sense indication at 1.0, 3.0, 10, and 20 mV settings; repeat reverse polarity if required.

Expected outcome: Threshold is 0.45–1.55 mV at 1.0 mV, 1.35–4.65 mV at 3.0 mV, 4.5–15.5 mV at 10 mV, and 9.0–31.0 mV at 20 mV.

13. Verify Rapid Atrial Pacing

Action: Enter RAP under the controlled workflow, press and hold the RAP activation, and verify only the atrial channel outputs while the ventricular channel stops. Test 180, 250, 360, and 800 ppm.

Expected outcome: Atrial RAP is 176–184, 245–255, 353–367, and 784–816 ppm respectively; ventricular output is absent during RAP.

14. Verify Emergency DOO Operation

Action: From a programmed 200 ppm condition, activate the DOO/Emergency key and measure the rate and both outputs.

Expected outcome: Emergency pacing remains 196–204 ppm, atrial output is 18–22 mA, ventricular output is 22.5–27.5 mA, and asynchronous emergency operation is correctly indicated.

15. Verify Current Drain

Action: Using the controlled battery-current fixture and procedure, measure inactive/off current and active/on current with backlight and lower screen off, away from pacing peaks. Do not improvise a fixture that can interrupt or reverse battery power.

Expected outcome: Inactive/off current is ≤10 µA; active/on current is ≤5.9 mA away from pacing peaks.

16. Verify Battery-Removal Pacing Continuation When Required

Action: Under the optional checkout test, use nominal 80 ppm and 10 mA atrial/ventricular outputs with backlight off and lower screen blank. After the specified charge time, remove battery power while monitoring output.

Expected outcome: Both channels continue at 10 mA ±10% for at least 30 seconds under the specified nominal conditions. This test does not authorize routine battery replacement while connected to a patient.

17. Perform Patient Leakage and Auxiliary-Current Tests

Action: Using the battery-powered/isolated method and exact connections in the controlled procedure, measure patient leakage current under IEC 60601-1 and patient auxiliary current under IEC 60601-2-31. Document standard edition, analyzer, applied-part connections, modes, limits, and highest readings.

Expected outcome: Every Type CF patient-output measurement meets the applicable standard and controlled Medtronic limits.

18. Restore, Finalize, and Document

Action: Return all settings to the approved ready state, remove test cables, install a fresh clinical battery set if required by policy, confirm the drawer is latched, perform final self-test and control/cable inspection, clean/dry the unit, and document every result. Return any failed/out-of-tolerance unit to Medtronic; do not adjust or repair it locally.

Expected outcome: No test setting remains, records are traceable, and the device is formally returned to service or quarantined.

Electrical Safety Testing

The 5392 is internally powered by two AA batteries and its patient connections are defibrillation-proof Type CF applied parts. The technical manual requires patient leakage-current measurements under IEC 60601-1 and patient auxiliary-current measurements under IEC 60601-2-31 during scheduled safety checks. Use the current standard edition, controlled Medtronic connections, and a battery-powered or properly isolated analyzer. Do not apply generic mains protective-earth tests to the battery-only pacemaker.

Pass / Fail Criteria

Pass

  • Identity, serial, service-life eligibility, accessories, history, annual due status, manuals, and calibrated equipment are documented
  • Sealed housing, battery system, labels, controls, displays, indicators, connectors, pole attachment, and exact cables pass inspection
  • Cleaning/disinfection is complete and no inaccessible contamination or liquid ingress exists
  • Fresh batteries are installed; self-test, keys, dials, lock/pause, menus, indicators, displays, and nominal startup values pass
  • Rate, output, pulse width, AV interval, atrial/ventricular sensitivity, RAP, emergency DOO, and current drain meet the stated limits
  • Battery-removal continuation passes when required; patient leakage and auxiliary current pass the controlled standard limits
  • No nonrecoverable error, out-of-tolerance measurement, unresolved field action, unsafe cable, or temporary test setting remains

Remove From Service

  • Annual calibration verification is overdue, service-life eligibility is unresolved, or qualified personnel/current controlled manuals/calibrated equipment are unavailable
  • There is case damage/tampering, a failed latch, corrosion, fluid/blood ingress, inaccessible contamination, connector damage, or an unsafe patient/surgical cable
  • Self-test, display, indicator, key, dial, lock, pause, battery behavior, or error recovery fails
  • Any pacing rate, output, pulse width, AV interval, sensitivity, RAP, emergency DOO, current-drain, continuation, leakage, or auxiliary-current result is outside tolerance
  • Any condition could cause loss of pacing, inappropriate pacing, missed sensing, unintended inhibition, inaccurate output, battery interruption, or patient electrical hazard

Copy/Paste CMMS Work Order Procedure

Do not return the Medtronic 5392 to service with overdue annual calibration verification, unresolved service-life status, case or cable damage, contamination or ingress, a failed self-test/control, any out-of-tolerance pacing/sensing/current/safety result, a nonrecoverable error, or a temporary test setting remaining.
